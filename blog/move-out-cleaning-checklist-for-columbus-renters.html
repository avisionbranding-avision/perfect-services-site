Move-out cleaning checklist for Columbus renters

A room-by-room move out cleaning checklist for Columbus, NC renters who want the full deposit back, plus when to hand the job to a professional team.

Perfect Services Team
November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Photograph every room after cleaning, so you have proof if the deposit is disputed.
  • Ovens, fridges, cabinets and bathrooms are where deposits are lost, so do those last jobs carefully.
  • A professional move-out clean includes the inside of the oven, fridge and cabinets, with a free in-home estimate first.

The landlord's walkthrough in Columbus is usually short, and it follows a pattern: kitchen, bathrooms, floors, closets. A rental with a clean oven and sparkling bathroom reads as a well-kept unit, and one with a greasy hood and dirty baseboards reads as a deduction. Here is the checklist, roughly in the order a cleaning team works, so you can do it yourself or know exactly what to hand off.

Before you start: three things to do first

  1. Read the cleaning clause in your lease. It tells you what the landlord expects and what counts as normal wear.
  2. Empty the home completely. Cleaning around boxes means skipping the floor under them.
  3. Gather supplies: microfiber cloths, a degreaser, a scrub brush, a vacuum and a mop that fits corners.

Work from the top of each room down and from the back of the house to the front door. That way you never walk across a floor you have already finished.

The kitchen, where most deductions start

Do the kitchen first, because it takes the longest and creates the most mess.

  • Inside the oven: racks out, baked-on grease off the floor and the door glass.
  • Inside the refrigerator and freezer: shelves, drawers and door seals, once the food is gone.
  • Inside the microwave, and the range hood and backsplash behind the stove.
  • Cabinets and drawers, inside and outside, including the corners where crumbs collect.
  • Stovetop, sink and counters, finished last so nothing drips back on them.

The oven is the one that people postpone and then regret. Baked-on grease needs time to soften, so spray it first and do something else for a while before scrubbing.

Bathrooms, in this order

Fixtures and glass

Start with the toilet, including the base and the area behind it, then the tub and shower. Hard water on glass doors and chrome needs a descaler and patience. Wipe mirrors last, with a clean cloth, so streaks do not return.

Grout and vanity

Grout lines that have gone gray count against the unit even when the tile is clean. A brush and a paste cleaner will help. Empty the vanity drawers and wipe them inside, then the shelves under the sink.

Bedrooms, closets and the rest

Closets get ignored in a rush. Wipe shelves and rods, vacuum the floor, and check door frames and light switches, which collect fingerprints. Dust ceiling fans and vents, and wipe window sills and the tracks. Finally, vacuum and mop edge to edge, finishing at the front door.

Before you lock the door, take photos of every room with the lights on and the doors open: inside the oven, the fridge, the cabinets, each bathroom and the floors along the walls. Date-stamped photos are the simplest way to show the condition you left the unit in if the landlord later disagrees. If you hired a team, ask for the checklist they worked from and keep it with the photos. Do it the same day, while you still have the key and the light.

Columbus is the Polk County seat, with foothill homes and quiet neighborhoods, and many rentals here change hands around a fixed date. If you are working a full week, packing a truck on the last day and cleaning at night, a team saves you the part of the checklist that takes the most hours.
