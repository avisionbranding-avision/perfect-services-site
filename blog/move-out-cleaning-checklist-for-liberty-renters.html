Move-out cleaning checklist for Liberty renters

Move-out cleaning checklist for Liberty, SC renters: room-by-room list for your deposit inspection, what the landlord checks and how the free estimate works.

You have a truck booked, boxes stacked in the hall and a landlord who will walk through on the last day. If you rent in Liberty, SC, that walkthrough decides how much of your deposit comes back. This checklist is built the way a landlord's eyes actually move through a rental, so you spend your last evening on the things that get noticed.

Start with the order, not the supplies

The mistake we see most in move-outs is cleaning too early. If you wipe a room and then carry boxes through it, you clean it twice. The order that works:

  1. Pack and empty every room first, including closets and the garage corner.
  2. Clean top to bottom: fans and vents, then shelves and cabinets, then counters, then floors.
  3. Work from the back of the home toward the front door so you never walk over clean floor.
  4. Do a last walk-through with a flashlight after the sun goes down, because that light shows the streaks daylight hides.

Kitchen checklist

The kitchen is where most deposit deductions start, because grease and food residue are easy to spot and hard to remove in a hurry.

  • Inside and outside of all cabinets and drawers, including the corners where crumbs settle
  • Inside the refrigerator and freezer, with shelves and drawers washed and the door gaskets wiped
  • Inside the oven, the racks and the stovetop, including the drip pans
  • Inside the microwave, plus the range hood and backsplash degreased
  • Sink and faucet polished, drain cleared of residue
  • Dishwasher: filter, door edge and the seal

Bathroom checklist

Bathrooms are second on a landlord's list. Soap scum and hard-water film are the two things that make a bathroom look unclean even when it is tidy.

  • Toilet, including the base and behind it where the floor meets the wall
  • Shower and tub walls, grout lines and the track of any glass door
  • Mirror, chrome fixtures and the inside of vanity drawers and cabinets
  • Exhaust fan cover, which gathers dust quickly
  • Floor edges along the tub and baseboard

Bedrooms, living areas and closets

Here the work is mostly dust and edges. Closets are the surprise: shelves, rods and floors should be empty and wiped. Check the door frames, light switches and handles for fingerprints, and wipe baseboards all the way around each room. Window sills and tracks collect dead bugs and pollen in the Upstate, and blinds often hold a gray film. A damp cloth over the slats, one at a time, takes the time it takes.

Floors last

Vacuum edge to edge, then mop from the far corner toward the exit. Hardwood and vinyl need only a lightly damp mop. Carpet should be vacuumed in overlapping rows, and if you notice stains or pet odor, say so to your landlord before the walkthrough rather than after.

What Liberty renters should check in the lease

Liberty sits between Easley and Clemson along SC-93 and US-178, so a lot of rentals here turn over with the school calendar. Many leases ask for the unit to be returned "broom clean," others require professional cleaning, and some are quiet on the subject. Read the cleaning clause before you decide how much to do yourself, and take dated photos of every room after you finish. Photos are your proof if there is a dispute about the deposit.

The spots people forget

Walk the home one more time looking only for the small things: the inside of the dishwasher door, the top of the refrigerator, the track of a sliding closet door, the inside of the garbage cabinet, the tops of door frames and the light fixtures where dead bugs collect. A quick wipe of ceiling fan blades is worth it too, since a gray fringe on the blades is one of the first things a landlord notices when looking up. None of these take long, and together they change how the whole unit reads.

  • The lease requires a professional cleaning receipt
  • You have a baby or pets and simply do not have the hours
