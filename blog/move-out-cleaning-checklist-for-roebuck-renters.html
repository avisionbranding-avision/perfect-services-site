Move-out cleaning checklist for Roebuck renters

Move out cleaning checklist for Roebuck, SC renters: room-by-room tasks, inspection spots landlords check, and a free in-home estimate before you book.

Key takeaways

  • Work through the kitchen first, then bathrooms, closets and floors last.
  • Inside the oven, fridge, cabinets and window tracks are the usual deposit-deduction spots.

The walkthrough at the end of a lease is rarely about the living room. Landlords and property managers open the oven, check the shower, slide a finger along the baseboards and look inside the refrigerator. If you are moving out of a rental in Roebuck, this checklist follows the order a cleaning team would work, so you can do it yourself or know what a professional crew covers.

Before you start

Empty the home first. Cleaning around boxes wastes time, and you cannot reach shelves and floors with furniture in the way. Open the windows, gather a vacuum, a mop, microfiber cloths, a degreaser and an old toothbrush for grout. Roebuck is a growing suburb south of Spartanburg off US-221 and SC-215, and many of its family homes have carpet in the bedrooms and hard flooring downstairs, so plan both vacuuming and mopping.

Kitchen checklist

Start here, because it takes the longest.

  • Inside and outside of all cabinets and drawers
  • Inside the refrigerator and freezer (empty, shelves washed, drawers removed)
  • Inside the oven and the microwave
  • Range hood, stovetop and backsplash degreased
  • Sink, faucet and the area behind it
  • Dishwasher door edges and filter

The oven is where most people give up. Heavy grease needs time to soften, so apply degreaser early and come back to it.

Bathroom checklist

  • Toilet inside, outside and around the base
  • Shower, tub, glass and grout lines
  • Vanity top and the inside of vanity drawers
  • Mirrors, chrome and the exhaust fan cover

Hard water spots on glass and soap film on tile are what inspectors see first. Work from top to bottom so drips do not undo your work.

Bedrooms, closets and living areas

Wipe closet shelves and rods and vacuum the floors in them. Wipe baseboards, door frames, doors and light switches in every room, because fingerprints cluster around switches. Clean window sills, tracks and blinds. A track in a sliding door holds a surprising amount of grit.

Floors last

Vacuum edge to edge, then mop hard floors working toward the exit. Do this after everything else, since dust and drips fall down while you work above.

A few habits save time. Work in one direction through the house, from the back rooms toward the front door, so you never have to re-clean a floor. Take photos of each room when you finish, since a dated record helps if a deposit is disputed. Keep your cleaning supplies and a trash bag by the door so the last pass is quick.

Two mistakes come up again and again in rentals. The first is cleaning the oven last, when you are tired and the grease needs time to soften. The second is forgetting the places no one looks at until inspection day: the inside of the dishwasher, the tops of door frames, the light fixtures with a few bugs inside and the bathroom exhaust fan cover. A few extra minutes on those spots often separates a full deposit return from a deduction.

Also read your lease before you start. Some leases ask for professional carpet cleaning or specific wording about the condition of appliances, and you will want to know before the final walk-through, not after. If you are unsure about what counts as normal wear, ask the property manager or landlord in writing. Normal wear and dirt are different things, and a good checklist focuses on dirt.

One last practical note for Roebuck renters. Take the time to check your keys, garage remotes and mailbox keys before the final walkthrough, and make sure all of the light bulbs work. Small items like these are not cleaning, but they appear on the same inspection list, and a clean house with a burned-out bulb still draws a note. If the home has pets, mention it. Hair collects in vents, closets and along baseboards, and a team that knows will plan extra time for it.

If the place was lived in by several people or pets, mention it up front, because it changes the plan. A shared rental with older appliances may need extra time on the oven and the refrigerator seals, and carpet in bedrooms often shows pet hair that you will want to vacuum slowly, in two directions.

How long does it take and when should you book?

An empty one-family home takes a team several hours, and a DIY clean often takes a full day and some of the next. If your lease ends on a Friday, booking early in the week gives you the best chance at a slot this week. Move-outs cluster at the end of the month, so ask for the date you need as soon as you know it.
