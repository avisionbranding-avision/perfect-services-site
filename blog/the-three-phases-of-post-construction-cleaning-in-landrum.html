The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Landrum

How post construction cleaning in Landrum, SC works in three phases: rough clean, final clean and touch-up. See what is included and how to get an estimate.

Key takeaways

  • Post-construction cleaning runs in three phases: rough clean, final clean and touch-up.
  • Drywall sanding creates the finest dust, and it needs multiple passes to remove.

A contractor says the job is done, you walk into the new kitchen, and there is a pale film on every surface that comes back an hour after you wipe it. That is drywall dust, and it is why post construction cleaning in Landrum is not one visit but a sequence. Here is how the three phases work, what happens in each, and when to book them.

Why one pass is never enough

Construction dust is fine and light. It settles on ledges, rides into cabinets and hides in window tracks, and it keeps moving until the last tradesperson leaves. If you clean too early, the next day's work puts it back. If you clean once at the end, the heaviest debris gets in the way of the finer work. Splitting the job into phases solves both problems, and it is how we approach a remodel or addition near Landrum, in the historic homes and in the newer builds along US-176 and I-26.

The three phases, one by one

Each phase has a different goal, a different pace and a different moment in the project when it makes sense to book it. Here is how they fit together, from the first pass to the last look before the keys change hands. Ask your contractor which trades are still coming, because that decides when each phase can start.

Phase 1: the rough clean

The rough clean happens while the structure is nearly done but before fixtures and finishes are final. It is about removing the bulk: debris, packaging, heavy dust from floors, ledges and trim. This phase is not about shine. It clears the way so the other trades can finish and so the final clean does not have to fight through grit.

  • Large debris and leftover materials cleared from rooms
  • Floors vacuumed to pull up loose dust
  • Heavy dust removed from window sills, trim and surfaces
  • Cabinets and closets cleared so they can be cleaned later

Phase 2: the final clean

This is the detailed one. After the last painter, installer and electrician are finished, the team goes through the home from top to bottom. We work in a set order: high surfaces first, then walls and fixtures, then cabinets, then floors, so dust falls onto surfaces we have not cleaned yet.

  • Fine construction dust removed from all surfaces, ledges and trim
  • Inside cabinets, drawers and closets
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills
  • Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive removed
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished
  • Floors vacuumed and washed, with multiple passes

Stickers and paint specks deserve a note. New windows, appliances and fixtures arrive with labels that leave residue, and a careless scrape can scratch glass or finish. We remove them by hand and with the right approach for each surface. If that is your biggest concern, read our guide to removing paint, stickers and residue after construction.

Phase 3: the touch-up before move-in

Even after a final clean, a house that has people moving furniture through it picks up a little dust and a few marks. The touch-up is a last pass, usually shortly before move-in or inspection, that catches what the move or the last small repair left behind. It is the quickest of the three phases, and the one that makes the home look finished when you walk in with your boxes.

Who books which phase

Landrum has everything from historic homes getting careful updates to new builds, and the right mix of phases differs between them. An older home being renovated room by room often needs a final clean after each stage, because the dust from one room travels to the next. A full new build usually runs the phases once, in order.

Homeowners finishing a remodel or an addition often book just the final clean and the touch-up. Builders and general contractors usually want all three, scheduled around their trades. Kitchen and bathroom renovations tend to need a focused final clean, because the dust travels through the rest of the house. New construction closings are the tightest on time; the closing date sets the schedule, and the team may need to be larger to meet it.

Frequently asked questions

What are the three phases of post-construction cleaning?

The rough clean removes debris and heavy dust, the final clean does the detailed top-to-bottom work after all trades finish, and the touch-up is a last pass before move-in or inspection.

Why does construction dust keep coming back after I wipe?

Drywall sanding makes extremely fine dust that settles in vents, cabinets and window tracks and gets stirred up again. Multiple passes, working top to bottom, are what remove it for good.

Can I book all three phases together?

Yes. Rough clean, final clean and touch-up can be booked together, and the price comes after a free in-home estimate with no commitment.
