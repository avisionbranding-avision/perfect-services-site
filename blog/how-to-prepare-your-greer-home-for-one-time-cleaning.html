How to prepare your Greer home for one-time cleaning

Prepare for one time cleaning Greer: a 60-minute prep routine, what to put away, what to leave for us and how the visit runs. Free in-home estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 14, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • You do not need to clean before the cleaners come. You need to clear the surfaces so the team can reach them.
  • An hour of prep, done in a set order, saves the team time and puts more of the visit into the actual cleaning.
  • Tell us about pets, alarms, parking and fragile items ahead of time and the visit runs smoothly.

Here is what we say to nearly every first-time customer in Greer: please do not scrub the bathroom the night before we come. We will do that. What helps is an hour of clearing, so our team spends the visit cleaning surfaces rather than moving things off of them.

That is the whole idea behind preparing for one-time house cleaning in Greer. Below is the order we suggest, a list of what to put away and what to leave, and a few details that make a visit go faster.

The 60-minute prep, in order

Work through the house in this order. It follows the way the team works, from the top of the room to the floor, so nothing you do gets undone.

  1. Walk the floors first. Pick up anything that is not furniture: shoes, toys, laundry baskets, charging cords, pet bowls. If we have to move it, we have to put it back, and that takes time.
  2. Clear the kitchen counters. Move small appliances, mail and the fruit bowl to the table or a cabinet. A wiped counter only works if it is clear.
  3. Empty the sink and dishwasher. Dishes in the sink block the one spot we need to degrease. If the dishwasher is full of clean dishes, unload it so we can wipe the front and edges.
  4. Bathroom counters and tub edges. Put toiletries into a drawer or a basket. Anything left on the shower floor gets lifted, cleaned under and put back, so thin it out.
  5. Bedside tables and dressers. Pile loose papers and jewelry into a tray. We dust around what is there but will not move personal papers.
  6. Set aside valuables. Cash, jewelry and prescriptions go in a drawer or a closet you can close.

That is it. You do not have to dust, mop or vacuum. If you feel you must do something extra, make the beds with clean sheets or strip them, whichever you prefer, and tell the team which.

What to leave for us

The more you leave alone, the more of the visit goes into the details. Skip these:

  • Baseboards and door frames, which we wipe by hand
  • Inside the shower and tub, where we work on soap film and hard water
  • Mirrors and glass, which we polish after the dust is gone
  • Floors, since we vacuum and mop at the end so the walkway stays clean
  • The kitchen exterior of the fridge, microwave and oven door

If you want the inside of the oven, fridge or cabinets done, flag it when you book. These are the common add-ons for a one-time visit, and they take extra time, so we plan for them rather than squeeze them in.

Tell us these things before the visit

A short message before the day removes most surprises. It helps to share:

  • Pets. Where they will be during the visit, and whether any of them are nervous with strangers. We are happy to work around a crate or a closed door.
  • Parking and access. Whether it is street parking, a driveway or a garage code, tell us which, whether you live near Trade Street or in a newer neighborhood near GSP Airport.
  • Alarms and codes. If you are not home, we need to know the entry steps.
  • Areas to skip. A home office with papers, a nursery that is napping, a room full of boxes. We stay out of anything you name.
  • Fragile or sentimental items. Point them out and we will handle them gently or leave them.

What happens on the day

When the team arrives, we start with a quick walkthrough with you, or by ourselves if you are out. We note the rooms, the add-ons and the spots you asked us to watch. Then we work from top to bottom, one room at a time. Dust falls, so we dust before we clean surfaces, and floors always come last. The kitchen and bathrooms get the longest stretch, since that is where most of the work lives.

That is the Brazilian detail-first method in practice: slow on the places people miss, such as the tracks of a sliding door, cabinet handles and the tops of door frames. A one-time visit covers the kitchen, bathrooms, bedrooms, living areas, floors, baseboards, mirrors and glass, and the trash. Two mistakes we see in Greer homes

The first is cleaning before we arrive. People spend their Saturday scrubbing, then pay for a visit that repeats the same work. Save your energy for the clear-out. The second is hiding clutter in closets and drawers we will be asked to clean. It is fine to put things away, but tell the team which closets to skip.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to tidy up before the cleaners arrive?

Clear surfaces and floors, but do not clean. Picking up toys, laundry and clutter helps the team get to every surface, and the cleaning itself is our job.

Do I have to be home during a one-time cleaning in Greer?

No. Many customers give us entry details and go to work. We just need to know about codes, alarms, pets and any rooms you want us to skip.

Should I move furniture for the cleaning?

No. We clean around and under what we can reach safely. Just let us know if you want us to skip any heavy pieces or fragile items.
