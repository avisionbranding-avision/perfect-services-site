Below is how the order of work goes when we do the floors and trim in an empty home, with the questions we hear most from renters and sellers.

Why do the edges matter more than the middle?

Because the middle of a floor is what everyone cleans. A vacuum passes down the center, a mop follows, and the room looks fine from the doorway. Then someone kneels or looks along the baseboard in raking light from a window, and the line of dust and dried grime shows up. A final inspection is exactly that kind of look.

Perfect Services is a Brazilian-owned team, and the part of our method that people notice first is hand-washing along the walls instead of running a mop head past them. The same attention is built into our move-out cleaning in Greer, where floors are vacuumed and mopped edge to edge.

The order we work in

  1. Top first. Door frames, switches and window sills get wiped before the floor, so nothing falls onto a clean surface later.
  2. Baseboards. Wiped by hand along their full length, room by room, including behind doors and around closet openings.
  3. Closet floors. Shelves and rods first, then the floor underneath them.
  4. Vacuum the whole floor, edges and corners with a tool, then the open area.
  5. Mop hard floors, starting in the far corner and working back toward the exit, so we never walk across wet floor.
  6. Final look along each wall with the lights on and the blinds open.

Moving toward the door is a small detail, but it is the kind of thing that keeps a finished floor finished.

Baseboards, door bottoms and the places a mop skips

Baseboards are the most common deduction we hear about, and they take longer than people expect. The top edge holds dust, the face holds scuffs, and the bottom edge catches whatever the mop pushed along. In a three- or four-bedroom home that is a lot of linear feet.

The bottom of interior doors and their frames are the next spot. They sit at exactly the height where shoes, pets and vacuum bumpers make contact. Sliding glass door tracks come third. Pollen and red clay settle in them, and they almost always need a vacuum before any wiping.

Other spots that tend to be missed:

  • The strip of floor under where the refrigerator or washer stood.
  • Vent covers on the floor and their surrounding frame.
  • Stair edges and the corner where each step meets the wall.
  • Thresholds between rooms, where two floor types meet.
  • The floor of every closet, not only the shelves.

Should I clean the floors myself before you arrive?

You can sweep up loose debris, but you do not need to mop. Sweeping the big pieces helps, since screws, nails from hung pictures and bits of tape can scratch a hard floor when a machine rolls over them. Remove rugs and mats and take down anything taped to the wall. Beyond that, leave the real cleaning to the order above.
