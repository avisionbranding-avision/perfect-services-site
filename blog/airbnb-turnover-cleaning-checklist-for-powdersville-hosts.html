Before you clean anything: the five-minute walkthrough

The first five minutes decide whether the turnover goes smoothly. Walk the whole property with your phone camera before touching a cloth. You are looking for three things: damage, missing items and anything the guest left behind.

  • Stains on sheets, towels or upholstery, which should be photographed before the linens go in the wash.
  • Chips or scratches on counters, tables and floors.
  • Missing remotes, kitchen items, decorative pillows or towels.
  • Food left in the refrigerator, freezer or pantry.
  • Windows and sliding doors left open.

Our Airbnb and vacation rental cleaning includes a damage and missing-item report with photos for exactly this reason. If something is wrong, you hear about it before the next guest does, and you have a dated record for the platform if you need one.

The turnover checklist, room by room

Order matters. Start with the room that makes the most mess in the air and finish with the floors, so you are never walking over a surface you have already cleaned. Here is the sequence that works in a typical three-bedroom home.

  1. Trash and linens first. Empty every bin, pull all sheets and towels, and start the first load. Reset the bins with fresh liners.
  2. Kitchen. Run the dishwasher, wipe counters, clean the sink and stovetop, and wipe appliance fronts and handles. Check the microwave and the inside of the fridge.
  3. Bathrooms. Disinfect the toilet, shower, tub and vanity, then polish mirrors and chrome. Look at the shower floor and the toilet base, where hair and soap film collect.
  4. Bedrooms. Make the beds with fresh linens, dust nightstands and headboards, and check under the bed for anything left behind.
  5. Living areas. Fluff and reset pillows, wipe remotes and light switches, and dust shelves and the TV stand.
  6. Floors last. Vacuum, then mop by hand, working toward the door you leave through.
  7. Stage it. Put everything back to match your listing photos, from the throw blanket to the welcome note.

The linens in this routine are host-provided, and laundry can happen on site or off site depending on what you arrange. That choice changes how long a visit takes, so it is worth deciding up front.

What to restock and what to report

A clean property that runs out of toilet paper still earns a bad review. We keep a restocking checklist for your supplies so the cleaner can tell you what is low instead of guessing. A simple version is a table you keep in the closet.

Toilet paper and paper towelsEach bathroom and the kitchenLess than one spare roll per bathroom remains
Soap, shampoo, dish soapDispensers and under-sink stockAny dispenser is below a quarter full
Trash bagsUnder the kitchen sinkFewer than a few left
Towels and linensCounts match the guest limitAnything is stained or missing
Coffee, filters, basicsWelcome supplies for the next guestAnything runs low

The point is to make shortages a message to you, not a surprise to the guest. For hosts who live out of town, that message after each turnover is often the only eyes they have on the house.

Getting the schedule right

Most turnover problems are schedule problems. If your platform lets guests check out at 11 and the next guest checks in at 3, the cleaner has a four-hour window and no room for surprises. Give your cleaner the booking calendar, flag back-to-back days, and keep a lockbox or smart-lock code that does not change mid-week. Busy weekends in the Upstate, such as football Saturdays, graduations and holiday weeks, are the ones to flag early so the schedule is set before the booking comes in.
