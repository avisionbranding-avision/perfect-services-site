Office cleaning before the holidays in Greer

Holiday office cleaning in Greer: a pre-holiday plan for client-facing offices, shutdown weeks and January restarts, with a free estimate for your price.

November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Plan one deeper visit before the holiday shutdown and one light visit right before you reopen.
  • Client-facing areas, the break room and the restrooms get the most attention.
  • Pricing is set after a free estimate and depends on square feet, frequency, restrooms and hours.

The week before the holidays is the last chance to clean an office while everyone is still in it, and the first week of January is when the clients come back. A good plan covers both. This is the schedule we suggest to Greer offices that want to close the year clean and open it with the lobby looking sharp.

Decide what the shutdown week will look like

Some offices close from Christmas Eve to New Year's Day, and some run on a skeleton crew. Both need different plans. If the office is empty for several days, make sure the trash and recycling are emptied and the break room fridge is cleared on the last day, because food left over a long closure is the one problem that gets worse by itself.

  1. Pick the last working day and book the cleaning for that evening.
  2. Clear desks and counters so the cleaning team can reach surfaces.
  3. Empty the break room fridge and run the dishwasher.
  4. Take out all trash, including the small bins under desks.
  5. Book a lighter visit for the day before you reopen.

Client-facing areas come first

If clients, patients or customers come in, the reception area is the room that matters most. Dust the counters and the waiting area furniture, clean the glass at the entry and the doors, and wipe the handles, since those are what people touch first. Offices along I-85 and near GSP Airport see more visitors in December than usual, between year-end meetings and holiday errands. A clean lobby carries the whole impression.

Restrooms and the break room before the break

Restrooms are cleaned, disinfected and restocked. In the break room, the microwave, the sink and the outside of the fridge get the attention, and the coffee area, where sugar and creamer collect. If your office hosts a party, ask for a short visit the next morning to reset the room, since cake on a carpet and sticky counters are easier on day one than on day three.

What to leave out of the holiday plan

Not every task needs to happen right before the break. Windows and blinds can wait for spring. Inside fridges and cabinets are for the staff to clear. Spend the budget on the things people see and touch: the entry, the restrooms, the break room, the floors and the high-touch points. We tell new offices what we would do in the first hour, in this order: trash, restrooms, break room, desks, then floors last.

A quick word on the walk-through

When we visit an office for an estimate, we walk it the way a client would: through the front door, to the reception desk, down to the restroom, into the break room. We note where the floors change from carpet to tile, how many restrooms there are and where the supplies are kept. Ten minutes is usually enough. You get a written scope for the visit, and you can see whether it matches what your office needs before the schedule is set.

It also helps to tell us the hours the building is empty and where the alarm or key access is. Access is the most common thing that delays a first visit, and it is easy to settle early.

Carpet, salt and the first week of January

December traffic leaves the entry carpet looking tired. A thorough vacuum before the break keeps dirt from grinding in over a week of nothing. When the office reopens, the first visit should focus on the entrance, the floors and the restrooms, because those show the shutdown most. If your floors are tile or hard surface, a damp mop before the doors open takes care of the dust that settled while it was empty.

Small touches clients notice

A few details are worth the extra minute before a holiday: wipe the glass on the front door at both sides, empty the umbrella stand, straighten the magazines in the waiting area and check that the restroom has a full roll in reserve. None of it is expensive. It tells a visitor that the office is looked after, and it is the kind of detail a regular cleaning team handles without being asked once the checklist is set.
