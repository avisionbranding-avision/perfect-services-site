This is the turnover checklist we would use for a busy rental along US-29, written as an order of work so nothing depends on memory.

The order of a turnover

The sequence matters as much as the list. We work in this order, every time:

  1. Walk the property and check for damage and left-behind items before touching anything
  2. Strip the beds and start laundry
  3. Reset the kitchen
  4. Clean and disinfect the bathrooms
  5. Dust, then vacuum and mop the floors
  6. Make the beds with fresh, host-provided linens
  7. Restock supplies and take out the trash
  8. Stage the space and do a final walk-through against the listing photos

Starting with the walk-through gives you an honest damage record from the moment the guest leaves. Finishing with a compare-to-the-photos check is how the listing stays accurate.

Kitchen reset

  • Dishes washed, dried and put away, or the dishwasher run and emptied
  • Counters, sink and stovetop cleaned
  • Refrigerator checked for forgotten food, shelves wiped
  • Microwave, coffee maker and toaster wiped inside and out
  • Appliance fronts and handles cleaned

Bathroom reset

  • Toilet, shower, tub and sink cleaned and disinfected
  • Mirrors and chrome wiped
  • Fresh towels laid out and trash emptied
  • Floors washed, with attention to the edges where hair collects
  • Toiletries and paper goods restocked

Bedrooms and living areas

Pull the linens, check the mattress protector and look under the bed and the sofa cushions. Guests leave surprising things behind. After dusting surfaces, furniture and light switches, vacuum and mop the floors, finishing at the door so you do not walk across a clean room. Make the beds tight, fluff the pillows and set the throw blankets the way your photos show them. Staging is small, but it is the difference between a clean rental and one that feels ready.

The restock list

Create a short list of what has to be in the house before every check-in, tape it inside a closet and keep the supplies in one bin:

  • Toilet paper, paper towels and tissues
  • Hand soap, dish soap and trash bags
  • Coffee, filters and anything else you promise in your listing
  • Spare linens and towels

The crew checks the bin against the list on each visit and tells you what is running low. It sounds basic, and it is the item guests mention most in reviews.

The damage and missing-item report

Every turnover ends with a short report, with photos, of anything damaged, stained or missing. It helps you claim from a guest, and it gives you proof of the property's condition. Hosts who skip this step find out about damage weeks later, when it is harder to tie to a stay.

Mistakes that cost hosts reviews

Three come up again and again. First, hair in the shower drain and along the bathroom floor edge, which guests notice at once. Second, a kitchen that looks clean but smells off because the refrigerator or trash was not checked. Third, beds made too fast, so the corners look rumpled in the photos guests take on arrival. None of these is hard to fix, but each needs to be on the list, because nobody remembers them at 3 p.m. with a guest arriving at four.
