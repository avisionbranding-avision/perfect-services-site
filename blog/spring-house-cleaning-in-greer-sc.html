If you live in Greer, you know the yellow film that settles on cars and porches every spring. What fewer people plan for is that the same pollen works its way into window tracks, vent covers and the entry floor, and it keeps coming back for weeks. The best time to prepare is now, in late November, when you still have time to book a team and split the work into small jobs. Here is how we would plan spring house cleaning in Greer, SC from the winter side.

Think of spring cleaning in Greer as three kinds of work: the dust and pollen jobs that must be finished before the season, the deep jobs that only happen once a year, such as kitchen degreasing and bathroom grout, and the upkeep that carries you through to summer. Separating them keeps the list from feeling endless, and it tells you which jobs to hand to a crew and which to do between other plans. A family with two kids and a dog will have a different list from a retired couple, so write yours from your own home rather than from a generic template. We have also found that people who book early get their pick of weekdays, which matters if you want the team there while you are at work.

Here is how to build the plan.

Step one: decide what spring cleaning means for your house

For most Greer homes it is not a total overhaul. It is a short list of jobs that get skipped during the year and get worse in pollen season. Write yours on one sheet before the holidays, then you can attach dates.

  • Window tracks and sills, inside and the glass you can reach.
  • Vent covers, ceiling fans and the tops of door frames.
  • Baseboards, especially along exterior walls.
  • Entry area: mats, door frame and the floor where shoes land.
  • Bathroom grout and the base of the toilet.
  • Kitchen degreasing: stovetop, hood and cabinet fronts.
  • Closets and bedrooms: wash bedding, vacuum under beds.

A winter-to-spring timeline

WhenWhat to do
Late fall to early winterWrite the list, book your estimate visit and choose a start date
January and FebruaryClosets, storage and the deeper kitchen and bathroom jobs, while the weather is cool and windows stay closed
Late February to early MarchVents, fans, baseboards and windows, before pollen is airborne
Pollen seasonKeep up a lighter routine: wipe entries, vacuum often, wash bedding weekly

Doing the deep jobs before pollen arrives is the point. If you clean windows in April, the dust blows back in the next day.

Booking tips for a February start

If you want the work done before pollen, ask for a visit in December or January, and tell us which rooms matter most. Give us a sense of your schedule, if you prefer mornings or afternoons, and if anyone works from home, so the team can plan around you. If you have a sliding door, a sunroom or a screened porch, mention it: those spaces collect the most pollen and take extra time. Keep the list short enough that every item can be finished in one visit, and add the rest to a second one.

Yes. Pollen collects in window tracks, vents, fans and entries, so those spots deserve attention before the season and light upkeep during it.
