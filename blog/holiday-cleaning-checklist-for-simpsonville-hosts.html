Holiday cleaning checklist for Simpsonville hosts

A countdown for Simpsonville hosts planning one time house cleaning before the holidays: what to do, what to hand off, and how to book a free estimate.

Key takeaways

  • Book the cleaning three weeks out; holiday weeks fill quickly
  • Handle clutter and guest bedding yourself so the team can focus on cleaning

The day the in-laws confirm they are staying through Christmas is usually the day a Simpsonville homeowner looks up at the second-floor hallway and wonders who is going to clean it. Two-story houses give you more bathrooms, more stairs and more baseboard than a ranch, and a holiday weekend does not forgive any of them. If you are searching for one time house cleaning in Simpsonville, SC before Thanksgiving or Christmas, this countdown shows what to do yourself, what to hand off and when.

Simpsonville, in Greenville County near I-385 and Fairview Road, is one of the Upstate's most popular family suburbs, with a lot of newer two-story homes. Those floor plans shape the plan below.

Three weeks out: book the clean, not the chores

Holiday weeks are the busiest time for any cleaning company, so the first step is the booking. A one-time cleaning in Simpsonville is the usual choice because it is a single visit with no contract. Decide on the date that gives you a clean house right before guests arrive, not a week earlier. The estimate visit can happen sooner, and then the date is held for you.

While you wait, do the work only you can do: pick up toys and papers, clear the kitchen counters and decide where coats and bags will go. Cleaners work faster in rooms with clear surfaces.

One week out: the checklist you handle

  • Clear and purge the fridge so there is room for holiday food.
  • Pack away anything fragile or personal in the guest room and bathroom.
  • Wash the guest bedding and set out towels, so the cleaners can make the bed fresh.
  • Decide if you want the oven, fridge or cabinets done as add-ons.
  • Move pet beds, laundry baskets and shoes out of the main walkways.
  • Tell the team about anything delicate: stone countertops, a wood table, a mirror in the entry.

What a one-time clean covers

A single visit is built around the rooms guests see. The team degreases and cleans the kitchen, cleans and disinfects bathrooms, dusts bedrooms and living areas, vacuums and mops floors, wipes baseboards and door frames, polishes mirrors and glass and empties the trash. Add-ons (inside the oven, fridge or cabinets) are separate and worth discussing if you are cooking a big meal.

In a two-story house we work in a set order: upstairs bathrooms and bedrooms first, then the main floor, then stairs and entry last, so no one walks on a finished floor. The stair rail and the spindles are the spots that usually hold the most dust, and the powder room by the front door is the one every guest will use.

Rooms people forget before company arrives

The guest bathroom gets attention, but the towel bar, the toilet paper holder and the light switch rarely do. The same goes for the front door handle, the stair rail, the dining chairs and the tops of door frames in the hall. Ask the team to focus on whichever rooms your guests will actually use, and tell them if the dining room only comes out once a year. The details matter more than the square footage, and a short list at the estimate keeps the visit on target.

If you have pets or kids at home

Family houses in Simpsonville tend to have a mudroom or a drop zone by the garage door, and that is where dirt, sports gear and pet hair collect. Tell the team which rooms are off limits during the visit, where the pets will be kept and if you want the playroom floor cleared. Pet hair along baseboards and under sofas adds time, so mention it at the estimate. With small children, we skip the toys and wipe only what is clear of them, so a quick tidy before we arrive helps the clean go deeper.

A first visit with a cleaning service is also the easiest way to find out if you want regular help. Many people book a single one-time house cleaning in Simpsonville for the holidays and then decide to continue.

The day before guests arrive

This is the finishing pass, and it takes about an hour once the cleaners have been through. Wipe the bathroom counters one more time, set out fresh hand towels, run the vacuum over the entry and put the trash out. Guests notice an empty sink, clear counters and a fresh bathroom before they notice anything else.

If you host regularly, a deep cleaning once a year before the holidays makes the rest of the year easier, and house cleaning on a recurring schedule keeps the baseline high.
