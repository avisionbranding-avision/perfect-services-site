Do I have to do the turnover cleaning myself?

No, and a turnover is the part we would hand off first. A turnover means stripping beds, resetting the kitchen, scrubbing bathrooms, mopping and hauling trash, all inside a window that can be a few hours. Bending over a tub, lifting a wet comforter and carrying bags to the bin are exactly the tasks that stop being worth it after a few seasons.

Our team takes the whole reset: the kitchen, the bathrooms, floors, beds made with the linens you provide, and trash out and bins reset. You keep the part that suits you, such as greeting guests or answering messages.

What happens between checkout and the next guest

We work in the same order every time, because order is what keeps a turnover from being rushed:

  1. Walk through every room first and look for anything left behind, broken or out of place.
  2. Strip the beds and start the linens.
  3. Reset the kitchen: dishes, counters, appliance fronts, sink and the inside of the microwave.
  4. Clean and disinfect the bathrooms.
  5. Vacuum and mop the floors, finishing at the front door.
  6. Make the beds, restock the supplies on your list and stage the rooms to match your listing photos.

The first step matters most to families checking in from a distance. If we find a damaged item or something missing, you get a report with photos, so a son or daughter can see it from another state without driving over.

Checklist for families managing a rental from a distance

If you are an adult child taking this over for a parent, set these up once and the weekly work gets lighter:

  • A lockbox or smart-lock code the cleaning team can use, with a backup contact who lives nearby.
  • A short written list of what to restock (paper goods, soap, coffee) so nobody has to guess.
  • One closet that stays locked for the owners' own belongings.
  • Clear notes about which linens belong to guests and which are personal.
  • A shared place for the damage and missing-item photos, so both generations see the same thing.
  • A standing arrangement for the days around holidays, when bookings and cleanings cluster.

Many older hosts also tell us that they would rather not be home when strangers arrive. If that is you, the staging step, where we set pillows, towels and the entry to look like your listing photos, means the guest sees the same home the photos promised.

How long does a turnover take?

A small suite can be reset quickly, while a larger home with several bathrooms and a full kitchen takes longer.
