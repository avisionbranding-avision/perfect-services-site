Move-in cleaning in Greer: what to clean before you unpack

Move in cleaning in Greer SC: a room-by-room order for what to clean before you unpack, plus how to get a free estimate for an empty-home clean.

Key takeaways

  • Clean an empty home before the furniture arrives; shelves, drawers and floors are only easy to reach once.
  • Start with the kitchen and bathrooms, then closets, then floors last.

The truck is not due until Saturday, the keys are in your hand, and the house is completely empty. That quiet window is the only time you will ever get to clean the inside of every cabinet, closet and drawer without moving a single box. Move in cleaning in Greer is really about using that window well, whether you do it yourself or hire a crew.

Previous owners and tenants rarely leave a home dirty on purpose, but "move-out ready" and "ready to put your dishes away" are not the same standard. Here is the order we would follow, and where we usually find the spots people regret skipping.

Start where the food goes

Open the kitchen cabinets and drawers first, before anything is stored in them. Shelf liners hide crumbs, and the corners near the sink often have a faint film. Wipe the inside and outside of each cabinet, including the tops of the handles, where fingerprints build up.

Then move to the appliances. The refrigerator and freezer need to be cleaned inside before they hold food, and the oven and microwave deserve a full scrub. Degrease the range hood, stovetop and backsplash last, so drips from above do not land on surfaces you already cleaned.

Bathrooms: the three places that get missed

Everyone scrubs the toilet bowl and the tub. In an empty home, check these instead:

  • The base of the toilet and the area behind it
  • Grout lines in the shower and on the floor
  • The inside of the vanity, under the sink, where moisture sits
  • Shower glass and tracks, which collect soap film
  • Exhaust fan covers, which hold dust that falls back on the room

If the home has been vacant for a while, run the water in every sink and tub for a minute first. It clears stale water from the traps and tells you right away whether anything is draining slowly.

Closets, laundry and the rooms nobody photographs

Listing photos focus on the kitchen and living room. The laundry room, hall closets and garage entry are where dust and lint pile up. Wipe closet shelves and rods, vacuum the floors, and check the dryer vent area and the back of the washer hookup, since these are much harder to reach once the machines are in.

Wipe the baseboards, door frames and light switches in every room. In a house this empty, they are the first things you see when the lights come on, and one pass with a damp cloth makes the whole place feel newer.

One more habit from the empty-house stage: open every window and door before you start, even in cooler months, and leave them open for a few minutes after you finish. Cleaning products and stale air are easier to live with when the house has had a full air change, and any lingering paint or carpet smell from the last occupants clears faster. Keep a bucket and a few cloths near the door for whatever turns up later, because there is always one more smudge on a wall once the lamps are plugged in.

Floors last, windows before them

Clean windows, sills and tracks before you do the floors, because dust falls downward. Spring pollen in the Upstate collects in window tracks, and a home that sat closed for a few months will have more than you expect. Vacuum first, then mop edge to edge, working toward the exit so you do not walk across wet floor. Carpet should be vacuumed in slow, overlapping passes. If you plan professional carpet work, schedule it before furniture goes in.
