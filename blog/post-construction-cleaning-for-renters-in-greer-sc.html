Your landlord finished the kitchen update on Friday, the painter left Saturday, and Monday morning there is a pale film on the counters, the window sills and the top of the fridge. Living in a rented home in Greer while work happens around you is its own situation, and post-construction cleaning for renters in Greer works a little differently from a homeowner's remodel.

First, who is responsible?

Renters often do not know whom to call. Check your lease and ask the landlord or property manager in writing. In many cases the owner arranges final cleaning after repairs; sometimes the tenant is told to handle it. Do not hire anyone to work inside the unit without permission, and keep any messages. If the owner agrees to pay, a team can work from a single estimate and invoice.

What construction dust really is

Drywall sanding produces the finest dust, lighter than household dirt, and it hangs in the air for hours before landing everywhere. It gets into return vents, ceiling fans, window tracks and the folds of blinds. Paint overspray leaves specks on glass, and new fixtures often arrive with stickers and adhesive still on them. Dry dusting pushes the powder around, so the team works in passes and washes floors more than once.

What to do in the first day yourself

  • Close doors to rooms that were not part of the work.
  • Cover or bag anything you cannot wash: books, open shelves, electronics.
  • Do not run the HVAC fan until the first clean is done, if you can avoid it.
  • Change the air filter once the dust has settled.
  • Photograph any damage or leftover mess for the landlord.
  • Leave floors alone; sweeping at this point mostly lifts dust back into the air.

What the team covers

Our post-construction cleaning follows the dust from top to bottom. That means fixtures, vents and ceiling fans first, then trim and ledges, the insides of cabinets, drawers and closets, windows, glass, tracks and sills, bathrooms and new fixtures, and finally floors vacuumed and washed in several passes. Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive are removed, and a touch-up pass catches what settled while we worked.

Renters sometimes ask whether the dust is harmful. We are cleaners, not safety inspectors, so we will not give medical advice, but the common-sense approach holds: ventilate when you can, keep children and pets out of the affected rooms until they are cleaned, and ask the contractor about anything unusual like an old finish being sanded. If you suspect something beyond ordinary drywall or paint dust, raise it with your landlord before cleaning starts.

Photos help here too. Take pictures of the finished work, of any chips in the new counters or marks on floors, and of the mess left behind. Your landlord may want them if the contractor needs to come back, and a dated record keeps the cleaning conversation separate from the repair conversation.

Something we notice in rentals is how quickly the work area spreads. Dust travels through the whole home on shoes and through open doorways, so a kitchen project can leave a film in the bedroom closet. A good clean follows that trail rather than stopping at the room that was renovated. Your estimate visit will include looking at the adjacent rooms and agreeing what is in scope.

When the dust is gone, it is a good moment to reset the household routine. A regular house cleaning or a deep cleaning visit afterward catches the last of the fine residue that reappears over the next couple of weeks, especially around vents and on dark furniture where it shows most.

How long it takes

A single room can be a matter of hours; a whole-home job takes longer and often benefits from two passes on separate days so the final dust is caught. We can tell you more at the visit.

A final reminder for renters: you are allowed to ask questions. Ask your landlord when the work will finish, whether a cleaning is already planned, and who to call if the dust comes back. Ask the contractor whether the air filter was changed. A few plain questions at the start usually prevent the most common frustration, which is living in a half-finished home with no end date. Write the answers down, and we will fit our visit around whatever schedule you end up with.
