Cleaning a home with a new baby in Greer

The first week with a baby, nobody wants to hear about the baseboards. What most parents tell us they want is simpler: the floor should be clean enough to put a blanket on, the kitchen should not smell like a chemical aisle, and somebody else should do the bathroom. This post is for the weeks around a birth, written by a team that cleans family homes in Greer every week.

Before you bring the baby home

If you can, schedule a deep reset in the last weeks of pregnancy, or ask a friend to book one as a gift. A clean baseline means you only maintain it afterward. Priorities, in order:

  • Floors in the nursery and living room, vacuumed and mopped
  • Bathrooms, the toilet and the shower, since you will not want to scrub them later
  • Kitchen counters, sink and the fridge, where bottles and food will live
  • Washing machine area, so laundry is not a chore on day one
  • Window sills and fan blades in the room where the baby will sleep
  • Entry, with a clear spot for shoes

A heads-up about timing: the days before a due date are the busiest for homes that want a team in. Ask for a free in-home estimate a few weeks ahead rather than the week of.

What non toxic house cleaning really means

There is no single legal definition of "non toxic" for cleaning, so ask what it means for the company you hire. For us, green cleaning in Greer means low-odor products that are family- and pet-friendly on request, gentler products in kitchens and bathrooms, microfiber cloths that capture dust instead of spreading it, and a fragrance-free option for sensitive households. You can also have us use your own products if you already have a brand you trust.

The method matters as much as the label. Hand wiping uses less product, and rinsing surfaces that touch food or mouths is simply good practice. For medical questions about your baby, your pediatrician is the right person to ask.

What the team does in a home with a baby, step by step

Here is the walkthrough we do in the first hour of a new family's visit, so you know what to expect.

  1. We ask where the baby sleeps and where they play, so those rooms are done carefully.
  2. We ask if any products are off-limits and note them for the visit.
  3. We start in the kitchen, wiping counters, sink and appliance fronts with the gentler products, and leave bottle drying areas as we found them unless you say otherwise.
  4. Bathrooms come next, disinfected, with the floor done last.
  5. Bedrooms and living areas are dusted top to bottom, and floors are vacuumed and mopped from the farthest room to the door.
  6. We move quietly in rooms where the baby is napping, or work around it, and finish those rooms last.

You do not need to leave the house, but many parents take the chance to go for a walk or sleep in another room.

Small habits between visits

Between professional visits, a few small habits keep the floor safe for a crawling baby. Take shoes off at the door, since Greer's red clay comes in on everything after rain. Wipe changing surfaces and high-chair trays every day. Wash play mats weekly. Keep one microfiber cloth for the kitchen and another for the bathroom, and wash them separately.
