Start with a simple cleaning plan

Before you pick products or a provider, write down three things:

  • Which areas customers see (entry, reception, restrooms)
  • Which areas staff use most (desks, break room, shared equipment)
  • What your floors are made of: carpet, tile or hard flooring

Different floors need different care, so the plan should name them. Once that is done, sort the tasks into daily, weekly and monthly lists.

Daily cleaning checklist

These tasks keep the office presentable and healthy day to day:

  • Empty trash and recycling
  • Wipe desks, counters and workstations
  • Disinfect high-touch points: door handles, light switches, keypads and shared devices
  • Clean and restock restrooms, including toilets, sinks, mirrors and soap
  • Wipe the break room counter, sink and the outside of the microwave and fridge
  • Vacuum or sweep and mop high-traffic floors
  • Clean glass doors and entry areas

Weekly and monthly tasks

These jobs do not need to happen every day, but when they are missed they show quickly:

WeeklyDust reception and waiting areas, vacuum under furniture, clean inside the break room fridge, wipe chair arms and phones
MonthlyDust vents and high shelves, clean baseboards, wipe light fixtures, spot-clean walls and doors, check window sills

DIY or hire a commercial cleaner?

Many owners start by cleaning themselves, then reach a point where it takes too much time away from customers. Hiring professionals makes sense when:

  • Your staff spends more time cleaning than working
  • You need cleaning after hours so customers never see it
  • Restrooms and shared spaces are used heavily
  • You want a consistent standard with a written scope

Match the checklist to your type of business

The same checklist does not fit every business. A hair salon needs extra attention to floors, sinks and station surfaces. A retail shop cares most about the entry, glass and display areas. A small professional office focuses on reception, desks, restrooms and the conference room. A studio or clinic will also want a firm routine for shared surfaces and waiting areas.

Write down your busiest hours and your quietest ones. Cleaning during the quiet stretch, or after closing, means your team and customers never walk through a wet floor or a pile of supplies.

Restrooms and the break room deserve extra attention

Two spaces shape how people feel about your business more than any others: the restroom customers use and the break room your team uses. A restroom should be checked and restocked regularly, with toilets, sinks, mirrors and floors cleaned and disinfected. In the break room, focus on counters, sink, microwave and refrigerator exterior, and clear out old food on a set day each week. Small habits like these cut odors and keep the team comfortable.

  • Check soap, paper towels and toilet paper every day.
  • Wipe handles and faucet knobs, which many hands touch.
  • Empty break room trash daily, not just when it is full.
  • Schedule a deeper restroom clean on a regular cycle.

Tips to keep an office clean between visits

  1. Put a small trash can at every workstation, and a recycling bin near the printer.
  2. Keep disinfecting wipes at the front desk and in the break room.
  3. Ask staff to clear desks at the end of the day so cleaners can reach surfaces.
  4. Put walk-off mats at the entrance to cut down on dirt and pollen.
  5. Review the checklist with your cleaner every few months and adjust it.
