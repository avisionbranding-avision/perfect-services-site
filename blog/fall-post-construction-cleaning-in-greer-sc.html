Fall post-construction cleaning in Greer, SC

Fall post construction cleaning in Greer, SC: finish a remodel before the holidays with a phase plan, dust checklist and a free in-home estimate.

Key takeaways

  • A fall remodel needs a firm cleaning date so the house is ready for guests, inspections or closings.
  • Booking rough, final and touch-up phases together keeps the schedule tight.

Most remodels in Greer that start in late summer are supposed to end before Thanksgiving. They rarely do, and the last week before guests arrive is when the question comes up: who is going to clean this? Fall post construction cleaning in Greer, SC works best when it is planned as part of the schedule, not left to the weekend after the contractor leaves.

Working backward from the holiday date

Start with the date you need the house ready, then count back. A countertop install or flooring job usually ends a few days after the date on the contract. Painters and trim carpenters tend to be last. Cleaning comes after all of them, which means the cleaning day has to sit in the gap between the final trade and the date you need.

Rough cleanAfter demolition and sandingDebris, large dust, protective coverings off

If the schedule slips, the rough and final phases can sometimes be combined, but we would rather see the space first. That is what the free estimate is for.

What is different about fall

Cooler weather means windows stay closed and the heat comes on. That keeps leaves and pollen out, which helps, but it also means dust has nowhere to go but the vents. The first time the furnace runs after a remodel, it often blows a fine layer of dust back across clean surfaces. We clean vent covers and the tops of fixtures first so the first heating cycle does not undo the work.

Mud is the other fall factor. Red clay on boots tracks across new floors every time a tradesperson walks in. Floors are therefore always the last thing we clean, after the crew is done and after the entryway is mats-and-shoes ready.

A checklist for the day before cleaning

  • Confirm all trades are finished and have taken their tools and ladders.
  • Remove leftover materials, paint cans and packaging from the space.
  • Leave the power and water on so we have light and can rinse.
  • Point out any surface you are worried about, like new stone or unsealed wood.
  • Leave a clear path to the main work area.
  • Note which rooms the family will use first, so those finish first.

What we clean, in order

Our team works from the top of the room down: fans, fixtures and vents, then trim, then cabinets and closets inside and out, then windows and tracks, then bathrooms, and floors last. Paint specks and sticker residue come off with hand tools, never harsh scraping on finished surfaces. What a good final result looks like

When the job is finished, you should be able to run a hand along a window sill, a baseboard and the top of a door frame and come away with a clean palm. Cabinets should open to clean shelves, bathroom tile should be free of grout haze, and glass should be clear without paint specks or stickers. Floors should have no gray film and no footprints. Those are the things we check before we leave, and they are the things you can check too. If something is missed, tell us the same day and we come back to fix it.

Fall light is low and golden, and it shows streaks that midday sun hides. We try to inspect glass and floors from the angle you will see them from at six in the evening when the lamps are on. That is also how guests will see the room. If your remodel includes a kitchen, give the fridge and oven a wipe after your first meal; new appliances can have a protective film that needs a second look.

Frequently asked questions

How far ahead should I book cleaning after a fall remodel?

As soon as you have a rough finish date. Holiday weeks fill early, and the free estimate lets us hold a day close to your deadline.

Why does dust come back when the heat first turns on?

Fine dust settles in vents and ducts during construction. The first heating cycle blows some of it out again, so we clean vent covers and high surfaces before the final floor wash.

Do you clean the whole house or only the project area?

We look at the whole path of dust. Fine drywall dust travels, so hallways, bedrooms and closets near the project are usually part of the estimate.
