5 deep cleaning mistakes Greer homeowners make

Deep cleaning mistakes Greer homeowners make, from scrubbing grout dry to skipping prep, with fixes and how a free in-home estimate works.

The five mistakes, one at a time

Each one is common and each one is cheap to fix before the day you start.

Mistake 1: calling a regular clean a deep clean

A regular visit keeps a home at a good level. A deep clean goes further: baseboards and door frames hand-wiped, cabinet fronts and handles degreased, grout scrubbed in the kitchen and bathrooms, ceiling fan blades, vents and fixtures dusted, window sills, tracks and blinds cleaned, and the area behind and around the toilet base.

The fix is to write the list before you start. If a task is not on it, it will not happen, and a day that goes to vacuuming leaves no hours for grout. Compare the two services in our deep cleaning versus regular cleaning guide.

Mistake 2: scrubbing before the product has time to work

Grout, soap scum and cooktop grease do not come off with force; they come off with time. Spray the shower and the stove, leave them for the time the label says, and then scrub. People who skip the wait end up with sore arms and the same stains.

The fix: work in two passes. Apply the product to the bathroom and the kitchen, move to the next task, then return to wipe. Never mix products to make them stronger, and open a window.

Mistake 3: using the wrong tool on the wrong surface

A steel scrubber on a stovetop, an acidic cleaner on natural stone and a wet mop on an unsealed floor have all caused damage we have seen. Hardwood in older Greer homes near Downtown Greer and newer finishes near GSP Airport call for different care.

The fix: test any product in a hidden corner first, and match the tool to the surface. If you are not sure about stone or an older finish, leave it, and ask us to check it at the walkthrough before an estimate.

Mistake 4: skipping the preparation

Deep cleaning moves fast only when surfaces are clear. Toys on the floor, dishes in the sink and clothes on the bed all add time. When a crew spends the first hour picking up, it is an hour not spent on grout.

  • Clear counters, bathroom shelves and the tops of dressers
  • Put away toys, laundry and loose papers
  • Decide which add-ons you want: inside the oven, fridge or cabinets
  • Tell us about pets, locked rooms and delicate surfaces
  • Leave access instructions for the day of the visit

Our preparation guide for deep cleaning goes room by room.

Mistake 5: waiting until the home is overwhelmed

The last mistake is timing. Build-up becomes harder with every month, and a home that has gone a year without a professional clean takes much longer than one that gets a seasonal reset. People often book before a holiday, a family visit or a listing photo shoot, when slots fill first.

The fix: schedule a deep clean twice a year, such as spring after pollen and fall before the holidays, and follow it with lighter regular visits.

A one-day plan that avoids all five

If you do the work yourself, order the day like this. Start with a written list and a clear counter. Apply products to the bathrooms and the stove first and let them sit. Dust fans, vents and tops of cabinets while they work. Then scrub the grout, wipe baseboards and door frames, and clean kitchen cabinet fronts. Do glass and mirrors next, and vacuum and mop last, finishing at the door you leave through. Eat lunch in the middle, because a deep clean done in a rush becomes a regular clean.
