Why spring move-outs are harder on a house

Spring in the Upstate brings heavy pollen, and a yellow-green dust settles on window sills, car-side door frames and anywhere a window was cracked. Red clay is the other factor. After a wet March, clay tracks in on shoes and leaves orange-brown marks along entry floors, mudroom corners and the first few steps of the stairs. Neither one is dramatic, but both show up the moment a home is empty and the afternoon light comes in low.

That is why a spring move-out clean puts extra time into the places pollen and clay collect: window tracks, screens and sills, the threshold of the back door, garage entry floors and baseboards near every exterior door.

A planning timeline you can work back from

You do not need to book a cleaner today, but a rough order of operations saves stress in April.

  1. Winter: decide your move date or lease end, and ask for a free in-home estimate so you know what the visit will look like.
  2. Six weeks out: start purging closets, the garage and the attic. Less stuff means a faster clean and fewer movers.
  3. Two weeks out: patch nail holes and touch up paint, which should happen before cleaning, not after.
  4. Move week: movers finish, then the cleaning team comes while the house is empty.
  5. Final walkthrough: the landlord, agent or buyer looks at the home, and you have a day of buffer if something was missed.

Renters and sellers plan differently

A tenant is usually working to a lease end date and a deposit, so the priority is matching the condition the landlord documented at move-in. Photos from the day you got the keys are the best reference, and the cleaning team can use them to know what to prioritize, such as inside the oven or the grout lines in the shower. A seller has a different clock. The house is often listed in March or April to catch the spring market, and the move-out clean may come after an accepted offer with a closing date only a few weeks off.

Landlords turning over a property between tenants have a third situation: a short gap, a new tenant waiting and a list of repairs. In all three cases the cleaning is easier when the date is known early, because a team can reserve the day and the estimate visit can be done while the home is still furnished. Seeing a furnished home is useful for us, because we can tell which items will be gone and which closets will need the most time.

A quick checklist for the last week

  • Remove the last trash bags, spare paint cans and anything left in the garage.
  • Open all the blinds and wipe off loose pollen from the sills so tracks are visible.
  • Leave the water and power on until the cleaning is done.
  • Photograph the clean house if you are a tenant, in case of a deposit dispute.
  • Put the keys and any garage remotes somewhere you will not forget.
