Winter deep cleaning in Greer: indoor season tips

Winter deep cleaning Greer SC: what to tackle while the house is closed up, the first hour of a deep clean, and how our free in-home estimate sets the price.

Perfect Services Team
November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Winter keeps windows shut and heat running, so dust, grease and humidity build up in places a regular clean skips.
  • A deep clean covers baseboards, grout, vents, window tracks and cabinet fronts.
  • Perfect Services prices a deep cleaning only after a free in-home estimate.

The first thing we notice when we walk into a Greer home in January is the air. The house has been shut since November, the heat has been running, and everything that normally leaves through an open window has stayed. Dust is on the vents. The kitchen has a thin film from months of cooking. The shower grout looks darker than it did in October. That is exactly why winter is a good time for a deep cleaning, and this guide explains what changes inside the house and what the work covers.

What the first hour of a deep clean looks like

People are often curious about order, so here is how the team starts. We walk the house with you first, noting problem spots and anything you want left alone. Then we start high: ceiling fan blades, vents, light fixtures and the tops of cabinets. Dust and grease fall as we work, so we do the floors last. Kitchens come next, with cabinet fronts and handles degreased, then the stovetop, hood and appliance fronts. After that, bathrooms: grout lines scrubbed, tub and shower scaled and wiped, mirrors and chrome polished. By the time the first hour ends, the house is only starting to show a difference, and the bigger change comes in the second and third.

Why winter builds up what summer does not

  • Closed windows: dust, pet dander and cooking residue stay inside.
  • Heating: forced air pushes dust through vents and settles it on shelves.
  • Humidity: Upstate winters can be damp, and a bathroom without an open window holds moisture, which feeds mildew in grout.
  • More cooking: roasts, baking and slow cooker meals leave grease on cabinets and hood filters.
  • Wet shoes and boots: red clay and rain track in and dry into the floor edges.

The indoor deep clean checklist

This is what our deep cleaning service covers on top of a regular visit:

  • Baseboards and door frames hand-wiped
  • Cabinet fronts and handles degreased
  • Grout lines scrubbed in kitchen and bathrooms
  • Ceiling fan blades, vents and light fixtures dusted
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds cleaned
  • Everything in a regular house cleaning, including floors, bathrooms and kitchen surfaces

Is deep cleaning worth it in winter?

People ask us this directly, and the honest answer is that it depends on how long it has been. If you had a thorough clean in the fall, a regular visit is probably enough. If it has been a year or more, or if you are moving toward spring with a baby on the way, a house full of pets or a plan to host, a deep clean resets the house so that regular visits are easier afterward. Many customers do one deep clean in winter and then move to a recurring schedule, because the deeper work does not need to be repeated every month.

How long does a winter deep clean take?

A two-person team usually needs most of a day for an average Greer home, longer if the home is large or very dirty. A first-time deep clean often takes more time than the same house the next time, which is one more reason to keep a regular schedule afterward. If you have a strict deadline, such as a visit from family in February, say so when you ask; we can often book this week or next if you ask early.

Spots that surprise people

A few things come up in nearly every winter deep clean. The first is the top of the refrigerator, which collects a sticky layer of dust and kitchen grease. The second is the vent cover in the bathroom, where lint and moisture bond into a gray mat. The third is the window track, where condensation from cold mornings leaves dirt and sometimes mildew. People who have lived in the same house for years often stop seeing these, and they come back to the same conversation: they did not realize it had been that long. Pointing them out helps you decide what you want covered when we walk the house.

What to do before and after

  • Clear counters and floors so we can reach every surface.
  • Tell us about delicate finishes, antiques or surfaces to avoid.
  • Wash curtains and bedding yourself, which saves time.
  • After the visit, wipe up spills quickly and open the window when you shower for a few minutes.
