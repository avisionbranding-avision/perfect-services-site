How to get your security deposit back in Greer, SC

How to get your security deposit back in Greer, SC: what landlords check, a move-out cleaning plan, documentation tips and how to get a fixed cleaning price.

Your security deposit is real money, and the last week of a lease is when most of it is won or lost. If you are renting in Greer, a clear plan for cleaning and documentation can make the difference between a full refund and a list of deductions. This guide covers how to get your deposit back, with a focus on move-out cleaning in Greer, SC.

Greer renters live in a mix of apartments, townhomes and single-family homes across Greenville and Spartanburg counties (ZIPs 29650, 29651 and 29652), near I-85, SC-14, SC-101 and US-29. Whatever you rent, the basic rules are the same: leave the home as clean as you found it, apart from normal wear, and prove it.

Know your lease and the law

Start with your lease. It should describe the condition the home must be in when you leave, any specific cleaning requirements and how to give notice. Landlords generally can deduct for damage and for cleaning needed beyond normal wear, but they cannot treat ordinary aging as damage. If you have questions about your legal rights, check the lease and consult official South Carolina resources or a legal professional, since we are a cleaning company and not a source of legal advice.

Document everything

Documentation is the cheapest way to protect your deposit.

  • Take dated photos or video at move-in, ideally with the move-in condition report
  • Take another set of photos in every room right after you finish cleaning
  • Photograph appliance interiors, bathrooms and floors, since these are the common sticking points
  • Keep receipts for any professional cleaning
  • Request a walk-through with your landlord or manager, and ask for any list of issues in writing

The cleaning that matters most

Landlords and property managers tend to look at the same places:

  • Kitchen: inside the oven, refrigerator and microwave; cabinets inside and out; stovetop and range hood
  • Bathrooms: toilet, tub and shower, grout, vanity and mirror
  • Floors: vacuumed carpets, mopped hard floors, clean corners and edges
  • Walls and trim: baseboards, switch plates and door frames
  • Windows and closets: sills, tracks and shelves

Common reasons deposits are reduced

  • Greasy ovens and stovetops
  • Dirty refrigerators with leftover food or odors
  • Stained or soap-scummed bathrooms
  • Pet hair or stains on carpet
  • Marks on walls and dust on baseboards
  • Items left behind or trash in the home

What counts as normal wear?

Understanding the difference between wear and damage helps you decide where to spend effort. Normal wear usually includes light scuffs, small nail holes, faded paint and carpet that has flattened in high-traffic areas. Damage and neglect usually mean burn marks, large stains, holes in walls, heavy grease, mold from unaddressed moisture or pet damage. You cannot always prevent wear, but you can show that you kept the home clean and reported problems, which is why photos and written maintenance requests matter so much.

In Greer, spring pollen and red clay add one more factor. Dust and clay tracked in on shoes show up on light carpet and entry floors, so give the entry, hallway and baseboards an extra pass before the final inspection.
