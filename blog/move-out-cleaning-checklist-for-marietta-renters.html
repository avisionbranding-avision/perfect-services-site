Move-out cleaning checklist for Marietta renters

You hand in the keys on a Friday, the landlord walks through on Monday, and the deposit decision comes from what that person sees in the first five minutes. The checklist, in the order we work

This is the sequence our team follows in an empty home. It works from the messiest room to the finishing touches, so you never clean the same spot twice. Even if you do the job yourself, keep to the order and the day goes faster. Set aside more time than you think: ovens and bathrooms always take longer than the rest of the house put together, and rushing them is how a deposit gets reduced.

Step 1: empty first, clean second

Nothing gets cleaned properly until the rooms are empty. Take out the last boxes, trash and anything from the closets, garage and the shelves above the fridge. Cleaning around furniture means skipping the floor under it, and that is where most deductions come from: crumbs, dust lines and marks along the walls.

Step 2: the kitchen, where deposits are won or lost

  • Inside the oven: racks out, baked-on grease off the floor and door glass.
  • Inside the refrigerator and freezer: shelves, drawers and door seals, after the food is gone and the unit has been unplugged and aired if you are leaving it that way.
  • Inside the microwave, plus the range hood and backsplash behind the stove.
  • Cabinets and drawers, inside and outside, including the shelf corners where crumbs hide.
  • Stovetop, sink and counters, finished last so nothing drips back on them.

Degreasing is the slow part. Grease on a range hood or oven door will not come off with a quick wipe, and it is the first thing a landlord notices.

Step 3: bathrooms

Scrub the toilet base and the area behind it, the tub or shower, the grout lines, the glass and the chrome. Do not forget the vanity interiors: the cabinet under the sink collects soap residue and sometimes a watermark. Mirrors go last, so overspray does not undo them.

Step 4: the details inspectors actually run a hand across

  • Baseboards, doors, door frames and light switches
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds
  • Closet shelves, rods and floors
  • Ceiling fan blades and vent covers

Window tracks are the spot renters miss most, and in a foothills home with older frames they hold a surprising amount of grit. Run a damp cloth along the track after vacuuming it.

Step 5: floors, edge to edge

Vacuum first, including corners and closets, then mop or wash hard floors, working toward the door so you do not walk across what you just finished. Carpeted rooms need a thorough vacuum along the edges where the carpet meets the baseboard.

What do landlords check first in a Marietta move-out?

After. Cleaning an empty home lets you reach floors, closets and corners that furniture and boxes block, and it is faster and more thorough.
