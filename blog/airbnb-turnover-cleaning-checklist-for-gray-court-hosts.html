Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Gray Court hosts

Airbnb cleaning in Gray Court, SC: a turnover checklist for hosts covering kitchen, bathrooms, linens, restocking and damage reports, with a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 22, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good turnover follows the same order every time, ending with staging to match your listing photos.
  • Report damage and missing items on the same visit, with photos, so nothing surprises you later.
  • Perfect Services prices turnovers only after a free in-home estimate of the property.

The hardest part of hosting in Gray Court is not the guests. It is the two or three hours between a late checkout and the next check-in, and a cleaner who has to get everything right inside that window. This checklist shows the order we follow for a short-term rental turnover and the details that earn five-star cleanliness scores.

The turnover checklist, in order

Working in the same order every visit means nothing gets forgotten:

  1. Walk the whole property first and note damage, left-behind items and anything out of place
  2. Strip beds and start the laundry, if you have laundry on site
  3. Kitchen reset: dishes, counters, appliance fronts, inside the microwave and refrigerator
  4. Bathrooms cleaned and disinfected, with mirrors and fixtures wiped
  5. Beds made with fresh linens you provide
  6. Dust surfaces and wipe door handles, switches and remotes
  7. Floors vacuumed and mopped
  8. Trash removed and bins reset
  9. Restock supplies: paper goods, soap, coffee
  10. Stage the rooms to match your listing photos, then do a final walk-through

The details guests notice first

Reviews rarely complain about a dusty ceiling. They mention hair in the shower, a sticky refrigerator shelf, crumbs under the toaster, or a smell. In our experience hosts get hurt by the small signals: a stray hair on a pillow, dust on the tops of door frames, a remote that feels greasy. Check the shower drain, the toilet base and the sliding door track, then smell the room before you leave it. Stage the space exactly like the listing, including the towel fold and the pillows, so the first impression matches the photos.

Damage and missing-item reports

Because we walk the property before cleaning, we can send you a damage and missing-item report with photos the same day. That matters for host protection claims, where timing and photos count. Catch a broken lamp or a missing towel on the turnover, not three guests later.

Gray Court specifics

Gray Court is a rural community in Laurens County, with farmland and new homes south of Fountain Inn, reached along SC-101 and US-276. Many hosts here own a cabin or guest house and live out of town, which is why remote coordination matters. Rural roads and gravel drives bring dirt inside, so entry mats and floors need extra attention after outdoor-focused stays. From Greer it is about 24 miles, so we schedule turnovers with travel in mind, and a tight window may mean sending a larger team.

Restocking, supplies and the host checklist

Between guests, someone has to notice that the coffee is low, the toilet paper is down to one roll and the dish soap is nearly empty. We follow a restocking checklist you give us: what to put out, where to keep it and what to report when you are running low. That way you buy supplies once in bulk instead of getting a panicked message from a guest. Many hosts also keep a small supply closet, with spare linens, towels, a vacuum and paper goods, so the crew never has to leave to find something.

Mistakes that cost hosts reviews

The common ones are cleaning too fast, skipping the bathroom grout, forgetting the top of the refrigerator and washing windows only once a season. Others are scent-heavy sprays that bother guests with allergies, and linens that smell of the storage closet. One more: leaving the property photographed in perfect condition but staged differently the next time, so guests feel it does not match the listing. A consistent staging routine fixes that, and it is worth writing down.

Seasonal changes matter too. Fall in the Upstate brings leaves tracked inside, spring brings pollen on porches and windowsills, and summer brings humidity that shows up in bathrooms. Your checklist should adjust for each.

Planning around your booking calendar

Share your calendar with the cleaner as far ahead as you can. Back-to-back stays, long weekend gaps and out-of-town owners all change how we schedule, and a clear calendar means fewer last-minute calls. Tell us about lockboxes or smart locks, where spare keys live and who we contact if a guest has not left yet. Those small answers keep a turnover smooth, especially when you are hours away and cannot step in.

How long does a turnover take?

A small one-bedroom may take a team under two hours. A larger home with several bathrooms and a full laundry cycle takes longer. Same-day check-ins need tighter scheduling, so give us your checkout and check-in times when you book, and we will tell you what is realistic.
