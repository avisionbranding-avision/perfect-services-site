A small office does not fail an inspection; it fails a visitor's first thirty seconds. A sticky door handle, a restroom that smells, a trash can nobody emptied. Start by splitting the work by frequency

The mistake we see most is a single long list nobody finishes. Break it into three layers, and assign each to a person or a crew.

Daily tasks

  • Empty trash and recycling, and replace liners.
  • Wipe reception counters, desks and shared workstations.
  • Wipe high-touch points: door handles, light switches, keypads, shared phones.
  • Clean and restock restrooms: soap, paper towels, toilet paper.
  • Wipe the break room counter, sink and microwave, and the fridge handle.
  • Vacuum high-traffic carpet and mop hard floors at the entry.

Weekly tasks

  • Vacuum all carpet, mop all hard floors.
  • Dust shelves, windowsills, monitor tops and reception displays.
  • Clean glass doors and the entry area, where fingerprints collect.
  • Wipe the outside of the break room fridge and cabinets.
  • Disinfect restroom walls near fixtures and the floor edges.

Monthly tasks

  • Dust vents, ceiling corners and baseboards.
  • Clean the inside of the microwave and wipe the fridge shelves.
  • Detail chair legs, door frames and under-desk areas.
  • Spot-clean walls and glass partitions.

Restrooms and break rooms decide the impression

If staff have to choose one place to cut corners, it is these two rooms, and they are also the two rooms people judge a business by. In the restroom, work from clean to dirty: mirrors and sink first, then fixtures, then the toilet, and the floor last. Use separate cloths for toilets and everything else. In the break room, the microwave and fridge are where trouble starts, so a weekly wipe saves a monthly crisis.

Setting up your routine

Start small. Choose the five tasks that visitors notice most, usually the entry, the front desk, the restroom, the break room and the trash, and make those non-negotiable every day. Everything else can move to a weekly or monthly rhythm. Write the names of who does what next to each item, because "someone" is the person who never does it.

One more consideration is access. Decide how a crew gets in after hours, where cleaning supplies and the alarm code live, and which rooms are off limits. Clear access rules, written down once and shared with the crew lead, prevent missed visits and keep staff comfortable with someone in the building when the office is empty.

Print the three lists, post them in the break room, and tick them for two weeks. You will see which tasks never get done, and that is exactly what to hand to a crew.
