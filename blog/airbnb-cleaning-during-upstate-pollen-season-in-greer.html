Airbnb cleaning during Upstate pollen season in Greer

Yellow pollen shows up on every surface of a Greer rental. See how Airbnb cleaning handles pollen season turnovers, what it costs to book and how to plan.

Perfect Services Team
November 26, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Spring pollen in the Upstate lands on porches, window tracks and bedding, so a rental needs a pollen-specific order of work.
  • Short windows between guests make the sequence matter: dust high, wipe soft surfaces, floors last.
  • Pricing comes after a free in-home estimate, because bedrooms, linens and turnover frequency change the job.

If you host a rental in Greer, you already know the week it happens. One morning the car is coated in yellow, the porch rail leaves a green-gold smear on your hand, and a guest checking in at four finds a film on the nightstand that was not there at noon. Pollen does not care about your five-star average. What you can control is the order your turnover cleaner works in, and that is what this article walks through.

Pollen season in the Upstate runs from late February through May, with the heaviest weeks usually in March and April. Pine, oak and birch all contribute, and the dust rides in on shoes, pets, open windows and the HVAC. For a short-term rental, that means every turnover during those weeks is a little harder than the same turnover in November.

Where pollen collects in a rental

After enough turnovers you learn to look in the same places. Here is where it shows up first in homes around Greer, from the older houses near Trade Street to the newer builds off SC-101 and near GSP Airport:

  • The front porch, door threshold and the track of a sliding patio door, where a thick line of yellow sits no matter how often you sweep.
  • Window sills and blinds, especially if a guest opened a window overnight.
  • Dark nightstands, headboards and TV stands, where pollen shows like flour.
  • The top of the refrigerator and the range hood.
  • Bedding and throw pillows, which hold it and release it again when guests fluff them.
  • Floors inside the entry, which collect it from every pair of shoes.

The order a turnover team should follow in March and April

A normal turnover can move room to room. In pollen weeks, the sequence should be vertical. The team starts high, with ceiling fans, blinds, door frames and shelves, using damp microfiber so the dust stays on the cloth instead of floating down. Then furniture and surfaces. Then the kitchen and bathrooms. Floors come last, vacuumed first and mopped second, so nothing that was shaken loose from above ends up on a clean floor.

Beds are the exception. The linens you supply should be pulled and replaced early, before the dusting starts, because a bare mattress and clean sheets are easy to cover while the room is still being worked. At Perfect Services, our Airbnb and vacation rental cleaning in Greer follows this order, and it comes from the Brazilian detail-first method the owners grew up with: finish the high work before the low work, and never go back over a room you have already closed out.

Keeping pollen out between guests

You cannot stop it, but you can slow it. A few rental-specific habits help:

  1. Put a coir or rubber mat outside and a washable mat inside every entry door, and note in your house guide that shoes come off at the door.
  2. Ask guests to keep windows closed in spring, and say why. Most people are happy to cooperate when they know the reason.
  3. Swap HVAC filters on a schedule during March through May, not just when they look dirty.
  4. Wipe the porch furniture and the door threshold yourself on arrival days if you are nearby, or schedule it into the turnover.
  5. Keep one spare set of pillow covers so a heavily dusted pillow can be swapped out rather than shaken.
