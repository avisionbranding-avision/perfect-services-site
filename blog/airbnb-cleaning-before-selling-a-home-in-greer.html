Airbnb cleaning before selling a home in Greer

Selling a home that was a short-term rental? See how Airbnb cleaning before a home sale in Greer, SC works, what buyers notice and how to book an estimate.

If you are selling a house in Greer that spent a few years as a short-term rental, the cleaning job is not the same as a normal turnover. Guests need a bed made and a fresh towel. Buyers walk through with a flashlight in their heads: they open the closets, look under the sink and notice what the listing photos did not show. This is where Airbnb cleaning before a home sale in Greer earns its keep, and it is a job we plan differently from a weekly Airbnb and vacation rental cleaning in Greer visit.

Why a rental needs a different reset before listing

A rental that has been turned over for months or years collects a particular kind of wear. Lockbox residue near the door, scuffs on the lower walls from luggage, coffee rings on the side tables, and a lived-in look on the grout that regular turnovers rarely have time to fix. Those turnovers are built for speed. A pre-sale clean is built for scrutiny.

The other shift is the goal. For a guest, the home should feel like a hotel room with a kitchen. For a buyer, it should feel like it could be theirs, so personal-looking extras, welcome baskets and the extra set of throw pillows tend to go. Cleaning is easier once that edit is done, because every object left on a counter is something to lift, wipe under and put back.

The order we work in, from the first walkthrough

Before any estimate we walk the home once, usually with the owner or the agent. We look at the kitchen, the bathrooms and the floors first because those decide how many hours the visit takes. Then we check the quiet spots: the inside of the dishwasher, the shower door track, the closet floors, the garage entry and the window sills in the guest rooms.

On the day itself the team goes from the top of the house down and from the back rooms toward the front door. Ceiling fans and vents first, then shelves and door frames, then counters and fixtures, and the floors last so that nothing we knock loose lands on a clean surface. It is the same detail-first method our Brazilian team uses everywhere, and it matters here because buyers touch things.

Pre-listing cleaning checklist for a former rental

  • Clear out host supplies: spare linens, labeled bins, guidebooks, key boxes and restocking stock.
  • Check each closet and the laundry room for items guests left behind.
  • Wipe baseboards, door frames and light switches, where hand and luggage marks gather.
  • Descale faucets and glass in each bathroom, then scrub the grout lines by hand.
  • Clean inside the oven, microwave, fridge and dishwasher.
  • Vacuum and mop every floor, moving the beds and sofas where possible.
  • Wash window sills and tracks, plus the sliding door glass.
  • Empty the trash, reset the bins and clear the garage entry.

One more thing we see in homes that were rentals: the fixtures that guests used hard. Shower heads clog with mineral scale, the kitchen faucet aerator collects grit, and the remote-control and thermostat area gets sticky. None of that is dirty in the way a spill is dirty, but a buyer who runs the tap and sees a crusty edge draws a conclusion about the whole house. We wipe and descale these details because they are quick to do and easy to forget.

Smell is the other quiet factor. Rentals that were closed up between guests can hold a stale, perfumed odor from plug-in fresheners and candles. We do not cover it with more fragrance. We clean the source, open the windows when the weather allows, and let the house smell like nothing, which is what most buyers prefer.
