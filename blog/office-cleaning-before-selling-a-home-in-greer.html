Office cleaning before selling a home in Greer

Selling a home with a home office or small business space in Greer? Learn how office cleaning for a home sale helps listings. Free in-home estimate.

Key takeaways

  • A home with an office, studio or storefront room is judged twice: as a house and as a workspace.
  • Commercial-style cleaning focuses on high-touch points, restrooms and floors that buyers inspect closely.
  • Prices come only after a free in-home estimate, since square footage, floors and restrooms differ.

Plenty of houses around Greer have a room that does double duty: a converted garage with a desk and shelves, a downstairs suite where someone sees clients, or a small shop attached to the property. When that home goes on the market, the buyer looks at the work space the way a tenant would. Handles, restrooms, carpet and glass all get noticed. This is where office cleaning in Greer fits into a home sale, and it is different from the move-out clean the rest of the house needs.

Which sellers this applies to

Not every seller needs a commercial clean. It is worth thinking about if any of these describe your property:

  • A dedicated home office with its own entrance or a separate restroom.
  • A salon, studio or treatment room that clients have used.
  • A small retail or showroom space attached to the house or sharing the lot.
  • A rental office or small commercial unit you own and are listing along with the home.
  • A property manager preparing common areas ahead of a sale.

For a plain bedroom used as an office, a regular house clean is enough. When the space has been used by clients or staff, the standard of cleanliness is closer to what a business owner expects.

What gets cleaned in a work space

The approach differs from a living area. A team cleaning an office thinks in terms of what people touch and what a visitor passes first. Our Perfect Services crew works through the space in a set order so nothing is missed.

  1. Desks, counters and workstations wiped, including the undersides of shelf edges.
  2. High-touch points: door handles, light switches, keypads and railing tops.
  3. Restrooms cleaned and disinfected, with fixtures and mirrors polished.
  4. Break room or kitchenette: sink, microwave, fridge exterior and counters.
  5. Trash and recycling removed.
  6. Glass doors and entry areas cleaned inside and out.
  7. Reception or waiting area dusted, then floors vacuumed and mopped.

Floors tell the story

In an office space the floor often carries the most wear, and it is also the first thing a visitor sees. Different floors need different care, which is part of what we check at the estimate.

Floor typeWhat we look for
CarpetTraffic lanes, stains near the desk and door, vacuum lines at the edges
TileDirty grout, film near the restroom, scuffs along walls
Hard floorsScratches from chair casters, residue from the wrong cleaner

Matching the method to the surface protects the floor and avoids a dull finish that photographs poorly.

A quick checklist before the listing

  • Clear desks, shelves and the break-room fridge of personal items and food.
  • Take down signs and documents that identify the business, if the sale excludes it.
  • Shred or box paperwork, and unplug equipment that will leave.
  • Check restroom supplies and replace worn fixtures such as seat covers or handles.
  • Ask for an estimate once the space is as empty as it will be.

Sellers sometimes ask how much the office needs to look like an office. If the buyer is likely to keep the space as a work area, leave it clean and clear, with the floor visible and the walls free of tape and hooks. If the buyer will turn it into a bedroom or playroom, a bare, neutral room is easier to picture. Either way, the cleaning is the same, and your agent can tell you which presentation suits the listing.

It also helps to think about smell. Carpet in a closed office holds odors from coffee, food and old paper, and a restroom that sees clients can carry a lingering scent. Opening windows on a mild day and running the HVAC fan a few hours before showings makes a difference after the cleaners leave.

Frequently asked questions

Is office cleaning different from a regular house clean?

Yes. It focuses on high-touch points, restrooms, break areas and floors, and follows a set order, whereas a house clean spends more time on bedrooms and living areas.

Can you clean the office while the business is still operating?

Often yes. After-hours visits are common, and the schedule is agreed during the free in-home estimate.

Do I need office cleaning if I only have a desk in a spare bedroom?

Usually not. A regular clean or move-out clean covers that. Office cleaning makes sense when clients, staff or a separate commercial space were involved.
