What the team does differently in a pet-friendly office

A standard office cleaning in Greer covers desks and workstations, high-touch points like handles and keypads, restrooms, the break room, trash and recycling, floors, glass doors and reception. That list does not change for a pet office. The order and the attention do.

  • Hair is tackled before surfaces are wiped, so nothing is re-coated.
  • Door handles, light switches and keypads get an extra pass, since animals and their owners touch them all day.
  • Floors are vacuumed first and mopped second, with attention along walls and under furniture where hair collects.
  • Trash is removed every visit, especially in the break room, to keep smells from building.
  • Entry mats are lifted and the floor beneath is cleaned.

Allergies, fresh air and reasonable expectations

Dander cannot be eliminated in a space where animals work. It can be kept low with a steady schedule, and that is the honest message for staff with allergies. Frequency matters more than intensity: a short visit several times a week keeps hair from building up, while a deep visit once a month does not stop it from recurring. If a coworker's allergy is the reason you are looking at services, say so at the estimate. We can use products with mild scents and build the route around the rooms that need the most attention. Green cleaning options are available for offices that prefer them.

A simple pet policy that makes cleaning easier

If animals are welcome in your Greer office, a few house rules cut the work in half. Keep a mat at the door, assign a spot for beds and bowls away from carpet edges, and ask staff to take food and water dishes to the break-room sink at the end of the day. A lint roller by the entry helps visitors in dark clothes. These rules also make a commercial cleaning visit in Greer more effective, because the team spends its time on surfaces instead of moving clutter. If a pet has an accident, tell the team the next morning, and the area gets extra attention.

Customers notice smell before they notice hair. A short, steady schedule keeps the waiting area neutral, and that first impression is worth the effort. Trash bags that leave every visit, a clean floor at the door and wiped chair arms usually do more than any air freshener.
