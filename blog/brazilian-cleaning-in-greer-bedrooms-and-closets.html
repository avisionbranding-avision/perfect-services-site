Lift the edge of a bed skirt in most homes and you will see how a room was really cleaned. Dust lines under the frame, a sock behind the nightstand, and a gray edge along the baseboard are all signs of a quick pass. The Brazilian approach to bedrooms starts by moving things. For Brazilian house cleaning bedroom Greer families book, that single habit is the big difference.

What we move, and what we leave

We move light furniture, such as nightstands, chairs, benches and laundry baskets, to clean under and behind them, and put everything back exactly where it was. We do not lift heavy dressers or beds with a person's belongings stacked on top. We also do not go through drawers or personal items. If something is valuable or fragile, set it aside before we arrive, and the team will clean around it.

A bedroom, step by step

  1. Open the curtains and look at the room in daylight, since dust shows best that way.
  2. Dust high: ceiling fan blades one at a time, light fixtures, the top of the door and picture frames.
  3. Wipe the headboard, nightstands and dresser tops with a damp cloth, then dry them.
  4. Clean mirrors and glass, and the window sill and track.
  5. Do the closet: door tracks, shelves, the floor under the shoe pile.
  6. Baseboards by hand, one section at a time.
  7. Vacuum under the bed and along the walls, then the middle of the floor.

The fan blades go first because the dust falls to the surfaces below. Doing them last would undo the work.

Closets get a real look

Closets are a favorite place for dust, and a bedroom closet in a Greer home with forced air tends to have a film on the top shelf. We do not reorganize your clothes, but we wipe visible shelves and rods, clean the floor where it can be reached and detail the door tracks. If you want the closet fully emptied for a deep clean, say so, and we can plan that into a deeper first visit.

Bedroom checklist

  • Ceiling fan blades and light fixtures dusted
  • Nightstands and dresser tops wiped, not just dusted
  • Headboard and mirror cleaned
  • Window sill, track and blinds wiped
  • Baseboards and door frames cleaned by hand
  • Closet shelves, tracks and floor
  • Light switches and door handle
  • Under the bed and behind the nightstands

Pollen, pets and kids' rooms

In spring, Upstate pollen settles on window sills and fan blades, and a bedroom with the window cracked open takes it in. Pets shed on beds and carpet edges, and children's rooms collect crumbs, marker marks and small toys in every corner. We adjust the routine: more attention to the sill in pollen season, a thorough pass along the carpet edges where pet hair gathers and gentle products on anything a child touches. Tell us before the visit if someone has allergies, and we will use fragrance-free products.

Why we clean the baseboards by hand

A mop head cannot reach the corner where the baseboard meets the floor, and a bedroom has four of them plus the closet. We go along each wall with a damp cloth, section by section, and then check the corners with a finger wrapped in cloth. It is the slowest part of the room and the one clients mention first. It also happens to be the part that makes a room feel finished even when the furniture is crowded.

We do the same at door frames, where handprints collect at kid height, and at the switch plates near the bed, which collect the oils from a hand reaching in the dark.
