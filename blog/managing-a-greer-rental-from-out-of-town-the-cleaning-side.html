Managing a Greer rental from out of town: the cleaning side

Own a Greer, SC rental from far away? Here is how VRBO cleaning in Greer works remotely: access, photo reports, scheduling and what to ask your cleaner.

Perfect Services Team
November 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • An out-of-town host needs three things from a cleaner: reliable access, photo proof, and a clear report after every visit.
  • Set up the handoff once (keys, codes, supply stock, house notes) and most turnovers need no phone call.
  • We give a price only after a free in-home estimate; call (864) 399-5168 or text (864) 666-8520.

You cannot walk through the house the afternoon before a guest arrives, so you have to trust what you are told about it. That is the real challenge behind VRBO cleaning in Greer, SC when you live somewhere else: the cleaning itself is only half of it, and the other half is knowing the house is actually ready.

Many of the owners we work with live out of town and run a lake or mountain house, or a rental closer to home, from a phone. Here is how we set up the cleaning side so it works without you being there.

The first visit is a walkthrough, not a clean

Before any recurring schedule starts, we like to meet the house. During a free in-home estimate our team walks every room with the host, or with a local contact if you cannot be there. We look at the kitchen setup, count the bathrooms, check the bed sizes and ask what a finished turnover should look like. Then we agree on the baseline: your listing photos.

That baseline is what lets us stage the space to match your photos on every visit, from how the throw pillows sit to which lamp is on. It also gives you a reference for judging any report you get later. Our Airbnb and vacation rental cleaning in Greer is built around this idea of a repeatable finish.

Access, without handing out copies of a key

Lockboxes and keypad codes are the usual choice for remote hosts. Give each cleaner access that you can change when needed, and tell us in advance if the code changes. Write down the quirks that only an owner knows: the back door that sticks, the thermostat setting, where the extra trash bags live. We put these in the house notes and follow them each time.

  • A lockbox or smart lock code that you can update
  • Instructions for alarms, gates and parking
  • A named local contact for emergencies, if you have one
  • A list of the laundry steps, whether linens go off site or stay on site
  • The location of your reserve stock for supplies

One more thing worth deciding at the start is who the cleaner can reach. If a guest leaves something behind or a problem turns up, a clear contact saves hours. Most of the time the answer is you, by text, and we keep messages short so you can reply from the airport or a meeting.

What you should receive after every turnover

When you are not there, the report is your eyes. After a visit you should hear whether the house was finished, whether anything is damaged or missing, and whether supplies are low. Our team sends a damage and missing-item report with photos, so you can see a scuffed wall or a missing lamp shade without making a trip. We take the photos before we leave, so they show the final state, and the guest checkout state when there is damage.

A good report is short. It says what was done, what was found and what needs your decision. We would rather send a clear photo of one problem than a long note about everything.

Over time you will also learn the rhythm of your own house. After a few visits we know which room usually takes longest, which guests tend to leave the kitchen heavy, and when the linens need replacing. We pass that on in the report so you can fix the cause, such as adding a second trash bin or a better doormat, instead of paying for the same problem every week.

How much does vacation rental cleaning cost for a Greer owner?

The answer depends on the property, so we do not give a number over the phone. The factors are the size of the home, its bedrooms and bathrooms, the length of your turnover window, whether laundry is done on site or off site, how much restocking you need, and how many turnovers happen each month. A rental that sits empty most of the winter and fills up in the summer needs a different plan than one booked every weekend.

Planning for the slow weeks and the busy ones

Out-of-town hosts often forget that an empty house still gathers dust. In the humid Upstate summers a closed-up house can build up a stale smell, and in spring, pollen settles on window sills and porch furniture. We can schedule a lighter visit between bookings so you do not return to a house that needs a deep clean. If one does, ask about deep cleaning as a one-time reset.
