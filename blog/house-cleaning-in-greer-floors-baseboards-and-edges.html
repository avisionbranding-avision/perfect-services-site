Stand in a hallway in Greer and look along the bottom of the wall. That line, where the floor meets the baseboard, tells you more about a house cleaning than the counters do. Here is how we handle floors, baseboards and edges during regular house cleaning in Greer, SC, and why those edges get the attention they do.

Why edges collect dirt first

A vacuum head is wider than the last inch of floor, and a mop swings away from the wall. Over a few weeks, that unreachable strip collects hair, crumbs and the fine red clay that comes in on shoes across the Upstate. It then gets wet when someone mops and turns into a visible line. That is why our team washes floors by hand along the walls, and why baseboards and door frames are touched up on a rotation during regular visits instead of left for a once-a-year project.

What we do by floor type

Greer has a mix of homes: older houses near Downtown Greer and Trade Street often have hardwood and tile, while the newer neighborhoods near GSP Airport and the BMW plant lean toward carpet, luxury vinyl and tile in the kitchen. Each surface gets its own method.

The order matters more than people think

  1. Dust high first: shelves, fans and window sills, so the fallout lands on floors that are not done yet.
  2. Wipe baseboards and door frames on that visit's rotation, working with a damp cloth.
  3. Vacuum the whole room, using the crevice tool along the walls and under the furniture edge.
  4. Wash hard floors by hand along the edges, then mop the open middle.
  5. Work toward the exit so no one walks across wet floor.

The rotation is the practical part. In a regular visit, we do not wipe every baseboard in the house every time, because the focus is on bathrooms, kitchen and surfaces. Instead, we pick a few rooms each visit, so across a month every edge gets a hand. That is a major reason a house stays fresh between visits without becoming a deep-cleaning project.

The spots most people miss

  • The bottom edge of the front door and the threshold
  • Under the rim of kitchen cabinets, where the toe kick collects crumbs
  • Behind a door that is usually open
  • The strip of floor beside the refrigerator and the stove
  • Stair edges and the underside of the handrail

Between visits: small habits that protect your floors

You do not need to do much to help a cleaning visit last longer. A doormat at each entrance catches most of the red clay before it reaches the hardwood. Shoes off at the door cuts the grit on carpet. A quick pass with a dry mop along the edges every few days keeps hair and crumbs from becoming a gray line. Felt pads under chair legs prevent scratches that hold dirt.

Spills are the other thing. Wipe sticky ones the same day, because they pick up dust and turn dark within a week. If you tell us about a stain at the beginning of a visit, we can treat it before we vacuum or mop it into the rest of the floor. Frequently asked questions

Do you clean baseboards during every regular visit?

We touch baseboards and door frames up on a rotation, a few rooms per visit, so every edge is covered over a month. A deeper baseboard clean is part of deep cleaning.

Can you clean hardwood floors without damaging them?

Yes. We vacuum first, then use a lightly damp hand wash and avoid standing water. Tell us about your finish at the estimate so we can plan the method.

Do I need to move furniture before you arrive?

No. We move light furniture and small items to clean under them. For heavy pieces, we clean around them and let you know.
