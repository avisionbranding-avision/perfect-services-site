How often should you book house cleaning in Greer?

How often should you book house cleaning in Greer, SC? Compare weekly, biweekly and monthly visits, plus Upstate pollen and clay, and book a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 13, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Every two weeks suits most Greer households; weekly fits kids and pets, and monthly means longer catch-up visits.
  • Pollen season, red clay and holiday guests are good reasons to adjust your schedule during the year.
  • Prices for each frequency come only after a free in-home estimate.

For most Greer households the right rhythm is every two weeks, with weekly for homes that have several kids or pets and monthly only as a step up from doing it all yourself. That is the short version. Below is the longer reasoning, so you can pick a schedule for house cleaning in Greer that fits your home and does not waste a visit.

Weekly and biweekly visits cost less per visit than monthly ones, which surprises people. The reason is simple: a home that is cleaned often never builds up much, so each visit moves faster.

Start from what bothers you most

Instead of choosing a number, ask which job you keep putting off. When we talk to people in Greer, the answer is almost always one of four things: the bathrooms, the floors, the kitchen, or the dust that returns two days after wiping. Each one points to a schedule.

  • Bathrooms bother you: go biweekly at least, because soap scum and hard water spots set quickly.
  • Floors bother you: pet hair and tracked-in dirt call for weekly.
  • The kitchen bothers you: a biweekly visit keeps counters and the stovetop in control if you wipe daily.
  • Dust bothers you: weekly or biweekly, and ask for the shelves and vents to be a priority.

Local things that change the answer

Upstate weather is a real factor. Spring pollen coats window sills and porch floors within days, and red clay tracks in on shoes after any rainy week. Humid summers make bathrooms and shower grout slower to dry. Many Greer homeowners move to weekly for the heavy pollen weeks and drop back to biweekly in winter. Others do the reverse around the holidays, when the house fills with guests from late November on.

Where you live matters a little, too. Homes near Downtown Greer and Trade Street are often older with more trim and doors, which collect dust at the edges. Newer homes near GSP Airport and the BMW plant tend to have open floor plans with long runs of hard floor, so the vacuum and mop do most of the work. The team adjusts the focus; the schedule question stays the same.

Signs your current schedule is off

People rarely notice a schedule is wrong until the day after a visit feels different. A few signals are worth watching. If you find yourself doing a quick pre-clean before the team arrives, you are likely on too long a gap. If the visit day feels like an empty house with nothing to do, you may be able to stretch it. If guests are coming and you wish the bathrooms had been done yesterday, a one-time extra visit is easier than moving everything to weekly.

Life events shift the answer too. A new baby, a dog that sheds in spring, a relative staying for a month or a home office that now has clients walking through are all good reasons to revisit the rhythm. Our team would rather adjust the plan than have you pay for visits that do not match how the house is used.

How to test a schedule without locking in

If you are unsure, start with a first visit and pick the schedule after you see how the house feels a week later. The first clean is usually deeper, to bring the home to a level the team can keep up. After that, notice what drifts back first. If the bathroom needs attention before the next visit, tighten the schedule. If the house is still fine on visit day, stretch it.

The house cleaning service includes the same core list on every visit: kitchen counters, sink and stovetop, appliance fronts, bathrooms, dusting, floors vacuumed and mopped by hand, beds made and trash emptied. Baseboards and door frames rotate through, so a longer schedule means those spots wait longer between touch-ups.
