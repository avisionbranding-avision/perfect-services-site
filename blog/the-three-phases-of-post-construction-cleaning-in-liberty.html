The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Liberty

Post construction cleaning in Liberty, SC happens in three phases: rough clean, final clean and touch-up. See what each includes and book a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Construction cleaning is done in three passes: rough clean, final clean and a touch-up before move-in.
  • Drywall sanding dust is the finest and hardest to remove, so timing matters.
  • Price comes after a free in-home estimate and depends on project size, dust level and deadlines.

The drywall crew finished Thursday, and by Friday the fine white dust had settled on every ledge in the house, including inside the closed cabinets. That is the starting point for most post construction cleaning in Liberty. A renovation or new build is not cleaned in one sweep. It is cleaned in three phases, each with a different job, and understanding them helps you schedule the work and avoid paying for a pass that comes too early. This is how our team handles post-construction cleaning in Liberty.

Phase one: the rough clean

The rough clean happens while the trades are still finishing, or just after the heavy work ends. Its purpose is not to make the house shine. It is to get debris out of the way so painters, flooring crews and installers can work without tracking it everywhere.

  • Remove leftover scraps, packaging and protective coverings.
  • Sweep and vacuum floors to get the bulk of the dust up.
  • Wipe down surfaces that painters or installers need to reach.
  • Clear out cabinets and closets of loose debris.
  • Take a first look at windows and frames for tape and stickers.

A rough clean makes later work cleaner. It does not replace the final pass, because dust keeps falling as long as work continues.

Phase two: the final clean

This is the long one. It begins after the last trade leaves, and it covers every surface from the ceiling down.

  • Fine construction dust removed from all surfaces, ledges and trim.
  • Inside of cabinets, drawers and closets wiped.
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills cleaned.
  • Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive removed.
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans dusted and cleaned.
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished.
  • Floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes.

We work top to bottom because dust falls. Fans and fixtures first, then walls and trim, then windows, cabinets, bathrooms, and floors last. Floors need more than one pass because the first one picks up grit and the second picks up what the first loosened.

Phase three: the touch-up

A day or two after the final clean, the house looks different. Dust that was suspended in the air has landed, and a walk-through with a homeowner or inspector turns up a handful of spots. The touch-up is a short return pass for those details before move-in or closing, including a last look at glass, floors and bathrooms.

Why dust from drywall is different

Drywall sanding creates the finest dust, and it travels. It gets into HVAC vents, inside cabinets and under door frames. A damp cloth smears it, and a regular household vacuum can blow it back out. That is why post-construction work needs the right equipment and a patient order of work, and why we do not recommend running the HVAC system hard until the final clean is done.

Liberty is a small town between Easley and Clemson with many family homes, and a lot of local projects are kitchen and bathroom renovations or additions. Smaller projects can still create a surprising amount of dust, because it spreads through the whole house, not just the room being worked on.

What you can do between the phases

Homeowners often ask how to help. The best thing is to protect the clean areas. Keep doors closed to rooms that are finished, ask workers to remove shoes or use mats, and have trash taken out regularly. If you can, keep pets and children away from the work zone, and avoid running the air handler without clean filters while dust is still in the air.

Replace the HVAC filter after the final clean, and consider wiping the vent covers. Dust trapped in the ducts can come out later, and a fresh filter catches it. If the project involved flooring, ask the installer about any care instructions before we wash the floor, since some finishes need time to cure.

Common leftovers we find

The things that trip people up are usually small: paint specks on window glass, stickers on new appliances and fixtures, adhesive on tile edges, and caulk smears near tubs. Each needs a different tool and a light hand, so we use plastic scrapers on glass and gentle solvents where the surface allows. Rushing this part can scratch new surfaces, and replacing a scratched window or countertop costs far more than the time spent doing it carefully.

With a deadline, ask us for a plan: which phase on which day, how many people, and what you will need to leave accessible. Clear communication keeps the project on schedule.
