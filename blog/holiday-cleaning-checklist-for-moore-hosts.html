Holiday cleaning checklist for Moore hosts

Holiday cleaning checklist for Moore, SC hosts: what to clean before guests arrive, a one-time cleaning plan, and how pricing works after a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 24, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Clean the rooms guests will use first: entry, kitchen, guest bathroom and the place they sleep.
  • A one-time cleaning can cover the full house or just the heavy-lift areas you do not have time for.
  • Holiday weeks book quickly, and prices come only after a free in-home estimate.

When relatives are coming for a few days, the house gets judged in three places: the guest bathroom, the kitchen and wherever people sleep. The rest of the house matters, but those three decide how the visit feels. If you are hosting in Moore this season, this checklist puts the work in the order that gives you the most for the hours you have, and shows where a one-time house cleaning in Moore can take the heavy lifting off your plate.

Plan backward from the first guest

Pick the day people arrive and count back. Anything messy, like the oven or the fridge, goes first. Dusting and bathrooms come next, then floors, and the last day is for quick touch-ups and cooking. If you plan to hire a cleaner, aim for a few days before arrival so the house stays clean while you cook and decorate. Holiday weeks fill up fast, so booking early helps.

The host's holiday checklist

  • Guest bathroom: toilet, shower or tub, mirror, sink, fresh towels and an empty trash can.
  • Kitchen: counters, sink, stovetop, appliance fronts, microwave and refrigerator shelves.
  • Guest bedroom: sheets changed, surfaces dusted, floor vacuumed, closet space cleared.
  • Entry and hallway: floor swept and mopped, mat shaken out, coat storage cleared.
  • Living room: dust surfaces, vacuum under cushions, clean glass and mirrors.
  • Dining area: table and chairs wiped, floor mopped under the table.
  • Baseboards and door frames in the rooms guests will use.
  • Trash and recycling taken out, with extra bags ready.

Cooking weeks leave their mark

Holiday cooking is hard on a kitchen in ways people do not notice until the day after. Grease mists onto the cabinets above the stove, flour and sugar fall behind the counter edge, and spills bake onto the bottom of the oven. If you plan to cook for a crowd, a thorough clean before the cooking starts gives you a fresh start, and a lighter clean afterward finishes the job. Some Moore hosts book one visit before the guests arrive and keep the next one in mind for the first week of January.

Remember the smaller things guests touch. Door handles, the refrigerator handle, light switches near the kitchen and the bathroom faucet are touched dozens of times a day when the house is full. Wiping them down is quick, and the team sanitizes them as part of every visit.

If anyone in your family has allergies, say so when you book. We can note it for the visit, and you can ask about gentler products. Pets are the other thing worth mentioning, since pet hair collects on couches and the guest bed. A good vacuum pass over the guest room just before arrival is a small effort that guests notice.

One last tip: leave a short note about anything you want the team to focus on, such as the guest bathroom or the dining room floor, and point out any surface that needs special care. It takes a minute and makes the visit match your priorities.
