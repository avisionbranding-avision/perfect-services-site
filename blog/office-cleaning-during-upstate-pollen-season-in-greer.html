Office cleaning during Upstate pollen season in Greer

Pollen comes in on shoes, clothes and delivery doors. See how office cleaning in Greer SC changes in spring for entryways, desks, vents and restrooms.

November 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • In spring, pollen enters an office mostly at the front door and through the HVAC, so entries and vents get extra attention.
  • A short weekly extra task list works better than a one-time spring blitz.
  • Perfect Services prices office cleaning only after a free in-person estimate of the space.

Walk into a small office in Greer in late April and look at the dark chairs by the front window. If they have a faint yellow dust on them, you have seen the Upstate spring at work. Pollen does not need an open window. It rides in on clothes, bags, shoes and delivery boxes, and it settles where air moves least: the edges of desks, the tops of monitors, window ledges and the carpet just inside the door. Here is how office cleaning in Greer SC shifts when pollen is the main guest.

Where pollen collects in an office

Offices in Greer run from a one-room insurance or accounting office on Trade Street to a showroom or clinic along SC-14 and US-29. The layouts differ, but pollen goes to the same places:

  • The entry mat, the first six feet of carpet and the glass door itself
  • Reception counters and waiting-area chairs
  • Window ledges and blinds
  • Air vents and return grilles, which pull pollen in and deposit it on the ceiling tiles around them
  • Keyboards, phones and the flat tops of monitors
  • Restroom sinks, where staff rinse pollen off hands and faces

What we add in spring

The regular visit is the same: desks and workstations wiped, high-touch points like handles, switches and keypads sanitized, restrooms cleaned and restocked, trash and recycling removed, floors vacuumed and mopped, and glass doors and entry areas finished. In spring we add a few tasks to that list.

A simple spring plan for a small office

  1. Put a washable mat inside and outside the main door, and ask the team to wash it weekly.
  2. Swap HVAC filters before pollen season starts, not in the middle of it.
  3. Keep one shared surface wipe at the front desk so staff clean their own keyboards during the day.
  4. Tell your cleaning crew which rooms have the most foot traffic, so they can allocate time properly.
  5. Ask for a mid-season check of vents and blinds.

Frequency and timing

Most small offices do well with weekly visits, and busier ones with two or three visits a week during pollen season. A clinic or salon with customers coming and going all day may want daily light cleaning of the entry and restrooms. A typical small office takes a couple of hours per visit, and larger spaces scale up from there. If you need service to start this week, mention it when you request the estimate, and we will try to fit the schedule.

Do not forget the break room

The break room collects pollen like everything else, but it also has food. The cleaning there covers the sink, the microwave, the outside of the refrigerator, the counters and the table. We do not clean out personal food or the inside of the refrigerator unless it is requested, so tell the team if you want that included on a monthly basis.

What the team checks on the first visit

On the first walk-through we look at three things before anything else: how people enter, where the air comes in, and which rooms the public sees. A reception area with a glass door and a south-facing window will collect pollen faster than a back office with no windows. Knowing that, we spend more time at the front and less in rooms that stay clean on their own. It sounds obvious, but a crew working from a fixed time-per-room formula tends to spend the same ten minutes everywhere.

We also ask who cleans the keyboards and screens. Wiping those is usually left to staff, and we are happy to include a light wipe if you prefer, using a product that is safe for electronics. Say so at the estimate so it is on the checklist from day one.
