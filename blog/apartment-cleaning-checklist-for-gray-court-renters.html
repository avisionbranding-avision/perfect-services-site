A one-bedroom apartment can be cleaned in an afternoon, and it can also stay half-cleaned for months, because the dirt is in the small places: the oven door, the shower track, the strip of floor behind the door. This is the checklist we would hand a renter in Gray Court who wants a unit that looks and smells clean without turning the whole weekend into a project.

Start with what you can see from the door

Renters in Laurens County often live in a mix of small apartments, duplexes and newer homes south of Fountain Inn, and the landlord's walkthrough is only a few minutes long. They look first at the kitchen, the bathroom and the floors. Start there, since those are the places where a good first impression is made or lost.

Clear everything off the counters and the bathroom sink before you begin. You cannot clean a surface you are working around.

The checklist, room by room

Kitchen

  • Counters wiped, including the strip behind the faucet
  • Sink scrubbed, drain area rinsed
  • Stovetop and appliance fronts degreased
  • Microwave interior and fridge shelves wiped
  • Cabinet handles and light switches sanitized

Bathroom

  • Toilet, including the base and behind the seat hinges
  • Shower or tub, tile and the track of the door
  • Mirror and vanity
  • Floor, down to the corners

Living area and bedroom

  • Dust surfaces, shelves and furniture top to bottom
  • Baseboards and door frames
  • Balcony or patio door glass and its track
  • Beds made, trash emptied
  • Floors vacuumed, then mopped

Work from the highest surface down, and finish at the door. That order saves you from cleaning the same floor twice.

A fifteen-minute weekly reset

Most renters do not need a long clean every week. A short reset keeps the heavy work from building up: wipe the counters and the stovetop after cooking, spray and rinse the shower after the last person is done, run the vacuum over the main walking path and take out the trash before it smells. On a small unit that is about fifteen minutes, and it makes the monthly checklist above far lighter.

Once a month, take one extra pass at the places that collect quietly: the top of the fridge, the vents, the window sills and the track of the patio door. Rotate them, one a week, if the full list feels like too much. Renters who do this rarely have a surprise at move-out, because nothing has had the time to build up into something a deposit deduction can be taken for.

Keep a bucket or caddy with your supplies in the same place every time. Half the work of cleaning a small apartment is finding the spray, and a ready caddy removes that excuse.

How often and how long

For a small unit, a good weekly clean is usually a short visit, and a biweekly schedule works if you keep up with dishes and trash yourself. First visits take longer because the team is catching up on baseboards and door frames. If you work long hours, a standing visit keeps the checklist from piling up. If you are planning a visit this week, ask early, since weekends fill first.
