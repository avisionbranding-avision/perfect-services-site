Winter house cleaning in Greer: indoor season tips

Winter house cleaning in Greer, SC: indoor-season tips on dry dust, wet boots, a post-holiday reset and a recurring plan, plus pricing after a free estimate.

Winter in Greer means closing the house up. The windows stay shut, the heat runs, and everything that used to drift out in spring and fall now stays inside with you. That is the real cleaning challenge of the indoor season, and it has little to do with how the house looks in December. This guide is about what changes from January to March and how to plan for it.

Why the indoor season is different

With less fresh air, dust, pet hair and cooking residue build up faster than people expect. Heating systems circulate the same air through the same vents. Kitchen grease settles on cabinet fronts and the top of the refrigerator. Our team can usually tell a home that has been closed up for two months by the film along the baseboards and the dust on the ceiling fan blades, even in homes that look tidy.

The January reset

The week after the holidays is a natural time to reset. The decorations come down, the guests leave, and you can see the house clearly again. Use this as your starting point:

  1. Put away decorations and wipe the shelves and mantels they sat on.
  2. Wash or replace bedding in the guest room.
  3. Degrease the stovetop, hood and cabinet fronts after weeks of cooking.
  4. Vacuum under furniture and along the baseboards.
  5. Wipe vent covers and the top of ceiling fans.
  6. Scrub bathrooms, with extra time on grout and fixtures.

Wet boots, rain and a muddy mat

Greer winters are mild compared with the mountains, but they are wet and cold enough that people track water and dirt in every day. Hardwood and tile near the door take the hit. A tray for boots and a mat that can be washed save time. When our team cleans a Greer home in winter, the floors by the entry get the most attention, and they are washed by hand along the walls and the thresholds, where water pools.

A winter checklist

  • Entry floors and mats washed weekly.
  • Kitchen surfaces and appliance handles wiped and sanitized.
  • Bathrooms scrubbed, with attention to mildew along caulk lines.
  • Pet bedding washed and furniture vacuumed.
  • Ceiling fans, vents and window sills dusted.
  • Baseboards and door frames wiped.
  • Light switches and handles sanitized, especially during cold-and-flu weeks.

Cooking, cold weather and kitchen grease

Winter is also the heavy cooking season: soups, roasts and baking. Grease lands on the stovetop, the hood and the cabinets above it, and with the windows closed it does not go anywhere. A film that you cannot see in the morning light shows up as a sticky patch when you touch a cabinet handle. Degreasing every few weeks keeps it from building into something that takes real effort to remove.

Bathrooms need attention too. Hot showers in a closed house raise humidity, and mildew can appear along the caulk lines and the bottom of the shower curtain. Run the fan during and after showers, and wipe the corners once a week. If you notice dark spots that return quickly, mention them at the estimate so we can plan time for them.

Finally, think about the guest bed and the closets. After the holidays, many homes have extra bedding, coats and boxes in odd places. Sorting these out before a cleaning makes the visit faster, and it makes the house feel lighter for the rest of the winter.
