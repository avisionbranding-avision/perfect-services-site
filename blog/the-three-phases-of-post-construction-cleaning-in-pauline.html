The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Pauline

The three phases of post construction cleaning in Pauline SC: rough clean, final clean and touch-up, plus how the free in-home estimate sets your price.

Perfect Services Team
November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Post-construction cleaning works best in three passes: rough, final and touch-up.
  • Drywall dust is the slow part and settles again for days after sanding.
  • Prices come after a free in-home estimate and walkthrough.

The first mistake people make after a build or remodel is trying to clean everything in one afternoon. Fine drywall dust floats for hours, and whatever you wipe in the morning has a new coat by dinner. That is why our team splits post-construction cleaning into three phases, and why homeowners in Pauline, where much of the building is country homes and additions south of Spartanburg off SC-56 and SC-215, get better results when they book the phases together.

Phase one: the rough clean

The rough clean happens while the contractors are finishing, or right after the last trade leaves. The goal is not shine; it is getting the heavy stuff out so the next steps are possible. That means picking up debris and packaging, sweeping and vacuuming floors, removing stickers from windows where it is safe to, and clearing dust from ledges, window tracks and the tops of cabinets. In a remodel, we also protect finished areas where we can.

Skipping this phase is how people end up scratching new floors with grit during the detailed work. If you are still waiting on painters or an electrician, hold off on the next phase.

Phase two: the final clean

This is the long one, and where most of the cost and time sit. The team works top to bottom: light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans first, then the interiors of cabinets, drawers and closets, then windows, glass, tracks and sills. Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive come off during this pass. Bathrooms and new fixtures get polished, and floors are vacuumed and washed more than once, because one pass never gets the dust out of the corners.

Phase three: the touch-up

A few days after the final clean, a little dust has always resettled. The touch-up is a shorter visit before you move furniture in or before an inspection or closing. We wipe the surfaces that collect fresh dust, check the glass for streaks, and do a last pass on floors. It is the difference between a house that looks done and one that feels move-in ready. You can read what each pass includes on our post-construction cleaning page.

What Pauline projects tend to look like

Pauline is a rural community with country homes, so many jobs are additions, detached garages converted to living space, or a new house on family land. Dirt roads and red clay get tracked in, and that clay dries into a fine powder that rides along with the drywall dust. Rural properties also often mean well water and wide-open windows during the build, so ledges collect more outdoor dust than a tight suburban lot. What homeowners should and should not do between phases

Between the rough clean and the final clean, resist the urge to wipe. Every dry cloth dragged across fresh drywall dust grinds it into the paint and the countertop. Keep the HVAC off while sanding is happening, and replace the filter once the dust work is finished. If the contractor still needs to touch up paint, ask them to finish before the final clean, not after.

It is also worth walking the house with the contractor before the final clean and taping a small note to anything you want us to look at, such as a stain on a new countertop or a scratch on a window. We can only remove what is removable, and a flagged spot means we can tell you honestly what is a cleaning issue and what is a repair issue. Paint specks come off most glass and tile with patience, but grout haze or cured adhesive on soft stone can need the installer.

For rural jobs like those around Pauline, access matters too. Tell us about gravel drives, gate codes and where the water is. Little details like these keep the visit on schedule, and the team can work longer into the afternoon if a long drive eats the morning.

How much does post-construction cleaning cost in Pauline?

We do not give a number from a text message, because a freshly sanded 2,000-square-foot house and a bathroom remodel are very different. The price depends on project size (square feet and rooms affected), the dust level (drywall sanding creates the most fine dust), whether you want rough, final and touch-up booked together, windows and glass with residue or stickers, and your deadline. A closing or inspection date can mean a bigger team.

Checklist before the cleaners arrive

  • Confirm the contractor is fully done, or tell us which areas are still active.
  • Remove leftover materials, paint cans and tools.
  • Make sure water and power are on.
  • Tell us about delicate finishes, such as unsealed stone or new hardwood.
  • Share your move-in or inspection date so we can plan the touch-up.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need all three phases?

Not always. Small remodels may need only the final clean, but new builds benefit from all three because dust keeps settling after sanding.

Can you remove paint specks and sticker residue?

Yes. Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive are part of the final clean, with care taken on delicate surfaces.
