Why back-to-back bookings need a tighter plan

Same-day turnovers are the hardest kind. Two or three hours between guests leave no slack for a stained towel, an overflowing bin or a missing remote. The most useful thing a professional host can do is give us the booking calendar early, tell us which gaps are tight, and agree on who decides when a delay happens. We would rather call you at 12:30 than send you a message at 3:50.

A few details make the biggest difference to the review score:

  • Bathrooms with no hair on the drain or the vanity edge.
  • A kitchen with no leftover food in the fridge door or the microwave.
  • Floors clean under the bed edge and along the baseboards.
  • Beds made the same way each time, so the listing photos stay true.
  • Supplies refilled before the guest has to ask.
  • Trash gone, bins back in place and the entry swept.

How long it takes, and booking this week

Most of the time goes to bathrooms and the kitchen, so the bedroom count matters less than the bathroom count. We will give you a realistic time range for your property after the visit. If you are planning for the holiday weeks, when family visits and travel fill rentals around Greer, book the estimate early so the dates are held.

What we ask before the first turnover

Before we ever clean, we ask a handful of questions that save time later. How many guests does the property sleep, and does it usually fill? Are there pets allowed, since pet hair changes how long the floors and furniture take? Is there a hot tub, a grill area or a patio that guests use heavily? Do you want the trash taken off the property or only moved to the bin? Do you have a preferred way of folding the towels and arranging pillows? None of this is complicated, but writing it down once means a different team member can cover a shift and the result looks the same.

We also ask what a bad review looked like in the past, if you have had one. Guests rarely complain about the big things. They write about a hair in the shower, a sticky cabinet handle or a faint smell in the bedroom, and those are the details we check on every visit.

One more practical point: keep a short log of every turnover for the first month. Note the date, the guest count and anything the report flagged. After four or five stays you will see a pattern, such as a bathroom that always needs more time or a gap that is always too tight, and you can fix the schedule instead of guessing. We are happy to go through that log with you at a follow-up call.
