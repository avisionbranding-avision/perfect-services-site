Why bedrooms collect so much dust

Bedrooms have soft surfaces, and soft surfaces hold fine dust. Carpet, mattresses, curtains and the shelves inside closets all trap it. The air returns and vents pull dust from the work area through the whole system and drop some of it in every room. After new construction or a large addition near Greer, we often find that the closet is the dustiest space in the house, because it has no air movement and nobody looked in it.

The closet first

New closets arrive with shelving that has been cut, sanded and sometimes painted. The shelf tops carry cut dust, the rod brackets hold paint specks and the floor carries the leftovers from the trim carpenter. We start by vacuuming the shelf tops and corners, then wipe each surface with a damp cloth, then vacuum the floor. Painted shelves are wiped gently so we do not lift the finish.

Walk-in closets with built-ins take more time. Every drawer front and every inside edge gets wiped, as do the hardware and the bottom of the drawer boxes if they are installed.

Our sequence for a bedroom after construction

  1. Remove tape, plastic sheeting, stickers and protective film.
  2. Vacuum high: ceiling corners, fan blades, window frames and the tops of doors.
  3. Clean vents and air returns, with the system off.
  4. Wipe windows, tracks and sills, which often hold paint specks.
  5. Wipe baseboards and trim, then the doors and handles.
  6. Vacuum carpet in several passes, or wash hard floors twice.

Windows deserve special mention. A lot of paint is rolled right up to the glass, and tiny flecks stay behind. We lift them carefully with a plastic scraper on painted surfaces and a flat blade only on glass.

A short checklist for the final walkthrough

  • Closet shelves, rods and floors free of dust
  • Vents and returns wiped
  • Window tracks vacuumed and wiped
  • Baseboards and trim wiped, no paint specks on the floor
  • Switch plates and outlet covers clean
  • Carpet vacuumed without gray lines near the walls
  • Light fixtures and fan blades dust-free

Carpet after a build

New carpet sheds fibers and old carpet near a renovation collects dust deep down. We vacuum thoroughly, but we do not treat paint or adhesive stains on carpet. If you plan to have the carpet professionally cleaned, schedule that after our final pass so dust is not tracked back into it.

Why a damp wipe beats a dry duster

A dry duster or a feather tool pushes construction dust into the air, and it settles again on the surfaces you just cleaned. We vacuum first, then wipe with a lightly damp cloth that traps the dust, and switch to a fresh cloth often. On a large closet, that can mean several cloths. It is slower, but it is the only way to get a bedroom to the point where your hand does not come away gray from a shelf.

Another thing we see after builds is plaster dust in the track of a window or in the groove of a door hinge. A small brush and a narrow vacuum nozzle are the tools for that, not a bigger cloth.
