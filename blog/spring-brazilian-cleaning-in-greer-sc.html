Why the method suits spring

Spring cleaning is detail work. You are not just tidying, you are cleaning the places a weekly visit rarely reaches. The Brazilian method fits because it starts from the corners. Every surface is wiped by hand, furniture and small items are moved and cleaned under, door frames and switches are detailed, and floors are washed by hand along the walls. At the end the team walks each room against a checklist.

Greer has its own spring conditions. Heavy pollen settles on sills and inside window tracks, and wet weather brings red clay onto floors near the doors. Both are the sort of thing hand work handles better than a quick pass.

What you can do before the team arrives

  • Put away loose items from floors, counters and nightstands.
  • Wash or swap the winter bedding, so beds can be cleaned around.
  • Open blinds so the sills and tracks are visible.
  • Decide whether the pets will stay in a room or go out.
  • Make a short list of the spots that bother you, such as a stained tub or a greasy cabinet.

You do not have to clean first. The point of the visit is to do what you cannot easily do yourself, so a short list of worries is more useful than a tidied house.

Pollen, pets and allergies

Spring is when many households start sneezing, and a house full of settled pollen does not help. A few things make a difference. Window tracks collect pollen and hold it, so we clean them by hand rather than vacuuming over them. Pet hair collects at baseboards and under furniture, which is exactly where a hand-detailed clean goes. Bedding and curtains are best washed by you, and we work around them.

If anyone in the family has allergies or asthma, mention it at the estimate. We can talk about which products suit the household and which rooms to focus on first, usually the bedrooms. You can also ask us to leave out strongly scented products. We do not make health promises, but a thorough clean that removes settled dust from edges and under furniture is a sensible part of a spring routine.
