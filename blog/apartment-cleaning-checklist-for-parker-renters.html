The 45-minute weekly reset

You do not need a full day. Set a timer and work in this order, so you never walk over a surface you have already finished.

  1. Open windows or the balcony door and collect dishes, laundry and trash.
  2. Spray the shower, tub and toilet, then leave the product to work.
  3. Wipe kitchen counters, the sink and the stovetop. Do the microwave by heating a bowl of water for two minutes, then wiping the steam-softened splatter.
  4. Dust shelves, TV stand and window sills with a damp microfiber cloth.
  5. Scrub the bathroom you sprayed, then mirror and faucet.
  6. Vacuum, then mop hard floors from the far corner out to the door.
  7. Empty the trash and put in new liners.

What a landlord or property manager looks at

Whether you are renewing or moving out, a walkthrough tends to focus on the same places. Keep these in mind during the year, not only on the last day.

  • Stovetop, drip pans and the inside of the oven door
  • Bathroom grout, shower door tracks and the base of the toilet
  • Baseboards and door frames, where scuffs and dust show up
  • Window tracks and the glass on a balcony or patio door
  • Carpet traffic lanes and any spots under furniture
  • The refrigerator, including shelves and the door seals

Small-space tricks that actually work

In a one-bedroom, clutter is what makes dirt visible. Clear the kitchen counter before you wipe it, and the job takes half the time. Keep a small basket by the door for shoes so grit stays off the floor. Use a squeegee on the shower wall after every use; it takes ten seconds and slows soap scum noticeably in humid Upstate summers.

Pets change the math. Cat and dog hair collects along baseboards, behind the couch and in the corners of stairwell landings. Vacuum a second time in a different direction and the hair that lifted the first pass will not be left behind. More small-space ideas are in our post on cleaning Greer apartments and condos.

Which jobs are worth handing off

Weekly upkeep is easy to do yourself. The jobs that eat a Saturday are the deeper ones: scrubbing grout, cleaning inside the oven, wiping baseboards in every room, polishing a balcony door and sanitizing every handle and switch. A professional visit covers all of that in one pass. If the unit has not been cleaned in months, a apartment and condo cleaning visit followed by regular upkeep is easier than catching up alone.

Gate codes and parking count too. If we need a code to get in, share it when you book so the team does not lose time at the entrance.

A realistic month in a rental

Think of the year in layers. Weekly, you do the 45-minute reset. Monthly, you add one rotating job: the oven one month, the refrigerator the next, then the inside of cabinets, then the window tracks. Every three months, you wipe baseboards and door frames, wash the shower curtain or door tracks and flip or rotate any rugs. Spread out like this, nothing builds up into a weekend project.

Mill-era buildings and older apartments can have uneven floors and tight corners where a standard mop leaves a damp edge. Wring the mop well, work in small sections and leave the windows open so the floor dries before you walk across it. In humid months, a bathroom without a strong fan holds moisture, so wipe the walls and leave the door open after a shower to help prevent mildew at the grout.

When the schedule slips, which it does for everyone, the sequence to restart is simple: bathroom, kitchen, floors, then everything else. If you only have twenty minutes, those three jobs change how the whole unit feels the moment someone walks in.
