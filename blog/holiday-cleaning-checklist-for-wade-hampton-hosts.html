Holiday cleaning checklist for Wade Hampton hosts

Prep a Wade Hampton, SC home for holiday guests with this one-time house cleaning checklist: what to do first, what to leave to pros and how to book early.

It is late November, which means the guest list for the holidays is probably already forming and your house still has the front-hall mess from fall. If you are hosting in Wade Hampton, the next four to five weeks are the time to plan cleaning, not leave it to the afternoon before. This checklist is written for hosts who want the house ready without losing a weekend to it, and it points out which jobs are worth handing to a one-time house cleaning crew.

Work backward from the day guests arrive

Pick the date your first guest arrives, then plan three passes.

  1. Two weeks out: declutter closets, guest room and the entry. Wash the bedding you will use. Decide what you will not use and put it away.
  2. Three to five days out: the main clean, done by you or by a crew: kitchen, bathrooms, floors, baseboards and glass.
  3. The day before: a light touch-up, fresh towels and a quick wipe of the bathroom and kitchen counters.

The rooms guests actually see

  • Entry and front door: wipe the door, switch plates and the floor where coats and boots land.
  • Kitchen: degrease the stovetop, wipe cabinet fronts and handles, clean the fridge shelf you will use for leftovers.
  • Guest bathroom: scrub the tub or shower, clean the toilet base, polish the mirror and restock towels and soap.
  • Living room and dining area: dust surfaces, vacuum under cushions, clean glass on tables and mirrors.
  • Guest bedroom: dust the nightstand, clean under the bed and make the bed with fresh linens.
  • Floors: vacuum and mop, ending at the entry.

Small details that make a house feel ready

Think about the order in which a guest moves through your home: the driveway, the front door, the entry, the kitchen where everyone gathers, the guest bath, then the room where they sleep. Spend your effort in that sequence.

Guests rarely notice a clean kitchen. They notice a sticky handle, a bathroom mirror with streaks, or the corner by the front door where dust has gathered. Our Brazilian detail-first method works through those spots by hand: switches, handles, door frames, the base of the toilet. If you are cleaning yourself, take thirty minutes at the end of the main clean to walk the house with a cloth and check exactly those places.

A few extra notes from the walkthroughs we do in the weeks before big family weekends. First, leave room in the refrigerator: clear out old containers before you shop, or the shelves will be crowded and hard to clean. Second, decide where coats and bags will go, because a bed piled with winter jackets cannot be cleaned or made. Third, run a load of towels, since guest towels that came out of a closet smell like the closet. Fourth, if someone in the family has allergies, tell us when you book, because vacuuming and dusting order changes: dust high first, vacuum last, and wash bedding before the guest room is dusted. None of this is difficult, but it is the type of thing that gets forgotten when the calendar is full.
