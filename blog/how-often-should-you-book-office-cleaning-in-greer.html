How often should you book office cleaning in Greer?

How often should you book office cleaning in Greer? A frequency guide by foot traffic, restrooms and business type, plus how to start with a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Foot traffic and restrooms decide the schedule more than square footage does.
  • Most small Greer offices land on weekly or twice-weekly visits; busy retail and clinics often need more.
  • Perfect Services sets the schedule and the price after a free in-home estimate, so you only pay for the frequency you need.

For most small offices in Greer, the honest answer is once or twice a week. A five-person office with one restroom and a quiet front door can stay in good shape on a weekly visit. A salon, a clinic or a showroom that sees customers all day usually needs more, because restrooms and entry glass show wear within a day or two. The right number depends on how many people walk through the door, not on how big the building is.

Start with foot traffic, not square feet

People often ask about square footage first, and it does matter for how long a visit takes. But for how often, the better questions are these: how many people use the space on a normal day, how many of them are customers rather than staff, and how many restrooms are shared. Two offices of the same size can need very different schedules. A quiet accounting office near SC-14 and a storefront on Trade Street with walk-in customers are a good example.

When our team walks a commercial space before an estimate, we look at the same few things every time: the entry glass and door handles, the restroom count, the break room, and how much carpet versus hard floor there is. Those tell us more about the right rhythm than any floor plan. If you would like that walkthrough for your space, you can book it on the commercial office cleaning in Greer page.

Signs your office needs more visits

You do not need a clipboard to tell when a schedule is too light. These are the signals we hear about most from business owners:

  • Restrooms look worn by mid-week, or you restock paper and soap yourself.
  • The break room fridge, microwave and sink are the first thing staff complain about.
  • Door handles, light switches and keypads look grimy before the next visit.
  • Floors show tracked-in dirt near the entrance every afternoon. In Greer, wet weather and red clay make this worse from late fall through early spring.
  • Customers or clients mention the front area before you do.

If two or three of these sound familiar, move up one step on the schedule. Going from weekly to twice weekly is often enough, and it costs far less than losing the good impression a clean front area gives you.

It works the other way too. If your team works from home two or three days a week, the space may not need the same attention it did a year ago. Fewer people means fewer trash bags, fewer coffee cups and less restroom traffic. Many offices keep the restrooms and break room on a regular rhythm and do the carpets and glass less often. Tell us how your week really looks and we will plan around it.

Regular visits and the occasional deeper clean

A regular schedule keeps surfaces, restrooms and floors under control, but it is not meant to strip carpet edges or scrub baseboards every time. Many offices add a deeper visit a few times a year, for example before the holidays or when a lease or inspection is coming up. If you are moving into a space or finishing a buildout, our commercial and office cleaning service pairs well with post-construction cleaning to clear dust before staff arrive. Working out your schedule in three steps

  1. Count your restrooms and note how many customers, not just staff, use them each day.
  2. Walk your entry at the end of a normal day and look at the glass, handles and floor.
  3. Pick a starting frequency, then ask us to review it after the first month.

Is weekly office cleaning enough for a small Greer office?
