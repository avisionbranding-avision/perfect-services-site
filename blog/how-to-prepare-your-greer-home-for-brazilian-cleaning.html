How to prepare your Greer home for Brazilian cleaning

Prepare for Brazilian house cleaning in Greer: what to pick up, what to tell the team and what we do in the first hour of a detail-first visit to your home.

November 14, 2026 · 4 min read

  • Pick up floors and surfaces; leave the cleaning itself to the team.
  • Tell us about pets, floor types and any fragile items before the visit.
  • A free in-home estimate comes first, so the plan and price are clear.

You do not need to clean first

The most common thing we hear before a first visit is "I have to clean before the cleaners come." You do not. Brazilian house cleaning is detail work: wiping every surface by hand, cleaning baseboards and corners, moving small items and cleaning under them, washing floors along the walls. What helps us most is not a cleaner house, it is a clearer one. Here is how to get a Greer home ready in about thirty minutes.

Pick up the floors and the flat surfaces

Our team works from the top of a room down to the floor, so anything on the floor or on a surface slows that order. Walk through each room with a laundry basket and a trash bag.

  • Pick up toys, shoes, cords, pet bowls and loose clothes from floors.
  • Clear kitchen counters enough that we can wipe them fully, not around small appliances.
  • Put away bathroom items you use every day, like toothbrushes and razors.
  • Remove loose papers and mail from tables and desks.
  • Clear the stairs, since steps and railings get cleaned too.
  • Take down anything fragile or sentimental that sits on a low shelf and might be bumped.

Tell us what we should know

A short note before the visit saves time on the day. The details we look for are the type of floor in each room (hardwood, tile, carpet, laminate), whether any pets will be home, any rooms you want skipped, and anything with a special finish, such as marble or a stone countertop. If someone in the house is sensitive to strong scents, tell us and we can plan green cleaning products. If you want extra attention somewhere, like a shower with hard water marks or a stovetop with baked-on grease, mention it now, because the estimate is the right time to plan for it.

What we do in the first hour

People ask what actually happens when the team arrives, so here is the usual order. It can vary with the size of the home.

  1. Walkthrough. We go room by room with you, or by ourselves if you are out, to see what needs extra time and what to be careful with.
  2. Kitchen and bathrooms first. These are the most detailed rooms. Kitchen work includes degreasing the stovetop, hood and cabinet fronts. Bathroom work includes grout, the toilet base and fixtures.
  3. Dusting high to low. Door frames, switches, handles, shelves, then baseboards, so dust falls onto surfaces we have not cleaned yet.
  4. Moving and cleaning under. Small furniture and items are lifted, cleaned under and put back.
  5. Floors last. We wash them along the walls by hand and finish at the door.

That last pass matters in Greer. Red clay and pollen collect along the edges of hardwood and tile near entries, and a mop that skips the edges leaves a line you can see in afternoon light.

A note for older Greer homes near downtown: original hardwood, plaster walls and older fixtures need gentler products and more care around edges. Point those out and we will adjust, since we would rather ask than guess. Newer homes near GSP Airport and the BMW plant often have open floor plans with large tile or hardwood areas, which take longer to wash by hand, so tell us the layout when you book.

The spots people forget to mention

Some places are easy to overlook, and they are often the ones that make a house feel clean. These include the tracks of sliding doors, ceiling fan blades, the tops of door frames, the inside of the microwave, and the base of the toilet. We cover many of these as part of the detail method. For anything unusual, like the inside of the oven or refrigerator, ask during the estimate and we will tell you what is included and what is an add-on. Many homeowners combine the first visit with deep cleaning for the kitchen.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to tidy my house before a Brazilian cleaning?

You need to pick things up, not clean. Clear floors, counters and surfaces of toys, papers and personal items so the team can wipe and wash everything properly. The cleaning itself, including corners and baseboards, is our job.

Should I be home during the first visit?

It is helpful but not required. If you are home, we can do a quick walkthrough and hear your priorities. If you are out, leave entry details and notes about pets, floors and rooms to skip.

What should I do with my pets on cleaning day?

Keep them in a closed room or outside if possible, and tell us beforehand about any pets in the house. It keeps them calm, keeps doors from being left open, and lets the team work on floors without interruptions.
