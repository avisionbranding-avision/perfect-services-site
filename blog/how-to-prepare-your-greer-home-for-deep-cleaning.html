How to prepare your Greer home for deep cleaning

Prepare for deep cleaning in Greer, SC: what to clear, what to leave, the spots we ask you to check, and how the free in-home estimate works.

Key takeaways

  • Clear access beats pre-cleaning: open rooms, empty shelves you want cleaned and clear counters let the team reach more.
  • Tell us in advance about stains, damage and delicate items so they are handled the right way.
  • Perfect Services prices deep cleaning after a free in-home estimate, where we see the home's condition first-hand.

People often spend the weekend before a deep cleaning scrubbing the house, which is the opposite of what helps. The team will do that scrubbing, and your effort is better spent on access: open floors, clear counters and a short list of notes. To prepare for deep cleaning in Greer well, think of yourself as clearing the runway, not doing the job.

What we look at in the first few minutes

On a deep cleaning day, the first thing the team does is walk the house. We look at the kitchen, the bathrooms, the floors and the edges, and we decide the order of work. In a lot of Greer homes, the same things slow us down: closets stacked to the door, bathroom counters full of products, and floors covered in toys or laundry. Everything on this page is aimed at those three problems.

The team usually works from top to bottom, so ceiling fans, vents and light fixtures come first, then shelves and surfaces, then baseboards and door frames, and floors last. If a room is blocked at the start, the whole chain slows down.

Do this before the team arrives

You do not have to finish every item, and a home with young kids or a full schedule will not look like a catalog. Do what you can, and tell us what you could not get to. We plan around it.

Here is the list we hand to most first-time customers. It covers the places where a little preparation saves the most time.

  • Clear kitchen counters, and empty the sink and dishwasher
  • Take everything off bathroom counters, shower ledges and the tub
  • Remove items from the shelves, cabinets or drawers you want cleaned inside
  • Pick up toys, shoes and clothes from the floors
  • Move small furniture, plants or boxes that block baseboards
  • Clear windowsills so the sills and tracks can be reached
  • Strip any bed that needs fresh linens and set them aside
  • Put away jewelry, medication, cash and personal papers

The kitchen

The kitchen is where the heaviest build-up lives, so give it the most room. Cabinet fronts and handles get degreased, and the range hood and backsplash get attention too. If you want the inside of the oven or fridge done, say so ahead of time and empty the fridge shelves you can. Take small appliances off the counter and leave the coffee maker only if it stays in daily use.

The bathrooms

Bathrooms hold the grout, the glass and the hard water spots. Clear the shower and tub, move bath mats out, and take bottles off the floor around the toilet. We clean behind and around the toilet base, which is hard when the floor is crowded. If you have a spot you already know is stubborn, tell us at the estimate so we plan the time.

Things to tell us, not fix yourself

Some problems need a note, not a cleanup. Please do not try them on your own before we arrive.

Tell us about stains on carpet or grout you have already treated, because the wrong product can set them. Mention any surface that scratches easily, such as natural stone, unsealed wood or older marble, so we choose the right method. Point out loose cabinet doors, cracked tiles or anything that could break under scrubbing. And mention any allergies or product sensitivities, because we can adjust the products we use, and a pet-friendly approach is worth planning in advance.

The same goes for pets and access. Keep nervous animals in a room with a closed door, and share the gate code and parking spot. Most Greer homes are easy to reach from I-85 or SC-14, but side streets near Downtown Greer can be tight.
