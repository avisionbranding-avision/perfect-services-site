Before you start: empty first, clean second

Saluda is a small mountain town in Polk County (ZIP 28773) with cabins, vacation homes and historic houses, and many of those older buildings have details that collect dust: deep window sills, painted trim, wood floors with gaps. Because of that, the order of work matters. Get every box, rug and piece of furniture out first. Then take photos of any damage that was already there when you moved in, so you can separate normal wear from anything you are responsible for.

Keep a bucket with a few rags, a scrub brush and a bag for trash. Then follow the list below from the top of each room to the bottom, so dust and drips fall onto floors you have not cleaned yet.

The room-by-room move-out checklist

  • Kitchen: empty and wipe the inside and outside of every cabinet and drawer, including the shelf liners you want to keep.
  • Kitchen: clean inside the refrigerator and freezer, then leave the doors propped open if it will be unplugged.
  • Kitchen: degrease the stovetop, drip pans, range hood and backsplash, and clean the inside of the oven and microwave.
  • Bathrooms: scrub the toilet including the base and behind the tank, the tub or shower, glass doors, grout lines and the inside of the vanity.
  • Bedrooms and closets: wipe shelves and rods, then vacuum the closet floors where hangers drop dust.
  • Whole house: baseboards, door frames, doors, light switches and outlet covers.
  • Windows: sills and tracks, plus blinds slat by slat.
  • Last: vacuum every floor, then mop hard floors from the farthest corner toward the front door.

That is the same list our team follows on a move-out cleaning visit in Saluda, and it is also the standard most property managers use in their inspections.

The spots renters miss most often

After enough turnovers you start to see a pattern. The oven gets cleaned on the inside glass but not the racks or the door seam. The bathroom fan cover has a gray fuzz that nobody notices until the landlord stands on a stool. Sliding door and window tracks hold a line of dirt, and in a mountain town with pollen in spring and damp air in summer, that dirt can get sticky or even grow mildew. Also check the tops of doors and door frames, the inside of the pantry and the corners of the floor where the mop does not reach.

A good test is to run a dry paper towel along a baseboard or a shelf. If it comes back gray, the surface is not finished.
