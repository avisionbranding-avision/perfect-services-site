The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Campobello

How post construction cleaning in Campobello, SC works in three phases: rough clean, final clean and touch-up. See what each covers and how to schedule them.

Perfect Services Team
November 10, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Post-construction cleaning works best in three passes: rough, final and touch-up.
  • Drywall sanding creates the finest dust, and it settles in vents, cabinets and trim.
  • Book the final clean and touch-up together to match your inspection or move-in date.

When the contractor says the job is "done," the house is rarely clean. Drywall dust sits on window ledges, paint specks dot the glass, and every drawer holds a fine gray film. For homes in Campobello, from farm properties to country homes in the foothills, post construction cleaning in Campobello, SC is usually done in three passes rather than one long scrub. Here is how the phases work and why the order matters.

Phase 1: the rough clean, while work is still going

The rough clean happens after the heavy trades are finished but before the final details. The goal is not shine; it is to get rid of the debris that blocks the next step.

  • Sweep and collect scraps, packaging, tape and sawdust.
  • Vacuum floors, subfloors and corners where drywall pieces hide.
  • Wipe down surfaces that painters, tile setters and cabinet installers must work on.
  • Clear dust from window wells so it is not smeared on new paint.

Doing a rough clean first protects the finishing trades. Dust that falls onto a fresh coat of paint is the kind of problem you pay twice to solve. In rural Spartanburg County, where long gravel driveways and open land carry extra dirt into a job site, this pass often has more to collect than people expect.

Phase 2: the final clean, the longest and most detailed

After the trades leave, the final clean brings the house to a livable state. This is where the fine dust gets removed, and where the order matters most:

  1. High surfaces first. Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans are dusted before anything below them.
  2. Windows, glass, tracks and sills. Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive are removed carefully so surfaces are not scratched.
  3. Inside cabinets, drawers and closets. Drywall dust gets in everywhere, even in closed cabinets.
  4. Bathrooms and new fixtures. Faucets, mirrors, vanities and tile polished.
  5. Kitchen. Counters, appliance fronts and new cabinet doors wiped by hand.
  6. Floors last, with multiple passes. Vacuumed, then washed. A single pass leaves fine dust in the grooves.

Fine dust is the heart of the work. Drywall sanding creates the finest dust of any trade, and it lingers on ledges, in vents and inside window tracks. A regular cleaning does not reach it, which is why this is its own service and not a larger version of a house clean.

Phase 3: the touch-up before move-in or closing

Even a good final clean gets disturbed. Furniture is delivered, a plumber returns for a leak, the builder does a punch-list. A touch-up pass catches what came back: a settled film of dust on freshly polished counters, boot prints on clean floors, marks on glass.

The touch-up works best scheduled close to the inspection or the day you receive the keys. It is short compared with the final clean, but it is the one that shapes first impressions.

What owners can do in the meantime

Ask the contractor to remove debris, bins and leftover materials first, since cleaning crews are not hauling construction waste. Seal off rooms still under work with plastic where possible, and keep HVAC filters changed, because dust moves through the system. Store the manufacturer stickers and instructions for new appliances; we remove adhesive from the glass but need to know which surfaces are delicate. Common mistakes with construction cleanup

The first is cleaning too early. A final clean done while a trade still has work left only gets covered with new dust. The second is using water on dust too fast: wet wiping fine drywall dust before vacuuming turns it into paste on trim and glass. We vacuum and dry-dust first, then wipe. The third is skipping the inside of cabinets and closets, because the dust you cannot see is the dust that ends up on your dishes.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need all three phases of post-construction cleaning?

Not always. Small remodels may only need a final clean, while new builds benefit from rough, final and touch-up passes. We recommend phases at the estimate.

Why is construction dust so hard to remove?

Drywall sanding creates very fine dust that settles in vents, tracks and cabinets. It needs multiple passes and careful wiping, not a quick sweep.

Do you remove paint specks and stickers from windows?

Yes. Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive are part of the final clean, removed carefully to avoid scratching glass.
