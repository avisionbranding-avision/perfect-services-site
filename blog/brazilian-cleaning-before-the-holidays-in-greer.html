Brazilian cleaning before the holidays in Greer

Brazilian house cleaning before the holidays in Greer, SC: a detail-first plan for the weeks before guests arrive and how to get a free estimate.

Key takeaways

  • Book the visit three to five days before guests arrive so the house stays fresh.
  • The detail-first method puts hand work into the spots visitors really see: baseboards, bathrooms and the kitchen.

Visitors do not look at your whole house. They look at the bathroom they use, the kitchen where everyone gathers, the entry and the spot at the table. A Brazilian-style clean before the holidays puts the hand work exactly there. If you are hosting in Greer, this is how to use a visit well and when to book it.

Timing: three to five days before guests arrive

A house cleaned the same morning still has the mess of last-minute prep on top of it. A visit three to five days ahead gives the house time to be lived in lightly while keeping the work done. It also leaves you the day before for cooking and the final touches: fresh towels, a quick floor sweep and a trash run.

Holiday weeks fill up fast. If you want a specific date, ask for the estimate a couple of weeks ahead, and for this week, ask early and see what is open.

Where the detail work goes

The bathroom guests will use

This is the room visitors judge most. Grout, the toilet base, the shower track and the underside of the vanity edge are hand-scrubbed, and the glass and the fixtures are polished.

The kitchen

The stovetop, hood and cabinet fronts are degreased, which matters before a big meal. Counters are wiped by hand, and the appliance fronts are cleaned so nothing shows fingerprints.

Entry, dining room and the guest bed

Door frames, light switches and handles are wiped, and the dining room table and chairs are dusted. Furniture and small items are moved and cleaned under, then returned. Floors are hand-washed along the walls.

  • Bathrooms scrubbed, grout and toilet base included
  • Kitchen degreased: stovetop, hood, cabinet fronts
  • Door frames, switches and handles detailed
  • Baseboards and corners cleaned by hand
  • Floors washed along the walls
  • Final walk-through against the room checklist

A Greer-specific note on old and new houses

Greer has historic homes near Downtown Greer and Trade Street, and newer neighborhoods near GSP Airport and the BMW plant. Older houses have more trim, more corners and floors that show scratches, so the edge work takes longer there. Newer open-plan houses have large tile or hardwood areas, so the floor work is where the time goes. In both, the holiday visit is easier when the surfaces are clear, so put away loose items before the team arrives.

What the walk-through catches

At the free estimate we look at the rooms guests will use and note what needs the most hand work: a stovetop with baked-on grease, grout that has darkened, baseboards that have not been wiped in a long time. You see the same list, so there are no surprises on the day. It also lets us tell you honestly if a deeper first visit makes more sense than a standard one before a house full of company.

What to do yourself, and what to leave

Leave the scrubbing to the team and do the planning yourself: clear counters, put away toys and decor you do not want moved, and tell us which rooms matter most. If you plan to use the guest room, mention it so we can start there. If you are cooking, wait to clean the oven until after the big meal, or ask about adding it to the visit. You can also pair the holiday visit with one-time cleaning add-ons like the inside of the fridge.

After the holiday, many families book a second visit to reset the house, which also tells you whether a recurring plan makes sense. A regular schedule keeps the edges done every time, and recurring visits cost less per visit.

A simple plan for the week of the visit

Before the team arrives, take ten minutes to walk the house with a notepad. List the rooms guests will use, the spots you know are bad and anything that should not be moved. Hand that list over at the start, since it saves guessing.

After the visit, keep the clean going with small habits: shoes at the door, a damp cloth on the kitchen counter after dinner and a towel swap in the guest bathroom each morning. Those three keep the house at the standard the team left it for the length of the stay, and they make the next visit easier.
