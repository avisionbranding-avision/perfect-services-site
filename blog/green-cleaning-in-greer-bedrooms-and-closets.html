Green cleaning in Greer: bedrooms and closets

Dust is the main job in a bedroom

A bathroom needs scrubbing. A bedroom needs dust removal, and that is a different skill. Dry rags and feather dusters push fine dust into the air, where it lands again an hour later. For allergy and asthma households in Greer, where spring pollen blows in and humid summers hold dust in the air, that is a real problem. Our eco-friendly cleaning in Greer uses microfiber cloths that capture dust instead of spreading it, and a hand-wiping technique that uses less product.

A bedroom clean without the scent

Many people sleep in the room that gets the most perfume from cleaners. A fragrance-free option solves that. We use low-odor products on request and leave the room smelling like nothing, which is the goal for sensitive sleepers. If you would rather we use only your own products, that is also fine.

What we do, top to bottom

  1. Dust ceiling fans, light fixtures and the tops of tall furniture with a slightly damp microfiber cloth.
  2. Wipe the headboard, nightstand, lamp bases and dresser surfaces.
  3. Wipe baseboards, window sills and door frames.
  4. Strip and re-make the bed if you set out clean linens.
  5. Vacuum the floor, then the edges and under the bed.
  6. Wipe handles, switches and mirrors last.

Closets and the places dust hides

  • Shelves above the hanging rod, which rarely get touched.
  • The floor of the closet, where shoes bring in outdoor dirt, including Upstate red clay.
  • The top edge of door frames and closet doors.
  • Vents and returns in the bedroom wall or ceiling.
  • Behind the bed and behind the dresser, if they can be moved safely.
  • The inside of lamp shades and the base of a fan.

Kids' rooms and pets

A child's room has a different list: toys, stuffed animals, art supplies and crumbs. We do not move your child's things around, and we work with gentler products on surfaces little hands touch. If the dog sleeps on the bed, we focus on the floors and the bed frame, and tell you what we saw so you can wash the dog bed. The green cleaning service is built for families with babies and young children, pet owners and sensitive households.

Mattress and pillow tips between visits

Between our visits, a few small habits keep a bedroom easier to clean. Wash sheets and pillowcases every week or two. Vacuum the mattress surface a couple of times a year with the upholstery tool if you are dust-sensitive. Open the window on dry days so the room airs out, and keep it closed on high-pollen days in spring. A damp cloth on the nightstand and the window sill every week removes the dust before it builds.

The closet floor and the shoes we do not see

The closet floor catches whatever comes in on your shoes, including the red clay that is part of life in the Upstate. Dry clay turns to a fine powder that spreads through the room. We vacuum the closet floor and the first foot of the room around it, since that is where the powder settles. If you keep shoes on a rack inside the closet, tell us and we will vacuum under it too.

Stuffed animals, throw pillows and décor

Fabric items collect dust, and kids' rooms have many. We dust or vacuum what we can reach, and tell you which items to wash on your own. A weekly shake outdoors and a monthly wash for stuffed animals keeps them manageable. We do not wash them, but we flag any that look heavily soiled so you can decide.

When you are choosing between regular and green cleaning for a bedroom, ask yourself what the room is for. A bedroom is where you spend about a third of your day with your face near the pillow, so product residue and fragrance matter there more than in a garage or a laundry room. For a baby's room, the case is even stronger. If you can only choose one room for the gentler approach, the bedroom is a sensible place to start, and many families extend it to the rest of the house once they see how it works.

We also suggest keeping a short list of what bothers each person in the house, such as dust, smell or pet hair, and sharing it at the estimate. The list becomes part of the checklist, and every visit follows it.
