Why the turnover clean matters so much

Guests rarely complain about a missing decoration, but they do mention hair in a shower, a sticky counter or a musty smell. Cleanliness is one of the categories that every guest rates, so a consistent routine is worth more than one heroic deep clean. The best turnover systems are boring: the same steps, in the same order, every time.

The Airbnb turnover checklist

On arrival

  • Walk through and photograph anything damaged, missing or unusually dirty
  • Open windows briefly for air and check for any odors
  • Strip all beds and gather towels, then start the first laundry load

Bedrooms and living areas

  • Make beds with fresh linens, tight corners and pillows arranged the same way each time
  • Check under beds, nightstand drawers and closets for left-behind items
  • Dust surfaces, TV and remote, lamp switches and ceiling fans
  • Vacuum all floors, including under furniture, and mop hard floors

Kitchen

  • Empty the dishwasher, wash anything left in the sink and wipe appliances inside and out
  • Clean the microwave, coffee maker, stovetop and counters
  • Wipe the refrigerator shelves and discard leftover food
  • Restock coffee, paper towels, trash bags and dish soap

Bathrooms

  • Scrub the toilet, shower, tub and sink, and polish glass and fixtures
  • Replace towels, bath mats and toiletries
  • Check for hair in drains and on floors, which guests spot instantly

Final walk-through

  • Take a few photos of each room as proof of readiness
  • Confirm the lockbox or smart lock, thermostat and lights are set
  • Note any supplies that are running low

Laundry and linen: the hidden bottleneck

For most hosts, laundry is what turns a two-hour turnover into a four-hour one. Decide early how it will be handled: washed on site, sent to a laundry service or swapped with a second set of linens kept at the property. A second full set of sheets and towels per bed is the simplest fix for tight windows, because the cleaner can make the beds immediately and wash the used set while finishing the rest of the home.

  • Keep linens white or light so stains and wear are easy to see
  • Use a labeled shelf or bin for clean sets, one per bed
  • Replace pillows and mattress protectors when they look worn, not only when a guest complains
  • Flag stains the same day so they can be treated before they set

Common issues guests mention in reviews

If you read enough short-term rental reviews, the same complaints show up. Most are preventable with a simple routine:

  • Hair in the bathroom. Check the shower floor, drain and bathroom floor corners last.
  • Dust on surfaces. Look at nightstands, TV stands and window sills with the lights on.
  • Kitchen odors. Empty trash and wipe the refrigerator, even if it looks fine.
  • Sticky floors. Mop with a clean mop head and change the water.
  • Missing basics. Check toilet paper, soap, towels and trash bags before leaving.
