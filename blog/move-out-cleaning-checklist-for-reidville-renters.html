Move-out cleaning checklist for Reidville renters

A room-by-room move-out cleaning checklist for Reidville, SC renters, so you can aim for your full deposit back. Free in-home estimate for move-out cleaning.

Key takeaways

  • Move-out cleaning goes inside cabinets, appliances, closets and window tracks, not just the visible surfaces.
  • Work empty, from the top down, and finish with the floors on your way out.
  • A free in-home estimate gives you an exact price before the lease-end deadline.

Landlords walking through a rental after move-out almost never complain about the living room. The deductions come from the inside of the oven, the refrigerator shelves, the bathroom grout and the closet floors. If you are renting in Reidville and want your full deposit back, this checklist follows the order the walkthrough usually goes in, so you can move out cleaning in Reidville yourself or decide what to hand to a team.

Before you start: the ground rules

  • Clean only after the last box is out. Anything you clean while furniture is still there gets dusty again.
  • Work top to bottom in every room: fans and light fixtures, then walls and doors, then surfaces, then floors.
  • Finish with the floors, and leave through the door you will lock last.
  • Take dated photos of each room at the end. They help if there is a dispute later.

Read your lease too. Many require the home to be returned in the condition you received it, apart from normal wear, and some spell out carpet or appliance rules.

Give yourself more time than you think you need. Even a tidy renter usually finds that the kitchen alone takes a couple of hours when the oven, refrigerator and cabinets are all included, and the bathrooms take another good stretch after that.

Kitchen checklist

The kitchen is where most deposits get docked. Plan on it taking longer than any other room.

  • Empty and wipe the inside and outside of every cabinet and drawer
  • Clean inside the refrigerator and freezer, including shelves and drawers, with the power off if you are defrosting
  • Scrub the inside of the oven, the racks and the door glass
  • Clean the microwave inside and out
  • Degrease the range hood, stovetop and backsplash
  • Wipe the sink and polish the faucet
  • Mop the floor, including under the spot where the fridge stood if you can move it

Bathrooms

  • Scrub the toilet, including the base and behind it
  • Remove soap scum and hard water spots from the shower, tub and glass
  • Scrub grout lines
  • Clean vanity interiors and drawers
  • Polish mirrors and chrome
  • Wash the floor last

Bedrooms, closets and the rest of the house

Bedrooms are quick if they were empty, but closets hold surprises: dust on the shelf above the rod, scuffs near the floor and small stains from boxes. Give each closet a proper wipe before you vacuum. With newer family homes common around Reidville, there are often several of these, plus a bonus room or a laundry area that renters forget until the last day.

  • Closets: wipe shelves and rods, and vacuum or mop the floor
  • Wipe baseboards, doors, door frames and light switches
  • Clean window sills, tracks and blinds
  • Dust vents, ceiling fan blades and light fixtures
  • Vacuum carpet in a straight pattern and mop hard floors edge to edge
  • Remove nail-hole dust and scuffs from walls only if your lease allows touch-ups

What inspectors and landlords check first

From the move-outs we see, the same spots come up again and again. Landlords open the oven door, pull out a refrigerator drawer, look at the shower floor and run a hand along the top of a door frame. They also check closet floors and the inside of the cabinet under the kitchen sink, where water marks and crumbs collect. If you only have an hour left, spend it on those places, not on wiping a counter that already looks fine. A little time on the hidden spots often protects more of the deposit than a perfect living room.
