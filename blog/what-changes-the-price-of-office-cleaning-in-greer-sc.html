What changes the price of office cleaning in Greer, SC

What changes the price of office cleaning in Greer, SC? Square footage, frequency, restrooms and floors explained. Exact pricing after a free walk-through.

Perfect Services Team
November 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Square footage matters, but restrooms and frequency often matter more.
  • Daily, weekly and monthly service are priced very differently per visit.
  • The exact price comes after a free walk-through, not a phone quote.

An office does not get cheaper just because it is small. A four-desk insurance office with two busy restrooms may need more attention than a bigger one with a single bathroom and few visitors. If you are trying to understand what drives office cleaning prices in Greer, this walk through the pricing logic should help, even before anyone visits your space.

Frequency usually moves the number most

The same space cleaned daily, weekly or monthly is a different job. Daily visits are lighter because dust and trash never pile up; the team keeps up. Weekly visits mean a little more per visit, and monthly visits are closer to a reset than a refresh. The per-visit price tends to fall as frequency rises, while the monthly total rises, so compare totals, not just per-visit numbers. Our commercial cleaning in Greer schedules run all three, and most small offices settle on weekly or a few nights a week.

Restrooms: the quiet price driver

Restrooms take the most time per square foot. They are disinfected, restocked and checked, and a busy salon or clinic restroom can need attention at every visit. Count them for your own estimate: how many toilets, how many sinks, how many people use them. A warehouse-office with one bathroom and an office with a waiting room and two restrooms cost very different amounts to maintain.

The other factors

  1. Square feet. Total area to clean, including halls and storage.
  2. Hours. After-hours or early-morning work can affect scheduling.
  3. Floor types. Carpet, tile, vinyl and hardwood each need different tools and time.
  4. Foot traffic. A retail floor off US-29 collects more than a back-office with six employees.
  5. Extras. Glass doors, break rooms, reception furniture and inside refrigerators.

How to compare quotes honestly

Ask every company for the same things: the tasks per visit, the number of visits, the supplies they provide and the contact person. Then line them up. A low price that leaves out restroom restocking or glass is not the same service. Check whether each company inspects their own work, and how problems are handled. If the cheapest quote cannot answer these, you will probably end up paying for the fix elsewhere.

A short note on contracts: be careful with long commitments you cannot leave. A fair arrangement lets you adjust frequency as your business changes, especially in seasons where more staff are in the office.

How to keep the cost sensible

  • Clear desks of loose papers so the team can wipe them
  • Take out food waste during the day, not just at night
  • Tell the company about spills and spots as they happen
  • Keep supplies in one labeled place
  • Choose a frequency that matches real traffic

Three small Greer offices, three different bills

To make this concrete without inventing anyone, picture three typical spaces and how each would be looked at. A quiet professional office with six desks, a small break room and one restroom is mostly floors, desks and the bathroom, so frequency is the main decision. A hair salon or clinic has constant restroom use, towels, glass and a reception area that clients see first, so restroom time and disinfecting weigh more. A small retail shop off US-29 has big glass doors, public foot traffic and floors that show every footprint, so entry areas and glass take a share of the time.

These are the kinds of differences the walk-through is meant to capture. None of them can be read off a square-foot number, which is why a flat per-foot price quoted over the phone often turns out wrong in one direction or the other.

One more thing worth knowing: offices change. A company that adds staff, opens a second treatment room or starts hosting customers on Saturdays will need a different plan within a year. A good cleaning partner revisits the scope when that happens instead of keeping an old price on a new reality.

What stays the same

Whatever the size, the checklist is stable: desks and workstations wiped, high-touch points sanitized, restrooms cleaned and restocked, trash removed, floors vacuumed and mopped, glass and entry areas finished.
