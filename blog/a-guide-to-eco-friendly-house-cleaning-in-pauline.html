What green cleaning means in practice

The phrase gets stretched, so here is what we mean by it. Our eco-friendly green cleaning uses low-odor, family- and pet-friendly products on request. It does not skip steps. The team follows the same room-by-room checklist as a regular visit, and the difference is in what is in the bottle and how it is used.

  • Low-odor, gentler products for kitchens and bathrooms, used on request.
  • Microfiber cloths that capture dust rather than spreading it into the air.
  • Surface-safe solutions for floors, matched to the material.
  • A fragrance-free option for sensitive households.
  • Hand-wiping technique that reduces how much product is used overall.
  • Your own products, if you already have ones you trust.

This is a safer choice for a household with a crawling baby, a dog that licks the floor or a family member with asthma or allergies. It is not a promise that nothing in your house will ever irritate someone, so tell us about specific sensitivities when you book.

A first-visit walkthrough for a country home

Before we price any green clean, we walk through the house with you. The questions are simple, and they shape the visit.

  1. Who lives here? Babies, small kids, pets and anyone with allergies or asthma.
  2. Is there a scent you want to avoid, or one you like?
  3. Which rooms get the most traffic? In country homes it is usually the mudroom, kitchen and back door.
  4. Which surfaces need care, such as hardwood, natural stone or unsealed tile?
  5. Do you want us to use your own products?

Dirt tracked in from the yard is part of life out here, and it is mostly dry soil and grit. We vacuum before we mop so the grit is lifted first rather than dragged around. If you have a pet, we spend extra time along the baseboards and furniture edges where hair gathers. Small habits that help between visits

A green clean works best when the house is easy to keep up. A few habits make a real difference. Put a mat inside and outside each door and shake them out weekly. Wipe pet paws before the dog crosses the kitchen. Open windows for ten minutes after cooking rather than covering smells with sprays. Keep a damp microfiber cloth under the kitchen sink for quick counter wipes, so you do not reach for a stronger spray.

None of this replaces a professional visit, but it keeps dust and grease from building up, which is what forces stronger products in the first place.
