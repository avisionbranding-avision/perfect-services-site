Kitchen remodel cleanup checklist for Greer homeowners

A room-by-room kitchen remodel cleanup checklist for Greer, SC homeowners: what to clean first, where dust hides and when to call a post-construction team.

Key takeaways

  • Clean a finished kitchen from the top down: ceiling fixtures and vents first, floors last.
  • Cabinet interiors, drawer tracks and the gap behind the range hold the most leftover dust.

The last day of a kitchen remodel usually ends the same way: the installer packs the truck, the countertop gleams, and an hour later you notice that every new drawer has a gritty film inside it. If you need remodel cleanup in Greer, the order you work in matters more than how hard you scrub. This checklist follows the order our team uses when we walk into a freshly finished kitchen.

Kitchens are the room where renovation dust does the most damage to your routine. You are about to put dishes, pots and food back into cabinets, so anything left behind ends up on things you eat from. Greer homes range from older houses near Downtown Greer and Trade Street to newer builds near GSP Airport, and both kinds see plenty of kitchen projects. The dust looks the same either way: fine, pale and clingy.

Before you start: what to clear and what to wait for

Give the work a day to settle. Cabinet installers, tile setters and painters all leave dust in the air for a while, and wiping too early just moves it around. Once the contractor confirms the punch list is finished, do three things first:

  • Remove any plastic sheeting and floor protection, folding the dusty side inward.
  • Check that paint and caulk are fully cured before you wipe near them.
  • Open a window if the weather allows, and swap the HVAC filter, since it has been pulling dust all through the project.

Keep the dishes and small appliances out of the room until the surfaces are done. Putting them back early means washing them twice.

The kitchen remodel cleanup checklist, top to bottom

Dust falls, so work from the ceiling down and finish with the floor. Skipping around is how people end up vacuuming twice.

  1. Ceiling, fan and light fixtures. Wipe fan blades, recessed light trims and any new pendant lights. New glass shades often carry fingerprints and a haze from the installer's hands.
  2. Vents and the range hood. Vacuum the grille of the return and supply vents. Wipe the outside of the hood and the underside, where sanding dust sticks.
  3. Tops of upper cabinets. You cannot see it from the floor, but a flat surface above eye level collects a layer of dust that falls onto the counters the first time someone opens a door.
  4. Cabinet interiors. Vacuum first with a brush attachment, then wipe each shelf with a damp microfiber cloth. Do the corners and the shelf-pin holes.
  5. Drawers and drawer tracks. Pull each drawer out if you can. Screws, wood chips and dust gather in the runners and make new drawers feel rough.
  6. Cabinet fronts, handles and hardware. Wipe with a barely damp cloth, then dry. Check for adhesive from protective film around hinges and pulls.
  7. Countertops and backsplash. Remove sealant haze or grout film carefully, using only products the installer approves for your material.
  8. Appliances. Peel remaining protective film, wipe the edges and the door gaskets, and look at the gap beside the range where debris falls.
  9. Sink and faucet. Clean the aerator area and the drain flange; plaster and sawdust like to hide there.
  10. Windows and sills. Scrape paint specks gently from glass and clean the track.
  11. Floors last. Vacuum, then mop. Pay attention to the toe kick, the baseboard edge and the transition strip to the next room.

Where remodel dust hides in a Greer kitchen

Most homeowners clean the visible surfaces well and miss the same handful of places. The top of the refrigerator and the space above it, the inside of the pantry, the back edges of shelves and the tracks of a sliding door that leads to the deck all keep dust for weeks. In open-plan homes, which are common around Greer, the dust does not stay in the kitchen at all. It drifts to the living room and settles on the TV, the mantel and the window blinds, so a kitchen project can mean a whole-first-floor cleanup.

If the project also touched a wall or ceiling, expect drywall dust, which is finer than sawdust and spreads farther. It is the main reason people end up calling for post-renovation cleaning in Greer, SC rather than finishing the job themselves.

Frequently asked questions

How soon after the contractor leaves should I clean the kitchen?

Give the dust a day to settle, then start from the top. Cleaning while installers are still sanding or painting just means doing it twice.

Can I use regular all-purpose cleaner on new countertops and cabinets?

Check the manufacturer's care instructions first. Some stone, laminate and painted finishes react badly to strong products, and a damp microfiber cloth is the safest starting point.
