Restocking checklist for Greer vacation rentals

Restocking checklist for rental property cleaning in Greer, SC: what to keep stocked, how cleaners flag low supplies, and how to avoid guest complaints.

Perfect Services Team
November 12, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Give your cleaner a written par list so low supplies get flagged at every turnover, not discovered by a guest.
  • Keep a back stock inside the property so a cleaner can refill on the spot instead of leaving a gap.
  • Restocking is part of our Airbnb and vacation rental cleaning, and the price comes after a free in-home estimate.

The one-star review that stings most is rarely about dirt. It is about the guest who got to the bathroom at 10 p.m. and found the last roll of toilet paper empty. If you are looking for rental property cleaning in Greer, SC, the restocking side of a turnover deserves as much thought as the scrubbing, because supplies are where a clean house still feels unfinished.

Our team at Perfect Services walks into Greer rentals every week, and the pattern is the same: hosts who give us a simple supply list get calm turnovers, and hosts who rely on memory get surprises. Here is how to set that list up.

Start with a par list, not a shopping list

A par list says how many of each item should be in the house when a guest arrives. A shopping list only says what you ran out of. The difference matters because your cleaner can only count against a number. If the par for hand soap is two full bottles and one is half empty, that gets flagged on the spot.

Write the par list once, tape a copy inside the supply closet, and share it with whoever handles your Airbnb and vacation rental cleaning in Greer. Our restocking checklist for host supplies is built into every visit, so the cleaner counts, refills from your stock and notes what is running low.

The checklist, room by room

Bathrooms

  • Toilet paper: one on the holder plus spares under the sink
  • Hand soap and a backup refill
  • Fresh towels per guest, plus a bath mat
  • Tissues, trash bags and a plunger that is clean and visible
  • Shampoo, conditioner and body wash if you offer them

Kitchen

  • Dish soap, sponges and a dishwasher pod or two
  • Paper towels, trash bags and dish towels
  • Coffee filters or pods if the listing promises coffee
  • Salt, pepper and cooking oil, only if you want to keep offering them

Bedrooms and living areas

  • A second set of linens for each bed
  • Extra blankets, especially from late fall through winter
  • Batteries for remotes and a spare light bulb or two
  • Laundry detergent if the unit has a washer

Where the stock lives and who counts it

The biggest restocking mistake is keeping supplies off site. A cleaner who finds an empty shelf at a same-day turnover cannot fix it. Put a lidded bin in a closet, label the shelves with the par numbers, and keep a small reserve above par. Then the visit goes in a set order: kitchen reset, bathrooms, beds with fresh linens you provide, floors, trash, and last the supply count.

That last step is why the count happens after the cleaning. Towels get used during a clean and soap gets wiped up, so counting at the end reflects what the next guest will actually find. Anything under par goes in the report we send with photos, together with any damage or missing items. Missing pieces such as a mug set or a remote often show up in this way first.

Small things guests notice

Guests rarely comment on a full shelf. They do notice a half-used bottle that has clearly been there a week, so we suggest swapping partially used items rather than topping them off. They also notice a lingering smell from a trash can, so the liner and the bin lid get wiped, not just emptied. In humid Upstate summers a closed rental can smell stale within days, so a quick air-out between stays helps more than any spray.

Staging counts too. Our team matches the space to your listing photos, so the throw pillows, the towel fold and the remote on the coffee table look the way guests expect. Frequently asked questions

What should a vacation rental restocking checklist include?

Bathroom paper goods, soap, towels, kitchen basics like dish soap and trash bags, spare linens, and small items like batteries and light bulbs. The list should show a par number for each so a cleaner can count against it.

Does Perfect Services restock supplies during a turnover?

Our visits include a restocking checklist for host supplies. The cleaner refills from the stock you keep at the property and reports what is running low, with photos of any damage or missing items.

Who buys the supplies?

The host supplies them and keeps a reserve on site. We flag shortages in our report so you can reorder before the next guest arrives.
