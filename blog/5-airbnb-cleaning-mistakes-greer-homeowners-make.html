5 Airbnb cleaning mistakes Greer hosts make (and the fix)

Airbnb cleaning mistakes Greer hosts make: tight turnovers, vague checklists, missing damage reports and more. Get a free in-home estimate for your rental.

Key takeaways

  • Most turnover problems come from timing and communication, not from the cleaning itself.
  • A written checklist, a damage report and a restock list protect your reviews and your property.

When a Greer host tells us a cleaner "did not work out," the story is rarely about scrubbing. It is a one-hour gap between checkout and check-in, or a linen closet nobody explained, or a missing towel found by the next guest. Here are five mistakes we hear about again and again from short-term rental owners, and what to do about each.

Mistake 1: Booking the cleaning for the exact minute of checkout

Checkout time on your listing is when guests are supposed to be gone, not when they are gone. A late guest, a family packing the car, someone who slept in. If your cleaner arrives at the exact checkout minute, the visit starts late and the next arrival crowds the end of it.

What works better is a buffer. A same-day turnover needs tighter scheduling, and that is the one factor that changes the plan most. Give the team the real window, including how early the next guest can show up, and ask them to tell you if it looks tight. It is better to hear that on Monday than at 3 p.m. on Saturday.

Mistake 2: Handing over the key and no checklist

A cleaner can see a dirty bathroom. They cannot see that you want the extra blanket folded on the bed, the thermostat set to 70, or the welcome book left open on the kitchen counter. Those details are how a rental looks like its listing photos, and without a written note they get lost.

A one-page checklist for the house solves it. Keep it short and put it where it will be found.

  • How to enter, where the key or lockbox is and any door codes
  • Where clean linens and spare supplies are stored
  • How you want beds, pillows and throws arranged
  • What the thermostat, lights and blinds should be set to
  • Anything guests are not allowed to move or touch

Our team also stages to match your listing photos, so send the photos you want matched. See how the process runs on our page for Airbnb cleaning in Greer.

Mistake 3: Letting one person do cleaning, laundry and inspection alone

Many hosts start by cleaning the rental themselves between guests, or by asking a friend. It works until a weekend where three bookings stack up. Then the laundry piles up, the inspection is rushed, and the mattress protector with a stain goes back on the bed.

Splitting the work helps. Some hosts have the cleaning team handle the whole turnover, with linens provided by you. Others handle laundry off site and ask the team to remake the beds. Either way, one person should own the final walkthrough. A turnover includes a damage and missing-item report with photos, which means you hear about a chipped glass or a missing remote while the guest is still on the booking, not weeks later.

Mistake 4: Ignoring the slow-build spots

A turnover is a reset. Over a season the places that matter to guests begin to wear: the shower door track, the grout line, the stovetop edge, the top of the refrigerator. Guests with a keen eye find them, and the review says "kitchen felt a little greasy."

Plan a deeper clean on a calendar, not when the review lands. A quarterly pass is a common rhythm for rentals that stay booked, and a slow month is a good time to do it. It also helps to look at how hosts in Simpsonville handle their turnover checklist, since the same slow spots show up there.

Mistake 5: Treating restocking as an afterthought

Running out of toilet paper at midnight is the kind of thing guests remember. The rental should start every stay with the basics in place: paper goods, soap, trash bags, coffee supplies if you provide them. The mistake is assuming someone will notice when they run low.

A restocking checklist for host supplies makes it a routine. The team notes what is low, you decide what to buy, and nothing is left to chance. If you want a quick comparison, here is how a good routine looks next to a risky one.
