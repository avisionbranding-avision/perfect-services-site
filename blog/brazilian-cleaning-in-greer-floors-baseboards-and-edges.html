Brazilian cleaning in Greer: floors, baseboards and edges

Brazilian cleaning in Greer, floors, baseboards and edges: why floors are hand-washed along the walls and how hardwood, tile and grout are cared for.

  • Brazilian-style floor care means hand-washing along the walls and baseboards, not just running a mop through the middle.

Where do you actually see a house cleaning mistake? On the floor, in the line where the floor meets the wall. That one inch of baseboard and edge is where fast cleaning gives itself away, and it is where Brazilian house cleaning in Greer, SC puts its time. Below are the questions we hear most from homeowners about floors, answered the way our team does the work.

What does "hand-washed along the walls" mean?

It means a cleaner on her knees with a cloth, washing the edge of the floor where it meets the baseboard. A mop head is round and wide, so it skips the last inch, the corner and the space behind a door. Over a few visits that inch turns into a gray border you can see from across the room. Our checklist lists it directly: floors hand-washed along the walls, and corners, edges and baseboards cleaned by hand.

The open middle of the floor is the easy part. We do it too, with a vacuum first and a wash after. But the edge is what separates a clean that looks right from a clean that looks right for a day.

How are baseboards cleaned?

Baseboards collect two kinds of dirt: dust settles on top, and scuffs and splashes mark the face. Wiping only the top leaves the face dull. We do both, by hand, along the full length of each wall, and finish at the corner and the door casing where the baseboard ends. Door frames, switches and handles are detailed in the same pass, since the cloth is already there.

It is the sort of work that does not look like much until you do it on a three-bedroom house with long halls. It adds up to a big part of the visit, and it is why a detail-first clean takes longer than a quick one. We explain how that works in the Brazilian house cleaning service overview.

Does it matter what kind of floor I have?

A great deal. The same wash that is right for tile can ruin hardwood. Greer homes usually have a mix, sometimes all in one level.

What gets missed under and behind things?

Furniture and small items are moved and cleaned under. That covers the floor beneath chairs, small tables, plant stands and the bottom of the couch. Behind the toilet and around its base, which gets the same hand treatment, is covered in our post on Brazilian cleaning in Greer bathrooms.

  • The strip of floor behind doors that open flat against the wall.
  • The floor inside closets and under low shelves.
  • Stair edges and the corner where each tread meets the wall.
  • Thresholds between rooms and the track of sliding doors.
  • The kitchen floor along the toe kick of the cabinets.

Does red clay change anything?

Upstate red clay does. It is fine, it stains, and it travels. In Greer it often shows at the entry and along the hall nearest the door, and it can turn a mop bucket orange fast. We change the water more than you would expect and work from the cleanest area to the dirtiest, so clay is not spread to rooms that were fine. In spring, pollen adds to it, which is one more reason edges and entries get the extra minutes.

Frequently asked questions

Why are floors hand-washed along the walls?

A mop skips the last inch next to the baseboard. Washing that edge by hand removes the gray border that builds up over time.

Is it safe for hardwood floors?

Yes. We vacuum first, use a damp cloth rather than a wet mop, and dry the floor right away. Tell us at the estimate if your hardwood has a special finish.

Do you scrub grout during regular visits?

Bathrooms are scrubbed including grout, and kitchen and entry grout can be detailed too. How much depends on the level of detail you choose at the estimate.
