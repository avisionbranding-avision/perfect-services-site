The turnover, step by step

Do these in order. The sequence matters because it keeps you from cleaning something twice.

  1. Walk the property first. Before touching anything, go room by room and look for damage, left-behind items and anything out of place. Photograph what you find.
  2. Strip the beds and start laundry. Linens take the longest, so they go in the machine early. Bed linens are host-provided, so we work with what you have stocked.
  3. Open the windows and do the trash. Empty every bin, including bathroom and bedroom cans, and reset the liners.
  4. Kitchen reset. Dishes, counters, sink, the stovetop, the microwave and the outside of the appliances. Check the fridge for forgotten food.
  5. Bathrooms. Toilet, shower or tub, vanity, mirror, and the floor along the wall. Replace towels and restock paper and soap.
  6. Dust and wipe. Tables, nightstands, remotes, switches and door handles.
  7. Floors last. Vacuum and mop on the way out so footprints from the work do not undo it.
  8. Make the beds and stage. Pillows, throws and decor go back to match your listing photos.
  9. Final walk-through. One more pass with the checklist, then lock up.

Staging so the house matches the listing

A clean house can still disappoint if it does not look like the photos. Staging is the last ten minutes of a turnover, and it is where a cleaner who knows your listing earns their place. Throw pillows go back in the same arrangement, the extra blanket is folded where it was in the photo, the remote sits on the coffee table, and the kitchen counters are clear except for what a guest should see. Send us your listing photos at the estimate and we will stage to match, so guests walk into what they booked.

Smell matters too. A house that has been closed up between guests can feel stale, so cracking a window during the clean and leaving the space airing while the crew works helps more than a strong air freshener.

The spots hosts forget

Guests notice the same few places again and again, and they leave comments about them. These are the ones to add to your list.

  • The track of a sliding door and the glass beside it.
  • Inside the microwave and the drip area under the coffee maker.
  • Hair on bathroom floors near the baseboard.
  • Remote controls, which are touched by everyone and cleaned by nobody.
  • Under the bed, where forgotten items collect.
  • The top of door frames and ceiling fan blades.

The Brazilian detail-first method at Perfect Services is built for exactly this: wiping by hand and finishing against a room checklist, not just a once-over.

What to do when something is missing or damaged

Part of our turnover service is a damage and missing-item report with photos. If a lamp shade is cracked or a set of towels has gone missing, you get the pictures before the next guest checks in. That is especially helpful for owners who live out of town, because the report is your eyes on the property. It also gives you the documentation if you need to raise something with a guest or the platform.

Restocking is a separate line on the checklist: coffee, paper goods, soap and anything else you supply. Tell us your par levels and we will flag what is low rather than guessing.

Same-day turns and tight windows

Back-to-back bookings need a plan. A same-day turnover leaves very little time, so the schedule has to be tighter and the laundry decision has to be made in advance: on site, off site, or a second set of linens waiting on the shelf. Many hosts keep a spare set of linens for each bed so the cleaning crew can swap instead of waiting on a dryer.
