High-touch areas every Greer business should clean daily

Which surfaces customers and staff touch most in a Greer, SC business, how to clean them daily and what to leave to a commercial cleaning crew.

Perfect Services Team
November 11, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Door handles, switches, keypads and shared devices are touched far more than desks and floors.
  • A five-minute daily wipe-down by staff handles the surface layer; a crew handles restrooms, floors and detail.
  • Entry doors, restrooms and the break room are the first places to look.

Count how many hands touch your front door handle on a normal Tuesday. Then count the card reader, the pen on the counter, the restroom door and the coffee pot. Those few objects collect more contact than every desk in the building, and they are the first things worth cleaning every day. If you run a business in Greer, this is the short list of what to wipe, in what order and who should do it.

This is not a deep-clean schedule. It is the daily layer that sits between your regular commercial cleaning visits, and it works for offices, salons, clinics, showrooms and shops alike.

The surfaces that get touched most

High-touch areas are the points many people handle in a day, often without thinking. In most Greer businesses they fall into the same groups:

  • Entry: exterior and interior door handles, push plates, the glass around the handle, and the mat inside the door.
  • Counters and reception: the counter edge, the sign-in pen or tablet, the card reader and keypad, the phone handset.
  • Switches and controls: light switches, thermostat, alarm keypad, elevator or lock buttons where they exist.
  • Shared equipment: printer panel, copier lid, shared keyboard and mouse, the cabinet pull where supplies are kept.
  • Break room: fridge handle, microwave buttons, coffee machine, faucet handles, the table.
  • Restrooms: door handle, faucet, flush handle, soap and towel dispenser, stall latch.

A daily routine that takes about ten minutes

You do not need a special product for each item. The order matters more than the bottle. Work from clean to dirty, finish with the restroom, and change the cloth between areas.

  1. Start at the front door. Wipe both handles and the glass at hand height.
  2. Do the reception counter, the pen, the card reader and the phone. Use a lightly dampened cloth on electronics, never a sprayed surface.
  3. Walk the building and wipe switches, the thermostat and door handles between rooms.
  4. Wipe printer panels and shared keyboards. Keep these cloths separate from the restroom cloths.
  5. Do the break room table, the fridge handle, the microwave keypad and the faucet.
  6. Finish with the restroom handles, faucet, dispenser and flush lever, then restock.

Always follow the label on whatever product you use, including the contact time before wiping dry. If a product is meant to disinfect, it needs to stay wet on the surface for the time stated. Rushing the wipe is the most common reason a surface is cleaned but not disinfected.

What staff should do and what a crew should do

A daily wipe-down by staff is useful, but it has limits. Staff can handle the quick surface layer. They usually do not have the time to scrub restroom grout, clean floors along the edges, wipe baseboards, empty every trash can or detail the glass. Those tasks are what commercial cleaning in Greer, SC is for. Making the routine stick

Tape a short list inside a supply cabinet and keep the cloths and product in one place. Rotate who does it by the week so it does not become one person's job. And mention at the estimate which of these items you already handle, so the crew can focus on the work staff cannot reach.
