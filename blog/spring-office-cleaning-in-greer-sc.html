What spring does to an office

Winter dirt is mostly salt and dry dust. Spring dirt is wetter and finer. Pollen drifts in through any open door, settles on window sills and desks, and gets pulled into the HVAC when the air conditioning starts working again. At the same time, rain moves clay and grass onto floors, and the entry area takes the brunt. A cleaning plan that was enough in January often leaves a film by May.

The seasonal checklist

  • Entry glass cleaned inside and out, including the door frame and handles
  • Entry mats shaken, vacuumed or replaced, and the floor beneath them washed
  • Window sills, blinds and tracks wiped of pollen
  • Air vents and return grilles dusted where they are reachable
  • Carpet traffic lanes vacuumed more slowly, in two directions
  • Restroom grout, fixtures and floors detailed
  • Break room fridge, microwave and sink given a deeper wipe than the weekly routine
  • Light switches, keypads and railings disinfected

This list goes beyond the usual weekly tasks, which are desks, counters, restrooms, trash and floors. We usually schedule the seasonal items as a one-time deep visit, then return to the normal rhythm.

Small habits that help between visits

The cleaning team can only do so much if the office works against it. A few small habits reduce what pollen and clay bring in, and they cost nothing.

  • Keep exterior doors closed when you can, and prop them open only for deliveries.
  • Use two mats at the main entrance, one outside and one inside, so shoes are wiped twice.
  • Ask staff to leave desks clear at the end of the day so surfaces can be wiped, not just worked around.
  • Replace HVAC filters before pollen season, because clogged filters push fine dust back into the room.
  • Report spills and stains the same day, since fresh marks come out of carpet far more easily.

Offices with clients, such as clinics, salons and studios, may also want an extra focus on waiting areas and restrooms during the spring peak, when visitors arrive with the outside on their shoes. We can add a short mid-week visit for those areas without changing the main schedule.

  4. Share any quiet-hours rules or building access details at the estimate.
