The 10 minutes that decide how your weekend goes

You get home from work, the stovetop has a week of splatter, the bathroom mirror is cloudy and the vacuum is in the closet behind a pile of boxes. A lot of renters in Greer apartments and condos live in that loop. Regular apartment cleaning in Greer breaks it, and so does a short plan. Here is the plan we would give a friend.

A weekly order that fits a small space

Apartments are small, so everything is close, and the mess from one room spreads to the next. We work this way in the unit, and you can copy the order on your own:

  1. Open windows or switch on the vent, then clear the surfaces that hold clutter.
  2. Dust from high to low: shelves, the top of the fridge, door frames, then baseboards.
  3. Clean the bathroom: toilet, shower or tub, mirror and vanity.
  4. Do the kitchen: counters, sink, stovetop and appliance fronts.
  5. Vacuum and mop last, finishing at the front door so you do not walk over wet floor.

The order matters because dust falls and floors always come last. It is the same sequence we follow in a unit, plus the extras renters tend to skip.

The spots that cost renters in a move-out inspection

  • Baseboards and door frames, where dust and scuffs collect
  • The stovetop and the oven door glass
  • Light switches and cabinet handles
  • The track of the balcony or patio door, and its glass
  • Shower glass and the base of the faucet
  • The inside edge of the trash bin and the bottom of the sink

Whether a deposit comes back in full is between you, your lease and your property manager, and we cannot promise a result. What we can do is clean every item on this list. If you are leaving, ask about move-out cleaning, which is built for an empty unit.

Different layouts, different problems

Greer has a mix of older apartment buildings and newer complexes near GSP Airport and the BMW plant, and we see different issues in each. Older units tend to have more painted trim, so baseboards and door frames show scuffs and dust quickly. Newer units often have open kitchens with a large stovetop and glossy cabinets that show every fingerprint. The team adjusts the order to fit what the unit shows, and tells you what we saw during the estimate.

Living with pets and a small floor plan

A small apartment and a dog share the same floor. Hair collects along the baseboards, behind the couch and in the corner by the door, and a vacuum alone moves it around. We vacuum slowly, wipe the baseboards by hand and finish with a damp mop on hard floors. If you have a cat, tell us where the litter box lives; we clean around it and leave it alone unless you ask.

For the bathroom, most renters have one, and it takes the most abuse. The shower walls, the base of the faucet and the track of the shower door are where hard water and soap film hide. Our team spends more time there than anywhere else, and we leave the mirror clear.

Setting a rhythm that fits your week

Most renters pick one of two rhythms: a visit every other week, or a monthly visit with a quick tidy in between. Recurring visits cost less per visit than one-off ones, and the home stays closer to the starting point, so each visit takes less effort. A good rhythm also means the same people learn your place, and you stop explaining where the vacuum lives. Choose the schedule based on how much the unit gets used, not on what you wish you had time for.
