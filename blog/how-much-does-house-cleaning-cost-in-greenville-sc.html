The short answer to "how much does house cleaning cost in Greenville, SC" is that it depends on your home, and anyone who gives you a firm number over the phone without asking about it is guessing. We would rather tell you how the price is built, so you can judge any quote you receive, ours included.

We do not list dollar amounts on this page on purpose. The exact price comes after a free in-home estimate. What we can do is show you everything that goes into it.

The five things that set the price

Cleaning is priced by time and team size. The question is how many hours of work your home needs to reach the standard you want. These are the factors we weigh at the estimate:

  1. Home size and layout. More rooms and more square feet take more time. A downtown condo near Main Street and a larger house off I-385 need different crews.
  2. Bedrooms and bathrooms. Bathrooms are the most time-intensive rooms, so a house with four bathrooms costs more than one with two, even at the same square footage.
  3. Frequency. Weekly and biweekly visits cost less per visit than monthly, because the home stays near the maintenance standard.
  4. Pets and kids. Extra hair and traffic add a little time on every visit.
  5. The first visit. The first clean is usually deeper, to bring the home up to the level where regular visits can maintain it.

Why Greenville homes vary so much

Greenville County covers the 29601, 29605, 29607, 29609 and 29615 ZIP codes and almost every style of home. Condos and townhomes near Falls Park are compact, with fewer floors to mop but often more glass and tighter bathrooms. Established neighborhoods across the city have older floors, more built-in shelving and sometimes more rooms than the family uses. Two houses on the same street can need two very different visits, which is why a flat citywide rate rarely fits.

What you get for the price

A regular visit with our house cleaning in Greenville follows a fixed list, so you always know what the time is buying:

  • Kitchen counters, sink and stovetop cleaned and sanitized, appliance fronts and handles wiped.
  • Bathrooms: toilet, shower, tub, mirrors and chrome.
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped by hand.
  • Surfaces, shelves and furniture dusted.
  • Beds made and trash emptied.
  • Baseboards and door frames touched up on a rotation.
  • Light switches and high-touch spots wiped.

Ask any company for its checklist. If the quote is low but the list is vague, the missing hours usually show up as skipped corners.

How long a visit takes

A regular maintenance visit in an average family home often takes a team a few hours, depending on size and condition. The first visit takes longer. We will give a time window once we have walked through the home, and we tell you if anything looks like it needs more than the booked time.

What changes the number after the first visit

Once your home is at the maintenance standard, the price tends to settle. A few things can still shift it later. If you add a pet, the visit gets a little longer. If you ask for extras such as the inside of the fridge or windows, we price those separately so the regular visit stays simple. If the family grows, or a bathroom is added in a remodel, we revisit the scope. We would rather adjust the plan openly than quietly rush your home to hold a price.

Many Greenville clients choose biweekly visits because it balances cost and how the house feels between visits. Weekly suits busy households with kids and pets, while monthly works if you mostly want a reset. We explain each trade-off at the estimate, and you can switch frequency as life changes.

It also helps to know what we cannot promise. We will not tell you a price before seeing the home, and we will not say a cleaning will fix damage such as worn grout or stained carpet. We will tell you what a clean can and cannot do.
