Getting your Greer home ready for overnight guests

Last minute house cleaning in Greer for overnight guests: a 48-hour plan for the guest room, bathroom, kitchen and entry, plus when to call for help.

Perfect Services Team
November 10, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Guests judge the bed, the bathroom and the kitchen first; spend your hours there.
  • Work in a 48-hour order so the fastest wins happen first and dusty jobs are done before floors.
  • Ask early for holiday weeks, since one-time cleaning dates fill up; estimates are free.

It is Tuesday night, your sister texts that she is driving up Thursday and bringing the kids, and the guest room has been a storage closet since March. If that sounds familiar, you are not behind, you are normal. Guests do not inspect your whole house. They notice a short list of things, and this plan puts hours against that list.

What guests actually notice

From years of cleaning homes before holidays and visits, we see the same pattern. Overnight guests check four places: the bed they will sleep in, the bathroom they will use, the kitchen where they will find coffee and the entry where they arrive. The living room matters less than you think, and the garage matters not at all.

That gives you a ranked list, and it changes how you spend limited time. A quiet guest room with fresh sheets beats a perfect hallway.

Hour by hour: the 48-hour plan

  1. Hours 1 to 3: the guest room. Be ruthless about what stays. Clear the boxes, strip and wash everything on the bed, dust the nightstand and the lamp, and vacuum under the bed. Open the closet and make space for a bag and a few hangers.
  2. Hours 4 to 6: the guest bathroom. Shower, toilet, vanity, mirror, then floor. Put out two towel sets, a fresh bar of soap and a spare roll of toilet paper where they can see it.
  3. Next morning: the kitchen. Empty the sink and the dishwasher, wipe the counters, clean the microwave and wipe down the fridge handle. Clear a shelf in the fridge for their food.
  4. Afternoon: common areas. Dust, straighten, vacuum the main walking paths and clear the clutter on the sofa.
  5. Last evening: floors and the entry. Mop hard floors, shake the mat and set a place for coats and shoes.

A quick note on timing: if your guests land on Thursday, treat Wednesday night as your real deadline, and leave Thursday morning for a last walk through with a cup of coffee and a lint roller. Doing floors last matters because every earlier job drops dust or hair. It is the order our team uses in a full house, and it works in a small one too.

What to hand off if time runs short

There are jobs that take more time than they appear to. The inside of the oven, the fridge shelves, baseboards through the whole house, and the track of a sliding door all look small and then eat an evening. They are also the jobs where a professional team is fastest. When we do a one-time house cleaning in Greer, the kitchen is fully degreased, bathrooms are disinfected, bedrooms and living areas are dusted, floors are vacuumed and mopped, and baseboards, door frames, mirrors and glass are done.

Kids, pets and the shared spaces

If guests include children, look at the house from knee height. Wipe low shelves, the bottom of the pantry door, the baseboards by the sofa and the lower rungs of chairs. Move anything breakable up and anything unsafe out. If a dog or cat shares the house, keep the guest room door shut once it is done, so hair does not come back before the visitors do.

If you have an air mattress for the overflow, give it the same attention as a real bed: fresh sheets, a clean floor under it and a lamp within reach.

The goal is not a magazine home. It is a house where a tired visitor can set a bag down, find a clean towel and sleep well, and that is a much smaller target than most hosts fear.

Small touches that make a guest room feel ready

  • A clear surface for a phone, glasses and a water glass
  • A lamp that works and a bulb you checked
  • An empty drawer or two, not full of your things
  • Window blinds wiped and a curtain that closes properly
  • A trash can with a fresh liner
  • A spare blanket folded where it can be found
