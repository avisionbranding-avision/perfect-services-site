A turnover in the order the team works

Hosts often ask how long this takes. It depends on the size of the home, the number of bathrooms and what the last guests left behind, which is why we look at the property before giving any time or price. What stays constant is the order.

When we reset a rental, we move in a set sequence so nothing gets cleaned twice and nothing is missed.

  1. Walk the house first. Look for damage, missing items and anything left behind. Photos go in the report before a single surface is touched.
  2. Start the laundry or strip the beds. Linens are the slowest step, so they begin first.
  3. Kitchen reset. Dishes, counters, appliances, microwave and fridge shelf.
  4. Bathrooms. Cleaned and disinfected, mirrors last so nothing drips on them.
  5. Beds made and staged to match your listing photos.
  6. Floors last, vacuumed and mopped from the farthest room back to the door.
  7. Final pass, trash out, bins reset, supplies checked.

Floors come last because everything else drops crumbs and hair. It is a small rule that saves a second sweep.

Same-day turnovers need tighter scheduling, and that is a real cost factor. They are doable, but only when the cleaner knows the home, the linens are accounted for and you have set a rule for late checkouts. A simple one is that the next guest's check-in time never moves earlier than the cleaner's finish time.

What stops a bad check-in

Look at the pattern in complaints a host might see: a hair in the shower, a smell in the fridge, a missing roll of toilet paper, a dusty ceiling fan above the bed. They are all small, and they all come from skipped checks rather than skipped cleaning. A host near the GSP Airport side of town gets guests who arrive tired and judge a home in the first minute. Make the first minute easy, and the rest of the stay tends to take care of itself.

  • Light on and temperature set before arrival
  • Fresh towels folded, extras in the same spot every time
  • Kitchen counters empty, trash can fresh-lined
  • Bathroom floor and shower base checked for hair
  • Restocking checklist done: soap, paper goods, coffee supplies
  • Photo of the finished living room sent to you

What to do when checkout runs late

It will happen. A guest asks for an extra hour and you say yes because the review matters. Decide ahead of time how that request reaches your cleaner and who says yes. We suggest one rule: the host approves late checkouts only after confirming the cleaner can still finish before the next arrival. If not, offer a luggage hold or a nearby coffee stop instead. A guest who waits an hour and walks into a fresh home writes a better review than one who walks into a half-cleaned kitchen.

Also keep a small buffer in your calendar after a long stay, especially in the busy fall and holiday weeks. A week-long booking leaves more dishes, more laundry and more floor to cover than a weekend, and the cleaning window should grow with it.

Build a backup before you need one

Ask your cleaner what happens if they are sick, and ask what you should do if a guest leaves a mess no one expected. Having the answer in writing is worth more than any discount.
