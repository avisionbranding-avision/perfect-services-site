First-time house cleaning in Greer, SC: what to expect

Booking house cleaning in Greer, SC for the first time? Here are straight answers on what to hide, what to tell the team and what the first visit covers.

Perfect Services Team
November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • The first visit is usually deeper than the ones that follow, so the home reaches a standard the team can maintain.
  • You do not need to pre-clean; put away valuables and tell the team about pets, fragile items and priorities.
  • Prices come only after a free in-home estimate, and the estimate visit is also your chance to ask questions.

The most common thing we hear from someone booking house cleaning in Greer, SC for the first time is some version of "I should probably tidy up before you come." You do not have to. Cleaners are not there to judge the house. They are there to make it better than they found it. Below are the questions first-time clients ask us most, answered the way the team would answer them at the door.

Do I have to clean before the cleaners arrive?

No, but a few minutes of picking up helps. Clear the surfaces you want wiped, such as kitchen counters and bathroom sinks, and put away anything you would not want moved: paperwork, jewelry, medication, small valuables. Toys on the floor, clothes on chairs and dishes by the sink slow the work, because someone has to decide where each item goes.

  • Clear counters and sink areas of loose items.
  • Put away anything fragile or valuable.
  • Move pet bowls and toys out of the walking path.
  • Leave a short note about rooms or spots you care about most.

One more thing worth doing: decide ahead of time how you want to handle pets. If a dog is nervous around visitors, a closed bedroom door and a bowl of water is enough. The team would rather work around a calm pet than a stressed one, and cats that hide under beds are common enough that nobody is surprised by them.

The visit is a quick walk-through to see the layout, count bedrooms and bathrooms, check floor types, and ask about pets, kids and priorities. If you have a stained shower or a rug you worry about, this is the time to point to it. After the walk-through you get an exact price, with no commitment to book.

What does the first visit actually include?

A regular visit from our house cleaning service covers the kitchen counters, sink and stovetop, appliance fronts and handles, bathrooms (toilet, shower, tub, mirrors and chrome), dusting of shelves and furniture, vacuuming, hand-mopped floors, beds made and trash emptied. Baseboards and door frames are touched up on a rotation, and light switches and other high-touch spots get wiped.

The first clean is usually a little deeper. Most homes have a few areas that have not been touched in a while, and the first visit brings everything up to a level the team can then maintain. That does not mean it replaces a full deep clean. If the house needs that, we say so during the estimate rather than after.

A lot of first-time clients are surprised by the small things the team notices. Dust sits in the tracks of a sliding door, cabinet handles carry a film, and the space behind a trash can collects crumbs. None of that is a criticism of how you live. It is simply what builds up between the weekly wipe-downs, and it is the part of a first visit that people tend to remember most.

What do the cleaners do first, and how long does it take?

Our team works top to bottom and from the back of the house toward the front door. Dust comes down before floors are done, so nothing has to be cleaned twice. Bathrooms usually start early because the shower needs time to work. The kitchen comes next, then bedrooms and living areas, and floors last.

Time depends on size and condition. A smaller home might take a couple of hours, while a larger one with several bathrooms takes much longer, which is why we give the estimate after seeing the space. The first visit nearly always takes longer than later ones.

How much does house cleaning cost in Greer?

People ask this before anything else, and the honest answer is that it depends on the house. These are the factors that move the price:

  • Home size and layout.
  • Number of bedrooms and bathrooms, since bathrooms are the most time-intensive rooms.
  • Frequency: weekly and biweekly visits cost less per visit than monthly.
  • Pets and kids, which add some hair and traffic.
  • Whether it is a first visit, which is usually deeper.

Tell us. Walk through the house after the first visit and point out anything that missed the mark, whether it is a streaky mirror or a corner behind the door. This is normal. The first visit is also how the team learns your home, and the second is usually where things settle into a rhythm.
