The honest answer to "how much does a cleaner charge to clean a house?" is that it depends on the house, and anyone who quotes a flat number without seeing it is guessing. This guide explains what moves the price of house cleaning in Saluda, NC, so you can judge any quote, ours included. At Perfect Services, you get the price after a free in-home estimate, and that is a deliberate choice.

Saluda is a small mountain town in Polk County (ZIP 28773) with cabins, vacation homes and historic houses, reached from I-26 and US-176. It sits about 22 miles north of our base in Greer. A mix like that, where a two-bedroom cabin and a large old house can be on the same street, is exactly why a standard price does not work.

The five things that set the price

Cleaners price by the time a job takes. Everything below changes that time.

FactorWhy it matters
Home size and layoutMore rooms and square feet take more time
Bedrooms and bathroomsBathrooms are the most time-intensive rooms
FrequencyWeekly and biweekly visits cost less per visit than monthly
Pets and kidsExtra hair and traffic add a little time
First visitThe first clean is usually deeper, to reach the maintenance standard

Layout deserves a closer look. An older house with many small rooms, built-in shelves, wood trim and old windows takes longer than an open modern plan of the same square footage. A cabin that is mostly one big room with a loft can be quick. Two homes of the same size can need quite different visits.

Why we price after a free in-home estimate

A walkthrough takes about as long as a cup of coffee, and it answers what no online form can. We look at how many bathrooms there are and what shape they are in, whether the floors are hardwood, tile or carpet, how much clutter has to be moved, and what the household looks like day to day. Then we give you one exact price. It is not a range, and there is no commitment to book.

We do not price by photo or promise a number over the phone. Photos hide the grout, the baseboards and the oven door, and a price that changes on arrival is worse than a price that takes one short visit to confirm. Our house cleaning and maid service page lists exactly what is included so you can compare it with other quotes.

Cabins, vacation homes and full-time homes

Saluda has all three, and each is priced differently. A full-time family home usually wants a regular schedule, so the first deeper visit is followed by lighter, faster ones. A vacation home or cabin that is used a few weekends a month may only need a clean before and after each stay, and that is closer to a one-time visit. A larger historic house may take more time on trim, windows and floors than its square footage suggests.

Tell us how the house is used and how often. That one detail changes the plan more than almost anything else, and it is why the walkthrough comes before the price.

What your money covers

When you compare estimates, compare the checklist, not just the figure. A cleaning that skips baseboards or leaves the stovetop for you is not the same product. Here is what a regular visit with us includes.

  • Kitchen counters, sink and stovetop cleaned and sanitized.
  • Appliance fronts and handles wiped.
  • Bathrooms: toilet, shower, tub, mirrors and chrome.
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped by hand.
  • Surfaces, shelves and furniture dusted.
  • Beds made and trash emptied.
  • Baseboards and door frames touched up on a rotation.
  • Light switches and high-touch spots wiped.

The Brazilian detail-first method means the team looks at the places that get missed: door frames, cabinet handles, the tracks of sliding doors. If you want a thorough first visit, ask about deep cleaning, which adds grout, vents and window tracks to the same checklist.

How to lower the cost per visit

You cannot change the size of your house, but a few choices help.

  1. Pick a regular schedule. Weekly and biweekly visits cost less per visit because the house never gets far from clean.
  2. Tidy the surfaces before we arrive. Clear counters and floors clean faster than rooms where everything has to be moved.
  3. Tell us about pets in advance. We plan the time instead of finding out halfway through.
  4. Be upfront about condition. If it has been months since a clean, the first visit may be deeper, and that is fine.
  5. Ask for the same day each week. A set schedule makes the plan simple.

For vacation homes and cabins that sit empty, ask for a clean before arrival. Dust and closed-up air show up after a few weeks, even with nobody living there.
