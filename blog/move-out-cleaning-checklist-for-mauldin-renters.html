Seven days out: the jobs that take the longest

Start with anything that needs soak time or that you can only do while the rooms are still empty enough to reach.

  • Check your lease for the cleaning clause and any move-out instructions from the landlord or property manager
  • Photograph each room before you start, so you have a record of the condition you left
  • Empty the refrigerator and freezer, then defrost if there is ice buildup
  • Spray the oven interior with degreaser and let it work, then repeat on the range hood filter
  • Pull the first load of belongings out of closets so the shelves and rods can be wiped
  • Patch nothing yet, but note nail holes and scuffs that look like damage instead of dirt

Oven grease is the first thing that turns a 20-minute job into a 90-minute one, which is why it goes at the top of the list. Closets are second, because that is where people stack boxes until the last day.

What the landlord checks and where the dirt hides

A walkthrough follows a pattern. The inspector opens things. The table below pairs the usual targets with the spot people forget.

What gets opened or checkedThe spot most people miss
Oven and stovetopGlass in the oven door and the drip area under the burners
RefrigeratorCrisper drawer rails and the rubber door gasket
Kitchen cabinetsShelf corners and the tops of the uppers
BathroomsToilet base, shower grout and vanity drawer interiors
ClosetsThe floor behind the door and the top shelf edge
Doors and trimDoor frames, handles and light switch plates
WindowsTracks and sills, where red clay dust settles
FloorsThe strip along the walls under where furniture stood

Upstate red clay is the reason window tracks and entry floors look worse than the rest of a house. It sticks to shoes, blows in through open windows in dry spells and turns to a gray paste when you wipe it wet. Vacuum or brush the grit out of the track first, then wipe.

Two days out: bathrooms, then floors

With the furniture mostly gone, bathrooms are easier. Scrub the toilet base and the area behind the seat hinges, work the grout with a stiff brush, and wipe the vanity inside and out. Hard water spots on shower glass respond to a vinegar solution on most surfaces, but test a small corner first on natural stone.

Floors always go last in each room, from the far corner toward the door, so you never walk across a freshly mopped surface. If you only have time to be thorough in one place, make it the edge of the floor along the baseboards. That strip is where inspectors look, and it is where most DIY mopping stops short.

Move day: the final ten-minute pass

When the last box is out, walk the house once more with the lights on and a dry cloth. Check the light switches, the inside of the microwave, the bathroom mirror and the spot in the garage where the trash cans stood. Take dated photos again. Turn off the lights, lock up and keep your keys receipt. Those photos are your answer if a deposit dispute ever comes up.
