Spring deep cleaning checklist for Greer homes

Spring cleaning Greer SC checklist: the deep-clean jobs by room, in the order a pro team works, plus what to do yourself and what to hand off.

Key takeaways

  • Spring deep cleaning is about the jobs a weekly clean skips: baseboards, grout, vents, tracks and cabinet fronts.

A spring deep clean is the list of jobs your regular cleaning never reaches. It runs room by room, top to bottom, and it takes a team several hours rather than one afternoon of effort. Start at the top: ceilings, fans and vents

Before touching floors, deal with what sheds dust. Ceiling fan blades, light fixtures, air vents and the tops of door frames all hold a gray layer that falls the moment you vacuum. Wipe the blades with a damp cloth folded over the blade so dust stays in the cloth. Wash the vent covers or wipe them clean, and dust the top of tall bookcases and wall-hung frames.

The kitchen, in order

  • Clear counters and degrease cabinet fronts and handles, especially near the stove
  • Wipe the range hood and the backsplash
  • Clean appliance exteriors, including the fridge door seal and dishwasher front
  • Clean the sink, drain edge and faucet base
  • Add-ons: inside the oven, refrigerator or cabinets
  • Scrub grout lines along the floor and behind the sink
  • Mop last, after everything above has fallen

Cabinet handles are the unlisted job: grease and fingerprints build up there and the surfaces feel sticky before they look dirty.

Bathrooms: the slow work

Bathrooms take the longest in a deep clean because of grout and hard water. Scrub the grout lines in the shower and floor with a stiff brush, treat hard water spots on glass and chrome, and clean behind and around the toilet base where the floor meets the bowl. Wipe the exhaust fan cover, the door frame and the baseboard, which collect moisture and dust together. If you have a tub, check the caulk line for mildew; heavy mildew or cracked caulk may need repair, not cleaning.

Bedrooms, living areas and the details between

Move small furniture and vacuum under it. Wipe baseboards and door frames by hand along the whole room. Clean window sills and tracks, which hold dead insects and soil after winter. Wipe blinds slat by slat with a microfiber cloth, or use a blind duster. Rotate or flip mattresses if the manufacturer says to, and wash comforters and pillows. In the living room, vacuum between sofa cushions and wipe the TV stand and shelves.

After the deep clean

A deep clean works best as a reset. Once the house is at that level, a regular deep cleaning service once or twice a year, plus recurring house cleaning in between, keeps it there. The Brazilian detail-first method means every baseboard and cabinet pull gets touched by hand. Homes in neighboring areas can see the same service in Taylors.

A sensible schedule for the project

Spread it over two or three weekends if you are doing it yourself. Weekend one is decluttering and bedding. Weekend two is the kitchen and bathrooms. Weekend three is tracks, blinds and baseboards. Breaking it up helps because the slow jobs, grout and oven, are tiring, and rushing leaves streaks.

Red clay and pollen are the Upstate's spring extras. Wash entry mats, wipe the door frame and sweep the threshold, since clay dust carries inside on shoes and hardens into a film on floor edges. If you have a garage entry, clean that doorway too. A few minutes at the doors makes the rest of the house easier to keep clean afterward.

Finish with a walk through each room from the doorway, looking at it as a guest would. Check the places near the floor, because baseboards and corners tell you whether the clean reached the edges. Anything you spot gets a quick touch-up with a damp cloth.
