Problem 1: hair that comes back the next day

Hair does not stay where the vacuum finds it. It rides air currents to baseboards, under the couch, behind doors and into the corners of stairs. The fix is the order of work. Dust high first, including shelves, window sills and the tops of baseboards, then vacuum, so the hair that falls is picked up. Go over carpet traffic lanes in two directions; the second pass lifts what the first rolled flat.

On hard floors, vacuum before mopping. A wet mop on loose hair turns it into gray clumps that dry in the grout. On upholstery, a damp rubber glove pulled across the cushion gathers fur faster than most attachments.

Problem 2: dander and the dusty film

Dander is tiny and sticks to soft things: curtains, throw blankets, dog beds, the fabric headboard. Wash pet bedding and throws on a regular rhythm, and use a damp microfiber cloth to dust, not a dry one that sends it back into the air. For Greer's pollen-heavy spring, pet dander and pollen travel together on a coat that has been outside, so a quick wipe of paws and fur at the door helps. Problem 3: odors

Pets rarely smell on their own. The smell lives in places they lie, drool or have accidents. Check these in order:

  • The dog bed and its cover, and the blanket on the couch
  • The floor at the back door, where wet paws land
  • Baseboards and the lower edge of cabinets near the food bowls
  • The litter area, including the wall and floor around the box
  • The rug under the water bowl, and the mat itself
  • Leashes, towels and anything else stored by the back door

Wash what can be washed, wipe hard surfaces with a suitable cleaner and let the area dry fully. If an accident soaked into carpet or a rug, mention it at the estimate so the team can plan time for it, and tell us if a particular product bothers your animal.

What the team does in a pet household

A standard visit covers kitchen counters, sink and stovetop, appliance fronts, bathrooms, hand-mopped floors, dusting, beds made, trash emptied, baseboards and door frames on a rotation, and high-touch spots. In a home with pets, the order shifts a little. We vacuum more slowly, check under beds and behind furniture, and pay attention to the corner where the pet eats. Please tell us about your animals before the first visit: whether they are friendly, whether they should be in a room or crated, and any doors that must stay shut.

Making it easier between visits

Keep a lint roller and a small hand vacuum by the door. Put a washable mat where your dog comes in. Brush the pet outdoors if you can, and keep the food area on an easy-to-wipe surface. These small habits do more for pet odor than any spray.
