Apartment cleaning checklist for Chesnee renters

Apartment cleaning Chesnee SC: a weekly, monthly and seasonal checklist for renters, plus what a cleaning crew handles and how to get a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Split apartment cleaning into weekly, monthly and seasonal jobs so nothing piles up before the lease inspection.
  • Bathrooms and the kitchen take most of the time in a small unit; start there.
  • Prices come only after a free in-home estimate, since unit size, pets and access all change the work.

In a small apartment, you cannot hide a messy kitchen behind a door. The sink, the stovetop and the bathroom are all within ten steps of the couch, so they decide how clean the whole place feels. This checklist is written for renters who want to keep a Chesnee apartment in good shape without losing every Saturday to it, and it shows which jobs are worth handing to apartment cleaning in Chesnee, SC.

Chesnee is a rural foothills town in Spartanburg County, reached along US-221 and SC-11, and about 25 miles from our base in Greer. Many renters here also work in Spartanburg or Greer, so evenings are short. The plan below respects that.

The weekly list: 30 to 45 minutes

Do these once a week and the other lists stay small. Put on one playlist and work through the unit in a loop, from the far end toward the front door.

  • Wipe kitchen counters, the stovetop and the sink, then dry the faucet
  • Wipe appliance fronts, the microwave handle and the refrigerator door
  • Scrub the toilet, rinse the shower or tub and wipe the mirror and vanity
  • Take out the trash and replace the liner
  • Dust flat surfaces, the TV stand and the window sills
  • Vacuum the carpet and sweep, then mop hard floors
  • Change bed sheets and wipe light switches and door handles

Work in a loop rather than room by room. Start at the far end of the unit, clean toward the front door, and do floors last. In a one-bedroom this keeps you from walking over a wet floor twice. If you have a pet, add a lint roller or a damp rubber glove pass on the couch and bedding, since hair settles into fabric and carpet faster than any weekly vacuum can pick it up.

Keep supplies in one caddy that can move from the kitchen to the bathroom. Most renters skip a job because the product is in another room, and a caddy removes that excuse. Four items cover nearly everything: an all-purpose cleaner, a bathroom cleaner, a pack of microfiber cloths and a scrub brush for grout.

The monthly list: the corners people forget

This is where a small unit starts to look tired. These spots collect grease and dust slowly, and landlords notice them at move-out.

  • Baseboards and door frames, wiped with a damp cloth
  • The inside of the microwave and the oven door glass
  • The track of a sliding patio door and the balcony door glass
  • Bathroom exhaust fan cover and the grout around the tub
  • Under the bed and behind the couch
  • Cabinet fronts around the stove, where grease settles

Seasonal jobs for the Upstate

Spring pollen turns window sills and balcony doors yellow, and summer humidity brings mildew to bathrooms with weak fans. In fall, leaves and mud come in on shoes. A quick seasonal pass helps: clean window sills and tracks in spring, wash shower curtains and bath mats at the start of summer, and wipe entry floors and mats in fall.

If you plan to move out, start with the monthly list two weeks early. A lease inspection looks at the same places this list covers, and our Berea renter checklist shows the same idea from another town.
