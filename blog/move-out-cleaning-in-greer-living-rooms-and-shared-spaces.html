Move-out cleaning in Greer: living rooms and shared spaces

Move out cleaning living room Greer: what landlords check in empty living areas, hallways and entries, and how Perfect Services cleans them. Free estimate.

Key takeaways

  • In an empty living room the walls, switches, window tracks and floor edges show everything the furniture used to hide.
  • Shared spaces like the entry, hallway and coat closet are checked just as closely as the kitchen.
  • Perfect Services prices a move-out only after a free in-home estimate, so the quote matches the actual home.

The first thing that happens when the couch leaves a Greer living room is that the floor tells the truth. Four pale rectangles of clean carpet where the legs sat, a gray line along the wall where the vacuum never reached, and a dozen small marks at hand height around the light switch. If you are doing a move out cleaning for a living room in Greer, that is the room to plan around, because it is large, it is the first thing a landlord or buyer walks into, and it has no cabinets to hide behind.

This guide covers the living room and the shared spaces around it: the entry, the hallway, the stairs, the dining nook and the coat closet. For kitchens and baths we have written separate pieces, including how we handle move-out bathrooms. Here the focus is the open, visible part of the house.

What the walkthrough looks at first

A lot of move-outs we see around Greer, from older homes near Downtown Greer and Trade Street to newer streets close to GSP Airport, share the same pattern. The kitchen gets the attention, and the living room gets a quick vacuum. Then the final inspection arrives and the notes say things like "marks on walls" and "dusty blinds," not "dirty oven."

When we walk through a home for the free estimate, we stand in the doorway of the living room and look at it the way an inspector would, in this order:

  1. The floor along the walls, where dust and pet hair pile up.
  2. Baseboards and the bottom of door frames.
  3. Light switches, outlet covers and the door handles people touch constantly.
  4. Window sills, the track the window slides in, and the blinds.
  5. Any closet that opens off the room.

Everything on that list is part of our move-out cleaning in Greer, so the estimate visit is mostly about condition, not about what is included.

The shared spaces people forget

Entry and hallway

The front entry collects more than any other part of the house. Greer has plenty of red clay in the yards, and it comes inside on shoes. Even months after a family leaves, a faint orange tint can sit in the corner by the door and under the mat. We check the threshold, the door frame, and the first few feet of flooring closely.

Stairs and landings

If the home has stairs, the risers and the corner where each step meets the wall get the same treatment as the baseboards. Handrails get wiped too, since inspectors tend to put a hand on them.

The coat closet and the "mystery cabinet"

Shelves, rods and floors inside closets are on our list. A hall closet usually has a dusty shelf and a floor with a ring of grit. Built-in shelving beside a fireplace or media wall deserves the same check, because tenants often forget it exists until the last day.

What you can do before we arrive

  • Take everything off the floor, shelves and window sills. We clean around objects, but a bare room cleans faster and better.
  • Patch or note any wall damage so the cleaners do not scrub at something that is a repair, not dirt.
  • Take down curtains and rods, and fold up rugs you are keeping.
  • Leave the lights and water on until the cleaning is finished.
  • Mention pets. Hair along baseboards changes how we plan the first hour.
