Move-out cleaning checklist for Duncan renters

What landlords actually look at

Every landlord is different, but the same few areas decide most deposit conversations. Read your lease first. Look for phrases like "broom clean," "move-out condition" or a list of items such as carpet cleaning. Then plan around the places that get the closest look:

  • Kitchen cabinets, the inside of the oven and the refrigerator
  • Bathroom grout, tub, shower glass and toilet base
  • Floors, especially corners and under where furniture stood
  • Baseboards, door frames and light switch plates
  • Closets and window tracks, which people forget

Move out cleaning checklist by room

Kitchen

  • Empty every cabinet and drawer, then wipe inside and out
  • Clean the oven, stovetop and range hood, where grease builds up
  • Empty the refrigerator and freezer, remove shelves and wipe them dry
  • Wipe the microwave inside, plus the dishwasher door and filter
  • Clean the backsplash, counters and sink fixtures

Bathrooms

  • Scrub the tub, shower and glass, and treat soap scum on the tile
  • Clean the toilet fully, including the base and behind the tank
  • Wipe the vanity inside and out and polish the mirror
  • Check the exhaust fan cover for dust

Bedrooms, closets and living areas

  • Remove nail pops and scuffs where you can, and wipe the walls near switches
  • Dust the ceiling fans, vents and window blinds
  • Wipe closet shelves and rods
  • Vacuum carpets in rows, then mop hard floors from the farthest corner to the door

The order that saves you time

Clean after your belongings are out, not before. Working around boxes means doing everything twice. Go top to bottom: ceiling fans and vents first, then walls and cabinets, then counters and fixtures, and floors last. Leave the trash bags and your own cleaning supplies for the final pass so you do not track dirt back over finished rooms.

How long does it take?

An empty apartment is often finished in a few hours by a small team, while a larger family home with a heavy kitchen can take most of a day. A team of two or three cleaners works much faster than a single renter with one bucket, which is why many people book a move-out clean for the day after the movers leave.

When to do it yourself and when to hire help

Doing it yourself saves money if the unit is small and you have a full weekend. Hiring makes sense when you are working, moving with kids, driving from another city, or when the deposit at stake is bigger than the cost of the clean. Common move-out mistakes in Duncan rentals

Most deposit disputes come from a short list of oversights, not from big problems. Knowing them ahead of time helps you spend your effort where it counts.

  • Cleaning before the move is done. Boxes and furniture get dragged across clean floors and leave marks.
  • Skipping inside the appliances. An oven with baked-on grease or a refrigerator with sticky shelves is the first thing noticed.
  • Forgetting the small stuff. Light switch plates, door frames, closet shelves and window tracks collect fingerprints and dust.
  • Ignoring the garage and patio. If they were included in your lease, sweep them and remove stains where possible.
  • Not taking photos. Take dated pictures of each room when you finish. They are useful if a question comes up later.

Newer Duncan subdivisions often have light carpet and large open kitchens, so floor edges and cabinet fronts show wear quickly. Walk each room at the end with your phone flashlight held low across the floor, which reveals streaks and missed spots that normal lighting hides.

What a professional team adds

A team brings equipment you may not own, such as a strong vacuum with attachments, microfiber systems and degreasers made for ovens and range hoods. It also brings speed: two or three people can finish a three-bedroom home in the time one renter would spend on the kitchen alone. The result is not magic, only a consistent routine that checks every room in the same order, so nothing is left to memory on a stressful moving day.
