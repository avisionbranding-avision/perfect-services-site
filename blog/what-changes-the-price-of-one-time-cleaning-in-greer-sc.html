What changes the price of one-time cleaning in Greer, SC

One-time cleaning cost in Greer, SC depends on home size, time since the last clean, add-ons, date and pets. See how the free in-home estimate sets the price.

Perfect Services Team
November 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • The time since the last professional clean often matters more than the square footage.
  • Add-ons like the oven, fridge and cabinets are what stretch a visit.
  • Exact pricing comes only after a free in-home estimate.

Imagine two Greer homes, both about 2,000 square feet. One was cleaned professionally last month. The other has not had more than a quick wipe since spring. Booking a one-time clean for each will not take the same hours, and that is the main thing to understand about one-time cleaning in Greer, SC pricing. Here is how the pieces fit together.

Where the price comes from

Perfect Services does not name a figure before visiting. After a free in-home estimate, a short visit with no commitment, you get an exact price. We skip price by photo and quick phone numbers because they are a gamble: either the company guesses high to protect itself or low and adjusts later. Seeing the home is fairer to both sides. The pieces below are what we look at during that visit.

Piece one: size, counted in rooms

Square footage is a starting point, but rooms are what we actually clean. Bedrooms, bathrooms, a kitchen, a living area and any extras such as a bonus room or a finished basement each carry their own time. A house with five bedrooms and two baths is different from a house with three bedrooms and three baths, even if the square feet match, because bathrooms take the most hands-on time.

Piece two: condition

How long since the last professional clean? This is the factor people underestimate. A home cleaned every few weeks needs little more than a standard pass. A home that has gone six months needs more time at the stovetop, in the shower and along the baseboards, and the dust on blinds and door frames has turned into a layer. If the home is very heavy with build-up, we may suggest a deep cleaning in Greer instead and explain why at the estimate.

Piece three: add-ons

A standard one-time clean covers full kitchen cleaning and degreasing, bathrooms cleaned and disinfected, bedrooms and living areas dusted, floors vacuumed and mopped, baseboards and door frames, mirrors and glass polished and trash emptied. Add-ons sit on top of that:

  • Inside the oven
  • Inside the refrigerator
  • Inside the cabinets
  • Extra attention on windows or tracks

Each one adds time, and the oven is the one most people want. A baked-on oven can take a long stretch by itself. If you book an add-on, say so at the estimate so the crew arrives with the time and the products, rather than skipping it when the visit runs long.

Piece four: the date

Holiday weeks fill fast. The days before Thanksgiving and Christmas are some of the busiest of the year for one-time work, and last-minute requests are harder to fit. If your date is flexible, a mid-week slot is often easier to schedule than a Friday before a holiday. If it is not flexible, book the estimate as early as you can.

Piece five: pets

Hair and dander add time. Vacuuming along every wall, under the sofa and around the pet bed takes longer than it does in a pet-free home. Tell us about the animals, and tell us if any of them are nervous around strangers, so the team can plan around it.

What the estimate visit is like

The visit is not long. Someone from the team walks through the kitchen, bathrooms and living areas, looks at the floors, asks about pets and checks what you want added on. They will often point out one or two things you may not have thought of: a stovetop that needs a soak, a shower door with a film, a ceiling fan that has not been dusted in a while. You see what the team sees, and the price follows from that conversation, not from a formula.

Because it is one-time work, we also ask about the deadline. If guests land on Wednesday and the house has to be ready Tuesday night, the schedule is built backward from there. If you have an open house on Saturday, we plan so the last room finished is the one the realtor will walk into first. That kind of detail is what the visit is for.

If one clean turns into a regular plan

Some people book a single clean to try us and then decide to keep going. If that happens, the first visit often becomes the reset, and later visits are lighter and cost less per visit than a single clean. There is no pressure either way, and you are welcome to book a single visit and stop there.

One trick for keeping the visit shorter

Pick up before the crew arrives. Not cleaning, just picking up: toys, shoes, mail, the clothes on the bedroom chair. When surfaces are clear, the team spends its time cleaning rather than moving things. It can make a real difference in a family house with kids and pets, and it is the easiest way to get more from the same visit.

Why does time since the last clean affect one-time cleaning cost?

Are oven and fridge cleaning part of a standard one-time clean?

They are optional add-ons. Mention them at the estimate so the time is planned.
