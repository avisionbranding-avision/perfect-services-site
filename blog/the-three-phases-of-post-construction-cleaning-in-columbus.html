The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Columbus

Post construction cleaning in Columbus NC happens in three phases: rough, final and touch-up. See what each phase covers and how to book a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 18, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Post-construction cleaning works best in three passes: rough clean, final clean and a touch-up before move-in.
  • Drywall sanding dust settles in vents, tracks and closets, so one wipe is never enough.
  • Perfect Services gives a price only after a free in-home estimate.

The first thing you notice walking into a freshly finished room in Columbus is how clean it looks from the doorway. The second thing is the white film on every ledge when you drag a finger across it. Post construction cleaning in Columbus, NC is not one job, it is three, and knowing the difference saves time with your builder and your cleaner.

Phase one: the rough clean

The rough clean happens while trades are still finishing, usually after drywall, trim and rough flooring but before paint touch-ups and fixtures are final. The goal is not shine. It is to get the debris out so painters, tile setters and the electrician are not working in a pile of offcuts.

  • Remove scrap, packaging, tape and screws from every room.
  • Sweep and vacuum floors, including inside closets.
  • Wipe the heaviest dust off windows, sills and cabinet boxes.
  • Clear the garage or work area enough to walk safely.

Not every project needs this. On a small kitchen remodel the builder's crew often handles it. On an addition or a full renovation in Polk County, it keeps the site safe and makes the later phases faster.

Phase two: the final clean

This is the main event. Once painting, flooring and fixtures are in, the team goes room by room from top to bottom. Fine dust from drywall sanding is the biggest challenge. It is almost powder, and it hides in places a regular vacuum never touches.

  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans wiped, not just brushed.
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills cleaned, with paint specks and sticker residue removed.
  • Inside every cabinet, drawer and closet.
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished, with grout haze removed from tile.
  • Floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes, since the first wash mostly lifts dust.

This is the work described on our post-construction cleaning page for Columbus, and it is where most homeowners see the biggest change.

A few things help the final clean go well. Ask the crew to leave the doors open for us on cleaning day, keep the power and water on, and clear tools out of the rooms. If new flooring was just installed, tell us how long it should cure before foot traffic, and we will plan around it instead of guessing. Protective film on windows, cabinet doors and appliances should be removed by the builder or by us, depending on the scope, but it is best settled before the visit.

Foothill homes also bring red clay on boots and in truck beds, and it ends up on subfloors and stair treads long before the final clean. That is one more reason the floors get several passes: the first picks up the loose clay and dust, the next ones actually wash the surface.

Phase three: the touch-up pass

Even after a careful final clean, a house keeps shedding dust for days. Air returns pull it out of the ductwork, and the movers or the builder's last punch-list items track in more. A touch-up pass right before you move in (or before a closing walkthrough) catches that second layer. It is quicker than the final clean, mostly wiping horizontal surfaces, checking glass and mopping floors one last time.

Many homeowners book it as part of the same job so the schedule is set from the start. Others skip it and regret it, because furniture arrives and dust lands on it.

How much does post-construction cleaning cost in Columbus?

Pricing depends on the project, so we do not quote it over the phone or from a photo. The factors that shape it are:

  • Project size, meaning square feet and how many rooms were affected.
  • Dust level. Drywall sanding creates the most fine dust.
  • How many phases you book, since rough, final and touch-up can be combined.
  • Windows and glass, where residue and stickers take time.
  • Your deadline, because a closing or inspection date may need a larger team.

As a rough guide to timing, the rough clean usually happens in the days before painters and floor installers arrive, the final clean after the last trade has left, and the touch-up just before you move furniture in. Booking all three early lets us hold the dates, which matters when a closing is already on the calendar. Keep a short list of anything the builder still needs to fix, because once cleaners have finished a room, new drilling or sanding sends us back to the start.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need all three phases of post-construction cleaning?

Not always. A small bathroom remodel may only need a final clean, while a new addition or whole-house renovation usually benefits from all three. We recommend the right mix during the free estimate.

Why does dust keep coming back after the first clean?

Fine drywall dust settles into vents, ceiling fans and trim, then floats back down when the air handler runs. That is why a second and a touch-up pass matter.
