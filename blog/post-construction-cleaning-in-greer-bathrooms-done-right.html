Post construction cleaning bathroom Greer SC homeowners ask about usually follows a remodel: new tile, a new vanity, a frameless shower door. The contractor leaves, the room looks finished, and then the white film shows up on the tile the next morning. That film is grout haze, and it is the main reason a fresh bathroom is not ready for use.

What is actually left behind

A remodeled bathroom collects a specific set of residues. Grout haze is a cloudy layer on tile where grout has dried. Thinset specks sit along tile edges. There is caulk smeared on the tub edge, paint speckled on the baseboards and fine drywall dust on every ledge, including the inside of the new vanity drawers. Stickers still sit on the glass and on the faucet box, and cardboard dust lingers in the corners.

It is not ordinary dirt, so it does not respond to ordinary cleaning. The wrong abrasive can leave scratches in a brand new finish, which is why this belongs to a team that does post-construction cleaning in Greer regularly.

The order we follow on a finished bathroom

  1. Remove stickers, tape and protective film from fixtures, glass and mirrors.
  2. Vacuum the whole room with a HEPA-style vacuum, ceiling to floor, before any wet wiping, so dust is lifted instead of smeared.
  3. Wipe light fixtures, the exhaust fan and the top of the door frame.
  4. Treat grout haze and thinset on tile with the right product for the tile type.
  5. Clean the glass, the track and the hardware of the shower door.
  6. Polish the faucets, drain covers and the mirror.
  7. Wash the floor in several passes, since the first mop water turns gray.

The vacuum-first step matters because construction dust is finer than household dust. Wet wiping it first turns it into paste on grout lines.

Tile, stone and glass need different handling

Porcelain tolerates more than natural stone, and some acidic cleaners that are fine on porcelain can etch marble or travertine. During the estimate we look at what your builder installed so the products match. If you have a special sealed stone, tell the contractor and us what sealer was used. Frameless glass should never be scraped dry; we lift residue with a razor held flat only on glass that allows it, and we use a soft pad elsewhere.

What to ask the contractor before we come

  • Is the caulk fully cured before water is used?
  • What sealer went on the grout or stone, and when?
  • Are touch-ups or punch-list items still pending?
  • Is the water on and working in the room?
  • Are there protective films still on fixtures or mirrors?
  • Who is in charge of hauling away leftover tile and debris?

Cleaning before the punch list is done means we may be back. If the plumber or painter still needs a day, wait and book after.

Why we test the shower before leaving

One habit we keep on remodeled bathrooms is a final look with the shower running, when the contractor allows water use. Soap and water reveal streaks on glass, a haze left on tile and any spot where the drain cover was set dirty. If the caulk is still curing, we skip the water and check dry, then tell you which spots to inspect after it is safe to use the shower. We would rather say that than leave a streak you notice the first morning.

Walls need a note as well. Tile walls above the tub often keep a faint trail of grout haze up high where nobody wants to look, and a remodel with a tall niche or a bench has more of it. We climb a small step stool for these.
