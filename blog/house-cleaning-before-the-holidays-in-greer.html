This is a practical guide to how to plan house cleaning through the holidays instead of scrambling in the last few days.

Think in four dates, not one

Most people picture a single big clean the day before guests arrive. In practice, four moments matter, and spacing the work across them keeps it from becoming a marathon.

  1. Early November: a reset after fall. Leaves, mud and wet shoes have been in and out for weeks, and the entry and mudroom floors show it.
  2. The week before Thanksgiving: the visit that gets the kitchen, bathrooms and floors ready.
  3. Mid December: a refresh before parties, school programs and out-of-town family.
  4. The first week of January: a reset after the decorations come down and the extra foot traffic leaves.

What we get asked most before the holidays

"Is it worth a recurring visit just for November and December?"

Many Greer customers do exactly this: a biweekly schedule through the holiday stretch, then they decide whether to continue. The benefit is that the house never falls behind, so each visit is lighter than a one-time clean after weeks of buildup.

"What if I only need the guest rooms and kitchen?"

That is a reasonable request. Tell us during the estimate which rooms matter most and we plan the visit around them. Rooms nobody will enter can wait.

"Can you work around my cooking schedule?"

Yes. A morning visit the day before is common, so the kitchen is clean when the cooking starts and nothing is left damp overnight.

Where the holidays wear down a home

Winter changes how a house gets dirty. Windows stay shut and the heat runs, so dust circulates through vents. Guests bring coats, boots and luggage into rooms that usually stay tidy. Candles and cooking leave a film on kitchen cabinets and the range hood. A few trouble spots we see year after year:

  • The range hood and the cabinet fronts near the stove, which pick up grease from holiday baking.
  • Glass doors and sliding door tracks, where fingerprints and dust gather.
  • Guest bathrooms, which go from lightly used to busy in one weekend.
  • Entry floors and mats, where red clay and wet leaves come in with every pair of shoes.
  • The refrigerator, after leftovers push everything to the back.

A realistic holiday cleaning week

If you do part of the work yourself, this split keeps it manageable:

  • You: laundry, guest bed linens, decorations, and putting away clutter.
  • Us: kitchen, bathrooms, floors, dusting and baseboards.
  • Day of: wipe the counters, empty the trash and run a quick vacuum by the door.

The holiday weeks book fast, so ask what is open this week as soon as you have your dates. Neighbors in Taylors house cleaning and Duncan are on the same rush, and our team travels between them.

Pets, kids and the holiday house

Homes with dogs and young children feel the holidays differently. A dog gets more excited with visitors and sheds on every sofa, and children carry cookies into rooms that are normally off limits. We plan extra time for upholstery, stair edges and the floor under the dining table, where crumbs collect. If your family has a pet that sleeps on the bed, tell us during the estimate so the guest room gets what it needs, including a vacuum along the bedding line and under the frame.

After the holidays

The days after New Year's are when the house shows how much it has lived. Floors are scuffed, the refrigerator is full and the corners where the tree stood need a vacuum. Many customers schedule a visit the first week of January to reset. If the home needs more than a regular visit, you can step up to a deep cleaning.
