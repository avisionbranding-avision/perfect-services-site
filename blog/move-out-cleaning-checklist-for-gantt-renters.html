Move-out cleaning checklist for Gantt renters

Move-out cleaning checklist for Gantt renters: the kitchen, bath, closet and floor tasks that protect your deposit. Free in-home estimate before any price.

Key takeaways

Open the oven. That is usually the first thing a landlord or property manager does at a move-out walkthrough, followed by the refrigerator, the shower and a look along the baseboards. If you rent in Gantt, the 29605 area south of downtown Greenville off I-85 and US-25, this checklist follows the same order so nothing that costs you deposit money gets missed.

Gantt is a community of residential neighborhoods in Greenville County, so most rentals here are houses and small apartments rather than large complexes. Either way, the inspection logic is the same. Here is the order we work in on a typical move-out.

Before you start

Empty the home first. Clean rooms with furniture and boxes still inside take far longer and always leave dirty strips along the walls. Pull out what you can, bag the trash, and keep a small kit: a scrub brush, a razor scraper for stuck-on spots, microfiber cloths, a bucket and a vacuum with a hose.

Work top to bottom and back to front: ceiling fans and vents first, then walls and doors, then counters, and floors last. Dust falls, so cleaning floors first means doing them twice.

The Gantt move-out checklist

  • Inside and outside of all kitchen cabinets and drawers
  • Inside the refrigerator, freezer, oven and microwave
  • Range hood, stovetop and backsplash degreased
  • Toilet base, shower grout, glass and vanity interiors
  • Closet shelves, rods and floors
  • Baseboards, doors, door frames and light switches
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped edge to edge

Room by room: where renters lose points

Kitchen

The oven and the range hood filter are the usual culprits. Baked-on grease softens if you leave oven cleaner on for the time on the label, but it still takes scraping. In the fridge, remove the shelves and drawers and wash them in the sink so the cavity can be wiped clean. Do not forget the top of the refrigerator and the cabinet handles.

Bathrooms

Hard water spots on glass and chrome and dark grout lines are what inspectors photograph. Scrub grout with a brush, not just a sponge, and wipe the base of the toilet and the wall behind it. Empty the vanity drawers and wipe them inside.

Bedrooms and closets

Closet floors and shelf edges collect dust from boxes. Wipe the rods, the shelves and the track of any sliding closet door. Check for nail holes and scuffs on walls, and tell your landlord about them rather than leaving surprises.

Floors and edges

Vacuum along the edges first, then mop. Carpets show their worst along the walls and in doorways, so go over those twice.

DIY or professional move-out cleaning?

Doing it yourself is realistic if the home is small and you have a full weekend. The risk is time: move-out week is when everything else piles up, and the last items on the list (oven, grout, windows) are the slowest. Photos before you leave

Take dated photos of every room after cleaning, including the inside of the oven, the fridge and the bathrooms. If a landlord later disputes a deduction, those photos are your record. Take them before you return keys, because once the door is locked you cannot go back.

Common spots people forget

A few small things are missed on almost every move-out. The top of the refrigerator and the top of kitchen cabinets collect grease and dust. Light switch plates and door handles show fingerprints in the light. The inside of the dishwasher and the garbage disposal gets a smell. Blinds are slow and dusty, so people skip them, and the tracks of sliding doors hold small gravel.

If the landlord requires carpet cleaning, check the lease, since that is a separate job from the interior clean. If you only have a few hours, the kitchen and bathrooms are worth more than the bedrooms.

If your lease has special requirements

Read the move-out section of the lease before you start. Some landlords supply a cleaning list, some ask for a receipt from a professional cleaner and some require carpet shampooing. Matching their list is the surest way to keep the deposit. If the lease is silent, the checklist above covers what inspectors normally check.

Timing the last week

Pack the kitchen last, since you will need it. Clean closets and bedrooms as soon as they are empty, then bathrooms, then the kitchen, then floors on the final day. Leave an hour for a last walk through with a flashlight, because low light shows streaks on glass and smudges on walls that daylight hides. If something is going to run over, it is better to know the night before.
