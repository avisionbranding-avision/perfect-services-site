Key takeaways

The guest checked out at 11 and the next one arrives at 4. In between, someone has to find the wine glass that ended up behind the couch, notice the shower curtain with the new stain, and still make the beds look like the listing photos. That is a turnover, and the order you work in decides whether it takes a calm few hours or a scramble. Below is the sequence we use for Airbnb cleaning in Woodruff, SC, written so a host can use it as a checklist or hand it to a cleaner.

The Airbnb cleaning checklist for Woodruff turnovers, step by step

Step 1: The two-minute walkthrough before anything moves

Walk every room with your phone before you touch a thing. You are looking for damage, missing items, anything left behind and anything that needs a maintenance call. Photograph what you find. It takes two minutes and it is the record that makes a conversation with a guest or the platform straightforward later. Our team builds this into every visit as a damage and missing-item report with photos.

Step 2: Strip, bag and reset the big stuff

Start with the things that take the most room and the most time.

  • Strip the beds and bag the linens, towels and bath mats
  • Start the first load of laundry if you do it on site, or bag it for the laundry service
  • Collect all trash and recycling and reset the bins with fresh liners
  • Open the windows or turn on the fan so the house airs out while you work

Linens are the biggest scheduling variable in any turnover. If you wash them on site, the washer and dryer set the pace. If they go off site, you need spare sets on the shelf so the beds can be remade right away. Either way, the linens are host-provided and we make the beds to match the way you want them presented.

Step 3: Kitchen reset

Guests use kitchens more than hosts expect, even for a weekend. Work top to bottom.

  • Run or empty the dishwasher and put everything back where the listing says it lives
  • Wipe counters, the sink and faucet, the stovetop and the outside of the appliances
  • Clean the inside of the microwave and check the fridge for anything left behind
  • Check the coffee station, because that is where crumbs and spills hide
  • Wipe cabinet handles and the drawer pulls the guests touched most

Step 4: Bathrooms, the part guests inspect hardest

Reviews mention bathrooms more than almost any other room. Clean and disinfect the toilet, shower, tub, sink and mirror, and check the drain for hair. Hard water spots on glass and fixtures show quickly in a bright bathroom, so give them an extra wipe. Replace towels, and put out the soap and toilet paper before you leave the room, so restocking is not forgotten later.

Step 5: Living areas and bedrooms

Dust surfaces from high to low, wipe the remote controls and light switches, and check under cushions and beds. A quick vacuum under the sofa is where guests most often leave items. Make the beds last, after the dusting, so nothing lands on fresh linens. Fluff the pillows, and square up the throw blankets so the room matches the listing photos.

Step 6: Floors last, then a final look

Vacuum and mop on the way out, ending at the door you will lock. Then do one last walkthrough with the listing photos in your hand. If something does not match, fix it now. This is the staging step: the angle of the pillows, the lamps on, the blinds open.

Restocking: the part that gets forgotten

A restocking checklist keeps small gaps from becoming review comments. Typical items are soap, toilet paper, paper towels, dish soap, trash bags, coffee supplies and spare linens.
