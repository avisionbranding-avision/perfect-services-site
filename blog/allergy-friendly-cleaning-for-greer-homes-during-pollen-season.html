Where pollen ends up inside a Greer house

Every spring in the Upstate the yellow film shows up on cars, porch rails and the hood of anything parked under a pine. Less obvious is where it goes after you open a window or walk in from the yard. In the homes we clean in Greer, it settles in the same places each year: window sills and tracks, the top of the front door, ceiling fan blades, the first few feet of floor inside the entry, and the fabric of whatever sits closest to a vent.

This is a cleaning guide, not medical advice. If allergies or asthma are serious in your household, talk with your doctor about what to avoid. What we can say is how we clean so that dust is removed instead of moved around.

Dry dusting versus damp wiping

A feather duster looks tidy and sends fine dust back into the air. A microfiber cloth, slightly damp, picks it up and holds it. Our team uses microfiber for that reason, and a hand-wiping technique that also uses less product. The difference is small to the eye and noticeable after a day.

A pollen-season routine that fits real life

Pollen peaks in spring, but the leftovers hang around into summer in carpets, curtains and the filter in your air handler. A short weekly routine beats one huge scrub in May.

  1. Take shoes off at the door and keep a mat on each side.
  2. Keep windows shut on the worst days and run the air handler with a clean filter, changing it on your usual schedule.
  3. Wash bedding weekly and wipe the headboard.
  4. Wash pet bedding, since pets bring pollen in on their coats.
  5. Vacuum floors twice a week near the main door.
  6. Wipe the sills and tracks every week or two.

None of this is complicated, and none of it needs special equipment beyond a vacuum with a decent filter. The trick is order: top to bottom, back of the house to the door, so you do not re-dirty finished areas. It is the same sequence we follow on every visit.

Room by room: the bedroom and the living room

The bedroom is where dust matters most because you spend hours there. Start with the ceiling fan and the top of the dresser, then the headboard, the nightstands and the sill. Pull the bed away from the wall once in a while, because the strip of floor behind it collects more than you would expect. Vacuum under the bed, then move to the mattress edge. Wash pillowcases weekly and the rest of the bedding every week or two during the heavy weeks.

In the living room, the sofa and the rug hold the most. Vacuum cushions and the crevices, lift the rug edge and clean the floor under it, and wipe the TV stand and shelves damp. Homes with hardwood do well with a dry pass followed by a damp mop; carpet needs slow, overlapping passes with the vacuum, because speed leaves dust behind.
