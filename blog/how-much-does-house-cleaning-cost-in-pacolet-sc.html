The five things that set the price

When people search for "house cleaners near me prices," they are usually hoping for a flat rate. Flat rates exist because someone averaged out jobs and hoped for the best. We would rather price your house.

  • Home size and layout. More rooms and more floor take more time. A single-story ranch with open living space is faster than a two-story with a long hallway of closed doors.
  • Bedrooms and bathrooms. Bathrooms are the slowest rooms in any house. Tile, grout, glass and fixtures all need hand work, so the bathroom count matters more than the bedroom count.
  • Frequency. Weekly and biweekly visits cost less per clean than monthly, because a house cleaned every two weeks never gets far from clean.
  • Pets and kids. Hair, tracked-in dirt and sticky fingerprints add a little time. That is normal, and we plan for it.
  • The first visit. The first clean is usually deeper, because we are bringing the home up to the standard we can then maintain.

What a typical visit includes

Our house cleaning service follows the same order every time, so the work does not depend on anyone's memory. We start high and finish low:

  • Kitchen counters, sink and stovetop cleaned and sanitized, appliance fronts and handles wiped
  • Bathrooms: toilet, shower, tub, mirrors and chrome
  • Surfaces, shelves and furniture dusted
  • Beds made and trash emptied
  • Baseboards and door frames touched up on a rotation, so every room gets its turn
  • Light switches and other high-touch spots wiped
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped by hand, last, so nothing we do afterward lands on a clean floor

The mopping is by hand, working along the walls, rather than a quick pass down the middle. It is slower and it is a big reason people tell us the house smells and feels different afterward.

Why recurring service usually works out better

Many older Pacolet homes have the kind of character that collects dust in the corners: original trim, deep window sills, wood floors with gaps. A one-time clean looks great for a week. A biweekly rhythm keeps that work from piling up, which means each visit is lighter and the per-visit cost drops. Comparing quotes fairly

If you are collecting prices from more than one company, compare like with like. Ask each one the same questions:

  1. Do they see the house before quoting, or quote from a phone call?
  2. Is the baseboard and door frame work included, or an add-on?
  3. Are floors mopped by hand or with a pad on a pole?
  4. Who actually shows up: the same team each time, or whoever is free?
  5. What happens if you are not happy with something? Do they come back?

Our questions to ask before hiring a house cleaner goes deeper on each one. A low number from a company that has never seen your kitchen is a guess, and the guess usually gets corrected on the invoice.
