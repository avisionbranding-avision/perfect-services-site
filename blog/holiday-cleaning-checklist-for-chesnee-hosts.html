Holiday cleaning checklist for Chesnee hosts

Hosting family this season? Use this holiday checklist for one time house cleaning in Chesnee, SC and see what a one-time clean covers. Free in-home estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Clean in the order guests will see the house: entry, kitchen, dining area, guest bathroom.
  • Book a one-time clean for the week before the holiday, since those dates fill early.
  • Price comes after a free in-home estimate and depends on size, condition and add-ons.

It is the third week of November, the turkey is on order, and the dining table is still holding a month of mail. Chesnee homes in the foothills tend to be roomy, with country-sized kitchens and long driveways that bring red clay straight to the front door. If family is coming for the holidays, a one-time clean can be the difference between a weekend of stress and a weekend you enjoy. This checklist is for hosts, and it is the same list behind our one time house cleaning in Chesnee.

Work backward from the guests

Think about which rooms people will actually see and use, and put your energy there. A guest will walk through the entry, sit in the kitchen, eat in the dining area and use one bathroom. The back bedroom closet can wait.

  1. The entry and mudroom: shake out mats, wipe the door handle and glass, and vacuum the red clay that settles along the baseboards.
  2. The kitchen: this is where everyone ends up, so it gets the most attention.
  3. The dining area: table, chairs, and the floor under them.
  4. The guest bathroom: mirror, sink, toilet, towels and a fresh trash bag.
  5. The room where someone sleeps: linens, dusting, and a cleared surface.

The kitchen before a big meal

A holiday kitchen is judged on the oven, the fridge and the sink. Take everything off the counters and wipe them. Clean the stovetop and the oven door so the room does not smell like last month. Throw out what has expired and wipe the fridge shelves, because you need the space for leftovers.

If the inside of the oven needs more than a wipe, that is an add-on we can do during a one-time visit, along with the inside of the fridge or cabinets. It is worth asking about when you book rather than trying to fit it into a busy week.

After the party

Hosting does not end when the last car leaves. Plan a clean for the days after, too, when dishes, crumbs, spilled drinks and extra trash take over the house. Many people schedule the clean before the holiday and a lighter one afterward, or just book one visit when the house is at its worst and the relatives have gone home.

A short checklist for the week before

  • Clear surfaces so the cleaners can reach them.
  • Decide which rooms matter most and tell the team.
  • Put away anything breakable or sentimental.
  • Ask about add-ons like inside the oven, fridge or cabinets.
  • Tell us about pets, since hair and dander add time.
  • Book early, because holiday weeks fill quickly.

What a one-time clean includes

You do not need a standing schedule to get this. A one-time clean is a full visit: the kitchen cleaned and degreased, bathrooms disinfected, bedrooms and living areas dusted, floors vacuumed and mopped, baseboards and door frames wiped, mirrors and glass polished and trash emptied. Hosts often also ask for Deep Cleaning if it has been a long time since the last professional visit.

What country homes need before guests arrive

Chesnee has farms and country homes with long driveways and gravel or dirt approaches, and in a wet fall the red clay comes inside on every pair of shoes. Put down a mat outside, one inside, and a shoe tray by the door. Ask guests to leave boots in the mudroom. It is easier than cleaning the whole floor twice during the visit.

Wood stoves and fireplaces add their own work. Ash and soot collect on the hearth and nearby surfaces, and a dust layer from a stove can sit on the mantel for days before anyone notices. Wipe these areas down, and remember that guests will gather around the warmest room in the house.

Beds and bathrooms for overnight guests

If people are staying overnight, wash all linens, wipe the nightstands, empty a drawer or two and clear a closet shelf. In the bathroom, put out fresh towels, a new soap, a spare roll of toilet paper and an empty trash can with a liner. A guest who finds these ready feels welcome, and it takes only a few minutes.

Most important, do not try to finish everything the night before. The kitchen, the guest bathroom and the floors take the longest. If you can book a clean two or three days ahead, you will have time to cook, set the table and relax.
