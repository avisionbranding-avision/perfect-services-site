Regular clean or deep clean?

A regular visit keeps a home level: floors, surfaces, bathrooms, kitchen. A deep clean goes into the layers. If you can run a finger along the baseboard and see a gray line, or if grout in the shower has darkened, the house needs a deep clean first. Here is a quick check.

The order of work

The team starts with the kitchen, since grease needs time to soften. While degreaser sits on the stovetop and hood, someone starts the bathrooms. Fans, vents and fixtures are dusted from above, then baseboards and door frames, then bedrooms and living rooms. Floors are the last step in each room.

Walking the house at the end, with the room list in hand, is part of the method. Small misses, such as a switch plate or a corner under a chair, are fixed before the team leaves.

How long it takes

Most homes take a team several hours to a full day, depending on size and condition. A home that has gone a year without a professional visit takes longer than one cleaned in the spring. We usually can schedule the visit within the week after the estimate, although busy periods before holidays fill up.

How to prepare

  • Pick up toys, clothes and papers so surfaces are reachable.
  • Tell us about pets and where they will stay.
  • Decide whether you want the inside of the oven or fridge done.
  • Note spots to focus on, like a shower with heavy build-up.

What deep cleaning does not include

It is worth being clear about the limits. A deep clean is not a repair or a restoration. We do not remove permanent stains, refinish floors, or take apart appliances. Exterior pressure washing is a different job and not something we do. Carpets are vacuumed thoroughly, but shampooing is separate. If you need any of these, tell us at the estimate and we will say honestly what we can and cannot do.
