The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Boiling Springs

The three phases of post construction cleaning in Boiling Springs, SC: rough clean, final clean and touch-up, with what each includes and how pricing works.

Perfect Services Team
November 21, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Rough, final and touch-up cleans each remove a different layer of construction mess.
  • Drywall sanding dust is the finest and slowest to remove.
  • Pricing comes after a free in-home estimate of the project.

Drywall dust gets into everything, including closed drawers, the top of door frames and the tracks of new windows. Anyone who has watched a Boiling Springs remodel finish knows the builder's sweep is not the same as a clean house. Professional post construction cleaning is done in phases, because a house cannot be cleaned once and be done while trades are still working in it.

Phase one: the rough clean

The rough clean happens after the heavy work is finished and before the final trades come back. The goal is to remove debris and the bulk of the dust so painters, flooring crews and trim carpenters are not working on top of it. Typical work includes:

  • Sweeping and vacuuming all floors
  • Removing leftover material and packaging
  • Wiping surfaces and ledges of coarse dust
  • Clearing inside cabinets, closets and drawers of debris

A rough clean is not pretty, and it is not meant to be. It is a reset so the next crew starts clean.

Phase two: the final clean

The final clean is the one homeowners remember. After all trades have finished, the team goes room by room:

  • Fine construction dust removed from all surfaces, ledges and trim
  • Inside cabinets, drawers and closets wiped
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills cleaned
  • Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive removed
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans cleaned
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished
  • Floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes

The multiple floor passes matter. Fine dust settles again after the first one, so a single mop pass leaves a film.

Phase three: the touch-up

A touch-up pass happens right before move-in or the final walkthrough. Fingerprints from a punch-list visit, a new smudge on glass, a footprint on a clean floor: the touch-up catches the last of it. For many homes this is the shortest phase, and it makes the difference at a builder inspection or closing.

Why Boiling Springs projects have a lot of dust

Boiling Springs is one of Spartanburg County's fastest-growing communities, with many new subdivisions around SC-9 and I-85 (29316). New-build and renovation work is common here, and builders and homeowners both need the finished house cleaned before closing or move-in. Red clay tracked in on boots adds to the film on floors, so plan for it.

A good sequence protects the finished work. Cleaning too early means the dust from the next trade lands on freshly wiped surfaces. Cleaning too late means paint, adhesive and stickers have cured and are harder to remove. The phases exist to avoid both problems, which is also why we like to talk with the builder or homeowner before the work starts, not after.

Homeowners are often surprised at how much cleaning is hidden inside things. Dust gets inside cabinets and drawers, on top of door frames, inside window tracks and in vents. If it is not wiped out at the final clean, it will come out of the vents on the first day the heat or air conditioning runs. That is why the final clean includes the inside of closets and cabinets, the light fixtures and the vents, not just the visible floors.

For builders and general contractors with several homes at once, a repeatable plan helps. Share the list of homes and the closing dates, and the schedule can be built around the order they finish. For a single homeowner finishing a kitchen or bathroom renovation, the same three steps apply at a smaller scale, and often one visit is enough.

How long does it take?

A final clean takes longer than a standard house clean of the same size, and large projects may need a team over more than one day. The estimate gives you the schedule. If you can tell us the date of your final inspection, we will do our best to work around it this week or next.

Before the cleaning crew arrives, a few things help. Make sure the water and power are on, since the team needs both. Remove remaining tools, ladders and materials so the floors can be cleaned, and tell us about any surfaces that are still curing or protected. If new flooring or countertops have protective film, leave it until the final inspection unless the builder says otherwise.

After the final clean, keep foot traffic low until move-in. Ask movers and delivery crews to wear shoe covers or take shoes off at the door, since one trip across a washed floor can undo the work. A short touch-up visit after the move-in boxes arrive is worth discussing if you want the house to feel finished on the first night.
