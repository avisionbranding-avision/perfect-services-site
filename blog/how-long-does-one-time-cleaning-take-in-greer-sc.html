How long does one-time cleaning take in Greer, SC?

How long does one time cleaning take in Greer SC? See what stretches a visit, how rooms add up and how to plan your day around the Perfect Services team.

Perfect Services Team
November 13, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Timing depends more on condition, pets and add-ons than on square footage alone.
  • Kitchens and bathrooms take the largest share of any visit, and floors always come last.
  • We confirm the time and the exact price during the free in-home estimate, never before.

Short answer: there is no honest single number. A tidy two-bedroom that was cleaned last month and a four-bedroom house with two dogs and a year of buildup are different jobs, even if both say "one time cleaning in Greer" on the booking form. What we can do is show you where the hours go, so you can plan your day and understand what you are paying for.

Where the time actually goes

On a one-time house cleaning in Greer, the team splits into a crew so several rooms are worked at once. Even so, a few areas eat most of the clock:

  • Kitchen: degreasing the stovetop and backsplash, cabinet fronts, sink, counters and appliance exteriors. Grease that has sat for months needs dwell time.
  • Bathrooms: soap scum on glass, grout lines, the base of the toilet, mirrors. These are the slowest rooms for their size.
  • Floors: vacuuming and mopping the whole house, done last so we do not walk dirt back over clean areas.
  • Detail work: baseboards, door frames, switch plates and glass, where the Brazilian detail-first method adds minutes in exchange for results you notice.

How to plan your day

Most homeowners prefer to be out for the visit, and that works fine. If you stay, pick a room that is done early or a home office, and tell us which it is so we can work around you. Here is a simple plan that has worked for plenty of Greer households:

  1. Pick a start time that suits your school run or shift.
  2. Clear counters and floors the night before so nothing delays the team.
  3. Leave pets in a closed room or let us know where they are.
  4. Plan to come back to a finished house, with floors dry and the trash out.

We would rather tell you the real window than promise a speedy one. A rushed clean misses the edges, and the edges are what you notice when guests walk in.

What the team checks first in the walkthrough

When we visit for the estimate, we look at three things that matter most for time. First, the kitchen: how much grease sits on the range hood and backsplash, and whether the cabinet fronts have a film from cooking. Second, the bathrooms: how many there are, and whether the glass has hard water marks. Third, the floors: how much hardwood, tile or carpet there is, and where the pets sleep. A home with two bathrooms and a big open kitchen can take longer than a larger house with simple surfaces.

We also ask about the date. If a holiday week is close, schedules fill, and we would rather suggest a day that gives the team room to do the job properly than squeeze it into a short slot.

Why the extra half hour matters

Most of the difference between a good clean and a great one is in the final walk-through. That is when we catch the fingerprints on the fridge, the dust on the top of a door frame and the streak on a mirror. Skipping it saves time, but you would see the difference the next morning. We think a one-time clean should hold up when guests look closely, so that walk-through stays in every visit.

If you only have a short window, say so at the estimate. We can propose a plan that fits, such as focusing on kitchen and bathrooms first and leaving lighter rooms for another visit.
