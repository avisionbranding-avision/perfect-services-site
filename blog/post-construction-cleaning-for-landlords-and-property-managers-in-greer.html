Post-construction cleaning for landlords and property managers in Greer

Post-construction cleaning for landlords and property managers in Greer SC: three phases, an acceptance checklist and a free in-home estimate for each unit.

November 22, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Plan three passes: rough clean, final clean and a touch-up before the tenant moves in.
  • Use a written acceptance checklist so you can sign off a unit quickly.
  • The exact price comes after a free in-home estimate, never from square footage alone.

Questions property managers ask us first

Who cleans after the contractor? Not the contractor, in most cases. Crews leave a broom-swept floor and a pile of debris at best. Is it the same as a regular turnover clean? No. After a renovation there is fine dust in places a tenant would never look until they move in. This page explains how post-construction cleaning in Greer fits into a rental renovation.

Phases that match a renovation schedule

PhaseWhenPurpose
Rough cleanAfter demolition or heavy workRemoves debris and bulk dust so trades can keep working
Final cleanAfter the last trade leavesDetail work on every surface, fixture and floor
Touch-upJust before move-in or showingCatches settled dust and scuffs from walkthroughs

The three can be booked together, which saves coordination. For plain turns without repairs, the move-in and move-out cleaning team is the better fit.

An acceptance checklist for the walkthrough

  • Fine dust gone from ledges, trim, shelves and the tops of door frames
  • Insides of cabinets, drawers and closets wiped
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills clean, with no paint specks
  • Stickers and adhesive removed from appliances and fixtures
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans dusted
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished
  • Floors washed in more than one pass

Run a clean white cloth along a window sill or the top of a door frame. If it picks up gray, the dust is still there. We use the same simple test.

What changes across Greer's housing stock

Greer spans Greenville and Spartanburg counties, and a manager here might look after a house near Trade Street one week and a newer property near GSP Airport the next. Older homes often have more trim, plaster dust and layers of old paint to sand, while newer homes have bigger open areas with more glass. Neither is better or worse; the estimate visit is how we learn which one we are walking into.

Rough cleaning is the phase managers skip most often and regret most. If trades keep working in a dusty unit, the dust gets into HVAC returns, cabinets that have just been installed and the grooves of new flooring. One early debris and bulk dust pass costs time but protects every surface that goes in after it. It also keeps the work area safer for the next crew, because floors are not covered in offcuts and screws.

Think about who holds the keys on each phase. The contractor usually does until the last trade finishes, then hands off. Agree on a handoff day so the final clean is not booked while someone is still caulking a bathroom.

The touch-up pass deserves a line of its own. Between the final clean and the day the tenant arrives, there are walkthroughs, deliveries and sometimes a punch list. Each adds footprints, fingerprints on glass and a light layer of dust. A short visit right before move-in or before showing the unit resets all of it, and it is far cheaper in time than apologizing for it afterward.

Finally, keep a simple record. After each phase, take photos, note the date and keep the checklist. When a tenant later reports a stain behind a new appliance, you will know whether it was there at handoff. We can share our room-by-room notes at your request.

How long it takes

A single kitchen or bathroom turn can take a few hours; a whole-home renovation takes more than one visit. I-85, SC-14 and US-29 keep travel across Greer short, so scheduling is mostly a question of when the last trade finishes. Many managers ask for the final clean to be booked the same week the work wraps, which we can often arrange once we have seen the unit.

One more thing managers tell us they wish they had done earlier: set the expectation with the contractor. A line in the work order such as "remove debris and broom-sweep before final clean" saves hours of our time and yours. It also avoids the awkward moment when a leftover paint can or a stack of tile is found in a closet that is supposed to be clean. If something gets damaged during cleaning, we want to know the same day, and the photos from each phase make that conversation straightforward.

The same approach works for homeowners who rent out a property after a remodel. The scale is smaller, but the order of work is the same.
