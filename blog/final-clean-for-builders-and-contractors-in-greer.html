A final clean is the last thing between your crew and a closing date, and it is the step that gets squeezed when anything upstream runs late. If you build or remodel in the Greer area, here is how we work with builders and general contractors so the clean fits your punch list instead of fighting it.

Three visits, three different jobs

Builders often ask for "the final clean" as if it were one visit. In practice we see it split into stages, and each has its own purpose. Our post-construction cleaning can be booked for any of them or all together.

  1. Rough clean. Debris, sawdust and packaging come out after framing, drywall or major trades finish, so the next trade is not working on top of a mess.
  2. Final clean. After paint, trim, fixtures and flooring are in. This is the detailed pass: dust off every ledge, glass cleaned, cabinets wiped inside and out, bathrooms polished.
  3. Touch-up. A shorter visit after the punch list or the buyer walk-through, to catch scuffs, new dust and anything the last trade left behind.

Telling us which stage you need when you call lets us plan the crew size and the hours. A final clean booked before the painter is done means paying twice for the same windows.

What a final clean should hit

  • Fine drywall dust on every surface, ledge, window sill and trim line
  • Inside cabinets, drawers and closets
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills
  • Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive on glass, tile and fixtures
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished
  • Floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes
  • A final touch-up pass before the buyer or owner arrives

Drywall dust is the part that surprises people. It is fine enough to settle on top of door frames and inside cabinet corners days after you thought everything was wiped. That is why we vacuum first, wipe second and wash floors in more than one pass.

Why dust is worse in a new Greer home than you would think

Greer has a lot of newer construction around GSP Airport and the BMW plant, and those homes tend to have open floor plans with tall ceilings, large windows and a lot of hard flooring. Great for buyers, but every vent, ceiling fan blade and window track becomes a dust trap during the build. Red clay is also part of the picture: it tracks in on boots, dries to powder and shows up on new floors and in garage entries. We plan for it by working top to bottom and doing the floors last.

What we look at in the first ten minutes on site

Before any cleaning starts, we walk the house with whoever is on site. We check which rooms are finished and which are still in progress, whether the floors have protective covering, where the dust is heaviest, and which surfaces are delicate: new stone counters, black fixtures, factory-finished cabinets. We also ask about the schedule that follows us. If an appliance delivery or a carpet crew is coming after, the floors may need to wait until the end. That short conversation is also when we spot anything that should be fixed by a trade first, such as caulk that is still wet or a window with a cracked pane we do not want to scrub around.
