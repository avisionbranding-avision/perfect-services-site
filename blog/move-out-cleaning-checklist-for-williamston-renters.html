Move-out cleaning checklist for Williamston renters

Move out cleaning Williamston SC: a room-by-room checklist for renters who want their full deposit back, plus what a pro adds and how pricing works.

November 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Landlords check the oven, fridge, baseboards, tracks and closets first, so start there.
  • Work room by room, top to bottom, with the home emptied as much as possible.
  • Perfect Services prices move-out cleaning only after a free in-home estimate.

The walkthrough with your landlord usually takes ten minutes, and it decides how much of your deposit comes back. After many move-outs, we can tell you where inspectors look first: the inside of the oven, the refrigerator, bathroom grout, window tracks and the baseboards. This checklist is built for renters in Williamston who want to leave the place in a state nobody can argue with.

Before you start

  • Read your lease for any cleaning clause. Some list specific tasks, such as carpet cleaning.
  • Take dated photos after you finish, room by room. They are your proof.
  • Empty the place first. Furniture and boxes make everything slower.
  • Gather microfiber cloths, a scrub brush, a degreaser, glass cleaner, a mop and a vacuum with a hose attachment.

Kitchen: the room that costs the most deposit

  1. Empty the refrigerator and freezer, remove shelves and drawers, wash them and wipe every wall. Leave the doors propped open so it dries.
  2. Clean inside the oven. Heavy grease takes real time, so do not leave this for the last hour.
  3. Degrease the range hood, stovetop and backsplash.
  4. Wash the microwave inside and out.
  5. Wipe inside and outside all cabinets and drawers. Crumbs hide in the corners.
  6. Clean the sink, run the disposal with a little dish soap and wipe the faucet base.

Bathrooms

  1. Scrub the toilet base, behind it and the hinge area of the seat.
  2. Clean the shower or tub, glass doors and the grout lines. Hard water spots may need a soak.
  3. Empty and wipe the vanity interiors, drawers and medicine cabinet.
  4. Polish mirrors and chrome fixtures.

Bedrooms, closets and living areas

  • Wipe closet shelves and rods, then vacuum the closet floor.
  • Wash baseboards, doors, frames and light switches. Scuffs on these are what inspectors see at eye level.
  • Clean window sills, tracks and blinds. Sliding door tracks collect more dirt than anyone expects.
  • Dust vents, ceiling fans and light fixtures.
  • Vacuum edge to edge and mop hard floors, finishing at the front door.

What people usually miss

From the houses we clean, the most overlooked spots are the inside of the dishwasher, the top of the refrigerator, the bottom of the oven door, drawer runners and the space behind the toilet. Williamston has a mix of older homes with historic details and plenty of rentals along US-29 and SC-20, and older homes tend to have extra trim and deeper window sills that need more wiping than a newer apartment.

A simple timeline for the last week

Move-outs go wrong when everything lands on the final day. Spread it out and the walkthrough becomes easy.

  1. Seven days out: Take down curtains, clear cabinets and start using up food so the fridge empties itself. Fill nail holes if your lease asks for it.
  2. Three days out: Clean the oven and the inside of the refrigerator. Both are slow, and you do not want to rush them.
  3. Day before: Clean closets, window tracks and bathrooms while the rooms are mostly empty.
  4. Move-out day: After the last box leaves, vacuum, mop, wipe the switches and take your photos.

Mistakes that cost renters money

  • Cleaning before the furniture is out, then re-cleaning when it leaves marks and crumbs behind.
  • Using abrasive scrubbers on the stovetop or tub and scratching the surface, which a landlord can charge as damage.
  • Forgetting the garage, patio or porch if they came with the unit.
  • Leaving trash or items in the bins and attic.
  • Skipping photos, so there is nothing to show if a charge appears later.

Renters who ask us for help often do the first half themselves and hire the team to finish the heavy kitchen and bathroom work. That is a fine way to split it, and we can scope it that way at the estimate.
