The office cleaning checklist: daily tasks

Daily work is about surfaces that get handled and rooms that get used. Skip these for a week and the office feels dirty even if the floors look fine.

  • Empty trash and recycling, and replace the liners
  • Wipe desks, counters and shared workstations, clearing papers only when the team agrees
  • Wipe high-touch points: door handles, light switches, keypads, elevator buttons, shared phones
  • Clean and disinfect restrooms, then restock soap, paper towels and toilet paper
  • Wipe the break room counters, sink and microwave, and the outside of the fridge
  • Vacuum carpet and entry mats, mop hard floors
  • Wipe glass doors and the entry area, where fingerprints show first
  • Dust the reception desk and the waiting area

A lot of offices only need the full daily list two or three times a week. A small accounting office with six people is a different job from a salon or a clinic with walk-ins all day, which is why frequency is the first thing we ask about.

The weekly list: what builds up quietly

Dust, smudges and sticky spots do not show on day one. By Friday they do. Weekly tasks catch them before a client does.

  • Dust vents, shelves, window sills, monitor stands and picture frames
  • Detail the break room: inside the microwave, the coffee station, cabinet fronts
  • Clean the base of toilets, the walls beside them and the restroom floor edges
  • Spot-clean walls and door frames around switches
  • Vacuum under desks and along the baseboards, not just the open floor
  • Wipe chair arms and conference table edges

The monthly list: the corners nobody owns

These are the jobs that belong to nobody in the office, so they never get done. Put them on a calendar.

  • Clean baseboards and the tops of door frames
  • Wipe the inside of the fridge and toss old lunches
  • Wash interior glass and partitions
  • Move chairs and small furniture to clean under them
  • Check mats, carpet edges and floor corners where grit collects

If the office has carpet, tile and hard floors in different rooms, say so up front. Each floor type needs a different method, and a mop that works on tile will leave a carpeted entry looking worse.

Spartanburg details that change the checklist

Spartanburg County is the hub of the Upstate, and offices here range from a single suite near Morgan Square to retail units and clinics along the I-85 and I-26 corridors. Foot traffic changes the list more than square footage does. A showroom that opens its front door to the public all day needs the entry glass and the floors by the door done more often than a back-office suite with four desks.

Weather matters too. Red clay from a muddy parking lot and the pollen that coats everything in spring both end up on entry mats and in the first ten feet of carpet. Winter brings salt and grit from trucks and boots. If your doors face the lot, the entry area deserves its own line on the checklist.

How much does office cleaning cost in Spartanburg?

The honest answer is that it depends on your space, and Perfect Services does not quote prices over the phone or from photos. We give the price after a free in-home estimate, which for a business simply means a quick walkthrough of your office. There is no commitment. The factors that decide the number are:

  • Square feet of the area to clean
  • How often you want service: daily, weekly or monthly
  • The number of restrooms and how much traffic they get
  • Whether you need after-hours cleaning
  • Floor types, because carpet, tile and hard floors need different care

During the walkthrough we look at the restrooms first, then the break room, then the entry. Those three areas tell us most of what we need to know. We also ask who has the keys, what the alarm routine is and where supplies are kept.

Before the first visit

You do not have to tidy up for the cleaning crew, but a few small steps make the first visit more useful.

  1. Clear desks of loose papers and personal items you want left alone.
  2. Tell us which rooms are off limits, such as a server closet or a private office.
  3. Note any floor that has a special finish or a history of stains.
  4. Decide on a schedule that works around staff, so cleaning does not interrupt work.

A first visit often runs longer than the regular ones, because the crew sets the baseline. After that, recurring visits are faster. If your space also needs a one-time reset, a deeper pass before starting regular service is an option, and we can talk about it during the estimate. Frequently asked questions

How often should a small Spartanburg office be cleaned?

It depends on traffic. A quiet office with a handful of desks may be fine with service two or three times a week, while a salon, clinic or retail store usually needs daily restroom and floor care. We set the schedule during the free in-home estimate after seeing your space.

Can office cleaning happen after hours?

Yes. Many offices prefer evening or early morning cleaning so staff are not disturbed. After-hours schedules are one of the factors we discuss during the estimate, along with keys, alarm codes and supply storage.

Do you clean restrooms and the break room?

Yes. Restrooms are cleaned, disinfected and restocked, and the break room gets the counters, sink, microwave and fridge exterior wiped. Deeper tasks like the fridge interior can go on a monthly list.
