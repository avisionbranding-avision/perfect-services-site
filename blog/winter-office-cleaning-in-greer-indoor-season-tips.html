Winter office cleaning in Greer: indoor season tips

Winter office cleaning in Greer, SC: how closed-up buildings, salt, grit and flu season change the routine, plus how a free in-home estimate sets your price.

Perfect Services Team
November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Winter brings grit, wet entry floors and closed windows, which changes what an office needs.
  • High-touch points and restrooms deserve more attention in cold and flu season.
  • Pricing follows square feet, frequency, restrooms, hours and floor types, and is set after a free estimate.

From December to February, an office in Greer works with the windows shut and the heat on. People track in rain, grit and leaves, the carpet by the door goes dark, and the same handful of people share the same few surfaces for eight hours. The cleaning plan that worked in September needs a few changes. Here is how a small business can adjust.

Start at the entrance, because winter starts there

In the Upstate, winter is more wet than snowy. A rainy day means water, red clay and gravel come through the front door and onto the lobby floor. If it dries into the carpet, it turns into a grey path that no vacuum fully removes. Put a mat both outside and inside, and plan for the entry to be mopped or vacuumed on every service visit rather than weekly.

Glass doors take a beating as well. Fingerprints and rain spots show up at an angle in low winter sun, so the glass at the entry and reception is something we check first at offices off US-29 and SC-14.

High-touch points matter more in cold season

When colds and flu go around, the surfaces people share matter more than the floor. A good office routine in winter puts extra attention on:

  • Door handles, push plates and keypads
  • Light switches and thermostat covers
  • Break room faucet, microwave handle and fridge door
  • Shared phones, copier buttons and conference table edges
  • Restroom handles, dispensers and stall locks

None of this requires a long list of special products. It requires the work being done on every visit, in the same order, so nothing gets skipped when a team is rushed.

Restrooms and the break room

Restrooms are cleaned, disinfected and restocked on each visit, and in winter the restock list is longer: paper towels, hand soap and tissue run out faster when more people are coming in with colds. In the break room, the microwave and the fridge exterior are the usual trouble spots, plus the sink, which holds the coffee mugs nobody got to. We wipe the outside of the fridge but leave the inside to you unless you ask for it.

Floors in winter: carpet and hard floors need different care

Carpet traps grit, and grit cuts the fibers. A vacuum pass with the right attachment on the entry runs will protect it for years. Hard floors and tile show salt and residue, so a damp mop with a clean head matters more than a strong product. Offices around the GSP Airport area and the BMW plant see a mix of both, and we adjust the tools to the floor rather than treating every room the same.

Dust, dry air and the vents

With the heat running, dust settles on the top of monitors, shelves and window sills faster than in summer. Dry indoor air also keeps it floating. Dusting from the highest surface down at every visit, and wiping the vent covers you can reach, keeps the room from feeling stale. It is a small job, and it is the one office managers tell us they notice the first week of a regular service.

Leave the ceiling vents and the high shelves for a seasonal deep visit rather than the weekly routine. Adding them every week only raises the time without changing how the office looks.

Which schedule fits a small office?

A salon, a clinic and a two-person office need very different frequencies. A retail store open six days a week may need the floors and glass every night. A small office with a few desks may do well with a visit once or twice a week. The restroom traffic, not just the square feet, is usually what sets the number. After hours is the most common request, because the team can work without moving around people, and the office opens clean in the morning.

Property managers with common areas often prefer a fixed day and a short written checklist, so everyone knows what has been done. Ask for one when you request your estimate.

Wet days and slip risk

A wet floor at the entrance is a safety issue as well as a cleaning one. Mats that curl or slide, and a tile floor that stays damp after a rainy lunch hour, are what lead to slips. We leave the entry dry at the end of every visit and flag mats that are worn. If your building has a long hallway from the parking lot, an extra runner mat is worth the small cost.
