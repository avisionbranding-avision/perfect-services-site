Clean Construction Dust in Your Greer Home After a Remodel

After the remodel: how to clean construction dust in your Greer, SC home, from drywall dust to vents, plus pricing factors and how to book a cleaning.

Why remodel dust is different

Drywall dust is extremely fine and clings to surfaces. Sanding dust from wood or floors is gritty. Tile and cutting dust can be abrasive. If you wipe any of it dry, you can scratch glass, stainless steel and new finishes. And because the dust is airborne for hours after the work, it settles on top of anything you have already cleaned.

A step-by-step plan

  1. Wait for the air to settle. Give the dust several hours to fall before you start.
  2. Protect and ventilate. Open windows if the weather allows and keep the HVAC off while you remove the first layer.
  3. Start high. Wipe ceiling fans, light fixtures, vents and the tops of cabinets and door frames with damp microfiber.
  4. Clean the walls and trim. Use a lightly damp cloth on painted surfaces, testing a small spot first.
  5. Do the surfaces. Wipe counters, appliances and windows. Rinse cloths often.
  6. Vacuum with a HEPA filter. Use the brush and crevice tools on floors, edges and upholstery.
  7. Mop last. Change the water as soon as it looks gray.
  8. Replace the HVAC filter. Then run the system and re-wipe horizontal surfaces after a day.

Where dust hides

  • Inside kitchen drawers and cabinets that were open during the work
  • Tops of doors, ledges and picture frames
  • Window tracks and blinds
  • HVAC vents, returns and ceiling fans
  • Sofa cushions, curtains and rugs near the work area
  • Rooms the crew "didn't touch" but that shared air with the work zone

Protecting your health while you clean

Remodel dust is not something to breathe in for hours. If you clean it yourself, wear a snug dust mask rated for fine particles, eye protection and gloves. Keep children and pets out of the area until the work is done, and avoid blowing dust with a leaf blower or compressed air indoors. If the home is older and the project disturbed old paint or insulation, ask your contractor how it was handled before you start sweeping.

Open the windows during cleaning when the weather allows, and wash or replace the filter on any vacuum that is not designed for fine dust, since a standard filter can release much of what it picks up straight back into the air. The tools matter as much as the effort.
