First-time office cleaning in Greer, SC: what to expect

Booking office cleaning in Greer, SC for the first time? Here is how the walkthrough, first clean and adjustment week work, plus a free on-site estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 13, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • The walkthrough comes first, so nobody cleans your office blind.
  • Decide access, schedule and off-limits spots before the first visit.
  • Expect small adjustments in the first week; prices come only after a free estimate.

The first night a crew cleans your office is mostly a lesson in how your space really gets used. Which restroom takes the traffic, where the coffee ends up, which door everyone pushes with a shoulder. If you are setting up office cleaning in Greer, SC for the first time, here is how the first couple of weeks usually go, in the order they happen.

Step 1: the walkthrough before anything starts

Nobody should clean your office blind. Before the first service, someone from Perfect Services visits, and you can see what a regular visit covers on the Greer commercial and office cleaning page. The walkthrough takes a short while. We look at the layout, count the restrooms, check what the floors are made of (carpet in the offices, tile in the break room, and hard floor at reception is a common mix), and ask who is in the building and when.

Bring up anything that matters to you, even if it seems small. A glass front door that shows every fingerprint, a server closet nobody should touch, a client waiting area that needs to look right at 8 a.m. These details become the written checklist for your site.

Step 2: what to settle before the first clean

A little preparation makes the first visit smoother for everybody. Most of it is decisions, not work.

  • Pick the days and the time window. After hours is the usual choice, but a morning slot works for some offices.
  • Decide how the crew gets in: a key, a code, or someone on site.
  • Mark off-limits spots, such as a locked office, an equipment room or a desk with open paperwork.
  • Tell us who to contact if something is off on a given day.
  • Ask staff to clear desks of loose items and leave cups and food out of the way.

Desks piled with files are fine, we simply wipe around them. The thing that slows a first clean the most is not mess, it is uncertainty about what may be moved.

Step 3: what the first visit includes

The first clean follows the same list as every later one, with a bit more attention because we are learning the building. The scope for commercial work typically covers:

  • Desks, counters and workstations wiped
  • High-touch points such as handles, switches and keypads
  • Restrooms cleaned, disinfected and restocked
  • Break room sink, microwave and fridge exterior
  • Trash and recycling removed
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped
  • Glass doors and entry areas
  • Reception and waiting areas dusted

The team works from the cleanest areas to the dirtiest, and restrooms come last so nothing is carried back into the offices. Cloths and mop heads used in restrooms never touch a desk.

Step 4: the adjustment week

Expect to tweak the plan after the first one or two visits. Maybe the break room needs attention every time but the conference room only weekly. Maybe the carpet by the entrance shows more wear than expected after a few rainy days in the Upstate, when everyone tracks in red clay. That is normal, and it is the reason we ask for feedback early. Tell us what you noticed on Monday morning, and the next visit reflects it.

If your building is on US-29 or just off I-85 and sees walk-in customers, the entrance and glass get extra focus. If you are a small team in a quiet suite near Trade Street, the break room and restrooms matter more. A similar office with a different daily rhythm in Taylors is covered in our Taylors office cleaning page, if you have a second location.

Questions owners usually raise in the walkthrough

Three come up almost every time. First, security: who has the key, and is the crew supervised? We agree on access in writing and keep it simple, one entry method and one contact. Second, supplies: we bring our own cleaning products and tools unless you have a preference for something specific, such as a fragrance-free product for a clinic or a salon. Third, communication: how do you report a problem? Pick one channel, a call or a text, and use it. A short message on Tuesday saying the break room fridge needs attention is much easier to act on than a general complaint at the end of the month.

It also helps to say what a good result looks like to you. Some owners care most about the restrooms, because customers judge a business by them. Others want the floors and entrance spotless for client visits. Knowing that lets the crew spend the extra minutes where you notice them.

Will the crew move things on my desks?

They wipe around items and avoid paperwork. If you want a desk fully cleared or left alone, say so during the walkthrough and it goes on your site checklist.
