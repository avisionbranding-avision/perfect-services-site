What is Brazilian house cleaning? A guide for Duncan homeowners

What is Brazilian house cleaning? Duncan homeowners learn how the detail-first hand method differs from a quick clean and how estimates work.

Perfect Services Team
November 19, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Brazilian house cleaning means working by hand, edge by edge, instead of passing over surfaces quickly.
  • The method pays off in bathrooms, kitchens and along baseboards.
  • Perfect Services gives a price only after a free in-home estimate.

The short answer: Brazilian house cleaning is a detail-first way of cleaning where nearly everything is done by hand and nothing is skipped because it is low, high or tucked in a corner. It is the method our Brazilian team works by, and we use it in every house we clean in Greer, Duncan and the surrounding Upstate. If you have had a cleaner who left the counters shiny but the baseboards gray, this is the kind of service people switch to.

How it differs from a typical cleaning

Most quick-turn services work on a time budget per room. Surfaces are sprayed, wiped once, and the crew moves on. Brazilian cleaning keeps the same rooms but changes what counts as finished. Here is the contrast in the way homeowners usually describe it to us.

AreaQuick cleanBrazilian method
FloorsMop down the middle of the roomHand-washed along the walls and in corners
BathroomsSpray, wipe, rinseScrubbed, including grout and the base of the toilet
KitchenCounters and sinkDegreased stovetop, hood and cabinet fronts
FurnitureDusted where it standsSmall items and light furniture moved, then cleaned under
DoorsSkippedFrames, switches and handles detailed

What a visit looks like from the front door

We start high and finish low, so dust falls onto surfaces we have not cleaned yet. In a typical Duncan home, that means ceiling corners and fan blades first, then shelves and window sills, then doors and cabinet fronts. Bathrooms and kitchen come after that, and floors are last so nothing gets tracked across a wet surface.

At the end, a team member walks the house with a room checklist and compares it against what we promised. That final walk-through is a big part of why the method feels different. It is not a surprise inspection; it is built into every visit.

  • Every surface hand-wiped, not only sprayed
  • Corners, edges and baseboards cleaned by hand
  • Furniture and small items moved and cleaned under
  • Door frames, switches and handles detailed
  • Bathrooms scrubbed, including grout and the toilet base
  • Kitchen degreased: stovetop, hood, cabinet fronts
  • Floors hand-washed along the walls

Why Duncan homes benefit from it

Duncan is a fast-growing town near the I-85 corridor, with many newer subdivisions in Spartanburg County. New homes show builder dust and fine drywall residue for a long time, and the traffic from I-85 and US-29 sends a steady film of road dust through open windows. Add the red clay that comes in on shoes, and corners and baseboards collect more than people expect. A hand-wiped edge picks up that fine grit; a quick mop pushes it around.

Homes with hardwood, tile and grout also need patient work. Grout lines in a kitchen or bathroom need brushing, not just rinsing, and hardwood needs a method that does not soak the boards. If you have been unhappy with rushed cleaning in the past, the detail-first approach is meant for you. Common questions we hear from new clients

People new to the method usually ask three things. First, do we use special products? No. The difference is time, order and attention, not a secret formula. Second, does it feel like a deep clean every visit? Not exactly. The detail work is part of every visit, but a first visit is deeper because the house is catching up, and later visits keep what was reached. Third, does it mean hard scrubbing? Often the opposite. A damp cloth used patiently, and a brush in the corners, does more than strength does.

Another thing we hear in Duncan is that homeowners worry the team will rearrange things. We move small items to clean under them and put them back where they were. If a room has something fragile, tell us at the estimate and we will leave it. Is it the right fit for you?

Brazilian cleaning suits families who want a truly detailed clean and anyone who wants the corners done every visit. If your home just needs a light weekly tidy, standard house cleaning may be enough, and we will tell you that honestly at the estimate. For a home that has gone several months without a pro, we often begin with deep cleaning and move to a regular rhythm after.

Frequently asked questions

Is Brazilian house cleaning only for people from Brazil?

No. The method comes from Brazilian cleaning traditions, but it is for any Duncan homeowner who wants a detailed, hand-done clean. Our team simply works the way we were taught.

How long does a Brazilian clean take compared to a regular one?

It usually takes longer because the work is done by hand and covers edges and corners. The first visit is the longest. We estimate the time during the free in-home visit.

Do I need to be home during the cleaning?

You do not have to be. Many clients give access and come home to a finished checklist. We do a final walk-through against the room checklist either way.
