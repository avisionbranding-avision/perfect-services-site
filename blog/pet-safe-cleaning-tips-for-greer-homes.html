Ingredients and products to be careful with

Not every product that is labeled "natural" is safe for pets, and not every strong cleaner is dangerous when used correctly. A few general rules help. This is general information, not veterinary advice, so ask your vet about any product you are unsure of.

  • Avoid mixing cleaning products, especially bleach with ammonia or vinegar
  • Be careful with concentrated essential oils, since some are harmful to cats and dogs
  • Check floor cleaners and disinfectants for pet-safe directions and dry times
  • Keep pets out of rooms while you clean and until floors are dry
  • Store products out of reach and rinse mop buckets and sponges

Gentler routines that work

Many pet messes respond to simple tools: warm water, mild dish soap, baking soda, microfiber cloths and a good vacuum. For tough pet stains, enzyme-based cleaners are designed to break down the organic material that causes odors, and they are widely used by pet owners. Always follow the label and test on a hidden spot first.

Controlling hair and dander

  1. Vacuum high-traffic areas several times a week with a pet-friendly vacuum
  2. Use a damp microfiber cloth on furniture and baseboards, where hair collects
  3. Wash pet bedding and throws weekly in hot water if the label allows
  4. Brush pets outside to reduce what ends up inside
  5. Replace HVAC filters regularly during heavy shedding seasons

Seasonal notes for Upstate pet owners

Spring brings pollen on fur and paws, which then spreads to couches and bedding. Wipe paws at the door and keep a damp towel by the entry. Summer humidity makes damp dog beds and litter areas smell faster, so wash them more often and let them dry fully. In fall and winter, wet paws and mud mean more mopping, and indoor time means more hair on furniture. A quick vacuum several times a week beats a big weekend effort.

  • Place a washable mat at each door and shake or launder it weekly
  • Keep litter boxes away from the kitchen and scoop daily
  • Rinse food and water bowls every day to prevent slime buildup
  • Check under furniture and along baseboards, where hair and dander settle
