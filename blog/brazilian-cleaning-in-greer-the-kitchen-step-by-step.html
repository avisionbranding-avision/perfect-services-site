Brazilian cleaning in Greer: the kitchen, step by step

Follow a Brazilian house cleaning team through a Greer, SC kitchen: the order of work, the spots most people miss and how to book a free in-home estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • The kitchen takes the longest part of a Brazilian visit because every surface is wiped by hand, in a fixed order.
  • Cabinet handles, the backsplash behind the stove and the edge of the floor are the spots people miss most.
  • Perfect Services quotes prices only after a free in-home estimate in Greer.

Walk into the kitchen of a Greer home on a cleaning morning and you will usually see the same thing: a full sink, a counter that has become a landing strip for mail and keys, and a stovetop that has had a long week. Here is the exact order a Brazilian cleaning team uses to turn that into a kitchen you can cook in, one step at a time.

Step 1: clear the surfaces first

Before any product comes out, the team lifts what lives on the counters: the toaster, the fruit bowl, the stack of mail. Everything goes to one side. That is not tidying for tidying's sake. A cloth cannot clean a surface it cannot touch, and a spray over a crowded counter just moves crumbs around. If dishes are in the sink, we run them or stack them so the sink is free, since the sink is cleaned late in the order.

Step 2: top to bottom, starting high

Dust and grease fall. So the first real cleaning is at the top: the tops of the upper cabinets if you can see a film from the room, the range hood and the light fixture over the table. In Greer, where many kitchens open into the family room, that dust has often drifted in from the whole house, and it settles in a thin gray layer on top of the fridge too.

The hood and filter get degreased next. A hood that has not been touched for a year holds cooking residue you cannot see until the cloth turns yellow. Our Brazilian house cleaning service includes this kind of reach because the method is hands first, spray second.

Step 3: the cabinets and the things your hands touch

Cabinet fronts are wiped with a damp cloth and dried so no streaks are left behind. The handles and pulls get their own pass, since that is where fingers touch the most. Then the light switch, the thermostat if it sits in the kitchen, and the door frame between kitchen and hallway. People rarely wipe these in an everyday clean, and in a home with kids the marks are plain once you look.

  • Cabinet fronts and handles
  • Light switch plates and outlets (damp, never wet)
  • Refrigerator door and the freezer handle
  • Dishwasher panel and microwave door
  • Door frame and the wall beside the trash can

Step 4: the stove and the backsplash

This is the slow part. Burner grates come off and soak while the cooktop is cleaned underneath, then the grates are scrubbed and set back. The backsplash behind the stove gets a degreaser and is wiped by hand, including the grout line where splatter collects. If there is a drip pan or knobs that pull off, they come off and are cleaned separately. We do not scrape hard against a glass top, since scratches are permanent; the right cloth and a little patience lift most baked-on spots.

Inside the microwave, steam does half the work. A bowl of water heated for a few minutes loosens everything, and the wipe-out takes thirty seconds. At the oven, interior cleaning depends on the service and the condition, which is why it comes up in the walkthrough.

Step 5: counters, then the sink

Counters come after the cabinets so that nothing drips onto a finished surface. Granite, laminate and quartz each have a product that is safe for them, and we use the gentlest thing that works. The sink is scrubbed last of the wet areas, faucet base included, because limescale builds in the crevice where the faucet meets the basin. A dry cloth finishes the chrome, and the sink shines without any effort once it is dry.

Step 6: the floor, edge first

The floor is the end of the kitchen, not the start. The team uses a vacuum with an edge attachment along the baseboards and the kick plates under the cabinets, where crumbs gather in a line. Then the floor is mopped from the far corner toward the door so no one walks on wet tile. In a house near US-29 or SC-14 where red clay gets tracked in, the entry side of the kitchen floor is the dirtiest area, and it gets a second pass.
