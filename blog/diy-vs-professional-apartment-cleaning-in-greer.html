DIY vs. professional apartment cleaning in Greer

Cleaning your Greer, SC apartment yourself or hiring a pro? See where DIY works, where a team saves time, and how to request a free in-home estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Small spaces are easy to keep tidy yourself but hard to deep clean without the right tools and an uninterrupted block of time.
  • Professionals add the most value before an inspection, after a move or when the bathroom and kitchen have fallen behind.
  • Perfect Services gives exact prices only after a free in-home estimate.

An apartment is the one place where DIY cleaning genuinely works. A one-bedroom takes less time than a house, you know where everything is, and there is no staircase to haul a vacuum up. So why do so many people in Greer still hire someone? Usually because of a specific moment: an inspection on the calendar, a lease ending, a week of overtime. Here is a practical way to decide which one fits.

What DIY handles well in an apartment

Everyday upkeep. Wipe the counters, run the dishwasher, scrub the toilet and sink on a weekly rhythm, and vacuum the main traffic path. In a small place a twenty minute reset after dinner keeps the whole apartment from slipping. If you rent near Downtown Greer or off Wade Hampton and your lease is long, that routine is worth building.

You also have an advantage over a house: fewer rooms, so you can put the hard jobs in order. One weekend a month, do one bigger job, like the oven or the tub grout, and you will rarely face a big backlog.

Where it falls apart

  • The bathroom. A small bathroom is humid and has the hardest buildup per square foot: soap film on glass, grout in the corners, mildew on the caulk.
  • The oven and the range hood. Baked-on grease needs time, the right product and patience.
  • Baseboards and door frames. Everyone skips them, and they make a rental look lived in.
  • The track of a sliding patio door. Gritty, awkward, and always overlooked.
  • Moving out. The inspection checklist is longer than your weekly routine.

None of these is hard in the sense of skill. They are hard because they come at the end of a long week, and in a small apartment you are living in the middle of the mess while you do them.

The hours add up differently than you think

People estimate a deep clean of an apartment at an afternoon. In practice, with a bathroom, a kitchen with an oven, floors, and detail work, a first-time clean is closer to a full day for one person, because you stop to find supplies and wait for products to work. A trained team splits the rooms between two or more people and works in parallel, so the job is shorter and the results are even.

That speed matters in the situations that bring most people to us. A property manager inspects on Monday. A friend arrives for the weekend on Thursday. A lease ends on the last day of the month, when everyone in Greer is trying to book the same days.

What changes with pets, kids and humidity

Greer summers are humid, and an apartment with limited ventilation shows it. Bathrooms without a window hold moisture, and mildew starts in the caulk lines long before you see it on the tile. Add a dog or a cat and the floors, the couch edge and the baseboards fill with hair in days. Families in two-bedroom units have the same problem from a different direction: crumbs, fingerprints on every low surface and a bathroom that is used by everyone at once. In those homes a recurring visit tends to pay off faster than a one-time rescue, because the buildup never gets time to set.

It also helps to be realistic about energy. Many renters work shifts at the plants near the airport or drive in on I-85 every day, and a weekend spent scrubbing grout is not rest. Spending the time elsewhere is a fair choice, not a luxury.
