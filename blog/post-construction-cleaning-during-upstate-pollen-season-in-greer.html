Post-construction cleaning during Upstate pollen season in Greer

Finishing a remodel or new build in Greer in spring? See how pollen and drywall dust combine, and how post construction cleaning in Greer SC handles both.

Perfect Services Team
November 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Spring pollen and drywall dust are different fine particles, and a job site open to the air collects both.
  • Cleaning in phases, with a final pass after the doors stay closed, keeps pollen from ruining a finished room.
  • Perfect Services prices post-construction work only after a free in-home estimate.

A kitchen remodel in Greer in April has two kinds of dust to deal with, and they are not the same. Drywall and sanding dust is pale, powdery and clings to everything. Pollen is yellow-green, sticky when damp, and blows in every time a contractor opens the back door. If both are sitting on a new countertop on the morning of the final inspection, a quick wipe will smear them together. This article is about how we handle post construction cleaning in Greer SC when the season is working against you.

Why a spring job site gets dirtier

Most remodels run with doors and windows open for ventilation, paint fumes and material deliveries. That is fine during construction, but it means the house has been breathing Upstate air the whole time. In March through May that air is full of pollen. The result is a layer of pollen on top of the construction dust, often on the very surfaces the crew finished last, such as window sills, new trim and the tops of cabinets.

Homes around Greer, from the older streets near Downtown to the newer neighborhoods close to GSP Airport and the BMW plant, are often renovated in stages, and a room that was dust-free on Friday can have a pollen film on Monday. That is why we ask when the doors will be closed for good.

The order we work in

  1. Rough clean. Debris, sawdust, drywall scraps and loose dust vacuumed from floors, ledges and cabinet interiors.
  2. Detail pass. Windows and tracks, trim, light fixtures, vents, fans, appliance fronts, bathroom fixtures.
  3. Final clean. Floors washed in multiple passes, surfaces wiped, glass finished.
  4. Closed-door touch-up. A last pass after the contractors are gone and the house has been closed, which catches the pollen and fine dust that settled in between.

Our post-construction cleaning service can be booked as one visit or as separate phases. For spring work, we usually recommend splitting the final clean from the touch-up.

What shows up that people do not expect

  • Fine drywall dust inside drawers, closet shelves and the tops of door frames
  • Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive on new windows and glass
  • Pollen mixed with dust in window tracks, which turns into a paste when wiped wet
  • Dust inside light fixtures and ceiling fan housings
  • Film on new bathroom fixtures and tile

A bathroom or kitchen renovation is usually a long day for a small team. A whole-house remodel or an addition can be spread over a couple of days, especially with the touch-up pass. Booking this week is possible depending on the calendar, so mention your deadline when you ask for the estimate.

Keeping pollen out after the clean

The best result comes from what happens after the team leaves. Pollen is lightest in the early morning and late evening and heaviest when wind picks up, so avoid propping a door open while furniture moves in. If you have new windows with factory stickers or a protective film, ask the installer whether it should be peeled before or after cleaning, since sun on a film can bake adhesive onto glass and make it harder to remove.

If you run an HVAC system during construction, change the filter before the final clean. Drywall dust clogs filters quickly and a dirty filter pushes fine dust back into the rooms we just finished. It is a five-minute job that saves a surprising amount of re-cleaning, and a lot of contractors forget it. Also ask the HVAC crew to cap open ducts while sanding is happening.

When the red clay comes in with the crew

Spring job sites around Greer have another problem besides pollen: red clay. After a rainy week, boots track it across subfloors and new hardwood, and it dries into a fine orange powder that is easy to mistake for ordinary dust. We vacuum it before any wet cleaning, because wetting dry clay turns it into a stain. If your crew has been working in and out of the garage, check the entry area and the stair edges first.
