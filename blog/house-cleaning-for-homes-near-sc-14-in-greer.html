When the team pulls up to a house off SC-14 for a first cleaning, the first ten minutes are spent without a cloth in hand. We walk the home with the owner, room by room, and decide the order of the day. This article follows that first visit in sequence, because people booking house cleaning near SC-14 in Greer often ask what actually happens once the door opens.

Step 1: the walkthrough at the door

We start at the entry, since that is where the heaviest dirt lives. Greer's red clay stains light floors and mats quickly, and it gets tracked further in if the entry is not handled early. During the walkthrough we note the flooring types, how many bathrooms there are, where the pets sleep, and anything you want left alone or done with extra care.

This is also where we ask what bothers you most. Some people care about the kitchen, others about the master bath. The answer shapes where the team spends its time. We also ask about anything fragile, any room that should stay closed, and whether a pet needs to be kept out of the way. People near SC-14 often have a commute through Greer, so we agree on a window for arrival rather than leaving you guessing, and we confirm the day before so nobody is surprised at the door.

Step 2: dust first, then kitchen and bathrooms

Dust from high to low

Dusting comes before any floor work, and it goes from the top down. Ceiling fan blades and vent covers first, then shelves, frames and furniture, then baseboards and door frames where the rotation calls for them. Doing it in that order means the dust we knock loose lands on a floor we have not cleaned yet, not on one we just finished.

Spots people usually miss are worth naming: door frames at hand height, cabinet handles, light switches and the track of a sliding door. All of them are touched all day and rarely wiped.

Kitchen next

The kitchen takes the most careful work of the day. The team cleans and sanitizes the counters, sink and stovetop, then wipes appliance fronts and handles. Greasy film near the stove is common in family kitchens, so we let the cleaner work for a moment rather than scrubbing at once. Sink basins, the faucet base and the edge of the counter by the stove get a second look at the end, because those are the places a cloth skips on the first pass. Fridge handles, microwave buttons and the front of the dishwasher are wiped too, since fingers touch them all day.

Then the bathrooms, one at a time

Bathrooms are the slowest rooms in any house, so we do them as a block. Toilet, shower, tub, mirrors and chrome all get done, and we change cloths between bathrooms so nothing moves from one to the next. If you have a lot of bathrooms, you will see that reflected in the time and the price.

Step 3: floors last, then the finishing pass

Floors are vacuumed and then mopped by hand, working back toward the door we came in through so we never walk across wet floor. After that comes the finishing pass: beds made, trash emptied, and a final check of light switches and mirrors. Before we leave, the team does one last lap through the house with fresh eyes, looking at each room from the doorway the way you will see it when you walk in.
