How often should you book apartment cleaning in Greer?

How often to book apartment cleaning in Greer, SC depends on pets, work hours and space. See a simple guide and get a free in-home estimate.

Key takeaways

  • Most apartments settle into a weekly, every-other-week or monthly rhythm.
  • Pets, work schedule and the number of people in the unit decide the pace.
  • The exact price comes after a free in-home estimate.

A one-bedroom apartment off US-29 and a three-bedroom condo near Downtown Greer do not need the same schedule, even though both are called apartments. People ask us whether every two weeks is enough, or whether monthly is a waste. The honest answer is that the rhythm follows how you live in the space, not how big it is on paper.

Here is how we help renters and owners pick a schedule for apartment cleaning in Greer, and how to tell when you should move up or down.

Start with how you actually live

Ask yourself these questions. They matter more than square footage.

  • How many people live in the unit, and how many days a week do they cook?
  • Is there a dog or a cat shedding on the couch and the bed?
  • Do you work from home, or is the apartment empty most of the day?
  • Do you have a balcony, a patio door or carpet that picks up outdoor dirt?
  • How long can you leave the bathroom before it bothers you?

Greer has plenty of pollen in spring and red clay that follows shoes into the entryway, so even a tidy one-bedroom collects more than you would think. Dust and hair settle quickly in a small space with less air movement.

That table is a starting point, not a rule. Some people begin with a monthly clean and move to every two weeks once they see how much more they enjoy the result.

Time of year matters too. In spring the pollen season in the Upstate coats balcony doors and window sills, and in fall and winter closed windows hold dust and pet hair inside the unit. Many of our recurring apartment clients keep the same rhythm all year, while some move to a shorter gap during the heaviest pollen weeks.

Moving from one rhythm to another does not need a new contract or a long conversation. We only need to know what you noticed, and the next visit adjusts. It is also normal to ask for a different focus each time, for example extra attention on the kitchen one visit and the bathroom the next, if you cook more or have guests.

Signs the schedule is too light

When we walk into an apartment during the free estimate, a few signs tell us the rhythm has slipped:

  • A soap film in the shower that comes back a few days after cleaning
  • Dust visible on the baseboards and door frames
  • Hair building along the edges of the floor
  • A faint smell from the kitchen sink or trash area
  • Light switches and handles that feel sticky

If you see two or three of those between visits, move to a shorter gap. If you see none and the unit looks the same on day twelve as on day two, you may be able to stretch it. For a better picture of what each visit covers, see our article on how long apartment cleaning takes in Greer.

What each visit covers

A regular visit includes the kitchen counters, sink, stovetop and appliance fronts, and the bathroom toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity. Floors are vacuumed and mopped, surfaces and shelves are dusted, beds are made and trash is emptied. We also do baseboards and door frames and clean the balcony or patio door glass, and we sanitize light switches and handles. These are details a lot of tenants skip when cleaning themselves.

If your unit is long overdue, we may suggest a first visit that goes further, for instance a deep cleaning, and then a lighter recurring rhythm once the space is reset. For people who are leaving a lease, a move-out cleaning is a separate service.
