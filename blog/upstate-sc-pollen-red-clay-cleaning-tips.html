Pollen, red clay and floors: cleaning tips for Upstate SC homes

How to deal with yellow pine pollen, red clay stains and dust in Greer and Upstate SC homes. Hardwood, LVP and carpet care tips from the Perfect Services cleaning team.

Perfect Services Team
October 30, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Spring pine pollen and Upstate red clay are the two biggest local cleaning challenges.
  • Stop dirt at the door with two mats and a shoes-off rule.
  • Clean hardwood and LVP with a barely damp mop; vacuum carpets in overlapping passes.

Every region has its own cleaning challenges. In Greer and the rest of Upstate South Carolina, two of them show up in almost every home we clean: the yellow pine pollen that coats everything in spring, and the red clay that gets tracked in from yards, driveways and job sites year-round. Here is how the Perfect Services team handles both, plus floor care tips for hardwood, LVP and carpet.

Pollen season in the Upstate

From late winter through spring, pine and oak pollen settle on porches, cars, window sills and anything near an open door. Inside the home, it builds up on floors, furniture and fan blades and makes allergies worse.

  • Keep windows closed on high-pollen days and run the HVAC.
  • Change HVAC filters more often during pollen season.
  • Dust with a damp microfiber cloth. Dry dusting just moves pollen into the air.
  • Wipe window sills and tracks weekly in spring.
  • Wash entry mats and pet bedding more often.

Red clay: prevention first

Upstate red clay stains grout, carpet and light-colored floors. The best strategy is keeping it outside:

  • Use two mats: a rough one outside and an absorbent one inside.
  • Adopt a shoes-off rule, especially after rain.
  • Keep a towel by the door for pets’ paws.

Cleaning red clay from carpet

  1. Let mud dry completely.
  2. Break it up and vacuum slowly.
  3. Blot, do not rub, with a carpet-safe cleaner and cool water.
  4. Repeat until the color stops transferring to the cloth. Test any product on a hidden area first.

Hardwood and LVP floors

Many Greer homes have hardwood or luxury vinyl plank floors, especially in open floor plans.

  • Vacuum or dust-mop first. Grit scratches finishes.
  • Mop with a barely damp microfiber mop and a pH-neutral floor cleaner. Standing water damages wood.
  • Avoid oil soaps and steam on finished hardwood unless the manufacturer allows it.
  • Put felt pads under chair legs.

Carpet care between cleanings

  • Vacuum high-traffic areas twice a week; overlapping passes lift more dirt.
  • Treat spills right away by blotting.
  • Plan a professional carpet extraction once or twice a year.

Those neat lines you see after a Perfect Services visit are not just for looks: overlapping passes mean every part of the carpet was actually vacuumed.

When to call a professional

A spring deep cleaning after pollen season resets the whole home: sills, blinds, fans, vents and floors. Then recurring maid service keeps pollen and clay under control. Compare deep vs. regular cleaning to choose the right one.

Humidity, mildew and bathrooms

South Carolina summers are humid. In bathrooms and laundry rooms, that means mildew in grout lines, caulk and around window frames.

  • Run the exhaust fan during showers and 20 minutes after.
  • Squeegee shower walls and glass after each use.
  • Clean grout with a soft brush and a bathroom cleaner weekly; reseal grout once a year.
  • Wipe the washer door seal and leave the door open to dry.
