Winter airbnb cleaning in Greer: indoor season tips

Winter Airbnb cleaning in Greer SC: how cold-weather turnovers, damp entryways and dry air change the checklist, plus how the free in-home estimate works.

Perfect Services Team
November 25, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Winter guests track in more grit and moisture, so entryways and floors take the longest at a Greer turnover.
  • Heating season changes what to check: vents, dry-air dust, blankets and space heaters.
  • Prices come only after a free in-home estimate, based on size, turnover window, linens and restocking.

A winter guest in Greer walks into your rental with wet boots, a suitcase that sat in a cold car, and the heat cranked to 74. By checkout, the mat is soaked, the floor by the door is gritty with red clay, and the bedroom smells like warm dust from the vents. That is the pattern we see in Airbnb cleaning in Greer from late November through February, and it changes how a turnover has to be planned.

What winter changes about a turnover

In summer the work is mostly kitchens, bathrooms and beds. In winter the entry becomes the hardest part of the house. Guests arriving for a weekend near GSP Airport or a visit with family off SC-101 come in through the same door, and Upstate clay carried in on wet shoes dries into a fine powder that spreads across hardwood and vinyl planks. If it is only swept, it comes back as a film on the next mop pass.

So the order matters. The team starts at the front door and works inward on the floors only after the surfaces above them are done, because dust from the vents and shelves falls first. Then the entry gets a proper wet pass at the very end, so nothing is walked across afterward.

The cold-weather turnover checklist

Here is what a good winter reset covers at a short-term rental, on top of the usual kitchen and bathroom work:

  • Entry mat shaken out and floor behind the door washed, including the threshold track.
  • Return air vents and baseboard heaters wiped, since dry air leaves a gray line of dust along them.
  • Extra blankets folded, checked for stains and put back where guests expect them.
  • Space heaters, if you provide them, unplugged, wiped and left in the right spot.
  • Windows and sliding-door tracks checked for condensation, which turns into sticky grime.
  • Coffee station, kettle and hot chocolate supplies reset and counted.
  • Trash and recycling bins emptied and relined, because holiday guests produce more of both.

The standard turnover tasks stay in place: a full kitchen reset, disinfected bathrooms, beds made with host-provided linens, floors vacuumed and mopped, and a restocking check so you know what to buy before the next arrival.

Where winter hides damage and wear

Cold months are when small problems show up. A bathroom fan that has been running nonstop leaves water spots on the ceiling edge. A damp towel left in a closet produces a musty smell two days later. Our team photographs anything unusual during the walkthrough and sends a damage and missing-item report, so you can decide what to do before the next check-in rather than after a bad review.

We also notice the odd things: a cracked ornament hook on the wall, a candle burn on a counter, a blanket that went home with someone. Reporting it the same day is usually the difference between an easy conversation with a guest and an awkward one.

Laundry and linens in the cold season

Linens take longer to dry in winter, and a rental that has a single set per bed becomes a scheduling problem. If you wash on site, the team starts a load on arrival so it is finished before the beds are made. If you use an off-site service, the swap has to be built into the window. Either way, tell us at the estimate which one you use. It is one of the main reasons two rentals of the same size end up with different quotes.

How long a winter turnover takes

A small rental can be reset in a couple of hours when the previous guests were tidy. A larger house after a family holiday stay takes a good deal longer, mostly because of the kitchen and the number of beds. We plan the day around your checkout and check-in times so floors are dry and the heat is on a comfortable setting before the next guests walk in.

If you have a gap of a few days around the holidays, that is also a good moment to ask about a deeper reset between bookings.
