The 10 details Brazilian cleaners never skip in Greer homes

Ask someone who has switched cleaners what they noticed first, and it is rarely the kitchen counter. It is the small stuff: the baseboards, the door frames, the line along the wall where the mop did not reach. A Brazilian cleaning service in Greer is built around those spots. Here are the ten we do every visit, and why they matter.

The ten details, in the order we do them

1. Baseboards, by hand

Baseboards hold the dust that falls from everything above them. We wipe them with a cloth by hand, room by room, because a quick pass with a mop head leaves a gray line along the top edge that you only see in afternoon light.

2. Floors washed along the walls

Most of the dirt on a hard floor sits at the edges, where traffic does not wear it away. We wash floors starting at the walls and corners and work out toward the middle, which feels slow but is the only way to leave the edges clean.

3. Door frames, switches and handles

These are touched all day and wiped almost never. We go over the tops of frames, the plates around switches and every handle in the house.

4. Under and behind small things

A lamp, a fruit bowl, a stack of mail. Furniture and small items get moved, cleaned under and put back in the same place. Sprayed surfaces with objects left on top are the most common complaint we hear about the last cleaner.

5. The toilet base and the grout

The base of the toilet, where it meets the floor, and the grout lines in showers. Both collect moisture, soap film and hair, and both are skipped by anyone in a hurry.

6. Kitchen degreasing

The stovetop, the range hood and the cabinet fronts closest to the cooking. Grease is invisible until it builds, and then it holds dust like glue. A few minutes of focused degreasing each visit prevents the heavy buildup later.

7. Sliding door tracks and window sills

Tracks catch pollen, red clay and dead bugs. In Greer's humid summers they also grow mildew. A small brush and a damp cloth clear them in minutes, but only if someone does it.

8. Corners and edges

Ceiling corners for cobwebs, corners of stairs, edges of rugs. We use small tools for tight spots rather than a full-size vacuum head.

9. Surfaces hand-wiped, not just sprayed

Spraying a product on a counter and swiping once leaves residue behind. Hand wiping means a clean cloth, the right product and a second pass where needed.

10. The final walk-through

Before we leave, someone walks the house with the room checklist and checks each space against it, often from the door and again at floor height, since that is where misses show. If something was missed, we fix it before leaving.

A short list you can use between visits

  • Run a damp cloth along baseboards once a month
  • Check the toilet base and shower grout weekly
  • Brush out sliding door tracks every few weeks
  • Wipe door frames and switch plates when you wipe counters
  • Lift small objects when you dust, not around them
