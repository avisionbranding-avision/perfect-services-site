Start with an empty unit

Cleaning around boxes wastes time. Take everything out first, including what hides in closets, the pantry and any storage space. Open windows and doors for air. Gather a degreaser, a non-scratch scrubber, microfiber cloths, a vacuum with a hose attachment and a mop. If you kept move-in photos or an inspection form, keep them close and match each room to them.

Work top to bottom, so dust and drips land on surfaces you have not cleaned yet. Finish each room with the floor, and leave the kitchen for when you have the most energy, because it takes the longest.

The room-by-room checklist

Kitchen

  • Inside and outside of all cabinets and drawers, emptied of crumbs
  • Inside the refrigerator and freezer, shelves and drawers washed
  • Oven interior, racks and door glass
  • Microwave inside and out
  • Range hood, stovetop and backsplash degreased
  • Sink and faucet descaled and wiped

Bathrooms

  • Toilet scrubbed, including the base and the area behind it
  • Shower and tub grout, glass and fixtures
  • Vanity top and the inside of vanity drawers
  • Mirrors, light fixtures and the exhaust fan cover

Bedrooms, living areas and closets

  • Closet shelves, rods and floors
  • Baseboards, doors, door frames and light switches
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped edge to edge, including corners

What inspectors tend to open first

Property managers often open the same places: the oven, the refrigerator, a few cabinets, the bathroom grout and the floor along the walls. Many move-outs we see have a clean-looking kitchen with an oven that tells a different story, and window tracks that hold months of grit. Spend your time on those spots first. Cabinet handles and light switches are the small things people skip and then notice during the walkthrough.

Check your lease too. Many leases say the unit must be returned in the condition received, apart from normal wear. We do not interpret leases or guarantee a deposit result, but a documented, thorough clean puts you in the best position. For another renter checklist, see our guide for Landrum renters.

Time is the real limit. Move-out day usually brings a truck, a deadline, and a list of other errands, so cleaning gets squeezed into the last hours when you are tired. Give the oven and the bathrooms the most time, since those are the hardest to fake. If you are short on either, split the work: do the closets and floors yourself and ask for help with the kitchen and bathrooms.

DIY or hire a team?

Doing it yourself costs only your time, and a small apartment can be manageable in an afternoon. A team makes sense when the closing or inspection date is tight, when the oven and bathrooms need heavy work, or when you are driving in from another town and cannot spend the last day scrubbing. A few practical habits help. Do the oven first and let the degreaser work while you clean something else. Wipe cabinet interiors before the floors, since crumbs fall. Keep a bag for trash and one for items you are donating, so nothing gets cleaned twice. In vacation homes and furnished rentals, check drawers and the tops of kitchen cabinets, since those are easy to miss. Take a last look at each room from the doorway with the light on, because streaks and missed corners show up best from there.
