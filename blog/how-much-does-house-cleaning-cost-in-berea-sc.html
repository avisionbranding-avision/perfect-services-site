"How much does a cleaner charge to clean a house?" is the question most people in Berea type first, and it is a fair one. The honest answer is that a cleaner cannot know until they see the home. Two houses with the same square footage can need very different amounts of time. This article explains how pricing works, so you can judge any quote you get, ours included.

What sets the price

Cleaning is priced by time and effort. The five things below decide how much of each your home needs.

FactorWhy it matters
Home size and layoutMore rooms and square feet take more time
Bedrooms and bathroomsBathrooms are the most time-intensive rooms
FrequencyWeekly and biweekly visits cost less per visit than monthly
Pets and kidsExtra hair and traffic add a little time
First visitThe first clean is usually deeper, to reach the maintenance standard

Why we do not quote by phone or photo

A picture of a living room does not show the grease on the stove hood, the grout in the shower or how many pets live in the house. A quote based on guesses either overcharges you or leads to a surprise later. That is why house cleaning in Berea with Perfect Services starts with a free in-home estimate. Someone comes by, walks through the home, notes what matters to you and gives you an exact price. There is no commitment, and the visit is short.

How to compare quotes

When you collect quotes, ask each company the same questions. They help you compare what you are actually buying and not just a number.

  • Is the price based on a visit to the home or a guess over the phone?
  • What is included in a regular visit, and what costs extra?
  • Is the first visit priced differently from later visits?
  • How many people come, and how long will they stay?
  • Do you bring supplies and equipment?
  • Is there a contract, or can I stop at any time?

What is included in a regular visit

For reference, a typical visit with us covers kitchen counters, sink and stovetop, appliance fronts and handles, bathrooms including toilet, shower, tub, mirrors and chrome, floors vacuumed and mopped by hand, dusting of surfaces and furniture, beds made, trash emptied, baseboards and door frames touched up on a rotation, and light switches and other high-touch spots. If a company's quote leaves out half of that, the lower number is not a better deal.

Weekly, biweekly or monthly

Frequency changes the price per visit more than almost anything else. A home cleaned every week stays near the maintenance standard, so each visit is lighter. A home cleaned monthly collects more buildup between visits, which means more time at each one. Many Berea households start with a first deeper clean and then pick the schedule that fits their routine and budget. Our house cleaning and maid service page explains the options.

Things that do not change the price much

People sometimes assume a messy house costs far more, or that a house with a lot of furniture is more expensive. In practice, clutter slows the work, but the main drivers are still size, bathrooms, frequency and condition. If you tidy the surfaces before the team arrives, you let them spend their time cleaning instead of moving things. That is one reason the walkthrough is useful: we can tell you what to put away and what we are happy to move ourselves.

Another common question is whether supplies are extra. Ask every company the same thing, because some charge for products and equipment and some include them. It is part of what you are comparing. Also ask whether the same people come each time. A familiar team learns the layout of your home, which makes each visit smoother and often a little faster.

Finally, watch for quotes that sound too low. A price that seems far below the rest usually means less time in the house or a shorter checklist. A cleaner who spends enough time can reach the baseboards, the door frames and the edges of the floors, which is where a rushed clean falls short.
