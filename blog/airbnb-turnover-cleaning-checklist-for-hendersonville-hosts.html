Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Hendersonville hosts

A turnover checklist for Hendersonville, NC short-term rentals: kitchen reset, linens, restocking and photo reports, plus how Airbnb cleaning is priced.

Perfect Services Team
November 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • A good turnover follows the same order every time, so nothing depends on memory between guests.
  • Hosts who live out of town need photo reports and a restock list as much as a clean floor.
  • Turnover pricing depends on bedrooms, bathrooms, linens and how many stays you have per month, set after a free estimate.

A turnover in a Hendersonville rental has one deadline: the next guest's check-in. Everything below is organized around that window, in the order a cleaner can actually move through a house. If you manage a vacation home in Henderson County from somewhere else, the checklist is also what you should hold your cleaning team to.

Hendersonville is a mountain city with a historic Main Street, retirement communities and a lot of vacation homes, reached from the Upstate by I-26 and US-25. Our team drives about 45 minutes from Greer, so we schedule these jobs ahead rather than as last-minute rescues. Zip codes in the area include 28739, 28791 and 28792.

The checklist, in working order

1. Walk in and look before touching anything

  • Check for damage, stains, missing items and anything left behind by the guest.
  • Take photos of problems before cleaning so the host has a record.
  • Open windows briefly if the house smells of cooking or smoke.

2. Strip beds and start laundry

Linens are the longest wait in any turnover, so they go in first. If linens are provided by the host, we use the set that is on site; laundry on site or off site changes the timing, which is why we ask about it at the estimate.

3. Kitchen reset

  • Run or empty the dishwasher and put dishes away.
  • Wipe counters, sink, stovetop and appliance fronts, inside the microwave.
  • Check the fridge for leftovers and clean spills.
  • Empty the trash and reset the bin with a fresh liner.

4. Bathrooms

Toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity get cleaned and disinfected. Hair in a drain and water spots on glass are what guests photograph in reviews, so those get a second look.

5. Bedrooms and living areas

Beds are made with fresh linens, surfaces dusted, remotes wiped, and floors vacuumed. In a cabin-style place with a fireplace or a porch, we check those corners too.

6. Floors last, restock, stage

Floors are mopped on the way out. Then the restocking list: toilet paper, paper towels, soap, trash bags and whatever the host stocks. The final step is staging the house to match the listing photos, so a guest walking in sees what they booked.

What hosts forget to ask for

Most hosts ask for a clean house. The ones with fewer bad reviews also ask for a report. Our Airbnb and vacation rental cleaning includes a damage and missing-item report with photos, and a restocking checklist, so a host in another state knows what the house looks like without driving over.

Other items that slip: the inside of the microwave, the tracks of sliding doors, outdoor furniture, and the grill if the listing advertises one. Put those in your written scope.

How long a turnover takes

A small unit can be turned over in a couple of hours with a team of two. A larger house, with several bathrooms and a full laundry load, takes longer. If your check-out is at 11 and check-in at 4, there is room for a careful job. When both are at noon, tell us up front.

Seasonal notes for Henderson County

Fall and holiday weeks bring more guests to the area, and those dates fill up. Book your recurring turnover days early. In winter, check heat settings and look for wet boots tracked across entry floors. For nearby areas, we also cover Flat Rock and Tryon.

Common turnover mistakes in mountain rentals

A few problems come up again and again in vacation homes. The first is a late check-out that squeezes the cleaning window, so the cleaner rushes the bathrooms. If your check-out is firm, say so in the listing. The second is linens that are not washed because the dryer was left full. Hosts who stay out of town should ask the cleaning team to confirm the laundry status in the photo report.

The third is skipping the outside. Entry mats, porch rails and the first step are the first things a guest touches, and a cobweb by the front door sets the tone for the whole stay. Add the porch to your written scope and the team will include it.

Deciding between a standard turnover and a deeper reset

Most stays only need the normal turnover. After a long family stay, a party, or a month with pets, ask for a deeper reset: baseboards, appliance interiors and the bathroom grout. The estimator can tell you at the walkthrough how often your type of rental benefits from it, and can plan a rotation so each deeper visit lands during a gap between bookings rather than on a busy weekend.
