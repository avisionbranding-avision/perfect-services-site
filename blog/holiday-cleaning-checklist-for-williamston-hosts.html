Three weeks out: the jobs with no deadline

Start with the places nobody sees until the oven is on for six hours. Clear the fridge of expired items so you have room. Wipe inside the cabinets where you keep serving dishes. Sort the linen closet and wash the guest bedding. If you are planning to use the dining room, move the table and vacuum under it now, so you are not doing it next to the casserole dish.

The week before: what guests will see

  • Entry: shake out the door mat, wipe the door handle and clear the coat area.
  • Guest bathroom: clean the sink, toilet, mirror and shower, then add fresh towels.
  • Living room: dust shelves and the tops of frames, vacuum under cushions.
  • Guest bedroom: dust nightstands, clear a drawer or hooks, make the bed.
  • Kitchen: degrease the stovetop, wipe cabinet handles and the front of appliances.

The two places that get missed are baseboards and door frames. They collect dust in a thin line that looks gray once the lights are on and guests are sitting at eye level.

What a one-time clean covers, and what to add

Our one-time cleaning is a full reset of the main rooms. The team cleans and degreases the kitchen, cleans and disinfects bathrooms, dusts bedrooms and living areas, vacuums and mops floors, wipes baseboards and door frames, polishes mirrors and glass and empties the trash. Add-ons include inside the oven, the fridge or cabinets, which are the three jobs most people want done before a big meal and least want to do themselves.

The day before and the morning of

If a cleaner visits two or three days ahead, you only need a light pass afterward: clear the counters, wipe up spills, take out the trash and light the candle. Do not mop the kitchen the morning of, since it will not stay clean for long. Put out a hand towel and a fresh bar of soap in the bathroom and check that toilet paper is stocked.

Small things that show on the big day

A few details are worth a minute each. Wipe the inside of the front door and the light switch by it, since every guest touches both. Polish the bathroom mirror and the glass on any picture frames in the dining room, where candlelight shows every smudge. Run a vacuum over the stair treads and under the edge of the sofa. In older homes, check the window sills and the corners of the hardwood floors, where dust sits in lines.

If you are staying in your house through January, do not feel you must clean it all at once. Pick the rooms guests will use and give those the full treatment. The rest can wait for a regular visit after the holidays.

Pets change the plan a little. Hair collects along baseboards and under furniture, so vacuum those edges twice and wash any throws on the sofa the week before. If guests are bringing children, wipe low surfaces such as table legs and the bottom of cabinet doors, which little hands reach first.

One more tip for hosts who cook: clear the counters before the cleaning visit, not after. The team can reach every surface only when the pots, cookbooks and grocery bags are out of the way, and you will find you have a better view of what is left to do.
