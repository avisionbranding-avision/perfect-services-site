Late November is when the questions start. Thanksgiving is days away, relatives are coming for December, and someone finally looks at the baseboards. If you live in Greer and have never planned a fall deep clean, the easiest way to think about it is as a timeline, not a single day.

Greer spans ZIPs 29650, 29651 and 29652, along I-85, SC-14, SC-101 and US-29. Homes here range from historic houses near Trade Street to newer builds near GSP Airport, but the fall pattern is the same everywhere: leaves and red clay come in on shoes, the heat comes on for the first time, and guests spend hours in the kitchen and living room.

A four-step plan before the holidays

  1. Clear first. Put away clutter, toys and mail so the rooms can be cleaned edge to edge.
  2. Book the deep clean. Do it before decorating, so decorations do not need to move twice.
  3. Handle the kitchen. Plan ahead for oven, hood and cabinets, which are the heaviest after a season of cooking.
  4. Do a light daily upkeep after. Floors and counters between visits keep the reset lasting.

What fall brings into the house

The first time the furnace runs, it blows the summer's dust out of the vents and onto surfaces. Leaves, mud and red clay arrive on shoes and get ground into entry floors. Cooking also ramps up, so the stovetop, range hood and cabinet fronts take a layer of grease that wipes off easily in September and not at all in January.

The fall deep-clean checklist

  • Kitchen first: range hood, backsplash, cabinet fronts and handles
  • Appliance exteriors, with the oven inside as an add-on if needed
  • Vents and ceiling fan blades dusted after the heat comes on
  • Baseboards and door frames hand-wiped
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds cleaned
  • Bathroom grout and the toilet base
  • Entry floors, mats and the area under the shoe rack
  • Guest bath and guest room finished last, so they stay fresh

    Why not wait for December

    Calendars fill quickly in the weeks before the holidays. People who book a deep clean early in the season have more choice of day and can finish decorating after the house is clean. Waiting also means cleaning around lights, trees and packages, which makes everything slower.

    What we look at first at the estimate

    When we walk through a Greer home in November, we usually start in the kitchen and the guest areas: the stove hood, the oven, the guest bathroom and the entry where visitors come in. These are the spots people see first and are the hardest to refresh in a rush. We will tell you honestly if a regular cleaning would do.

    Hosting without the stress

    Set aside one room as the drop-off point for coats and bags, and keep the guest bath stocked. A deep clean makes everything else easier, because upkeep before guests arrive becomes a quick wipe instead of a scrub.

    A realistic timeline

    If you are reading this in the last week of November, think about it in three blocks. This week is for booking and clearing. The first week of December is for the deep clean, with the kitchen and guest areas first. After that comes decorating, with a quick wipe before the first guests. Homes that follow this order rarely need a second big clean before the new year.

    Extra attention for pets and kids

    Fall is when pets shed their summer coats and children track in more leaves and mud. Baseboards, the edges of rugs and the sofa edges collect hair quickly. Entry mats and the area around the door get the most traffic. We note these spots at the estimate and plan extra time for them.

    One practical tip for hosts: do the guest bathroom last and then close the door. It stays fresh until the first visitor opens it, and you avoid wiping it twice.

    Finally, keep a small basket by the door for wet shoes and wipe the floor under it each evening during leaf season. It takes a minute and keeps the entry from undoing the work.
