If you run a small shop, salon or office in Liberty, you probably know the feeling of locking up at night and realizing the restroom mirror is still streaked and the entry mat is full of grit from SC-93. A written checklist fixes that. It tells whoever cleans, whether that is your staff or a crew, exactly what gets done each day, each week and each month. Below is the checklist our team uses as a starting point for office cleaning in Liberty, SC, which you can adjust for your own space.

The daily list: what customers and staff actually notice

Daily work is about the things people touch and see within the first thirty seconds of walking in. We start at the front door and work toward the back, so we never walk across a freshly mopped floor.

  • Glass entry doors wiped, including the push plate and the bottom edge where fingerprints collect.
  • Reception desk, counters and waiting-area chairs wiped and straightened.
  • Desks and workstations cleared of dust, with keyboards and phones wiped only if the desk is cleared.
  • High-touch points: door handles, light switches, keypads, copier buttons and the coffee pot handle.
  • Restrooms cleaned and disinfected, with paper, soap and toilet tissue restocked.
  • Break room counter, sink, microwave and fridge exterior wiped.
  • Trash and recycling emptied, liners replaced.
  • Hard floors mopped and carpets vacuumed, with extra passes at the entry.

Liberty sits in Pickens County between Easley and Clemson, and many small offices here see steady foot traffic from customers who come straight off US-178. Entry areas get dirty faster than anything else, so give them the most attention.

Restrooms: the room people judge you by

A restroom tells a customer how you run everything else. The order matters: spray fixtures first so the product has time to work, then do mirrors, then sinks, then the toilet, and only then the floor, working backward toward the door. Check the toilet base and the wall behind it, because that is the spot most often missed. Look at the soap dispenser too. A refilled dispenser with a crusty nozzle still looks dirty.

If your office shares a restroom with other tenants, agree on who covers what. Written responsibilities save a lot of awkward conversations.

How we walk a space before an estimate

Before we quote any commercial job, someone from our team comes to see the space. We check the square footage, count the restrooms, note the floor types and ask when the building is empty. A small salon with two chairs and one restroom is a very different job from a clinic with a waiting room and three exam rooms. Floor type matters too: carpet, tile and sealed hard floors each need different tools and products.

Picking a schedule that fits your hours

Most small offices land on one of two patterns. The first is a light daily or three-times-weekly visit for trash, restrooms and floors, with a deeper weekly session. The second is a single thorough weekly visit for low-traffic offices. If your doors open at nine, an early evening or very early morning visit keeps cleaners out of the way.
