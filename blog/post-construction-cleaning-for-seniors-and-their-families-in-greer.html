The walk-in shower is finished, the contractor has packed the saw and the drop cloths, and the bathroom looks new. Then you run a hand along the top of the vanity and it comes away white. That fine powder is the part of a remodel that nobody puts on the invoice, and it is the reason post-construction cleaning in Greer is worth booking before you start using the room again.

Why a remodel for an older homeowner needs its own cleanup

Many remodels we hear about from seniors in Greer are the practical kind: a bathtub replaced with a walk-in shower, a wider doorway, a new floor that is easier to walk on, or a kitchen with lower counters. Those jobs involve cutting tile, sanding drywall and often opening a wall. Drywall dust is extremely fine. It travels into the hallway, the bedroom closet and the vents, and it keeps coming back each time someone walks past.

If anyone in the house is sensitive to dust, it is reasonable to ask your doctor before returning to the room, and to have the cleaning done before you move back in. A senior or a family helping them should not be wiping down ledges and light fixtures after a long project. That work involves ladders and awkward reaching, and it needs several passes.

What the team does, in the order they do it

We follow the dust, so we begin high and finish low. This is the post-construction cleaning service in practice:

  1. Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans are dusted first, so nothing falls on cleaned surfaces.
  2. Ledges, trim, window sills and door frames are wiped.
  3. Inside cabinets, drawers and closets are cleaned, since dust gets into every opening.
  4. Windows, glass, tracks and sills are cleaned, and paint specks or sticker residue removed.
  5. Bathrooms and new fixtures are polished, including the shower glass and hardware.
  6. Floors are vacuumed and washed in multiple passes.
  7. A final touch-up pass catches what settled while the rest was being done.

The multiple passes are not extra for show. Fine dust floats back down as the room is worked, so a single pass can leave a layer behind.

How the family can help without doing the cleaning

Often the person managing a remodel for a parent is a son or daughter. The most helpful things they can do before the cleaning team arrives are small and specific:

  • Ask the contractor for a written list of what is finished and what is still open.
  • Make sure the contractor has removed the debris, tools and leftover materials.
  • Take photos of any scratches or damage the contractor should fix, before the cleaning begins.
  • Decide whether the cleaning is for the remodeled area only or for the whole house.
  • Keep pets and grandchildren out of the work zone until the cleaning is done.

We recommend booking only after the contractor has finished the work, since cleaning before the last visit means doing it twice. If the contractor is still planning a touch-up, wait.

The spots people forget after a bathroom remodel

When the shower is the star of the project, it is easy to overlook everything around it. In bathrooms we look at the tops of door frames, the inside of the vanity drawers, the exhaust fan cover, the window sill and the gap along the new baseboard. Grout haze on new tile is another one: it looks like a pale film and does not wipe off with a damp cloth. The team checks each of these before calling a room finished, and the same habit applies to the bedroom or hallway the contractor walked through carrying materials.

In a kitchen remodel the list changes. The inside of new cabinets collects sawdust, the tops of the upper cabinets hold dust nobody sees until a family member stands on a chair, and appliance fronts often keep protective film or adhesive. We take these off carefully so nothing scratches.

How long it takes

A single remodeled bathroom and the hallway beside it is a short visit. A kitchen remodel with the open rooms around it takes longer, and a whole-floor renovation may need more than one day. We tell you after we see the work, and we can split the job into a rough clean and a final clean if the contractor is still coming back.

Yes. Inside cabinets, drawers and closets are part of the service, along with windows, light fixtures, vents, fans, new fixtures and floors in multiple passes.
