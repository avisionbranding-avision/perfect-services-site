The Brazilian way to clean a kitchen: tips for Greer homes

How Brazilian cleaners work through a kitchen in Greer, SC: degrease first, hand-wipe every surface, do the edges and finish with the floor, step by step.

Perfect Services Team
November 11, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Brazilian kitchen cleaning is done in a fixed order: degrease, soak, wipe by hand, then floors.
  • Cabinet fronts, the range hood and the edge where the floor meets the cabinet are the details most often skipped.
  • Perfect Services gives prices after a free in-home estimate.

Open the cabinet door nearest your stove and run a finger along the edge. If it comes away sticky, the cooking grease has been settling there for months, and no amount of countertop spraying will touch it. That sticky edge is where a Brazilian-style kitchen clean begins. Here is the method we use in Greer homes, in the order we do it, with notes you can try yourself.

The idea is simple: soften the grease first, then wipe everything by hand, and never spray a surface and move on. It takes longer than a quick wipe-down, which is exactly the point.

Step 1: clear and look before you spray

Take everything off the counters. Move the toaster, the knife block and the dish rack. The team does this in every home, because the dirt that matters is under things. While you do it, make a mental list of the grease zones: the stovetop, the wall behind it, the range hood, the cabinets above the stove, and the cabinet pulls closest to where people cook. Greer kitchens vary, from older homes near Downtown Greer to newer open-plan houses near GSP Airport, but the grease zones are the same everywhere.

Step 2: degrease the hot zones and let it work

Apply a degreaser to the stovetop, the hood and the backsplash, then leave it. This waiting time is the step that people skip. The product has to soften the film before you wipe, or you end up spreading it. Use the label's contact time as your guide. While it sits, you can move on to other jobs, which is how the team keeps the whole visit moving.

  • Remove grates, knobs and drip pans and soak them in hot water with dish soap.
  • Take off the hood filter and soak it separately, if yours is removable.
  • Test any product on a hidden corner of painted or wood cabinets before using it.

Step 3: wipe by hand, top to bottom

Now wipe, starting high. Hood exterior, cabinet fronts, the wall behind the stove, then the counters, and the stovetop last. A damp microfiber cloth works better than paper towels, and folding it into quarters gives you eight clean faces to use before rinsing. This is the Brazilian detail-first method in practice: the cloth does the work, not the spray bottle.

The details we always include:

  1. The cabinet pulls and the wood around them.
  2. The sides of the refrigerator and the top, where dust meets grease.
  3. The backsplash grout lines, with a soft brush.
  4. Light switches and the outlet cover near the stove.
  5. The sink: faucet base, drain ring and the strip of caulk behind it.
  6. The inside of the microwave and the door seal.

Step 4: the edges and the floor

Floors are done last and by hand along the walls. In a kitchen that means getting down to the toe kick under the cabinets, the corners where the floor meets the wall and the gap by the refrigerator. We move small items and clean under them, and for a sink base we check for drips that cause odors. Hardwood and tile need different handling; our article on how Brazilian cleaners care for hardwood and tile floors in Greer explains the difference.
