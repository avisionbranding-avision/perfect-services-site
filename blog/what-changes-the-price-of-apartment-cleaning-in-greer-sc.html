What changes the price of apartment cleaning in Greer, SC

Apartment cleaning cost in Greer, SC depends on unit size, bathrooms, frequency, pets and access. See how each factor works and how the free estimate sets it.

Perfect Services Team
November 16, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Unit size and bathroom count set the baseline for any apartment or condo.
  • Recurring visits cost less per visit than a single clean.
  • The exact price comes after a free in-home estimate, never from photos.

People searching "how much does apartment cleaning cost" in Greer are often comparing a studio near Trade Street with a three-bedroom townhome off SC-101, as though they were the same job. They are not. This article walks through what really moves the price of apartment and condo cleaning in Greer, SC, one factor at a time, so an estimate makes sense when you get it.

The short answer on cost

Perfect Services does not state a price before seeing the home. We do a free in-home estimate, a quick visit with no commitment, and give an exact price afterward. We do not price by photo and we do not promise a figure over the phone, because two units with the same bedroom count can need very different amounts of time. What we can do is tell you exactly what shifts the number.

Factor one: the size of the unit

A studio, a one-bedroom, a two-bedroom and a three-bedroom townhome each need a different amount of time. More rooms mean more floor to vacuum and mop, more dusting and more baseboard. Layout matters as much as footage: an open living and kitchen area is faster than the same space broken into small rooms with doors to clean around.

Factor two: bathrooms

Bathrooms are the slow rooms. A single bath with a standard tub takes a certain amount of attention: toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity. A second bathroom, a glass shower door or a soaker tub adds to it. A one-bedroom with two baths can end up closer in time to a larger unit with one.

Factor three: how often you book

Frequency is the factor you control most. Recurring visits cost less per visit because the home stays near the maintenance standard, and the crew does not start from heavy build-up every time. A first visit is usually a little deeper to reach that standard. After that, weekly or biweekly visits are lighter work. Monthly visits sit in between, since a month is long enough for dust and soap film to settle again.

Factor four: pets

Hair and dander take time. A cat or two does not change much. A shedding dog in a small apartment can mean extra vacuuming along every wall and a second pass over the couch area. Tell us about the pets at the estimate so the time is planned in, not discovered halfway through.

Factor five: access and parking

This one surprises people. Gate codes, parking restrictions and elevator rules take time for the crew. If the team has to wait at a gate, carry supplies up several flights or hunt for a legal parking spot, that time belongs to the job. Share the details at the estimate and, if you can, arrange a visitor pass.

Why a smaller space does not always mean a smaller bill

Hosts of small spaces sometimes assume a one-bedroom is quick no matter what. Often it is. But a renter who has not had a professional clean in a year, with a greasy stovetop and soap scum on the glass door, faces a longer first visit than a tidy two-bedroom that gets regular attention. Condition is why we like to see the place first, and why the estimate is a conversation rather than a form.

What every apartment visit includes

  • Kitchen counters, sink, stovetop and appliance fronts
  • Bathroom cleaning: toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped
  • Dusting of surfaces, shelves and furniture
  • Beds made and trash emptied
  • Baseboards and door frames
  • Balcony or patio door glass
  • Light switches and handles sanitized

The list is the same whether your unit is small or large. What changes is how long it takes to do it well.

What the estimate visit looks like

People worry the estimate will feel like a sales pitch. It is closer to a walkthrough. Someone from the team looks at the kitchen, opens the bathroom door, notices the flooring type and asks how you want the home to feel when the visit ends. They check where the vacuum cleaner will plug in, whether the stairs are carpeted and whether the balcony door has the kind of track that collects grit. Ten minutes later you know what the team sees and what it will cost. If the answer does not suit you, there is no obligation.

Condo owners and renters have slightly different needs

An owner in a condo off US-29 may want a regular schedule for years, which makes a recurring plan the natural choice. A renter on a twelve-month lease may want one thorough visit before relatives arrive, or a clean timed to a landlord's inspection. Both are fine. The point is to tell us at the estimate which situation is yours, because it changes whether we plan a one-time reset or a repeating rhythm, and the price follows that plan.

Seniors in smaller homes often book a regular visit that keeps floors, bathrooms and the kitchen safe and fresh without the work of doing it themselves. Students and young professionals tend to book less often, usually before a visit from family or at the end of a term. Neither group needs a long checklist of extras. A plain, well-done standard clean usually covers it.

Does a second bathroom change apartment cleaning cost in Greer?

Yes. Bathrooms are the most time-intensive rooms, so a second bath or a glass shower door adds time and is reflected in the estimate.

Are weekly visits cheaper per visit than monthly ones?

Recurring visits cost less per visit, and weekly or biweekly work is lighter because the home stays close to the maintenance standard.

Does a gated complex affect the price?

It can, if gate delays, far parking or building rules add time. Share access details at the free estimate.
