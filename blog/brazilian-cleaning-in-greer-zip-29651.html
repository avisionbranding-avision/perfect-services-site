The first hour, in order

We work top to bottom and back to front, so nothing we clean gets dirty again. Here is the order for a typical visit:

  1. Walk-through. Before the team starts, they look at each room with you, note anything fragile, and agree on what needs extra time.
  2. Kitchen first. The stovetop, hood, cabinet fronts and counters are degreased by hand. This is the room that takes longest.
  3. Bathrooms. Grout, the toilet base, fixtures and the corners of the tub or shower are scrubbed, not just sprayed.
  4. Living and bedrooms. Surfaces wiped, small furniture and items moved and cleaned under, door frames, switches and handles detailed.
  5. Floors last. Hand-washed along the walls, where a mop usually skips, then the open floor.
  6. Final walk-through. The team checks each room against the checklist before leaving.

What is different about the method

It is not a secret product. It is a way of working that many of us grew up with at home, and Perfect Services is Brazilian-owned and based in Greer. Surfaces are wiped by hand with a cloth, not just misted and passed over. Corners, edges and baseboards get a cloth or small brush. The difference is visible in the places you look when guests arrive.

How long and how often

The first visit takes longer because the team is resetting corners and edges that have not been touched in a while. Honest limits

Hand cleaning cannot remove damage. A scratched floor stays scratched, and stained grout may lighten but not return to new. We tell you that during the estimate rather than after the visit, so you know what to expect from the first clean.

What the team notices that others skip

After enough houses, you learn where the dirt hides. In bathrooms it is the base of the toilet and the line where the tub meets the tile. In kitchens it is the top of the cabinet fronts near the stove, where grease builds a film that a dry cloth only smears. In bedrooms it is the door frame at hand height and the baseboard behind the bed. In living rooms it is the underside of a sofa's skirt and the track of a sliding door. The Brazilian method treats each of these as part of the room, not as an extra.

That is also why the checklist is room by room. A checklist keeps the work the same on a busy day and a calm one, and it gives you something to compare against at the final walk-through. If anything is missed, tell the team on the spot and they fix it before leaving.

Preparing the house before the team arrives

You do not need to clean before we clean. A few small steps do help, though. Clear counters and the bathroom floor of loose items, move anything fragile or sentimental, and tell us about pets so the team knows which doors to keep closed. If there is a room you want left alone, say so at the estimate and it goes on the checklist as off limits.

Families with kids often ask whether toys need to be put away. A quick sweep into a basket is enough. The team works around what is left, but surfaces that are covered cannot be wiped, so the less on them the better the result.
