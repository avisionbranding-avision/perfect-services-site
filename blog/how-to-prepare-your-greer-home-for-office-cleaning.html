How to prepare your Greer home for office cleaning

Prepare for office cleaning in Greer with a short checklist for home offices and home-based studios: what to clear, what to lock up and what to tell the crew.

Perfect Services Team
November 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Fifteen minutes of clearing before the crew arrives saves real cleaning time.
  • Tell us which areas are client-facing and which hold private paperwork or equipment.
  • A free in-home estimate lets us plan the visit around how you actually work.

More Greer business owners work from a spare room, a finished basement or a converted garage than you might guess. Some see clients there: a consultant, a tutor, a massage or lash studio, a small bookkeeping practice. When that space is part of your house, office cleaning looks a little different from a storefront job. The crew has to clean the work area properly without wandering into the rest of the home, and you have to leave things in a state where that can happen. This guide covers what we ask people to do before a visit from the commercial office cleaning team in Greer.

Step 1: Decide what is in scope

Before anything else, write down which rooms and areas you want cleaned. A typical home-based setup includes the work room, a client entry route, a restroom clients use, and maybe a waiting corner. Our commercial service covers desks and workstations, high-touch points like handles and switches, restrooms, floors, glass doors and entry areas, and a kitchen or break area if you have one. If you want the family bedrooms and living room included too, say so, and we can plan that as part of the estimate or point you to regular house cleaning.

Step 2: Clear the surfaces

This is the step that changes the visit most. Cleaners cannot wipe a desk that is covered in loose paper, and they should not be moving documents around to get to the surface. Fifteen minutes before the crew arrives is enough for most spaces.

  • Clear desks, counters and shelves of loose papers, mail and open files.
  • Put away anything private: client folders, ID copies, laptops left open, notes with names.
  • Pick up cords and small items from the floor so the vacuum and mop can run edge to edge.
  • Empty personal cups, plates and food from the work area.
  • Move chairs off their usual spot only if they are in the way; we move and clean under furniture as part of the work.
  • Take valuables, cash and equipment you cannot replace out of the room, or lock them away.

One more thing about timing: if clients arrive early in the morning, ask for the visit to be scheduled before opening so floors are dry and the restroom is ready. We can plan the day and hour around your appointments, and we would rather know about a busy Thursday than clean around a client sitting in the waiting area.

Step 3: Sort out the restroom and entry

If clients use a bathroom in your home, treat it like a shop restroom. Remove personal toiletries from the counter, leave the paper and soap stock where we can reach it, and tell us if you want us to restock. Then look at the path from the front door to the work room. In the Upstate, rain and red clay leave marks on entry floors from fall through spring, so a mat near the door helps a lot between visits. Glass doors and sidelights should be reachable.

Step 4: Tell us about the space

A short message before the visit saves guesses. These are the details our team finds most useful:

  1. Floor types: carpet, hardwood, tile, vinyl, or a mix.
  2. Any area that is off limits, such as a locked closet or a child's room.
  3. Pets in the house, and where they will be during the visit.
  4. Alarm codes or door instructions, if you will not be home.
  5. Any scent sensitivities, especially if clients come in with allergies; we can plan green cleaning products for that.

A single office room with one restroom is usually a short visit, and a larger home studio with a waiting area and break corner takes longer. The first visit runs a little longer than the ones after it, because we are cleaning to a baseline. After that we keep to a routine. Wipe the desk at the end of the day, keep a small trash bin by the work area, and set a rule that client paperwork goes in a folder before the weekend. Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be home during office cleaning at my house?

No, as long as we agree on door or entry instructions beforehand. Many clients are out while we work. Clear and lock away private papers and valuables first, and tell us any areas that are off limits.

Can you clean just my home office and leave the rest of the house?

Yes. We define the scope during the free estimate, such as the work room, a client restroom and the entry route. If you later want the rest of the home included, we can add it or plan regular house cleaning separately.

Should I move my furniture before the cleaners arrive?

Usually not. Clear the surfaces, pick up cords and small items, and we handle moving and cleaning under furniture during the visit. Tell us if something is fragile or too heavy to shift.
