Apartment cleaning checklist for Tryon renters

A practical apartment cleaning Tryon NC checklist for renters: what to clean weekly, what to do before the landlord visits, and how our free estimate works.

In a one-bedroom apartment there is nowhere for dust to hide, which is also why it shows. The mirror, the stovetop, the bathroom floor and the strip of baseboard under the window are what a renter sees every day and what a landlord looks at first. This checklist is for people renting in and around Tryon, NC, and it splits the work into a short weekly pass and a slower monthly one so you are not scrubbing everything on a Sunday night.

The weekly pass: about an hour of the right work

You do not need to clean everything weekly. You need to clean the surfaces that get touched and the floors that get walked on. Work top to bottom and finish with the floor.

  • Wipe kitchen counters, the sink and the stovetop. Wipe appliance fronts and the fridge handle.
  • Clean the toilet, shower or tub, vanity and mirror.
  • Dust surfaces and shelves, including the top of the TV and the windowsills.
  • Wipe light switches, door handles and the microwave handle.
  • Make the bed and change the sheets.
  • Empty the trash and recycling.
  • Vacuum, then mop hard floors.

Use a damp microfiber cloth for dusting instead of a dry duster, which mostly moves dust around. In a small unit that difference is noticeable within a week.

The monthly pass: the places renters forget

These are the spots our team checks on a first visit to an apartment, because they are the ones that turn into a deduction later.

  1. Baseboards and door frames. Wipe them by hand. They collect a gray film of dust and scuffs from shoes.
  2. Sliding door tracks and the balcony or patio door glass. The track holds grit and dead insects. Vacuum it first, then wipe.
  3. Behind and around the toilet base. This is the most overlooked spot in a bathroom.
  4. Inside the microwave and the front of the oven door. Splatter bakes on and gets harder each week.
  5. Range hood filter and the backsplash above the stove. Grease builds up where you cannot see it.
  6. Vents and the top of the fridge. Dust settles here and rarely gets wiped.

Room by room: what to look for in a small unit

Small apartments mix every function into a few rooms, so one spill can affect three surfaces. In the kitchen, check the gap between the stove and the counter, where crumbs fall and stay. In the bathroom, look at the caulk line along the tub, which collects soap scum and mildew first. In the living area, vacuum along the edges of the rug and under the couch, since the middle is rarely the problem.

Bedrooms in apartments often double as offices. That means more dust on the desk, the monitor and the cables behind it. A dry cloth just pushes that dust onto the floor, so use a slightly damp microfiber cloth and unplug what you move. Closets matter too: shelves and the floor under hanging clothes collect lint, and a quick vacuum there keeps the whole room smelling fresher.

If you have a pet, add a lint roller pass on upholstery and vacuum twice a week near the door and the pet bed. Hair and dander add time to every visit, which is one reason we ask about pets during the estimate.

Before the landlord or property manager visits

If you have a lease renewal inspection or a maintenance visit coming, a focused 90 minutes is usually enough for a small unit. Clear the counters, run the dishwasher, clean the bathroom top to bottom and vacuum. Then walk the apartment the way the landlord would: open the closet, look under the sink, check the corners of the bathroom ceiling. We wrote a longer guide for getting an apartment ready for an inspection that goes further into move-out standards. If you are leaving, ask about a one-time clean after you box things up, since a floor you cannot see is a floor you cannot mop.
