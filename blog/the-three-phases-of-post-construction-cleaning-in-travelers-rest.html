Phase one: the rough clean

The rough clean happens while the project is still open, usually after drywall, sanding and the heaviest work are done. Its goal is simple: get the bulk debris and loose dust out so the other trades can finish without grinding it into new surfaces.

  • Scrap, packaging and sheet-rock offcuts removed
  • Heavy dust vacuumed from floors, sills and ledges
  • Cabinet boxes and closet shelves emptied of sawdust
  • Window tracks cleared of drywall grit
  • Bathrooms cleared so tile setters and plumbers can finish

Drywall sanding is what creates the finest dust, and it gets into everything, including the inside of cabinets that were closed at the time. A rough clean is not pretty. It just keeps a messy site from becoming a damaging one.

Phase two: the final clean

This is the main event, once painters, flooring crews and fixture installers are done. The team works top to bottom and room by room so that dust falling from above never lands on a surface you already finished.

  1. Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans
  2. Walls where needed, trim and door frames
  3. Windows, glass, tracks and sills, including stickers on new panes
  4. Inside cabinets, drawers and closets
  5. Kitchen surfaces, appliances and hardware
  6. Bathrooms, with new fixtures polished and glass streak-free
  7. Floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes

Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive come off during this phase, with the right tool for each surface so the finish is not scratched. The multiple floor passes matter more than people expect. Fine dust sits in the seams of hardwood and the grout lines of new tile, and one pass only moves it around.

One habit worth copying: before the final clean begins, we walk the home with the owner and mark anything that looks like permanent damage rather than dirt, such as a paint drip that dried into a groove or a scratch on a new window. That way nobody scrubs at something that needs a contractor instead, and you leave the walkthrough knowing what is left on the punch list.

Phase three: the touch-up before move-in

Even a good final clean gets dusty again. Doors get opened, a punch-list repair gets finished, and furniture is carried in. The touch-up pass is a short visit in the last day or two before you move belongings back in.

PhaseWhen it happensWhat it protects
Rough cleanDuring the projectNew surfaces from grinding grit

The touch-up wipes down what the punch list touched, re-vacuums the floors and checks the glass. In a home near the Swamp Rabbit Trail with a screened porch or large windows, we often see pollen and trail dust settle on sills in a single dry week, so this pass earns its place.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need all three phases for a small remodel?

Not always. A bathroom or kitchen remodel often needs only the final clean and a touch-up. We decide at the free in-home estimate after seeing how much dust and debris the project left.

Why does dust keep coming back after the final clean?

Fine drywall dust settles in vents, ceiling fans and the inside of cabinets, and then drifts down when doors open. That is why a short touch-up visit just before move-in is useful.

Can you clean while contractors are still working?

Yes, the rough clean is designed for that. It removes debris and heavy dust between trades. The detailed final clean is saved for when all the work is complete.
