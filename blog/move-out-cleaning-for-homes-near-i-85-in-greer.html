What does the crew actually clean?

Everything a landlord, buyer or inspector will look at in an empty house. The list below is the working checklist.

  • Kitchen cabinets and drawers, inside and outside
  • Inside the refrigerator, freezer, oven and microwave
  • Range hood, stovetop and backsplash degreased
  • Bathrooms: toilet base, grout, glass and vanity interiors
  • Closets: shelves, rods and floors
  • Baseboards, doors, frames and light switches
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped edge to edge

What if the house is not empty yet?

It can still be cleaned, but an empty home is faster and the result is better. Furniture hides baseboards, floor corners and closet shelves. If you are moving in stages, tell us which rooms will be clear on the cleaning day and we will plan around them. Occupied homes are often easier to clean after the last load leaves the driveway.

How long does it take?

A small, lightly used place can take a part of a day, while a large house with several bathrooms takes a full crew longer. We confirm the time after we see the home. If you need it done before a closing, say so during the estimate, because a larger team is often the way to meet a tight deadline.

Can I book this week?

Often, yes, but it depends on the calendar. Call or text early in the week and give us your key date. We prefer to be honest about availability than to promise a slot we cannot keep.

Should I add another service?

Think about what the home needs after the move, not only before it. Some people only need the one visit, and that is fine.

Two combinations come up a lot. A deep cleaning before the move-out pass helps when a home had a long stay with pets. And a family arriving at the new place sometimes books move-in cleaning before the furniture comes, so the first night starts in a clean house. What to do before the crew arrives

The goal is a home the crew can reach into every corner of, so the first hour is cleaning and not moving things around.

A little preparation goes a long way, and it does not take much. Use the steps below in this order.

  1. Empty every cabinet, drawer, pantry shelf and closet, since the crew cleans surfaces that are visible.
  2. Remove food from the refrigerator and freezer, and unplug nothing unless the owner asked you to.
  3. Take down anything you plan to keep, such as curtain rods, shelves and wall hooks.
  4. Take out trash and recycling so the crew starts with clear floors.
  5. Make sure the crew can get in, whether that means a key, a code or you being home for the visit.

Renters often ask whether they should clean the oven themselves first. You can, but it is not necessary. The inside of the oven and refrigerator are part of what we do, and a pass of oven cleaner the night before can make heavy grease harder to deal with if it is left half done. Leave it to us, and mention the condition during the visit.

Keep a short list of anything the crew should treat gently, such as a scratch-prone countertop or a loose cabinet door, and mention it at the visit. Details like that are easy to share and hard to guess.

Landlords and homeowners listing a house get a similar benefit. A clean, empty interior photographs better and feels bigger at a showing, and buyers notice when closets and baseboards are clean.
