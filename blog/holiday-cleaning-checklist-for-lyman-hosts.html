Holiday cleaning checklist for Lyman hosts

Hosting the holidays in Lyman, SC? Use this one-time cleaning checklist to get the house guest-ready, with timing, add-ons and free in-home estimate info.

Perfect Services Team
November 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Do the high-traffic rooms and the guest bathroom first; they are what visitors actually see and use.
  • Holiday weeks book quickly, so a one-time visit is easiest to schedule if you ask early.
  • Pricing follows a free in-home estimate and depends on size, time since the last clean, and add-ons.

The week before a family holiday, a house in Lyman has a short list of rooms that matter: the kitchen, the guest bathroom, the entry and wherever people will sit. Everything else can wait. This checklist puts the work in that order, so even if the day runs short you have done the parts guests will notice.

Lyman, in Spartanburg County (ZIP 29365), is a former mill town along US-29 and SC-292, about six miles and fifteen minutes from Greer. The housing mix is unusual: older mill homes with small rooms and original trim, next to newer construction with open living areas. Old homes tend to hold dust in trim and window sills, while new ones show it on large floor areas, so both get slightly different attention.

Two weeks out: the planning pass

  • Decide which rooms guests will actually use and which doors can stay closed.
  • Clear counters and the dining table, since clutter is what makes a clean house look dirty.
  • Wash guest bedding and towels so you are not doing laundry on the day.
  • Book help if you want it. A one-time house cleaning is easiest to place in the calendar before the holiday week fills up.

The week of: room by room

Kitchen

This is where holiday work lands hardest. Degrease the stovetop and the range hood, wipe cabinet fronts and handles, clean the inside of the microwave, and clear the fridge of old containers before you buy new groceries. If the oven will run for hours, a quick clean inside beforehand avoids smoke.

Bathrooms

Guests spend more time in the bathroom than hosts expect. Scrub the toilet, the base and the area behind it, wipe the mirror and vanity, and put out fresh towels. Check the grout near the tub edge; it is the first place to look dirty in a bright light.

Living areas and bedrooms

Dust surfaces, shelves and the tops of picture frames. Vacuum under cushions and furniture. Wipe baseboards and door frames, which in mill-era homes often hold years of dust in the trim. Make beds last so they stay fresh.

Entry and floors

Clean the entry mat and the floor inside the door, because that is the first thing a guest sees. Vacuum and mop all floors last, working toward the door you will leave by.

When you hire it, the visit covers the full kitchen cleaning and degreasing, bathrooms cleaned and disinfected, bedrooms and living areas dusted, floors vacuumed and mopped, baseboards and door frames, mirrors and glass, and trash emptied. After the party

A second visit after the holiday is useful if you had a large gathering, especially with kids and pets. Crumbs between sofa cushions, sticky floors and dishes stacked on every counter can take half a day to clear. Booking the follow-up at the same time as the first can secure a date.

If this is your first time hiring someone

Many Lyman homeowners start with a one-time visit to see whether a service fits. Put away valuables, tell the team about pets, and say which room worries you most. The team will walk through before starting so nothing is a surprise. If you like the result, a recurring schedule usually costs less per visit than repeated one-time bookings, and our other guides, such as the Fountain Inn holiday checklist, show how neighbors prepare.

Nearby, we also serve Wellford, if family is coming from there. With a handful of tasks done early and the rest handled by the team, the house is ready when the first car pulls up.

How long it takes

A typical Lyman house takes a team a few hours, longer for a first visit or an untouched kitchen. If you are hosting dinner at four, schedule the clean for the morning so there is time for a final tidy and for any smell from cleaning products to fade.

The two-hour version

If the holiday is tomorrow and you have only two hours, skip everything except the essentials. Spend the first thirty minutes on the guest bathroom and the kitchen counters. Then thirty on the living area: clear, dust, vacuum. Another thirty on the entry and the floor path from the door to the table. Use the last half hour for trash, a fresh hand towel and a quick wipe of the fridge handle. Close doors on the rooms you did not reach. Nobody will open them, and the house will read as clean.

Small touches that make a house feel ready

A clean house still feels cold if the little things are missing. Set out fresh hand towels in the guest bath, empty the trash cans so guests are not the first to use them, and clear the entry of coats and shoes. Light a candle only if guests are not sensitive to fragrance, and open the curtains for daylight. None of it takes long, and together it makes the house feel prepared rather than rushed.
