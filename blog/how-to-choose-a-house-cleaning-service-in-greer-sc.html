How to choose a house cleaning service in Greer, SC

How to choose a house cleaning service in Greer, SC: the questions to ask, red flags to watch for, and how to compare quotes, teams and checklists fairly.

Perfect Services Team
November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Ask who comes to the house, what the written list covers and how the price is set before you say yes.
  • Compare services by their checklist and their process, not by the headline price alone.
  • Perfect Services gives an exact price only after a free in-home estimate, with no commitment.

Ask three questions before you hire anyone to clean your Greer house: who will actually walk through the door, what is on the written task list, and how the price gets set. Nearly everything that separates a good house cleaning service from a frustrating one shows up in those three answers.

Greer sits across Greenville and Spartanburg counties (ZIPs 29650, 29651 and 29652), and the houses vary a lot, from older homes near Downtown Greer and Trade Street to newer builds near GSP Airport and the BMW plant. A service that cleans a 1,100-square-foot older bungalow well may not be set up for a four-bathroom house off SC-101, so match the company to the home you actually have. A local house cleaning service in Greer that sees both kinds of home every week will have a feel for the difference.

Start with who shows up

Some companies send the same two people every visit. Others rotate whoever is free that day. Neither is wrong, but you should know which one you are buying, because consistency is what makes a clean feel the same from week to week. Ask whether you get a regular team, what happens when someone is sick, and who you call if something is missed.

It also helps to ask how long the company has worked in your part of the Upstate. A team that cleans in Greer every week already knows the pollen that coats window sills in spring, the red clay that gets tracked onto entry floors after rain, and which side of town is a ten-minute drive and which is thirty.

Compare the checklist, not just the quote

Two quotes can look similar while covering very different work. One service may wipe baseboards every visit, another only when asked. One may mop by hand, another may push a wet pad over the floor. Ask for the list in writing and read it line by line.

For reference, a regular visit from Perfect Services covers kitchen counters, sink and stovetop, appliance fronts and handles, bathrooms (toilet, shower, tub, mirrors and chrome), floors vacuumed and mopped by hand, dusting of surfaces and shelves, beds made, trash emptied, and light switches and other high-touch spots. Questions worth asking on the phone

  • Will the same people clean my home each visit, and what happens when one is out?
  • Can I see the written list of what is included in a regular visit?
  • Do you visit the home before giving a price, or quote over the phone?
  • What is the plan if I am unhappy with something after the visit?
  • How do you handle pets, alarm codes and rooms I want skipped?
  • Is the first visit different from the ones after it?

Red flags, good signs and the detail-first method

A good sign is a company that asks you questions: how many people live here, whether there are pets, which rooms matter most. A warning sign is one that agrees to everything instantly and has no list. Be careful with anyone who will not explain how the first visit differs from later ones, or who is vague about what happens if you are unhappy.

Pay attention to how the company handles small things during the first conversation. If they confirm a time clearly, repeat back your priorities and explain the process in plain language, that is usually how the cleaning itself will go.
