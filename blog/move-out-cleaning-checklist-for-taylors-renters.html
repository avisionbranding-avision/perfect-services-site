Move-out cleaning checklist for Taylors renters

Moving out in Taylors? Use this move out cleaning Taylors SC checklist to cover every room, protect your deposit and decide when to hire a cleaner.

Perfect Services Team
November 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Clean in a set order, top to bottom and room by room, so you do not redo work.
  • Kitchens and bathrooms take the longest and are where deposit disputes often start.
  • If time is short, a professional team can clean an empty home in one visit.

Handing back the keys is stressful enough without worrying about the cleaning. If you are renting in Taylors and planning to leave, this move out cleaning checklist walks you through each room in a sensible order. It also explains when it makes sense to bring in move-out cleaning in Taylors instead of doing it all yourself.

Taylors, in Greenville County (ZIP 29687), is a community of established neighborhoods and family homes. Whether you are leaving a house, a townhome or an apartment off US-29 (Wade Hampton Blvd) or SC-290, the same rule applies: landlords look closely at the condition of the kitchen, bathrooms and floors.

Before you start: set yourself up

Read your lease first. It may describe what "broom clean" or "move-out condition" means, and it may list items like carpet cleaning. Then gather supplies:

  • All-purpose cleaner and a degreaser
  • Glass cleaner and a bathroom cleaner
  • Sponges, microfiber cloths and a scrub brush
  • A vacuum and a mop
  • Trash bags and a step stool

Plan to clean after your belongings are out. Cleaning around boxes means doing it twice.

Room-by-room move out cleaning checklist

Kitchen

  • Empty and wipe the inside and outside of all cabinets and drawers
  • Clean inside the refrigerator and freezer, and leave them empty and dry
  • Clean the oven, the stovetop and the range hood
  • Wipe the microwave inside and out
  • Degrease the backsplash and wipe down the sink and counters

Bathrooms

  • Scrub the toilet, including the base and behind it
  • Clean the tub, shower, glass and grout
  • Wipe vanity interiors, mirrors and fixtures
  • Check the exhaust fan cover for dust

Bedrooms, living areas and closets

  • Wipe shelves and rods in closets, then clean the closet floors
  • Dust baseboards, door frames and light switches
  • Clean window sills, tracks and blinds
  • Dust ceiling fans and vents
  • Vacuum carpets and sweep and mop hard floors edge to edge

DIY or hire a team?

Cleaning yourself saves money, but it takes time you may not have while packing, returning keys and handling utilities. Consider hiring help if:

  • Your move date is close to your lease end date
  • You are moving out of the area and cannot come back to fix anything
  • The oven, bathrooms or floors need heavy scrubbing
  • You would rather spend your last weekend on the move itself

Timing your move-out cleaning

The best day to clean is the day after the movers finish and before you hand back keys. That leaves a gap for anything you notice at the last minute. If you are scheduling a cleaning team, book as early as you can, because move-out dates cluster around the end and start of the month and around summer, when many families change homes.

A realistic plan looks like this: pack and move everything out, do a quick sweep for leftover trash, then clean from the top down. Start with ceiling fans and light fixtures, move to cabinets and appliances, finish with bathrooms, and save the floors for last so you are not walking over a freshly mopped surface.

Little extras that make a big difference

  • Fill small nail holes only if your lease allows it, and check what it says about touch-up paint.
  • Replace burned-out bulbs with the right type.
  • Wipe light switch plates and outlet covers, since fingerprints show clearly.
  • Clean the garage or storage closet floors if you used them.
  • Take out all trash and recycling, because many landlords count leftover items against the unit.

None of this is complicated, but together it adds up to many hours. That is why many renters decide the cleaning is worth handing off, especially when a new lease or a new job is already waiting.

Final walkthrough tips

  1. Do a last pass with the lights on and the blinds open, so you see streaks and dust.
  2. Check inside every cabinet, drawer and closet one more time.
  3. Remove all trash from the home and the garage or storage areas.
  4. Photograph each room and keep the photos.
  5. Return keys as your lease describes and keep a record of when you did.
