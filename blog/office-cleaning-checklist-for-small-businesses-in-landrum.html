Office cleaning checklist for small businesses in Landrum

A practical office cleaning checklist for Landrum, SC small businesses: daily, weekly and monthly tasks, plus how pricing works after a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Split office cleaning into daily, weekly and monthly tasks so nothing gets skipped.
  • High-touch points and restrooms matter most to staff and visitors.
  • Pricing depends on square footage, frequency and restrooms, and comes after a free in-home estimate at your location.

A small office in Landrum usually has the same handful of problems: a break room sink nobody owns, a restroom that gets busy on Fridays, and a front door that tracks in red clay and pollen from the foothills. A checklist fixes most of that, because it turns "someone should clean that" into a task with a day attached. Here is the one we use as a starting point for office cleaning in Landrum, which you can adjust for your own space.

Start with how your office is actually used

Before any list, walk through the office at closing time and note where things pile up. In a salon or small clinic it is the sinks and the waiting area. In a two-person accounting office it is the kitchenette and the carpet by the door. A retail shop near US-176 needs the glass doors and entry floors done more often than a back-office suite does.

That walkthrough is the first thing we do at a free estimate. We count restrooms, check what the floors are made of, and ask which hours are easiest for us to come in. The list below is written for a typical small office, so cross out what you do not have.

The daily list

These are the jobs that keep an office presentable and sanitary from one day to the next.

  • Trash and recycling emptied, liners replaced
  • Restrooms cleaned and disinfected, supplies restocked
  • High-touch points wiped: door handles, light switches, keypads, shared phones
  • Reception desk and waiting area wiped and straightened
  • Break room counter, sink and microwave exterior wiped
  • Entry mats and hard floors swept, carpets vacuumed in traffic lanes
  • Glass entry doors cleaned of fingerprints

The weekly and monthly list

Dust and build-up do not show on day one, which is exactly why these tasks get forgotten. Put them on a rotation.

Private offices and storage closets deserve a line on the list too. A supply closet with an open mop bucket or a stack of old boxes collects dust and, in our humid Upstate summers, a musty smell. We spot-check these on the monthly round.

Common mistakes small offices make

The first is hiring on price alone and finding out later that restrooms were not included. Write down what is in scope: restrooms, break room, trash, floors, glass. The second is cleaning only when someone complains. By then the carpet near the door has a gray path, and the restroom smells even after a wipe. A steady schedule takes less effort than rescue cleaning.

The third mistake is leaving access to chance. If our crew arrives and the alarm code does not work, the visit is lost. We set up keys, codes and the preferred entrance at the start, and confirm who to text if something changes. Finally, think about supplies. Paper towels, soap and trash liners can be stocked by you or by us, and it is easier when one side owns the job.

Foothills weather adds its own pattern. In spring, pollen drifts through every propped-open door and settles on entry floors and window sills, and in wet months mud follows people to the reception desk. That is why the entry mat and glass doors are on the daily list and not the monthly one. In winter, the problem flips to dry air and static dust on screens and keyboards, which a damp microfiber cloth handles better than a feather duster.
