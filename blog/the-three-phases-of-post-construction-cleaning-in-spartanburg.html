The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Spartanburg

Post construction cleaning in Spartanburg, SC happens in three phases: rough, final and touch-up. See what each covers and how the free estimate works.

Perfect Services Team
November 11, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Post-construction cleaning is best done in three passes: rough clean, final clean and touch-up.
  • Fine drywall dust settles slowly, so a single pass rarely gets everything.
  • Perfect Services gives prices only after a free in-home estimate.

Wipe a freshly sanded wall with a dry cloth and the cloth turns white. Come back the next day, wipe the same shelf, and more dust is there. That is drywall dust, and it is the reason post construction cleaning in Spartanburg, SC works better in three passes than in one marathon visit.

Whether it is a kitchen remodel near Morgan Square, an addition in one of the larger suburban neighborhoods or a newly finished house near I-26 or I-85, the pattern is the same. Here is how our team phases the work, and what you can expect at each step.

Phase 1: the rough clean

The rough clean happens while the work is nearly done but trades may still be around. It is not about shine. It is about getting debris and heavy dust out so the final crew can work in a clean room.

  • Remove leftover debris, packaging and scraps
  • Vacuum floors, ledges and window sills of heavy dust
  • Wipe down cabinets, counters and flat surfaces roughly
  • Clear vents and light fixtures of loose material

This pass is useful for painters and flooring installers, who need clean surfaces to finish well. It also gives us a first look at the condition of the space, which helps when we set the plan for the later phases.

Phase 2: the final clean

This is the main event. After the last trade leaves, the team works through the house room by room, top to bottom, with several passes on the floors. Our post-construction cleaning service in Spartanburg covers:

  • Fine construction dust removed from all surfaces, ledges and trim
  • Inside cabinets, drawers and closets
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills
  • Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive removed
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished
  • Floors vacuumed and washed, multiple passes

The Brazilian detail-first method shows here. Edges, door frames and corners get cleaned by hand, the way we would do a regular home, because new baseboards and fresh trim collect fine dust on every ledge. Stickers on new windows and appliances are removed carefully, and paint specks are lifted without scratching the glass or finish.

Phase 3: the touch-up pass

Dust you cannot see during the final clean often shows up after the HVAC runs for a few days. A touch-up pass before move-in catches that, plus anything the movers or the last punch-list items left behind. It is usually the shortest visit, and the one homeowners appreciate most on closing week.

What the team checks in the first walkthrough

Before any phase is planned, we walk the space. We look at the floor type, because new hardwood, tile and luxury vinyl each need a different approach to the first wash. We check windows for factory stickers and paint overspray. We open a few cabinets and drawers to see whether plaster or sawdust has settled inside, and we look at the HVAC returns, which tend to hold more dust than people expect. If the walls were sanded recently, we plan for an extra pass on trim and ledges.

That walkthrough decides how many people we bring and how many hours to plan, which is why a number given without it would only be a guess.

Access matters as much as dust. Tell us where the water and power are, whether there are lockboxes or alarm codes, and which rooms are still being used by other trades. Clear a path to the main areas so the team does not spend the first hour moving materials.

Who books these phases

The service fits homeowners finishing a remodel or addition, builders and general contractors, new construction closings and kitchen or bathroom renovations. Contractors in Spartanburg often book the rough and final clean together; homeowners usually choose the final and the touch-up. Frequently asked questions

Do I need all three phases of post-construction cleaning?

Not always. Contractors often book the rough and final clean, homeowners the final and touch-up. We recommend phases based on the project size and dust level we see at the estimate.

Why does dust keep showing up after the final clean?

Fine drywall dust settles slowly and gets stirred by the HVAC. A touch-up pass a few days later catches what appeared after the first cleaning.

Can you give me a post-construction cleaning price over the phone?
