Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Pelzer hosts

Hosting in Pelzer? Use this turnover checklist for Airbnb cleaning in Pelzer, SC, from the kitchen reset to the damage report. Free in-home estimate.

Key takeaways

  • A turnover is a reset to the listing photos, not just a tidy-up, so the order of work matters.
  • The damage and missing-item report is what protects your review score and your deposit claims.
  • Price is set after a free in-home estimate, based on size, turnover window, linens and how many turnovers you book.

The listing photos show a clean kitchen island, a made bed and a bare bathroom counter. The guest who arrives at 4 p.m. expects exactly that, and the person who left at 11 a.m. left something else. If you host in Pelzer, that gap is what a turnover has to close. Here is the checklist we use for Airbnb cleaning in Pelzer, written so you can hand it to any cleaner or check our work against it.

Start with a walk-through, not a vacuum

Before anything gets wiped, the cleaner walks every room with a phone in hand. They look for what the last guest changed: furniture pushed to a new spot, a lamp unplugged, a cabinet door left ajar, a stain on a pillowcase. Anything odd gets a photo. This takes five minutes and is the reason a damage report is useful instead of a guess.

Pelzer is a historic mill village on the Saluda River, and a lot of the homes people rent out here are older, with real character and the quirks that come with it: original trim, narrow closets, creaky floors that show every footprint. Those details photograph well, and they also collect dust in places newer houses do not.

The turnover checklist, room by room

This is the order that keeps the team from re-cleaning anything. Top to bottom, dry before wet, and the kitchen before the bathrooms so nothing greasy travels on a cloth.

  • Strip beds, bag used linens and check mattress protectors for stains.
  • Kitchen reset: empty the dishwasher, wipe counters, sink, stovetop and appliance fronts, check the fridge for leftover food.
  • Bathrooms cleaned and disinfected, mirrors streak-free, towels counted against your list.
  • Dust surfaces, shelves, lamp bases and remote controls, then door frames and light switches.
  • Make beds with fresh linens that you provide, and fold towels the way the listing photos show them.
  • Vacuum and mop floors, finishing at the door so nobody walks over clean boards.
  • Remove trash, reset bins with new liners, and check the porch or patio for leftovers.
  • Final pass: stage cushions, throws and decor to match the photos.

What the damage and missing-item report should say

Hosts often tell us they only found out about a broken glass or a missing remote when the next guest complained. A good report is short and specific: what, where, and a photo. It goes to you right after the visit, while you can still message the guest about it and, where the platform allows, file a claim within its time limits.

We also flag low supplies in the same message. Running out of toilet paper or dish soap is the kind of small thing that ends up in a review, and it is easy to prevent if you hear about it a day early.

Same-day turns and tight windows

Some weekends in the foothills are full, especially when people come for the mountains, the lakes or an event in Pickens or Greenville County and want a quiet place to sleep. When checkout and check-in are only a few hours apart, the schedule matters more than the scrubbing. The team arrives at checkout, works the list in the same order every time, and finishes with enough room that a late departure by the guest does not wreck the afternoon.

If you have a long gap between bookings, that is a good time to ask for a deeper pass. Our Deep Cleaning service gets the baseboards, cabinet fronts and grout that a normal turnover only touches lightly, and many hosts book it between seasons.

Seasonal notes for Pelzer rentals

Fall and winter turnovers have their own list. Wet shoes mean more mud at the door, so the entry floor and mat get a second look. Heating vents collect dust once the system turns on, and a guest with allergies will notice. In spring, pollen settles on porch furniture and window sills, and in a humid Upstate summer the bathroom needs an extra wipe on the shower walls so mildew does not get a start between stays.

Hosts who run a rental near the river or on a quiet road often get guests who want to cook rather than eat out. That puts more load on the kitchen, so the oven, the stovetop and the inside of the microwave deserve a check at every turnover, not just the counters.

A note on staging

Staging is the last ten minutes of the visit, and it is easy to skip. The pillows go where the photos show them, the throw is folded on the same arm of the sofa, and the welcome note sits where guests will see it. Guests compare what they find with the listing, so this small step supports your rating more than people expect.
