The order we work in when pets live in the house

  1. Talk first. We ask where the animals sleep, what they chew and which rooms they avoid.
  2. Top to bottom. Ceiling fans, shelves and window sills before floors, because dander drifts down.
  3. Kitchen and bathrooms. Full kitchen cleaning and degreasing, then bathrooms cleaned and disinfected. Pet bowls get washed and returned to the same spot.
  4. Soft surfaces. Couch seams, chair fabric and the edges of area rugs are vacuumed slowly, since fur holds on until the nozzle is pressed into the seam.
  5. Floors last. Vacuumed and mopped, working toward the door.

Details that pet households care about

A standard one-time clean includes baseboards and door frames, mirrors and glass, and trash emptied. For pet owners, we add attention to the places animals live, such as the wall behind the sofa where a dog sleeps and the glass on the back door where noses leave marks. Optional add-ons include the inside of the oven, fridge or cabinets. If your cat sits on the stove, that oven add-on may be worth a look.

Greer homes range from older houses near Downtown Greer and Trade Street to newer builds near GSP Airport, and the flooring changes the job. Hardwood shows hair quickly but is easy to clean. Carpet hides it, then releases it when guests sit down. Tile grout around the back door picks up red clay from Upstate yards, which mixes with fur into a gray film we wipe up at the edges.

Odor deserves its own note. A pet smell usually lives in fabric, not in the air, so we focus on what holds it: throw blankets, the edges of rugs and the baseboard where an animal rests its head. We can only clean what we can reach, so tell us if a rug or cushion needs to be washed by you before the visit. If a stain or smell comes from damage rather than dirt, we will say so honestly at the estimate and not promise a result we cannot deliver.

Preparing the house for the visit

You do not need to clean before we clean, but a few small steps help the team spend time where it matters:

  • Put pet toys and bedding in a basket or a single room
  • Pick up cords, medication and anything chewable from the floor
  • Tell us if a pet is nervous around strangers or if a door must stay closed
  • Leave a note about any spot that matters, such as a stain you want us to look at
