Office cleaning in Greer: the kitchen, step by step

Office break room cleaning in Greer, SC: the exact order for counters, sink, microwave and fridge, plus how our free on-site estimate sets the price.

Perfect Services Team
November 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • The break room is the one place in an office where food, hands and shared surfaces meet.
  • A fixed order keeps clean surfaces clean.
  • We give the price after a free on-site estimate.

Ask anyone who works in a small office what they dislike most, and the break room tends to come up. It is the shared kitchen: one sink, one microwave, one refrigerator and a dozen people. In commercial office cleaning in Greer, this room gets a fixed routine, since food and shared hands make it the most sensitive space after the restroom.

Start with the trash and the dishes

The trash is emptied first, with the bin wiped and re-lined. Dishes left in the sink go in the dishwasher or are left for their owners, depending on the office policy we agreed on at the start. We never wash personal mugs without being asked. Clearing first makes everything after it easier.

Counters and the coffee station

The coffee station is the dirtiest spot in most offices. Sticky rings, sugar crystals and drips collect around the machine. The counter is sprayed, left a minute, then wiped in overlapping strokes. The coffee maker's drip tray is rinsed, and the handle, the buttons and the area underneath are cleaned. A quick pass on the cabinet fronts and the cup shelf follows.

Sink, faucet and microwave

  • Sink basin scrubbed and rinsed, drain strainer emptied
  • Faucet and handles cleaned with disinfectant, as they are touched by dirty hands
  • Microwave inside, including the ceiling and the turntable
  • Door handle and keypad wiped
  • Refrigerator exterior, handle and the door seal edge
  • Paper towel dispenser and soap dispenser wiped

The microwave is usually the biggest surprise: dried tomato sauce on the roof and a smell that keeps coming back. A bowl of water heated for a few minutes loosens everything before we wipe it, and a splatter cover helps in between visits.

Tables, chairs and the floor

Tables are wiped, chair seats and backs checked and the floor swept and mopped. Break room floors in a Greer office see crumbs, spills and shoe traffic from the parking lot, so the mop pass goes from the farthest corner to the door. Wet floor signs go out when we mop, and the room is dry before the morning shift.

What about the refrigerator interior?

Inside the shared refrigerator is where policy matters. We do not throw out staff food unless you ask us to. A common practice is a weekly cleanout day, announced to staff, when we discard anything labeled with an old date. This is part of the plan we write at the estimate.

Common problems we find

Three things come up again and again. First, a faucet handle with a layer of film that nobody sees but everybody touches. Second, the gap between the counter and the wall, where crumbs and coffee spills pool. Third, the bottom shelf of the trash cabinet, where a leaking bag has dried for weeks. None of these is hard to fix, and all of them are easy to miss when you use the room every day. A cleaner coming in cold sees them in the first minute.

Floors in a small Greer office

Vinyl and laminate are common in the strip-mall and small-office spaces around Greer. They clean well but do not like standing water, so we use a damp mop and not a wet one, and dry the edges. Carpet in an adjoining area gets vacuumed first, from the break room outward, so crumbs are not tracked in. If there is tile with grout, we scrub it on a monthly rotation and not every visit.

Appliances that need their own plan

Some offices have a dishwasher, an ice machine or a water cooler. Each one has its own routine: filters, drip trays and spouts. We add them to the checklist if you want them covered, and leave them alone if the manufacturer or building manager handles them. It is better to say so at the start than to find out after a missed month.

How often should the break room be cleaned?

Daily in a busy office, two or three times a week in a small one. High-use surfaces like the handle and the faucet deserve attention every visit, and the refrigerator interior and cabinets can run monthly. Offices near the I-85 corridor with lunchtime traffic tend to need daily service for this room alone.

Small habits that help between visits

A few rules cut the work in half. Cover food in the microwave. Label what goes in the refrigerator, and agree on a day for cleanout. Keep a spray and a cloth near the sink so spills are wiped at once. And put the trash bag in before it overflows. These are things every office can do without hiring anyone.
