What a first-time deep clean covers

A deep clean starts with everything in a regular visit and then goes further. The team follows the list on our Greer deep cleaning page:

  • Baseboards and door frames hand-wiped, not just touched up.
  • Cabinet fronts and handles degreased.
  • Grout lines scrubbed in the kitchen and bathrooms.
  • Ceiling fan blades, vents and light fixtures dusted.
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds cleaned.
  • Behind and around the toilet base.
  • Appliance exteriors, range hood and backsplash.

The sliding door track is a good example of where the extra time goes. In Greer, red clay and pollen pack into those tracks, and a vacuum alone will not lift them. The team uses a small brush and a damp cloth, then wipes the frame. Multiply that attention across every window and door and you can see why a deep clean is a different job from a weekly one.

The first hour inside the house

People ask what the team actually does when they walk in. The first hour is mostly setup and the highest-dust work. The cleaners confirm your priorities, then start at the top of each room: fan blades, vents, light fixtures, then window sills and blinds. Dust falls, so floors come last. Kitchens and bathrooms usually run in parallel with two people, since grout and degreasing need soak time. While a cleaner waits on one surface, they move to the next.

Because the team works room by room, you will rarely see a half-finished house. A room is done before the next one starts, and the floors are left for the end so nobody tracks dust across them.

Getting the house ready, and what happens after

You do not have to pre-clean, but a little preparation lets the team focus on the deep work instead of moving things around. Move small items off counters, clear the floor of toys and laundry, and put away anything fragile. If anyone in the house has allergies, mention it; the team pays attention to dust removal rather than moving it from one surface to another.

Expect the day to run longer than a regular visit, and plan to be out of the way or let the team work around you. The next morning, you will probably notice small things: a doorframe that looks a shade lighter, a bathroom that smells like nothing, a kitchen where the cabinet handles no longer feel tacky. Many homeowners then move to a maintenance rhythm with our deep cleaning service as the starting point, so the home stays at that level. Questions to ask the team before you commit

A first deep clean is a real investment of a day, so it is fair to ask questions at the estimate. Ask who will be working in the house and how many people come. Ask which tasks are in the standard list and which count as add-ons, such as the inside of the oven or fridge. Ask how they handle wet floors and where they will put supplies. Ask what happens if you want one more room done once they are there.

It also helps to say what you are not comfortable with. Some homeowners prefer certain rooms to stay closed, or have a cabinet with medications they want left alone. A good team writes that down and respects it. Perfect Services works with a detail-first Brazilian method, meaning each room is finished in order rather than skimmed, and the team is happy to explain the order they will follow.

The people who are most pleased with a first deep clean are usually the ones who told the team their one or two real worries. A mildew smell in a bathroom, a sticky pantry shelf, a bedroom where dust returns every week: those clues change where the time is spent, and the result is something you notice the day after instead of only on the list.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to clean before a first-time deep cleaning?

No. Put away valuables and fragile items, clear floors and counters if you can, and tell the team about your priorities. They do the scrubbing.

Is a deep clean the same as a move-out clean?

They overlap, but a move-out clean also covers empty-home details such as inside cabinets and closets. For an occupied home, deep cleaning is the right fit.
