Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Berea hosts

A room-by-room Airbnb cleaning Berea SC checklist for hosts: what to reset between guests, what to report, and how a free in-home estimate sets your price.

Perfect Services Team
November 10, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Work the turnover in a fixed order, from the top of each room down, so nothing gets cleaned twice.
  • Photos of the finished unit and a damage report protect you when a guest leaves a complaint.
  • Prices come only after a free in-home estimate, because turnover window, linens and restocking change the job.

A guest checks out at 11 and the next one arrives at 4. That gap is the whole game for an Airbnb host, and it is why a written turnover checklist matters more than any single cleaning trick. This is the order we use for Airbnb cleaning in Berea, SC, laid out so a host, a co-host or a cleaner can follow it without guessing.

Berea sits in Greenville County (ZIP 29617) along US-25, the White Horse Road corridor, about 14 miles and 25 minutes from our base in Greer. That drive time is the main thing to plan around when a unit needs a same-day flip.

Start with the walkthrough, not the vacuum

Before anything gets wiped, the cleaner walks the whole unit with a phone in hand. The goal is to find problems while the guest's mess is still untouched: a stained towel, a cracked glass, a missing remote, a smell from the trash or the dishwasher. Photos taken now are the record you will want if a guest disputes a deposit or the platform asks questions.

This is also when the team checks the supply closet against your restocking list. Running out of toilet paper at 3 p.m. on a check-in day is a review waiting to happen.

The turnover checklist, in working order

Work from the dirtiest, wettest room outward, and from high to low inside each room. Dust falls, so ceiling fans and shelves come before floors.

  • Strip the beds, start the first load of linens, and bag the trash from every room.
  • Spray the bathrooms with product first so it can sit while you do other work.
  • Kitchen: empty the dishwasher and refrigerator of guest leftovers, wipe counters, sink, stovetop and microwave, and check inside the oven door.
  • Dust ceiling fans, shelves, headboards, lamps and the tops of picture frames.
  • Bathrooms: scrub the toilet, shower, tub and vanity, then polish mirrors and chrome.
  • Make the beds with fresh linens you supply, then fluff pillows and fold the throw blanket the way your listing photos show it.
  • Wipe light switches, remote controls, door handles and cabinet pulls, the spots guests touch most.
  • Vacuum, then mop hard floors last, finishing at the front door so you do not walk back across wet tile.
  • Restock soap, paper goods, coffee and whatever else you leave for guests.
  • Take final photos and note anything damaged or missing.

The spots that cost hosts reviews

Guests rarely complain about a dusty fan. They complain about hair in the shower drain, a sticky remote, crumbs under the toaster and a smell in the bedroom. In rentals we see, the usual misses are the track of a sliding door, the inside of the microwave, the underside of the toilet seat, and the gap between the couch cushions where a phone charger or a stray sock ends up.

Smell deserves its own pass. Open the windows if the weather allows, check the trash can liner and the bottom of the bin, and look at the dishwasher filter. A listing can look perfect in photos and still lose stars to a stale odor. For more on how the details show up in guest feedback, read how cleaning affects your Airbnb reviews in Greer.

Linens, laundry and tight windows

Laundry is the part of a turnover that most often decides whether you make the window. Sheets and towels take the longest to wash and dry, so the machine should be running within the first ten minutes. Hosts handle this two ways: laundry on site, where the cleaner runs loads during the visit, or off site, where fresh sets are swapped in and the used ones go out. On-site laundry adds time to the visit. An off-site swap is faster but needs a second full set of linens for every bed.

If you have same-day turnovers, tell us up front. A tight window means more hands or a fixed arrival time, and that is part of what we look at in the estimate.
