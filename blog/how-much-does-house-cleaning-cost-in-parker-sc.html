The reason is simple. Cleaning is paid in time, and time depends on the house. The visit lets us see that instead of assuming it.

The five things that set the price

  • Home size and layout. More rooms and more square feet take more time. An older mill-area house with many small rooms and doors can take longer than an open plan of the same size.
  • Bedrooms and bathrooms. Bathrooms are the most time-intensive rooms in any house, so a home with three baths costs more than one with two.
  • Frequency. Weekly and biweekly visits cost less per visit than monthly ones, because the house stays close to maintenance standard.
  • Pets and kids. Extra hair, tracked-in dirt and toys to move add a little time.
  • The first visit. The first clean is usually deeper, because the team is bringing the house up to the standard it will keep from then on.

What the estimate visit looks like

Parker sits on Greenville's west side, in the 29611 ZIP code along US-123 and US-25, and it is an area of historic mill neighborhoods. That matters for the walkthrough. Older homes often have original trim, narrow bathrooms and plenty of baseboard, and the team checks those details first.

During the visit we count bathrooms, look at the kitchen, note the floor types, and ask what matters most to you. Do you want the baseboards done every time or on a rotation? Is there a dog that sheds? Should the inside of the oven be included? Those answers go straight into the number. When we leave, you have a price, and nothing is booked until you say so.

Why weekly costs less per visit than monthly

A house cleaned every week rarely builds up anything the team has to fight. Dust is light, bathrooms are quick, and the floors only need a normal pass. A house cleaned once a month collects a month of grease on the stovetop and soap film in the shower, and removing it takes more hours.

How long does a house clean take?

A regular maid service visit usually takes a team a few hours, and a larger home or a first visit takes longer. The estimate tells you the expected time for your house specifically, so you can plan the morning around it.

One thing we hear from Parker families is that they were quoted a number once and then charged more after the first visit, because nobody had looked at the kitchen. That is the situation the free estimate is meant to prevent. The person who looks at the house is the same person who gives the price, so the number you hear is the number you pay for the work you were shown.

It also helps to think about what you are comparing. Two quotes of different size are rarely for the same job. One may include baseboards and the inside of the microwave, the other only the surfaces you can see at a glance. When you ask for the estimate, tell us what you expect, for example if you want the refrigerator shelves wiped or the bedroom closets vacuumed. We write that down, and it becomes part of the plan for every visit.

If you are on the fence between weekly and biweekly, ask for both. Seeing the two options side by side, based on your actual rooms, is far more useful than any average. Many homeowners pick biweekly and move to weekly in the busy months, such as before the holidays or during spring pollen season, when yellow dust settles on every sill and porch rail in the Upstate.

What is included in the price

A standard visit covers the kitchen counters, sink and stovetop, appliance fronts, bathrooms from toilet to chrome, floors vacuumed and mopped by hand, dusting of surfaces and furniture, beds made, and trash emptied. Baseboards and door frames are touched up on a rotation, and light switches and other high-touch spots are wiped. That is the Brazilian detail-first method: surfaces are hand-wiped instead of just sprayed, and the corners get attention every visit.
