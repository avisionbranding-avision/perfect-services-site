Move-out cleaning for pet owners in Greer, SC

Move out cleaning for pet owners in Greer SC: where hair, dander and odors hide, the order the team works in, and a prep list before the walkthrough.

Perfect Services Team
November 20, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Pet hair and dander collect low (baseboards, closets) and high (fans, sills), so the team works top to bottom.
  • Odor usually sits in a few spots, such as closet floors and lower cabinets, and gets wiped out instead of sprayed over.
  • Move pets out for the day and remove crates and beds before the team arrives.

When a landlord or a buyer's agent walks through a home that had a dog or a cat in it, the stain on the carpet is rarely the first thing they notice. It is the hair packed along the baseboards, the dull smell inside a closet, and the gray fuzz in the corner of the stairs. Move out cleaning for pet owners in Greer is mostly about those spots, and the people who get a full deposit back are usually the ones who planned for them.

What a walkthrough looks for in a home with pets

A property manager checking a unit in Greer, whether it sits near Trade Street or out past GSP Airport, tends to follow the same path: front door, kitchen, bathrooms, bedrooms, closets. In a pet home the eye goes low. Baseboards, the bottom edge of doors, the space behind a toilet where a cat liked to sit, and the floor register where dander collects.

Dander is the part owners forget. It is the fine, dry skin that animals shed, and it settles on window sills, blinds, ceiling fan blades and the top of door frames, not only on the floor. A vacuum pass will not touch it. A damp cloth, used by hand, will.

The order we work in a pet home

Order matters because hair and dander fall down. A move-out cleaning in Greer that starts with the floors ends up with hair on freshly wiped surfaces. The team works top to bottom and back to front:

  1. Ceiling fans, vents and light fixtures first, so loose dander drops before the floors are touched.
  2. Window sills, tracks and blinds, wiped by hand.
  3. Cabinets and closets: shelves, rods and the floor of each closet, where pet beds and carriers often sat.
  4. Kitchen and bathrooms, including the baseboards behind the toilet and under the vanity.
  5. Baseboards, door frames and switch plates in every room.
  6. Floors last, vacuumed and mopped edge to edge, with extra passes along the walls.

Odors: where they actually hide

Most owners assume the smell lives in the carpet. Often it is somewhere less obvious. We see odor concentrated in a few places: the bottom shelf of a closet, the wall behind a feeding station, the grout near a back door where muddy paws came in, and the lower cabinet where a litter box was kept.

Wiping these with the right product removes the source instead of covering it. Heavy scented sprays tend to make a landlord more suspicious, not less. If a carpet itself holds the smell, that is a different job, and we will tell you so during the free walkthrough instead of promising something a surface clean cannot deliver. A move-in and move-out cleaning service covers the surfaces, and a deep cleaning visit can be added for buildup that has had months to settle.

A checklist for pet owners before the cleaners arrive

  • Move pets to a friend's home, a kennel or a fenced yard for the day. Cleaners work faster and safer without a nervous animal at the door.
  • Take down pet gates, crates, beds and scratching posts so the floor and walls behind them are reachable.
  • Empty the litter area and throw away old food bowls and mats.
  • Pull up any pet pads or throw rugs you do not plan to keep.
  • Note scratch marks on doors or trim. Cleaning will not repair them, but knowing about them avoids surprises at the inspection.
  • Leave the oven and fridge empty. Both are cleaned inside as part of the service.

What the team sees in pet homes before a move

A lot of move-outs we see in Greer share one pattern: the owners cleaned the rooms they use daily, and nobody looked at the places the dog or cat used. A pet bed leaves a pale rectangle on the floor. A cat tree leaves a dusty patch on the wall. A crate leaves a ring of hair along the baseboard. None of this is a sign of neglect, it is just what a lived-in home looks like, and our end of lease cleaning in Greer is planned for it. Tell the team where the animals slept and ate, and those places go first on the checklist.

One more tip from the walkthroughs: do not wait until the last night. Hair you vacuum a week early comes back after one more shed, so clean after the animals are out of the house, not before.

Closing day often brings one more worry: a buyer's inspector with a sensitive nose. Even a spotless house can hold a pet smell in the air handler return or the carpet near the garage door, so open windows for an hour after cleaning and let the house air before the walkthrough. If the smell lingers, it is better to know before the keys change hands than after.
