Move-out cleaning checklist for Pacolet renters

Move out cleaning Pacolet SC: a room-by-room checklist for renters chasing their full deposit, with what inspectors check and how to book a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 22, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Landlords usually check the kitchen, bathrooms, floors and closets first, so start there.
  • Inside the oven, fridge and cabinets are the spots renters miss most.
  • Perfect Services gives an exact price for a move-out clean after a free in-home estimate.

The deposit usually comes down to the last walkthrough, and the last walkthrough usually comes down to the kitchen. If you are moving out of a rental in Pacolet, this checklist follows the order our team works in, starting with the rooms an inspector looks at first. If you would rather hand the whole list over, move out cleaning in Pacolet is what we do every week across Spartanburg County.

Work from the top of each room down and from the back of the house to the front door. Dust falls, so you wipe high places before you do the floors, and you finish at the exit so you do not walk over clean floors. Empty the home first. A room still holding boxes cannot be cleaned edge to edge, and baseboards behind furniture are the first thing an inspector sees once the furniture is gone.

A few supplies make the job easier: a stiff brush, a non-scratch pad, microfiber cloths, a bucket for floor water you change often, and a plastic scraper for stuck-on residue. Skip steel wool on rental surfaces, since a scratch on a stovetop or a tub can cost more than the dirt you were removing.

Kitchen checklist

  • Inside and outside of all cabinets and drawers, including the corners where crumbs gather
  • Inside the refrigerator and freezer, shelves and drawers removed and washed, and the rubber seals wiped
  • Inside the oven and microwave, plus the racks
  • Range hood, stovetop and backsplash degreased
  • Sink, faucet and the area around the drain
  • Appliance fronts, handles and the top of the fridge
  • Floor edges and under the fridge if it can be pulled out

The oven and the range hood are where most renters lose points. Baked-on grease takes soaking time, not just elbow grease, so start them first and let the product work while you do other tasks.

Bathrooms

Scrub the toilet base and the area behind it, the shower or tub, grout lines, glass doors and the vanity, including inside the drawers. Wipe mirrors and chrome last so they finish streak-free. Hard-water spots on glass need a slow soak. If grout has gone dark, a stiff brush and patience do more than any spray.

Bedrooms, closets and living areas

  • Closet shelves, rods and floors cleared and wiped
  • Baseboards, doors, door frames and light switches wiped
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds dusted and cleaned
  • Ceiling fan blades and light fixtures dusted
  • Vents and returns vacuumed
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped edge to edge, carpet vacuumed in lines

Many older homes in Pacolet, a historic mill town on the Pacolet River, have wooden window frames and deep tracks where grit collects. Use a small brush and a damp cloth, since a vacuum alone does not lift what has been packed in for years.

Carpet, walls and the small things

Vacuum carpets in slow, overlapping lines, then go back over high-traffic paths and the spots where furniture stood. Look at walls for scuffs near door frames and light switches, and wipe them with a damp cloth before you decide whether anything needs touch-up paint, which your lease may or may not allow you to do. Clean the inside of the washer and dryer if they came with the unit, take down curtain rods you installed and fill small nail holes only if the lease allows it. These are minor items, but together they decide whether the walkthrough feels easy.

What a landlord or manager actually checks

We see the same short list again and again: the oven, the inside of the fridge, bathroom grout, baseboards and the floors under where furniture sat. If those five areas are clean, the rest of the house usually passes. Take photos of every room when you finish, with a date, in case the deposit is questioned later. Keep your lease handy so you can match the cleaning standard it describes.
