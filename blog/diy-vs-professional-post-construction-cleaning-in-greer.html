DIY vs. professional post-construction cleaning in Greer

After a remodel in Greer, SC, can you clean up the dust yourself? Compare DIY and professional post-construction cleaning, with a free in-home estimate.

The contractor hands you the keys and says the job is done. You walk in and the floor looks swept, but a finger run along the windowsill comes back gray. Post-construction dust is a different material from ordinary house dust, and that is the central point when deciding between doing it yourself and hiring a team for post-construction cleaning in Greer.

Why construction dust is different

Drywall dust, sanding dust and cut tile leave particles so fine they pass through a normal vacuum filter and settle again an hour later. They are also mildly abrasive. Wiping them dry with a cloth drags them across paint and glass, and a dust left on a new floor can scratch it. A builder's final sweep rarely goes beyond big debris, and that is a fair split of the work: builders build, cleaners clean.

What a DIY clean really takes

  • A vacuum with a sealed or HEPA filter, and spare bags
  • Microfiber cloths by the dozen, since they fill with dust quickly
  • A ladder for fans, light fixtures and the tops of cabinets
  • Plastic scrapers for paint specks and sticker residue
  • Several passes, because dust settles overnight
  • Time, often two or three full days for a kitchen and bathroom remodel

It can be done. A small project like a new vanity or a single repainted room is a good fit for a weekend. A full kitchen remodel or an addition is where the hours multiply, especially once you start on vents, tracks and the inside of cabinets.

What a professional team does differently

A post-construction crew works in rounds. The first pass removes debris and heavy dust from top to bottom. The second wipes every surface, inside cabinets and closets included, and handles windows, glass, tracks and sills. Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive come off with the right scraper and solvent. Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans are cleaned, bathrooms and new fixtures polished, and floors are vacuumed and washed more than once. Then comes a final touch-up pass before move-in, once the dust has settled.

Why that last pass matters

Dust you cannot see in the morning appears in the afternoon light. A second look a day later catches what the first missed. This is the part most DIY cleaners skip, and it is what distinguishes a finished-looking house from a pretty-good one.

What the walkthrough shows us

At the estimate we look at the rooms the work touched and the ones it did not, because dust travels. A bathroom remodel in the back of a Greer house often leaves a gray film on the hallway floor and the bedroom closet doors. We check the HVAC returns, since a system that ran during sanding carries dust in its filter, and we point out when the filter itself should be changed. We also ask what floors were installed: new hardwood needs a gentle product, tile grout needs time to cure before heavy wet cleaning, and sealed concrete is easy. Those details change the plan more than the square footage does.

Safety and old houses

Older homes near Downtown Greer may have been painted many times. If a remodel disturbed old paint or insulation, ask your builder about testing before anyone sweeps. Cleaners are not testers, and we would rather tell you to check than guess. For ordinary construction dust, normal cleaning with proper equipment is appropriate.

Timing it with your builder

Ask the contractor when the last trade will finish. Cleaning before the painter touches up or before the flooring is installed means doing the job twice. If you are working with a builder around Greer, the best time to book is as final inspections are scheduled, a few days before move-in. If a punch list is still open, tell us and we will plan a return visit.

A realistic way to split the work

Some clients do the first step themselves: they pick up debris, break down boxes, pull protective film and remove trash. That is a real help and keeps the cost sensible. The fine-dust passes, the glass, the cabinet interiors and the finishing details are what we take on. Dividing the job this way is sensible, and it is easy to plan at the free estimate.

Frequently asked questions

Can I clean construction dust myself?

For a small project, yes, with a good vacuum and many microfiber cloths. Large remodels usually need several passes.

When should post-construction cleaning be done?

After the last trade leaves and before you move furniture back in, with a final touch-up once dust settles.

Do you remove paint splatter and stickers?

Yes, paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive are part of the checklist, taking care not to scratch glass or finishes.
