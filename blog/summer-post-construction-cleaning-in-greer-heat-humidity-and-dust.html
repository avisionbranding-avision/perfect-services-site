Summer post-construction cleaning in Greer: heat and dust

Summer post construction cleaning in Greer, SC: how heat, humidity and AC spread drywall dust, a step-by-step plan and a free in-home estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 24, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • In summer, humid air makes drywall dust cling and the running AC pulls it into the ducts, so vents matter as much as surfaces.
  • Cleaning goes top to bottom in multiple passes, with floors last.
  • The price is set after a free in-home estimate, based on size, dust level, phases and deadline.

The drywall sanding finished on a Tuesday, the HVAC stayed on all week because it was ninety-five degrees, and by Friday the fine white dust was in places the crew never touched: the top of the refrigerator, a bedroom nightstand, the inside of a closet down the hall. That is the pattern we walk into during summer post construction cleaning in Greer, SC, and it explains why a hot-weather remodel needs its own plan.

Why summer makes construction dust worse

Two summer habits turn a normal mess into a whole-house mess. The air conditioner runs constantly, so every return vent pulls dust out of the work area and pushes it through the house. And the humid Upstate air gives that dust a little moisture, so it clings to surfaces and smears into a film when wiped with a dry cloth.

Open doors and windows do not help either. In winter a crew might seal doors with plastic and keep the heat off; in July the house is closed and cooled, so the ducts become the highway for dust.

Before we clean: the walkthrough

The first hour of a job is a walkthrough. The team checks where the work happened, whether the ducts were covered during sanding, how much dust is on the high surfaces, and what is still stuck to glass and fixtures. We look for paint specks on trim, adhesive left from stickers on new windows, and a gray haze on dark floors.

If you want to know what to expect from the full service, our page on post construction cleaning in Greer lists the rooms and the order of work. Here is how a summer job usually runs.

The step-by-step order

  1. Rough clean: debris, large dust and protective coverings come off, and the work area is vacuumed with a HEPA-style filter where the crew has one.
  2. High to low: ceiling fans, light fixtures, vents and the tops of cabinets get wiped before anything on the floor.
  3. Cabinets and closets: interiors of drawers and shelves, since dust settles inside even closed doors.
  4. Glass and tracks: windows, sills and sliding-door tracks, with razor-safe tools on paint specks.
  5. Bathrooms and fixtures: new faucets, tile and glass polished, with grout haze lifted by hand.
  6. Floors, multiple passes: vacuum first, then wash, then a second look under good light.
  7. Touch-up pass: a final walk-through before you move furniture back in.

What to do with the AC

Ask your contractor to change or cover filters during sanding, and replace the air filter once the work is done. If the system ran during the dusty phase, consider an HVAC professional for the ducts; that is outside what we do, but we will tell you honestly when we see heavy residue at the vents. We clean the vent covers and surrounding surfaces so dust does not blow back out right after we leave.

Questions to ask your contractor first

A good handoff makes the cleaning faster. Before the final day, ask your contractor three things. Did anyone cover the vents while sanding? Is there leftover material (tile boxes, trim, paint) that should leave the house before cleaning starts? And are there any surfaces, like unsealed stone or fresh caulk, that should not be touched for a day or two? The answers shape the plan. Fresh paint needs time to cure, and caulk should dry before anyone wipes near it. Telling us in advance keeps the team from damaging the new work.

It also helps to know who is responsible for hauling away debris. Cleaning teams clean; we do not remove drywall scraps, packaging or large materials, so those should be gone before we arrive. A house where the crew can see the floor and reach the walls is a house we can finish in one visit. In summer, parked trucks, open garage doors and propped doors let in pollen and yard dust, so ask the crew to close up when they leave for the day. Small habits like these save an extra pass at the end.

Frequently asked questions

Why is there still dust after the contractor cleaned?

Drywall dust is extremely fine. Contractors usually sweep and vacuum the work area, but dust remains on high surfaces, in vents and inside cabinets. A dedicated cleaning with several passes removes what a general sweep leaves behind.

Does running the air conditioner during a remodel spread dust?

Yes. The system pulls dust in through returns and pushes it through the house. Covering vents and changing filters during sanding helps, and we clean the vent covers and nearby surfaces afterward.

Can post-construction cleaning be done in more than one visit?

Yes. Many Greer projects use a rough clean after demolition or sanding, then a final clean, then a touch-up before move-in. The phases can be booked together after the free estimate.
