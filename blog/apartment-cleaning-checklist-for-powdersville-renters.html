The apartment cleaning checklist, in the order that saves time

Work in this sequence and you will never clean the same spot twice: top to bottom, dry to wet, and the floor last. In an apartment that usually means:

  1. Open a window or door and take out the trash.
  2. Dust high surfaces and shelves.
  3. Do the kitchen.
  4. Do the bathroom.
  5. Wipe doors, switches and glass.
  6. Vacuum, then mop, ending at the front door.

Kitchen checklist

Kitchens in apartments are small, so grease and crumbs land on everything. Do the kitchen before the bathroom, while your cloths are still clean.

  • Wipe counters, the sink and the faucet
  • Clean the stovetop and the front of the oven, range hood and dishwasher
  • Wipe cabinet fronts and the handles you touch daily
  • Clean the inside of the microwave
  • Empty the fridge of old items and wipe the shelf edges
  • Sweep under the toaster and the appliances you can slide

Bathroom checklist

Apartment bathrooms often have only a fan and a small window, so soap film and moisture hang around. Clean from the top down.

  • Spray the shower and tub, then wipe the mirror and fixtures while it sits
  • Scrub the shower, tub, toilet, including the base, and the vanity
  • Wipe the faucet and the handles, where water spots show
  • Clean the mirror last, with a dry cloth to avoid streaks
  • Wash the bath mat and empty the trash

Living room, bedroom and entry

These rooms are mostly dusting and floors, but a few spots get ignored for months:

  • Baseboards and door frames
  • Balcony or patio door glass, and the track it slides in
  • Light switches, thermostat and door handles
  • Tops of the fridge, shelves and the TV stand
  • Under the bed and behind the couch
  • Beds made and the trash emptied

A few habits keep the list short. Wipe the stovetop right after you cook, squeegee the shower glass after the last shower of the day, and keep a small bin by the door for the mail and packaging that otherwise piles up on the counter. Shoes off at the door cuts the grit on the floor by a lot, and a doormat on each side of the entry catches most of the rest. Five minutes a day on these habits makes the weekly clean feel like a touch-up instead of a project.

Renters also tend to forget the things the lease says they are responsible for. Blinds, window sills and the inside of the oven are the usual ones. Look at your lease or the move-in condition report, so you know which of those count before an inspection.
