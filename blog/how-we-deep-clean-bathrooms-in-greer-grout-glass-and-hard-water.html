How we deep clean bathrooms in Greer: grout, glass and hard water

Bathroom deep cleaning in Greer, SC: how the team handles grout lines, shower glass, hard water spots and the toilet base, and what to do between visits.

Perfect Services Team
November 11, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Grout and hard water are the slowest jobs in a bathroom and the reason it takes longer than other rooms.
  • Different spots need different products, so the team matches the cleaner to the surface.
  • The price comes after a free in-home estimate, where we see the bathrooms first.

The bathroom the team spends the most time in is almost never the one that looks worst. It is the one with grout that has darkened, a shower door clouded by mineral spots and a toilet base nobody has reached behind in a year. Here is how we work through a bathroom in a deep clean, in the order we do it.

The same steps apply to a hall bath in an older Greer house and a double-vanity primary bath in a newer one, though the new ones take longer because there is simply more surface. Our deep cleaning in Greer treats each bathroom as its own job with its own checklist.

The walkthrough comes first.

Before any product comes out, we look. We check the grout color in the shower and on the floor, how the glass looks in side light, whether the caulk line is intact, and what is under the vanity. The walkthrough tells us what we are dealing with: soap film, mineral spots, mildew or something that needs repair rather than cleaning.

Cracked or peeling caulk is a good example. Cleaning it will not fix it, and if you see black marks under the surface of the caulk, that usually means the caulk needs replacing. We will tell you so, rather than scrub it forever.

Grout: the slow part

Grout is porous, so it holds soap, skin oil and moisture. Scrubbing it is stubborn work done by hand with a stiff brush, line by line. It is slow because there is no shortcut, only patience and a brush. We apply cleaner first and let it sit, then scrub along the line rather than across it, and rinse well. Floor grout around the toilet and in front of the vanity gets the same treatment, because that is where dirt collects most.

If grout is stained rather than dirty, cleaning improves it but may not return the original color. We set those expectations at the estimate, so the result is never a surprise.

Glass, chrome and hard water

Shower doors tell you more about a bathroom than any other surface, because the glass shows every streak and spot in side light.

Hard water leaves a chalky layer of mineral on glass and chrome that ordinary spray does not cut. It needs an acidic or purpose-made product, left on the surface for a few minutes, then wiped and buffed. Faucet bases and shower heads are where the build-up is thickest. Wipe chrome dry afterward, with a soft cloth rather than paper towel, which can leave lint behind. It is the water drying on chrome that leaves marks, because it is the water drying on it that leaves marks.

A different surface needs a different approach. Natural stone does not tolerate acidic cleaners at all, and some finishes scratch under abrasive pads. This is why a deep clean is not one bottle for the whole room, and why the walkthrough comes first.

Bathroom deep clean checklist

  • Exhaust fan cover and vent dusted
  • Mirror, light fixtures and the top of the door frame wiped
  • Shower walls, floor and glass scrubbed and descaled
  • Grout lines scrubbed in the shower and on the floor
  • Faucets, handles and drain covers detailed
  • Toilet cleaned inside and out, including behind and around the base
  • Vanity top, sink and the inside of drawers wiped
  • Baseboards and door frames hand-wiped
  • Floor vacuumed and mopped by hand, corner to corner

Between visits: what keeps a bathroom clean

Humid Upstate summers are the hardest on bathrooms, because moisture stays in the air longer and mildew shows up first in grout and silicone. Running the fan well after a shower helps more than any cleaner.

Frequently asked questions

Can you remove hard water stains from shower glass?

Light and moderate mineral spots usually come off with the right product and time. Etched glass, where the minerals have damaged the surface, can improve but may not look new.

Do you scrub grout by hand?

Yes. Grout lines in the kitchen and bathrooms are scrubbed as part of a deep clean, with a cleaner left to sit first. Stained grout may stay slightly discolored even when clean.
