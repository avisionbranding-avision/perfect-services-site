Why larger Reidville homes need a different plan

Reidville sits in Spartanburg County (ZIP 29375), about nine miles southeast of our base in Greer, along SC-296 and SC-290. It is a growing community with larger lots and newer family homes, and that shapes a holiday cleaning list. Open kitchens flow into family rooms, ceilings run high, and guests tend to use the front door, the garage entry and the back patio door, all in one afternoon.

More space means more floor to vacuum and mop, more glass to polish and more guest-facing rooms. It also means the work adds up faster than most hosts expect. A home that takes an hour of quick tidying on a normal weekend can take a full day to get truly guest-ready.

The pre-holiday checklist

Whether the professional visit is booked or not, walk through the house with this list. It is the order the team follows when we reset a home for a holiday:

  • Oven and range hood: clean these before you start cooking, not after, because a greasy oven smokes the first time it runs hot.
  • Refrigerator: toss expired items and wipe the shelves so holiday leftovers have room.
  • Dining table and chairs: clean chair legs, the underside of the table edge and the buffet or sideboard.
  • Guest bedroom: wash bedding, dust the headboard, nightstands and lamp bases, and check the closet floor.
  • Guest bathroom: scrub the tub or shower, polish the faucet, and clear the exhaust fan cover of dust.
  • Entry and garage door: shake out mats, wipe the door handles and light switches, and clean the glass.
  • Floors last: vacuum and mop after everything above, so you do not track dust back across clean tile or hardwood.

If the deep work is done, the morning of the gathering is calm. Run the dishwasher, wipe the kitchen counters, set out clean towels, empty the trash cans and open the blinds. Give the entry floor one last pass. That is genuinely all most homes need.
