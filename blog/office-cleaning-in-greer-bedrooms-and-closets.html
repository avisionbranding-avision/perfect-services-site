A cleaning crew can do a perfect job on an open floor and still leave a private office untouched, because nobody said whether the papers on the desk could be moved. Office cleaning in Greer works best when those small rules are decided on the first visit. This piece covers the rooms that are not the lobby or the restroom: private offices, supply closets, storage rooms and the coat closet by the door.

Private offices: decide what is allowed

A private office usually has a desk covered in papers, a monitor, a keyboard and sometimes a client's folder. We do not shuffle paper. We clean around it, which means desktop edges, monitor stand, the phone and the chair arms, and we leave anything left in a stack exactly where it is. If you want the desk fully cleared, tell us; if you want it fully untouched, tell us that too. A short written list at the estimate prevents most misunderstandings.

What we do clean in a private office every visit:

  • Desk surface edges and visible areas wiped
  • Phone, keyboard and mouse wiped with a suitable cloth
  • Door handle, light switch and chair arms
  • Window sill and blinds dusted
  • Floor vacuumed or mopped under the chair
  • Trash and recycling emptied

Supply closets and storage rooms

These are the rooms most often left out of the routine, and they become the dustiest. Boxes on the floor, old files on shelves and spare chairs stacked in a corner collect dust and make the floor hard to reach. We do not sort or move stored items, but we do wipe the shelf edges that are accessible, dust the tops of boxes we can reach and vacuum the floor around them. If you want a full storage clean-out, that is a different job: it needs the boxes cleared first, and we will say so at the estimate.

Closets with a sink, such as a janitor's closet, get extra attention because they hold the mop sink and, often, a water stain or odor.

Coat closets, server nooks and the front hall

The coat closet by the entry is used by clients and staff, and in wet weather it holds wet umbrellas and muddy shoes. Greer's red clay and rainy stretches show up on the floor right there. A small IT nook or server closet is a different matter: we do not enter if you ask us not to, and we do not touch equipment or cables at all. Most offices ask for the door to stay closed and the floor outside to be cleaned.

A first-visit routine

On the first visit, the crew does a longer pass than usual: baseboards, door tops, vents and the window sills. After that, the regular schedule is shorter, because the heavy dust is gone. Offices near busy roads like SC-14 and US-29 tend to collect road dust on front windows and door tracks, so we check those at the start.

If you hand us a key or alarm code, we log in and out the same way each time and tell you what we did. Keeping a short note on a shared board works well for offices with several staff, and the team can leave a note if something needs attention, such as a leaking tap.

One more practical point is keys to inner rooms. If a private office is locked at night, tell us in advance whether to skip it or whether a staff member can leave it open. A locked door with no instruction is the most common reason a room is missed for weeks, and nobody notices until a client sits down in it.

A note on confidentiality

Many Greer offices hold client files, medical records or financial papers. Our crew does not read, photograph or move documents. If a room needs to stay closed, you can lock it and we will clean the hallway and leave a note. Tell us during the walkthrough which rooms are off limits, and we write that into the schedule so a new team member knows from day one. Keys are logged, and alarms are set the way you instruct.

A short list posted on the inside of a supply closet door works well: what stays, what can be thrown out and who to call.

Frequently asked questions

Will you move papers on my desk?

No. We clean around papers and leave them where they are, unless you ask for a full clear in writing.

Can you clean a storage room full of boxes?

We wipe accessible shelves and vacuum the floor around boxes. A full clean-out needs the boxes cleared first and is quoted separately.

Do you go into server closets?

Only if you ask. We never touch equipment, and most offices ask us to clean outside the door only.
