If you have a dog that sheds on the sofa or a cat that sleeps on the stairs, you already know the pattern: you clean on Saturday and by Tuesday there is a fine layer on the floor again. Pet owners in Greer are not doing anything wrong; the hair simply comes back faster than it does in a house without animals. Here is what we do on house cleaning visits for pet households, and what you can do between visits to make them count.

What we see when we walk into a pet home

The pattern is similar from house to house. Hair gathers along the baseboards and in the corners where air currents drop it. Nose prints and paw marks show on glass doors and the lower part of the wall. The sofa has a crescent of hair along the back cushion. Near the back door, red clay from the yard mixes with water bowls and turns into a stain on the floor. Those are the places we start looking.

A room-by-room plan for pet hair

  1. Soft surfaces first. Sofa cushions, throws and pet beds are vacuumed before anything else, because hair lifted off them lands on the floor.
  2. High to low. Shelves, TV stands and window sills are wiped, then baseboards, where hair clings to the top edge.
  3. Floors twice. One vacuum pass picks up the surface; a second pass in the other direction gets what the first lifted. Hard floors are then washed by hand along the walls.
  4. Glass last. Nose prints on doors and low windows are wiped at the end so nothing falls on them.

Most of the work in a pet home is in floors, so the same edge care described in our post on bedrooms and closets applies to the room where your animals sleep too.

Dander, odors and the stuff you cannot see

Hair is the visible part. Dander is not, and it settles on fabric, ceiling fan blades and vents. For families with allergies, we dust high spots with damp cloths so the dander is captured, not pushed around. Odors usually come from three places: the pet bed, the area near the food and water bowls, and the doormat. Washing the bed cover weekly and wiping the floor around the bowls at every visit does more than any spray.

  • Pet beds and blankets washed on a weekly schedule
  • Floor around bowls wiped at each visit
  • Litter area wiped down on the outside, and the floor around it vacuumed
  • Entry mats shaken and vacuumed
  • Furniture covers or throws used where your pet sleeps

Simple habits that help between visits

A few small habits make a pet household much easier to keep clean. Brush your dog or cat outdoors if you can, since most of the loose hair then never reaches the floor. Keep a towel by the back door for wet or muddy paws, especially after rain, when Upstate red clay is at its worst. Put a washable throw on the spot where your pet sleeps, and wash it each week.

Vacuum the two or three places where hair collects most, such as the hallway and the edge of the sofa, between our visits. It takes a few minutes and means the next service starts from a better place, so more time goes into baseboards, glass and bathrooms. Tell us about any areas where your pet is not allowed, any rooms that must stay closed, and any health concerns for people in the home.
