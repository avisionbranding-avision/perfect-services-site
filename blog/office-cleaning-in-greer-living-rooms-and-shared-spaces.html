Office cleaning in Greer: living rooms and shared spaces

Office cleaning in Greer: how reception, waiting areas, break rooms and shared spaces are cleaned after hours. Free in-home estimate before any price.

Key takeaways

  • Reception, waiting areas, break rooms and entry glass are the shared spaces clients and staff judge first.
  • High-touch points like handles, switches and keypads get wiped on every visit, not just when they look dirty.

A client forms an opinion of your office before anyone says hello. It happens at the front door: the glass, the mat, the reception counter and the chairs in the waiting area. Office cleaning in Greer, SC, for most small businesses, is really about these shared spaces, because that is where customers and staff overlap. This article walks through them in the order we would clean them, from the entry to the break room.

We keep the focus on common areas. For restrooms, see our piece on office restroom cleaning in Greer, which covers fixtures and restocking.

Start where the customer starts: entry and glass

Glass doors and entry areas are on every visit's checklist, and for good reason. A handprint on the door at eye level and a film at the push plate are visible from the parking lot. We clean the door glass, the frame, the handle and the threshold, then the mat edge and the floor just inside.

Greer offices near I-85, SC-14 and US-29 see a lot of foot traffic carrying grit from the lot. Red clay and spring pollen are the two regulars. They come in on shoes and settle at the door, so that first few feet of floor gets the most attention.

The reception counter and the waiting area

Reception and waiting areas are dusted, not just wiped. That means the ledge behind the counter, the top of the monitor stand, the shelf with brochures, the frames on the wall, and the arms and legs of the chairs. The things people touch most, such as pens, sign-in tablets and counter edges, are wiped every time.

A short list of what our team checks in a waiting area:

  • Chair arms, seat edges and the backs where hands rest.
  • Magazine tables and the underside of the lip.
  • Window ledges and blinds.
  • Door handles and push plates.
  • Floor edges beneath furniture, where lint collects.
  • Trash and recycling, emptied and relined.

The break room: the space employees notice most

Staff may not notice a clean reception area, but they always notice the break room. Our checklist covers the sink, the counters, the microwave and the outside of the fridge, plus trash and recycling. Inside the microwave is a regular problem, and the fridge handle collects more fingerprints than any other surface in the building.

We do not clean out the inside of the office fridge unless it is arranged in advance, since the contents belong to the staff. A simple end-of-week reminder to throw out old lunches helps more than any cleaner can.

After hours, and how often

One more thing to decide before the first visit is who is in the building. If staff are still working when we arrive, we start with the areas they are not using and come back to the rest. Reception and the break room are usually the first to be free, and the glass at the entry is easiest when no one is coming through the door.

Most offices choose after-hours cleaning so staff come in to a finished space. That works well for clinics, salons, studios, retail, and small professional offices. Frequency matters more than most people think: a daily or weekly schedule keeps the same hours each time and holds the finish, while a monthly schedule needs more detail work each visit.
