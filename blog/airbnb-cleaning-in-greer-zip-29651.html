What does Airbnb cleaning 29651 actually include?

ZIP 29651 sits in the Greer area of Greenville and Spartanburg counties, close to I-85, SC-14 and US-29, so a lot of the homes we see are listed for guests who are driving through or visiting for work. A turnover is not a regular house clean. The goal is to put the place back to how it looks in your listing photos.

  • Kitchen reset: dishes, counters, appliances, sink and stovetop
  • Bathrooms cleaned and disinfected, including the grout line behind the toilet and the shower door track
  • Beds made with the fresh linens you provide
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped, with edges and under the beds checked
  • Trash removed and bins reset
  • A restocking checklist for the supplies you leave out
  • A damage and missing-item report with photos
  • Staging so the room matches the listing

How long does a turnover take?

It depends on the size of the home and how the last guests left it. A tidy two-bedroom after a weekend stay is a quicker visit than a full house after a week with kids and a dog. During the walkthrough we look at the bathrooms and the kitchen first, because those two rooms decide most of the time. We then tell you what we expect for your property, so you can place the cleaning between checkout and check-in with room to spare.

What happens when a guest leaves a mess or breaks something?

We photograph it and send it with the report. A missing towel, a stained duvet cover or a cracked glass is easier to deal with when the host sees it the same day, while the booking is still fresh. We do not make decisions about damage claims for you. We just give you clear pictures and a short note so you know what to raise with the guest or the platform.

Who provides linens and supplies?

You do. We make the beds with the linens you leave, and we check your supply list: toilet paper, soap, coffee pods or whatever you stock. If something is running low, it goes on the restocking checklist. Hosts who live out of town like this part most, because they get a message about what to order before the next guest arrives.

One habit that saves hosts trouble: we check the same spots in the same order, every visit. Door handles and light switches, the underside of the toilet seat, the drawer where guests leave things, the inside of the microwave. A host who gets the same report format each time spots a pattern quickly, such as a bathroom fan that never gets used or a couch cushion that keeps ending up stained.

Which kinds of rentals do you clean?

All three, though the schedule differs. A whole house near SC-101 or US-29 may take a team for a longer visit. A basement or garage unit is usually shorter, but access matters: lockbox codes, a side door or a shared driveway. Tell us how guests enter and where they park, and we plan around it. If you own a lake or mountain home farther out, our vacation rental cleaning service covers that too, and we can talk about travel time during the estimate.

What if I also need a deeper clean once in a while?

Most hosts add a deep cleaning every few months, since oven interiors, baseboards and the tops of door frames do not get touched in a normal turnover. We usually suggest doing it on an empty week. Hosts moving between guest-ready and owner-use sometimes also ask about recurring house cleaning for the weeks they stay there themselves.

A word on guests with pets, since many Greer listings allow them. Hair collects along baseboards, under beds and in the seams of couch cushions, and a normal vacuum pass leaves it behind. If your listing is pet friendly, say so at the estimate and we build that extra attention into the visit. The same goes for rentals with a fireplace, a screened porch or a deck, where leaves and pollen come inside on shoes.

It also helps to keep a short note for the team on the kitchen counter: where the extra linens live, which supplies you want refilled, and anything the last guest reported. We read it first and follow it, and we leave a note back if something needs your attention.
