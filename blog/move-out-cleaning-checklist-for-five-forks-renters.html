Move-out cleaning checklist for Five Forks renters

On the last night of a lease, most renters in Five Forks are standing in a half-empty kitchen with a roll of paper towels wondering how far they have to go. The honest answer is further than a normal tidy-up: a move-out inspection looks inside things, not just at them. This checklist goes room by room in the order that saves the most time, then covers when it makes sense to hand it to a team.

Work in this order

Move-out cleaning goes faster when you work from the emptiest space toward the kitchen, and from top to bottom in each room. Dust falls, so ceiling fans and shelves come before floors. Floors come last in every room, and the very last job is the exit path through the front door.

  1. Take everything out of closets first, so shelves and rods are empty.
  2. Clean bedrooms and living areas, high to low.
  3. Do the bathrooms next, while products can sit on soap scum.
  4. Finish with the kitchen, since the oven and refrigerator are the most time-consuming.
  5. Vacuum and mop edge to edge, working back to the door.

Before you start, check your lease and your move-in paperwork. Some leases list what "broom clean" or "professionally cleaned" means, and some ask for a receipt. If you took photos at move-in, keep them handy, since they show what normal wear looked like. Normal wear is not the same as dirt, and knowing the difference helps you decide where to spend your last evening.

The room-by-room checklist

Kitchen

  • Inside and outside of all cabinets and drawers, including the shelf liner crumbs.
  • Inside the refrigerator and freezer, with shelves and bins washed.
  • Inside the oven and microwave, and the racks.
  • Range hood, stovetop and backsplash degreased.

Bathrooms

  • Toilet base and behind the toilet.
  • Grout lines, shower glass and tub.
  • Vanity interiors and drawers.
  • Mirrors and chrome fixtures.

Bedrooms, living areas and closets

  • Closet shelves, rods and floors.
  • Baseboards, doors, door frames and light switches.
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds.
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped edge to edge.

A few small things get forgotten again and again: the drip pans or glass top on the stove, the track of a sliding door, the inside of the dishwasher filter, the light fixtures with dead insects in them, and the shelf above the washer. Doors and door frames collect fingerprints near the handles, and light switches need a wipe. Keep a small bag for the last items you find and take it with you.

What landlords and property managers usually check

From what we see on a lot of move-outs, inspectors reach for the same places: the oven, the refrigerator, the bathrooms, baseboards and closets. They also run a hand along window tracks. Those are the places a quick clean misses, and those are the ones that tend to turn into deductions. Take photos of each room when you finish, since a dated set of pictures helps if there is ever a disagreement about the condition you left.

If you do decide to clean it yourself, set aside more time than you think you need. A note on timing

A typical move-out clean for an average home takes a good part of a day for a small team. Keep utilities on until the clean is done, since the team needs water and light.
