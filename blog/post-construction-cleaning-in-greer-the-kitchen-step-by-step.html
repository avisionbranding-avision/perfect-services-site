The new kitchen looks finished: cabinets hung, counters in, appliances in place. Then you open a drawer and find a fine white powder over the bottom, and the shine on the countertop disappears under a film. That is what the last stage of a remodel looks like in Greer, SC. This guide covers the kitchen part of post-construction cleaning in Greer, in the order the work should go.

Before anyone cleans: what the trades leave behind

Countertop installers leave cutting dust and silicone smears. Cabinet crews leave sawdust, staples and protective film. Painters leave specks on the window glass and trim. Electricians and plumbers leave drywall dust and bits of wire. Appliances arrive with stickers, tape and foam. None of this is a flaw in the work. It is the natural leftover of five different trades, and each leaves something different.

Round one: debris and top-down dust

We begin with the big stuff: tape, cardboard, loose screws and plastic. Then the dust, top down. Ceiling fans, recessed lights and the range hood go first, followed by the tops of the cabinets, where dust gathers in a thick layer. The vents and the return air grille matter here, since they spread whatever is left through the house when the heat or the air comes on.

Round two: cabinets, drawers and shelves

Every drawer and cabinet is emptied if needed, vacuumed with a brush attachment and wiped inside. Hinges, rails and the edges of shelves hold fine dust that does not show until a plate goes in. Fronts are wiped last, with handles and hardware cleaned and any protective film peeled where the installer has not done it.

Stickers, adhesive and paint specks

  • Plastic scrapers lift paint from glass and tile without scratching
  • Adhesive from labels is softened first, then wiped, never scraped dry
  • Silicone smears on counters are removed with the right solvent for the surface
  • Protective films come off appliances and fixtures
  • Tape residue on trim is tested on a small spot first

Round three: counters, sink and appliances

New stone or quartz counters are cleaned with a product that will not dull the seal. The sink, faucet and any new fixtures are polished, and the appliances are wiped inside and out, including the inside of a new oven, which often has a factory residue that smells when first heated. Window glass, tracks and sills are done with the same care, since paint specks gather there.

The vents, the fridge and other hidden places

Dust from a kitchen remodel does not stay in the kitchen. It settles on the top of the refrigerator, inside the microwave vent, along the track of the sliding door and on the sills of the nearest window. In a house along SC-14 or near Trade Street it also lands on the dining room table and the shelves in the next room. We check those too, as part of the same visit, so the remodel does not follow you through the house.

Newly installed appliances deserve their own look: the fridge gaskets, the dishwasher filter basket and the oven rack slots often hold packing debris. A short wipe is all they need, but only if someone thinks of it.

Final round: floors and the second look

Floors are vacuumed, then washed, then given another vacuum after they dry, because dust lifts as the mop moves it. Then the team leaves the room for a while and comes back to check with fresh light. A kitchen that passes the second look is ready for dishes.

How we plan the order for your move-in

If you are returning to the kitchen after weeks of takeout, the order matters. We work from the kitchen outward, so every room that gets a lot of foot traffic is cleaned last. That prevents carrying dust back into a finished space. It also lets us tell you which areas are ready to use right away and which need a day to settle.

What to do before and after we come

Keep the paper on the floor until the work is finished, if you can. Do not unpack dishes until the final clean. After we are done, run the dishwasher empty once to flush the lines, and run the hood fan for a minute to clear dust from the filter.
