The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Roebuck

Post construction cleaning in Roebuck, SC in three phases: rough clean, final clean and touch-up, and what each covers. Free in-home estimate first.

Perfect Services Team
November 24, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Post-construction cleaning works best in three passes: rough clean, final clean and touch-up.
  • Drywall dust is the hardest part, so it is cleaned before the finish work and again afterward.
  • Perfect Services gives prices only after a free in-home estimate.

Construction dust does not behave like ordinary dust. It is fine enough to travel through a whole house, it sticks to damp surfaces, and it keeps settling for days after the crew leaves. That is why a single quick clean after a remodel rarely works, and why we split post-construction cleaning into three phases.

Roebuck, in Spartanburg County off US-221 and SC-215, is a growing suburb south of Spartanburg with many family homes, and about 15 miles from our Greer base. Homeowners here finishing a kitchen, an addition or a bathroom renovation usually ask the same thing: when should the cleaning actually happen? The answer is in stages.

Phase 1: the rough clean

The rough clean happens while the project is nearly done but before final finishes. The goal is to remove debris and the heaviest dust so painters, floor installers and trim carpenters are not working in it.

  • Sweep and vacuum floors, subfloors and corners
  • Remove packaging, scraps and leftover materials
  • Wipe ledges, window sills and the tops of door frames
  • Clear dust from vents and inside empty cabinets

Skipping this phase means the final clean has to fight caked-on dust that has been walked into the floors.

Phase 2: the final clean

This is the detailed clean, done when every trade is finished. It is the longest visit and where post-construction cleaning earns its keep. The team works top to bottom, so dust falls to surfaces that are cleaned later.

  • Fine dust removed from surfaces, ledges and trim
  • Inside cabinets, drawers and closets
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills
  • Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive removed
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished
  • Floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes

Phase 3: the touch-up pass

A day or two after the final clean, a thin film of dust often appears on flat surfaces, because the house is still releasing what was in the walls, ducts and trim. The touch-up pass wipes that off before move-in or inspection. It is short, and it is the difference between a home that looks clean in the afternoon and one that still looks clean the following week.

Why dust is different after construction

Drywall dust is fine and pale and gets into everything: inside cabinets, on top of door frames, along window sills and in the vents. Water alone turns it into paste. A dry pass with a vacuum comes first, and wet wiping follows. That is why the order of the phases matters as much as the tasks themselves.

What homeowners can do before the crew arrives

Clear the area. Remove tools and leftover materials, keep a path to the water, and ask the contractor to dispose of large debris. Let us know about any areas still unfinished, such as touch-up painting, since a final clean before paint is touched up has to be repeated.

For builders, the useful question is who owns the debris and who owns the dust. Debris goes with the crew. Dust belongs to the cleaning phases. Putting that in writing at the estimate avoids confusion on the day of closing.

A note on timing with other trades

The most common scheduling problem is a final clean booked before the last painter, electrician or flooring installer is done. Each of those trades leaves new dust and a few footprints. We suggest confirming with your contractor that every trade is finished, then booking the final clean for the next day. If a punch-list is still open, the touch-up phase picks up what those repairs leave behind.

What stays out of scope

Cleaning is not repair. Scratched glass, damaged floor finish and stained countertops are issues for the contractor. We point them out in the walkthrough so you can raise them before final payment, since once the area is clean they are easier to see. If you are unsure about a residue, ask, and we will test a small spot rather than risk the surface.

Why does post-construction cleaning take more than one visit?

Can I book only the final clean in Roebuck?

Yes. The three phases can be booked together or separately, and we recommend the visit that matches where your project stands.

Does post-construction cleaning include windows?
