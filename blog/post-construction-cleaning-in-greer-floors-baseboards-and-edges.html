The floor is the last thing that gets clean after a remodel, and it is the one that gets walked on the next morning. If you are searching for post construction cleaning on floors in Greer, SC, you probably have the same situation we see often: new flooring or fresh paint, a crew that swept up, and a surface that still feels gritty under bare feet.

This article is about floors, baseboards and the edges where the work ends, and why the floors take more than one pass.

Why a swept floor is not a clean floor

Contractors usually sweep and haul out the large debris. What stays is the fine layer: sanding dust, plaster powder, cut-off material and paint flecks that stick to a new surface. A vacuum with a weak filter pulls some in and blows the finest part back out. A mop turns the rest into a paste that dries in the seams.

That is why the floors are a multi-step process in our post-construction cleaning in Greer. The service includes floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes, and a final touch-up pass before move-in.

Start with the edges, not the open floor

When a crew walks into a finished room, the instinct is to attack the big area. We do the opposite. The edges and trim hold the concentrated residue, so they go first:

  • Baseboards, top, face and the joint where they meet the floor.
  • The joints along cabinet toe kicks and door thresholds.
  • Door frames and the floor underneath the door swing.
  • Closet floors and the corners inside them.
  • Stair treads, risers and the line along the wall.
  • Floor vents and the frames around them.

Once the edges are wiped, the open floor can be vacuumed without throwing dust back onto trim we already cleaned. It is a small difference in order that changes the result.

New flooring versus old flooring

A Greer remodel can mean brand-new flooring in one room and original flooring in the next, whether the house is near Downtown Greer and Trade Street or in a newer neighborhood by GSP Airport. Each material reacts differently to construction dust.

SurfaceTypical construction problemOur approach
New hardwoodFine dust in seams, fresh finish that scuffsCareful vacuuming, then a damp wash, nothing abrasive
TileGrout haze and thin-set specksHand work along grout lines and edges
Vinyl plankDust and adhesive residue at the seamsMultiple passes, gentle wash
CarpetDust trapped deep in the pileSlow, detailed vacuuming in several directions
Concrete or subfloorHeavy powder and debrisRough clean first, then repeat after trades finish

What does a typical sequence look like?

Every home is different, but a floor-focused clean tends to go like this. We walk the space to see which rooms had work and where the dust is thickest. We clean top to bottom so that anything falling lands on floors we have not done yet. The first vacuum lifts the loose layer. The wash picks up the film. A second vacuum or wipe after drying catches what resurfaced. Before move-in, a touch-up pass follows once the furniture is in place.

It sounds like a lot, and it is. The finest drywall dust takes the most time, and sanding creates the most of it. If your project included drywall repair or new walls, expect more passes than a project that was only flooring and paint. Timing and what to do while you wait

One practical point on timing: floors that were washed while the painters still had touch-ups to do will need another look. We would rather arrive once, after the last trade leaves, than come twice for the same room. If the schedule keeps slipping, say so when you book, and we can move the date instead of cleaning on top of unfinished work.

If the trades are still working, wait on the final floor clean. Anything that gets sanded or painted afterward will undo it. Keep shoes off new floors if you can, and protect them with paper or a floor covering for the last days of work. When the last contractor leaves, that is the time to call.

Frequently asked questions

Why do you clean floors in multiple passes?

Fine construction dust keeps resurfacing from vents, trim and ledges. One pass lifts the loose layer, a wash removes the film, and a final pass catches what settled, so the floor stays clean longer.

Can I walk on new floors right after construction?

That depends on the finish and how long it has cured, which your contractor can tell you. Once it is safe, we clean carefully without abrasive tools.

Do you clean baseboards after a remodel in Greer?

Yes. Baseboards, trim and door frames are part of the final clean, and we wipe them by hand because that is where the dust concentrates.
