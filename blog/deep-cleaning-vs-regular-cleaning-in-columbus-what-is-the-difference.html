Deep vs. regular cleaning in Columbus: the difference

Run a finger along the top of a door frame in almost any home and you will see the difference between regular cleaning and deep cleaning. A regular visit keeps what you see every day under control. A deep clean goes after what you do not see: the dust on the frame, the film on the cabinet handle, the grout that went gray. This post lays out where the line sits, so homeowners in Columbus, NC can decide which one they need.

What a deep clean actually adds

The extra work is not mysterious. It is the list of jobs that do not fit inside a standard visit, because they take time and the time is the point.

  • Baseboards and door frames hand-wiped, not just touched up.
  • Cabinet fronts and handles degreased.
  • Grout lines scrubbed in the kitchen and bathrooms.
  • Ceiling fan blades, vents and light fixtures dusted.
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds cleaned.
  • Behind and around the toilet base.
  • Appliance exteriors, range hood and backsplash.

The slowest work is usually in the bathrooms. Grout and hard water spots take patience, and that is where most of the extra time goes. If your oven needs attention, that can be added on, as can inside the fridge or cabinets.

Who should start with a deep clean

You do not need a deep clean every time you hire help. These are the situations where one is worth doing.

  1. You have not had a professional clean in months. Build-up is easier to remove once, thoroughly, than to chip away at over many regular visits.
  2. You are about to start recurring service. A deep clean first sets the baseline, and the regular visits stay quick afterward.
  3. Spring or holiday preparation. Pollen in spring and guests in November both make the details visible.
  4. Allergies. Dust that is removed rather than moved matters for allergy sufferers. Our pollen season deep cleaning plan shows how that works.
  5. A new home or a long vacancy. Cabin and second-home owners often want a reset before the season.

If your home gets a regular clean already and just looks a little tired, a deep clean once or twice a year is often enough. For a year-round view, our spring deep cleaning checklist explains what to look at.

What the first hour of a deep clean looks like

Many homeowners are surprised by how a deep clean starts. The team does not begin with the floors. They begin high: ceiling fans, vents and light fixtures first, so dust falls onto surfaces that have not been cleaned yet. Next come window sills and blinds, then cabinet fronts and appliance exteriors in the kitchen. Baseboards and door frames follow, room by room, and the floors are always last. Working this way means nothing is cleaned twice and nothing gets dusted over afterward.

If you have pets, a baby or a home office, mention it at the walkthrough. We will plan the order around the rooms you use most.
