Seven days out: tidy as you pack

The cheapest cleaning you can do is the cleaning you do while packing. Empty one room at a time, and as each closet or cabinet goes bare, wipe it right then. Shelves are far easier to clean empty and before dust settles back on them. This is also the time to take down curtains and wipe the window sills while the room is still open.

Use this week to decide who does what. Are you cleaning on your own, or booking a team? If it is a team, the next step is the visit.

One more habit helps. Keep a small bag of the things you will need on the last day: a vacuum or broom, a few cloths, trash bags and a roll of paper towels. When the movers load the truck, those supplies are the first things buried. Set them by the front door and they stay reachable.

Moving day and the day after: the empty-house window

The best moment for the real cleaning is the gap between the last box leaving and the key handoff. The house is empty, every cabinet is reachable, and the floors are clear. Here is what a team does in that window.

  1. Dust ceilings, fans, vents and light fixtures.
  2. Clean inside and outside every kitchen cabinet and drawer, then the appliances inside and out.
  3. Scrub the bathrooms: toilet base, grout, glass and the insides of vanities.
  4. Do the closets, then baseboards, doors, frames and light switches.
  5. Wipe window sills, tracks and blinds.
  6. Vacuum and mop edge to edge, finishing at the front door.

Done in that order, nothing needs to be cleaned twice. If the house needs more than a standard turnover, we may suggest deep cleaning as a first step.

Cleaning before you unpack at the new place

People forget half of the timeline: the home you are moving into. Even a house that looks fine has dust inside cabinets, a film on the shelves, and floors that have seen a lot of foot traffic from showings and movers. Moving-in cleaning is easiest while it is empty, so schedule it before the furniture arrives or in the first hours after the truck is parked. Shelves, drawers and closet floors are all within reach. Once boxes are stacked on the counters, you will never get that chance again.

Families with kids or pets often have a stronger reason to do this: you know exactly what is on those floors and what will go in those drawers.
