How to prepare your Greer home for airbnb cleaning

Prepare for Airbnb cleaning in Greer, SC with a simple host routine: supplies, linens, access and a note for the team. Ask for a free in-home estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 14, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • A ten-minute host prep saves the cleaning team the most time.
  • Clear access, linens and a supply note matter more than tidying up.
  • The first visit is a free in-home estimate where we walk the property together.

The best thing you can do before a cleaner arrives at your Greer Airbnb is not to clean. Hosts sometimes scrub the bathroom themselves out of habit, and then the team has a half-finished job to read around. What actually helps is a short list of setup tasks, and most of them take less than ten minutes. Here is the order we would follow if we were hosts ourselves.

Step 1: Make sure the team can get in

Access problems cause more late turnovers than dirty houses do. Before the first booking, decide how the team enters: a lockbox, a smart lock code or a host meeting them at the door. Put the code in the schedule you share, and tell us if it changes between guests. If the property is in a gated community or has limited parking, say so up front. Some homes near GSP Airport and in the newer neighborhoods around the BMW plant have tight driveways or shared parking, and it is easier to plan for that on day one.

Step 2: Set out linens and know where they live

Our visit includes beds made with fresh linens, but the linens are yours. Tell the team where the clean sets are kept, how many sets each bed needs, and where the used ones should go. If laundry is done on site, the washer and dryer area has to be clear. If it goes off site, a labeled bag by the door keeps things moving. One mistake we see often is a house with plenty of sheets for the beds but no spare towels when a guest leaves late, so think about a backup set.

Step 3: Take away what should not be cleaned around

Personal items are the other big slowdown, and a lot of hosts underestimate them. A guest-ready house is one where the cleaner can see at a glance what stays and what goes, and it also protects your own belongings. Before the team arrives, move anything valuable or private into a locked closet or cabinet. Cleaners should not have to guess whether a personal item belongs to the property or to you. We usually suggest leaving the home as your guests will find it, with these in mind:

  • Lock away valuables, documents and anything you would be sad to lose
  • Clear the counters of loose mail and personal bottles
  • Empty the refrigerator of food left by the last guest, or tell us what to keep
  • Leave a clear path to the shower, the oven and the laundry room
  • Mention any surface that needs special care, such as a stone countertop or unsealed wood

Step 4: write a short note for the team. A note on the counter or in a shared message beats a long phone call. Include the checkout time, the next check-in time, any problem from the last guest (a spill, a smell, a broken item) and anything the team should restock. We include a restocking checklist for your host supplies, so tell us what you stock and what you want flagged when it runs low. We also send a damage and missing-item report with photos, which works best if we know what the room looked like before.

Step 5: Plan the turnover window

The tighter the window between checkout and the next check-in, the more important the first four steps become. Same-day turnovers need tighter scheduling, and a house that is ready for us on arrival gives the team the time it needs. If you leave an hour of slack in the calendar, that hour often saves a guest from arriving to a half-finished room. Checklist before every visit

  • Entry code or key plan confirmed
  • Clean linen sets and towels in a known spot
  • Valuables locked away
  • Note for the team with checkout and check-in times
  • Supply levels known so the restocking checklist is useful
  • Any special surface or problem from the last stay written down

Nothing on that list requires scrubbing. Done once and kept up, it turns each visit from guesswork into routine. The Brazilian detail-first method relies on a team that arrives with a clear job and a clear house to work in, so they can spend time on the baseboards, the door tracks and the shower glass rather than on tracking down a lost key.
