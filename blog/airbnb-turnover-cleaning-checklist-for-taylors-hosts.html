Before the guest leaves: set the expectations

Most turnover problems start with the host's instructions, not the cleaning. Put these in the listing and the welcome message:

  • Check-out time, and a gentle request to start the dishwasher and bag trash.
  • Where used towels and sheets go.
  • Whether the washer can be used.

It saves the cleaner twenty minutes on a typical visit.

The turnover, in order

  1. Photo walk-through. Note damage, stains and anything left behind before touching it.
  2. Linens off, laundry on. It is the slowest part, so it starts first.
  3. Kitchen. Dishes away, counters, stovetop, sink, microwave inside, appliance fronts, the fridge shelves, trash out.
  4. Bathrooms. Toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity cleaned and disinfected.
  5. Bedrooms. Fresh linens on, surfaces dusted, under-bed glance, closet check for forgotten items.
  6. Living areas. Couch cushions lifted for crumbs and coins, surfaces and remotes wiped.
  7. Floors. Vacuumed, then mopped on the way out.
  8. Restock and stage. Paper goods, soap, trash bags, then the house is set to match the listing photos.

Timing a Taylors turnover

For a one-bedroom or two-bedroom home, the window needed is usually short. Larger houses take longer, especially if laundry runs on site. If you take back-to-back bookings, build in a buffer rather than hoping the dryer finishes in time.

What Taylors guests tend to notice

Taylors guests are often staying a week or more, which means they open drawers and use the kitchen. They check the pantry shelf, look inside the fridge and run the washer. A turnover for a stay like that goes past the surface: the shelves get wiped, the drawers are checked for crumbs, and the closet hangers are counted. It takes more time than a one-night rental clean, and it is worth saying so in the estimate request.

Pet-friendly listings need extra attention to hair on couches and bedding, and a quick pass along the baseboards where fur gathers. If your listing allows pets, list that in your request so we plan more time.

Entry and parking details

With lockboxes and smart locks, we need the code ahead of time. For neighborhoods along Wade Hampton Blvd or near SC-290, check whether street parking is allowed, since a cleaning team may arrive in more than one vehicle. A text before the first visit solves most of these questions.

A simple month-end review

Once a month, read your last few guest reviews together with the cleaning reports. If the same word keeps appearing, such as "dusty," "smell," or "sticky," it points to a spot to add to the checklist. Hosts who do this tend to need fewer corrective visits, and our team can adjust the scope without starting over.

Handling a bad turnover day

Sometimes a guest leaves the house in worse shape than expected: spilled food on a couch, a stained rug, trash that never made it out. When that happens, the cleaner should stop, photograph it and message the host before deciding what to do. Hosts who get that photo within minutes can contact the guest through the platform while the booking record is fresh. We include this in the report so you are never surprised by a damage claim window that has already closed.
