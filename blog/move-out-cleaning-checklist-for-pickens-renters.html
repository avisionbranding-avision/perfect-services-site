Move-out cleaning checklist for Pickens renters

Move out cleaning checklist for Pickens, SC renters: what landlords check, the spots people miss and how to book a free in-home estimate today.

Perfect Services Team
November 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Clean an empty home from the top down, and do it after the furniture is out.
  • Inside the oven, fridge, cabinets and closets are what landlords look at first.
  • The price comes after a free in-home estimate and depends on size, condition and timing.

You hand in the keys on Friday, and the landlord walks through on Monday with a clipboard. In Pickens, where rentals range from small apartments to houses near the foothills, that walk-through decides how much of your deposit you see again. This checklist is built around what the inspector actually opens and looks at, and it follows the same order as our move out cleaning in Pickens.

Do it when the house is empty

The biggest mistake is cleaning around furniture and boxes. Wait until the last item is out, then clean once. Floors, shelves and closets are far easier to do in an empty room, and you will not undo your work by carrying a sofa through the hall.

The order that saves time

  1. Start at the top: ceiling fans, light fixtures, vents and the tops of cabinets.
  2. Windows next: sills, tracks and blinds, then glass.
  3. Kitchen: cabinets inside and out, oven, microwave, fridge and freezer, range hood, backsplash.
  4. Bathrooms: toilet and its base, tub, shower glass, vanity inside and out, grout.
  5. Closets: shelves, rods and floors.
  6. Baseboards, doors, door frames and light switches in every room.
  7. Floors last: vacuum edge to edge, then mop, finishing at the front door.

What the landlord checks first

Ask any property manager and the list is short. The oven, the refrigerator, the bathroom and the carpet or floor in the main room. These are the places where a tenant either did the work or did not, and a missed oven is hard to argue with because it is easy to photograph.

  • Inside the oven and under the burners.
  • Inside the fridge, including drawers and the door seal.
  • Inside kitchen cabinets and drawers, where crumbs collect.
  • The base of the toilet and grout lines in the shower.
  • Closet shelves and the floor in the corners.
  • Window tracks, which are rarely wiped.

Spots people miss

After enough move-outs a pattern shows up. The inside of the microwave is done, but the vent above it is not. The shower walls are done, but the soap tray is still gray. The doors are done, but not the tops of the door frames. The garage or entry floor is swept, but the corners hold the red clay that Upstate yards leave behind. Slow down on these and the walk-through goes better.

Documenting the condition

Before you hand over the keys, take dated photos of every room, including the inside of the oven, the fridge, the closets and the bathrooms. If a dispute comes up over the deposit, photos taken right after the clean are your best evidence. Keep a copy of your move-in condition report if you have one, and compare it with the state of the unit when you leave.

Read your lease for any cleaning requirements. Some landlords ask for carpets to be cleaned or specific items to be done, and the lease will say so. Knowing that early keeps surprises off the final day.

Moving day and the final walk-through

Pickens is the county seat and the gateway to Table Rock and the mountains, and a lot of renters here are relocating for work or school on a tight timeline. Build a buffer between the moving truck and the key return so the cleaners have an empty home and time to finish. If you can, schedule the clean a day before the walk-through. That leaves room to fix anything you notice at the last minute.

Finally, remove everything you do not want to leave: food in the pantry, items on shelves, trash in the bins, anything in the garage or on the porch. Cleaners cannot clean around leftovers, and a missing bag of trash can cost you in a final inspection. A clear, empty home is the quickest to clean and the easiest to hand back.
