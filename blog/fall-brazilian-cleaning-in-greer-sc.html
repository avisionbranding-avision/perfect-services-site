The fall cleaning rush in Greer starts about two weeks before Thanksgiving. People look at the guest bathroom, the kitchen floor and the entry, and then they start calling. If you are looking at your own house and thinking about fall Brazilian house cleaning in Greer, SC, this plan shows what to prioritize and what a detailed team does room by room.

The hosting house: what gets noticed

Guests rarely open your closets. They do notice the bathroom they use, the kitchen where everyone ends up, the floors near the door, and the table where the meal is served. A team working detail first spends its time there. The rest of the house still gets cleaned, but if you have only one visit before the holiday, start with those four spots.

A room-by-room walkthrough

Kitchen

The kitchen takes the longest. We degrease the stovetop, the hood and the cabinet fronts, wipe handles and the edge of every drawer, and wash the backsplash by hand. If you plan to cook a big meal, ask us to leave the oven for last so we can check what needs attention.

Bathrooms

Grout, the toilet base and the glass on the shower get scrubbed, and the vanity is cleaned inside and out. In fall, dry heated air does not hide dirt, so a bathroom looks cleaner or not cleaner very quickly under warm lighting.

Entry and living areas

Leaves and red clay come in on shoes, and the entry floor takes the beating. We wash floors along the walls, not just down the middle, and we move furniture to clean under it. Door frames and light switches get a hand wipe.

Bedrooms and guest room

Dust on nightstands, under the bed and along baseboards is usually what is left. A guest room that was closed since summer needs a real dusting, including the fan and the vents.

Fall checklist: the week before guests

  • Clear counters and pick up clutter so the team can reach every surface.
  • Take down anything breakable from shelves you want dusted.
  • Tell us about pets, which rooms are off limits and any fragile finishes.
  • Decide whether you want the oven and fridge interior added.
  • Plan to be away for the visit or leave one person at home to answer questions.
  • Book the day, not the week, and keep a second choice open.

Turning a single visit into a routine

Many Greer families call us in November for a single visit and then keep a schedule because they like how the house feels in January. If you are thinking about the same, a biweekly or monthly rhythm after the first deep clean keeps corners and baseboards from building up again. The detail work stays easy because it is repeated, and the house never needs a full reset. You can also ask for a rotating focus, such as a different room's baseboards or closet every visit.

Fall is a good time to start because the season is heavy on indoor life: cooking, gatherings, guests staying over and the heating on. A schedule that fits your calendar, rather than the calendar of the cleaning company, works best. We set days and times together at the estimate and write them down. If something changes, tell us a few days ahead and we adjust. And if you are happy with the work, a short note after a visit helps the team know what to keep doing. If you plan to host in your home for the first time this year, send us a short list of your worries, from the guest bathroom to the dining room floor. The walkthrough is a good moment to point at what bothers you most, because that is the area we spend the extra time on before we leave.
