A 700-square-foot apartment with a dog or two cats has a different cleaning problem than a house. Everything the animal sheds lands within a few steps of the couch, the bed and the kitchen. Apartment cleaning for pet owners comes down to a few places in a small footprint, and once you know them, the unit stays livable between visits.

Why small spaces hold on to pet hair

A house has a garage, a mudroom and a back porch to absorb the mess. An apartment or condo in Greer usually has a single entry, one carpet and one set of vents. Hair works into the carpet near the door, into the couch fabric and into the corners behind furniture. Dander is lighter and floats, so it settles on window sills, TV stands and the tops of door frames.

That is why our apartment and condo cleaning in Greer runs a little differently when pets live in the unit. We work from the top of each room down, and we vacuum last, so nothing we shake loose lands on a floor we already did.

The odor question, answered plainly

Odor usually comes from three places, not from the whole apartment: the litter box or the spot where the dog sleeps, the soft surfaces that soak things up, and the bathroom floor near a pet's water bowl. Opening the balcony door helps, and so does washing the pet's bedding on a regular schedule. We clean hard surfaces around feeding areas, wipe baseboards where pets lie against the wall, and vacuum the upholstery seams. If you prefer gentler products around animals, ask about green cleaning in Greer, which uses low-odor, pet-friendly products on request.

One more detail from the units we see: fabric on a balcony chair or a floor cushion often holds more smell than the couch does, because the animal lies there in the sun. A quick vacuum and a wash of the removable cover do more than a spray ever will. Hair on hard floors is easy to see, but hair under the refrigerator and behind the stove stays there for months, so we check those edges whenever they can be reached safely.

A pet-owner checklist for the days between cleanings

  • Brush the animal near a hard floor or outside, not over the couch
  • Vacuum high-traffic carpet and the couch seams twice a week if the pet sheds
  • Wash pet bedding and any throw blankets weekly
  • Wipe the area around food and water bowls daily
  • Keep a lint roller by the door for last-minute guests
  • Empty the litter box and wipe the tray rim every day
  • Leave the balcony door glass for us; nose prints build up quickly

What we clean in a pet household

The standard visit covers the kitchen counters, sink, stovetop and appliance fronts, the bathroom toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity, vacuumed and mopped floors, dusted surfaces and furniture, made beds, emptied trash, baseboards and door frames, balcony or patio door glass, and sanitized light switches and handles. With pets, the extra time goes to the baseboards, the door frames at nose height, the glass on the balcony door and the floor along the walls, where fur drifts. Cabinet handles and switch plates get a wipe too, since paws and noses find them.
