In December and January, a house in Greer is lived in all day. The windows stay closed, the dog sleeps on the couch, the kids are home more and the oven runs. That is when the difference between a quick clean and a detailed one shows. Here is how Brazilian house cleaning handles the indoor season, and what a homeowner can expect from a winter visit.

What changes inside the house in winter

Dust does not leave when the windows are shut. It settles on baseboards, vent covers and the tops of door frames, and it comes back the week after a quick wipe. Wet shoes bring red clay and grit across hardwood and tile, and it collects along the walls where a mop does not reach. Kitchens get used more, so the stovetop, hood and cabinet fronts pick up cooking residue faster than in summer.

That is exactly the territory the method was built for. Instead of a single pass with a spray, every surface is hand-wiped, and the edges, corners and baseboards get done on every visit.

A winter visit, step by step

We work in the same order in every house, so nothing is skipped on a busy day.

  1. Walk through the house with you, noting pets, fragile surfaces and what you want left alone.
  2. Dust high first: door frames, vents, shelves, light fixtures you can reach.
  3. Kitchen next: degrease the stovetop, hood and cabinet fronts, wipe counters by hand.
  4. Bathrooms: scrub grout and the toilet base, clean glass and fixtures.
  5. Bedrooms and living areas: dust, move small items and clean underneath, put everything back.
  6. Floors last, washed by hand along the walls, then a final walk-through against the room checklist.

Hardwood, tile and grout in the cold months

Many homes near Downtown Greer and Trade Street have hardwood that shows grit as fine scratches, while newer homes off I-85 and near GSP Airport tend to have tile and open floor plans. Both need care in winter. On hardwood we use little water and a gentle product, and hand-wash the edge along the wall. On tile, grout lines hold winter damp and soap film, so they are scrubbed rather than wiped over. If you are not sure what a floor can handle, tell us at the walk-through, since it is much easier to ask than to repair.

Is it worth it in winter?

For households that stay in, yes. A detailed clean costs time out of your weekend, and in winter that weekend is the only one you have for baking, wrapping gifts and visiting family. Families who were disappointed by rushed cleaners often book their first visit in the cold months, because the indoor mess is hardest to ignore then. The spots people forget in winter

Winter brings out a short list of places that are easy to miss: the inside track of a sliding glass door, the bottom of the coat closet where wet boots sit, the vent covers above the heater and the ledge under the kitchen window where condensation collects. Each one takes a minute with a cloth, and together they separate a house that is clean from a house that looks clean. We check them on every winter visit.

How long it takes and when to book

A first visit takes longer than the ones after it, because the team is catching up on edges that were not touched in a long time. After that, a recurring visit is faster and keeps the detail work from building back up. If you want a visit this week, ask early, since the weeks before the holidays are busy. We are based in Greer, so there is no travel delay for homes in 29650, 29651 or 29652.

Pets, kids and the winter mess

Dogs that stay in all day shed on couches and along the baseboards, and the water bowl area gets a film that needs scrubbing, not a wipe. With kids home on break, the spots that collect are door frames at hand height, light switches and the floor under the dining table. In the walk-through, point these out. The team will start in those places and move small items to clean beneath them before putting everything back where it was.

If anyone in the home has allergies, say so early. The method is dry-dust first, wet-wipe second, which keeps dust from being pushed around the room.

Prepare, then let the team work

Clear counters, pick up toys and put away small valuables. You do not need to scrub before we arrive. If someone in the house is sensitive to strong smells, ask about green cleaning products, and if the house has been neglected, deep cleaning is a sensible first visit.
