5 apartment cleaning mistakes Greer renters and condo owners make

Apartment cleaning mistakes Greer renters make: wrong products, skipped bathroom fans, missing access info and more. Get a free in-home estimate.

November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Strong products on rental surfaces cause more damage than dirt does.
  • Access details, pets and the bathroom are what usually change the time a visit takes.
  • A free in-home estimate tells you the exact price before you commit.

A renter in Greer scrubs a stained bathtub with a harsh powder for an hour, and the stain is still there, plus a dull patch. A condo owner skips the fan every shower, and the ceiling corner turns gray. These are not careless people. They are following habits that are common and easy to miss. Here are five mistakes we see in apartments and condos, with what to do instead.

Mistake 1: Using the strongest product on every surface

Rental bathrooms and kitchens are often fiberglass, laminate or coated tub surfaces, and abrasive powders can dull them for good. A dull tub holds soap film and looks dirty after cleaning. A harsh spray on a laminate counter can lift the finish near the edge, and a landlord notices that more than a little grime.

Use the mildest product that does the job, test it in a corner, and let it sit before you scrub. For stubborn spots, time works better than force. If you are unsure what a surface is, ask your property manager before you try something strong.

Mistake 2: Forgetting the bathroom fan and the window

Humid summers in the Upstate make this worse. A bathroom without ventilation grows mildew at the grout line and the ceiling corner, and no amount of scrubbing keeps up with it if the moisture stays. Run the fan during the shower and for a while after. If there is a window, crack it when the weather allows. Squeegee the glass or wipe the tub walls when you can.

These small steps are part of what keeps the next professional visit from turning into a mildew project. Our apartment and condo cleaning in Greer covers the bathroom fully: toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity.

Mistake 3: Cleaning the floor first and the rest after

This one is about order. If you mop and then dust the shelves, the dust lands on a wet floor. If you wipe the counter and then sweep, the crumbs land back on it. The working order is top to bottom and back to front: dust high surfaces and baseboards first, then counters and bathrooms, then vacuum and mop, ending at the door.

It is the order our team uses, and it is the cheapest improvement for anyone cleaning their own place. Here is a short checklist you can keep on the fridge.

  • Open the windows or turn on the fans for a few minutes
  • Dust shelves, door frames and baseboards, working from high to low
  • Clean the kitchen: stovetop, sink, counter, appliance fronts
  • Bathroom last of the wet rooms: toilet, shower, mirror, vanity
  • Vacuum, then mop, and finish at the exit
  • Empty the trash and wipe handles and light switches

Mistake 4: Leaving out the details that change the visit

People book a cleaning and leave out the info that makes it easy: the gate code, the parking spot, the cat that slips out the door, the pet hair on the couch. In an apartment complex, a gate or a parking rule can slow a team by half an hour, and a surprise at the door can derail the schedule.

Tell the team about access, pets and any surface to be careful with. It is one of the main factors that shapes the visit, along with unit size and the number of bathrooms. A note about the cat on the first visit is one of the things we ask in the walkthrough. A good comparison is how Berea renters prepare for an apartment cleaning, since the access questions are much the same.

Mistake 5: Waiting for move-out week to clean properly

A lot of renters in Greer clean only when the lease is ending, and then find a year of build-up behind the stove and along the baseboards. The deposit worries start, and the cleaning turns into a long weekend. A regular visit every couple of weeks, or even a monthly one, spreads that work out so the apartment is never a project.

When the lease does end, ask about move-in and move-out cleaning, which covers the inside of appliances and other things a regular visit does not. For a unit that has not had a professional clean for a while, a deeper first visit may be a better start, and the estimate is where we decide that together.

Frequently asked questions

What products are safe for rental bathrooms and kitchens?

Use the mildest product that works and test it in a hidden corner. Abrasive powders can dull tubs and some counters, so ask your property manager if you are unsure.

Why does my apartment bathroom get mildew so fast?

Moisture is the cause. Run the fan during and after showers, wipe wet surfaces and open a window when the weather allows.

What should I tell the cleaning team before the first visit?

Share gate codes, parking, pets and any delicate surfaces. These details change how long a visit takes.
