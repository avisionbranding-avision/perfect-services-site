Post-construction cleaning for pet owners in Greer, SC

Post construction cleaning for pet owners in Greer SC: how drywall dust mixes with hair and dander, what the team cleans in order, and pet safety tips.

Drywall dust is the finest dust a house will ever see, and a dog or cat walks straight through it. Two weeks after a remodel finishes, pet owners in Greer often find a pale film on the dog's bed, paw prints in the hallway that nobody can explain, and a cat that keeps sneezing. Post construction cleaning for pet owners in Greer has to deal with that dust and with the hair and dander it mixes with.

Why dust and pets are a different problem together

Construction dust is mostly drywall powder, sawdust and plaster. It is light enough to float, so it lands on ledges, ceiling fan blades and inside vents. Pet hair acts like a magnet for it. Hair in a corner becomes a gray mat, and a vacuum that pushes it around only redistributes it.

Animals also spread it. A dog that spent the renovation in a crate or a bedroom comes out and carries dust from room to room on its coat. Cats walk through it and clean their paws, which is why vets often tell owners to keep pets out of the work zone entirely until a proper clean is done.

Where the dust goes in a house with pets

When the team begins a post-construction cleaning in Greer, we find dust in predictable places, and pet homes add a few more:

  • Tops of cabinets, door frames and ceiling fan blades.
  • Inside vents and returns, which can push dust back into the air when the system restarts.
  • Baseboards and quarter round, where hair and fine dust pack together.
  • Window tracks and sills, including blinds that were not covered during work.
  • The space behind and under furniture where pets nap.
  • Door thresholds and the rubber edge of stair treads where paws leave marks.

The order that keeps hair and dust from coming back

A construction site gets a different sequence from a regular visit. The team works in passes, not rooms. The first pass takes dust from the highest point down, including light fixtures, vents and fan blades. A second pass wipes cabinets inside and out, windows and glass, and removes paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive. Floors come last and are washed more than once, because one pass leaves a film on tile and hardwood.

If you are replacing floors or finishing a kitchen, ask the contractor for a rough clean first. Perfect Services can book a rough clean, a final clean and a touch-up together, and for pet households the touch-up pass is the one that matters, since it catches dust that settles after the vents run for a day. Our breakdown of the three phases in Liberty explains how they fit together.

Keeping pets safe before the team arrives

Do not assume everything that looks tidy is safe underfoot. Small screws, nails, staples and bits of tile can end up in corners long after the crew leaves, and a curious dog finds them. Paint and adhesive residue is another reason to keep animals out of the work area.

  1. Walk the area slowly with the contractor and pick up any loose hardware.
  2. Keep pets in a clean room or at a friend's home until the final clean is finished.
  3. Replace the HVAC filter after the heavy dust work ends, not before.
  4. Wash pet bedding that was in the house during the remodel.
  5. Wait until floors are fully dry before letting animals walk on them.

What to ask your contractor first

A few questions save trouble later. Did the crew seal doorways with plastic so dust stayed in the work area? Which rooms had sanding or demolition? Where were materials stored? The answers tell the cleaning team where the fine dust travelled, which matters most when a cat or dog has been loose in the house. Share them when you book your post construction cleanup in Greer, and the team can plan the passes before arriving instead of discovering the dust room by room.

It also helps to keep a pet-free room clean from the start. A bedroom with the door shut during the remodel gives the animals a safe place to wait out the final clean.

After the final clean, give it a day before the animals roam freely. Vents often release a last puff of dust when the system restarts, and the touch-up pass takes care of it. A cat or dog that is allowed back too early undoes part of the work in an afternoon.

Is construction dust harmful to pets?

Fine dust can irritate airways and paws. Keep animals out of the work area until the final clean is done and floors are dry, and ask your vet about any lasting coughing or sneezing.

Should I clean before or after the pets come home?

After. A final clean removes dust from surfaces and floors, so pets return to a house that is ready for them.
