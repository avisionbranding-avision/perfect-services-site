What is Brazilian house cleaning? A guide for Greenville homeowners

What is Brazilian house cleaning in Greenville SC? See how the detail-first method works, what is included, how it differs from a standard clean, and cost.

Key takeaways

  • Brazilian house cleaning is a detail-first method: surfaces are wiped by hand and corners, edges and baseboards are included.
  • The biggest difference you will notice is under furniture, along walls and around fixtures.
  • Prices come only after a free in-home estimate, because home size, condition and frequency change the work.

If you searched for Brazilian house cleaning in Greenville SC, you probably heard it from a neighbor who said, "They did the corners." That is the shortest honest answer to the question. Brazilian house cleaning is a way of cleaning built around detail: every surface wiped by hand, every edge reached, and a walk-through at the end against a room checklist. This guide explains what that means in a real Greenville home.

What does "Brazilian" mean in house cleaning?

It does not mean a different set of products or a special trick. It describes how the work is done, and it comes from how many Brazilian families clean their own homes: slowly, by hand, room by room, and with the baseboards and door frames included. Perfect Services is Brazilian-owned, and we call this approach the detail-first method.

In practice, the team does not rely on a quick spray and wipe. Counters, tables and shelves are wiped with a cloth, moved items are put back where they were, and the edges are cleaned rather than skipped. The result is a home that looks clean up close, not only from the doorway.

What is included in a Brazilian clean?

Here is what the team does in a typical visit:

  • Every surface hand-wiped, not just sprayed.
  • Corners, edges and baseboards cleaned by hand.
  • Furniture and small items moved and cleaned under.
  • Door frames, light switches and handles detailed.
  • Bathrooms scrubbed, including grout lines and the base of the toilet.
  • Kitchen degreased: stovetop, hood and cabinet fronts.
  • Floors hand-washed along the walls where mops usually stop.
  • A final walk-through against the room checklist.

Notice how many of those are small, easy-to-miss spots. That is where the difference between a standard clean and a detailed one lives.

What the first visit looks like

The first visit is slower on purpose. The team starts with a short walkthrough of each room, notes the surfaces that need special care, such as hardwood, natural stone or glass, and agrees with you on anything to leave alone. Then the work goes room by room, usually bathrooms and kitchen first because they take the most time, and finishes with floors so nobody walks over a wet edge. At the end the team checks each room against the list and asks you to look around. If something is not how you wanted it, that is the moment to say so, and it gets fixed before the team leaves.

After a first visit like that, later recurring visits go faster, because the heavy buildup is gone and the team already knows where your home needs extra attention. Many clients notice that the baseboards and the kitchen hood stay clean far longer once they have been done properly one time.
