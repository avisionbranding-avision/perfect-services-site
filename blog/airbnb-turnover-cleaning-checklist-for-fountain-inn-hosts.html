Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Fountain Inn hosts

Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Fountain Inn, SC hosts: kitchen reset, bathrooms, linens, restocking and photos, plus how pricing works.

Why order matters more than speed

The fastest turnover is the one that does not get redone. Cleaning top to bottom and back to front means you never track dirt across a finished floor. We start with the trash and dishes, move to bathrooms and kitchen, make the beds, and finish with floors and the final walk-through. Guests are very good at finding the one thing a crew skipped, so the final pass is always against the same list.

The turnover checklist

  • Walk in and photograph any damage, stains or missing items before touching anything
  • Strip beds, start laundry, remove all trash and reset the bins
  • Kitchen reset: dishes, counters, sink, stovetop, microwave, fridge shelves cleared of leftovers
  • Bathrooms cleaned and disinfected, mirrors and faucets polished, toilet paper and towels restocked
  • Beds made with fresh linens, pillows plumped, throws folded
  • Dust surfaces, remote controls and light switches wiped
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped, entry mat shaken out
  • Supply check: coffee, soap, trash bags, paper goods
  • Stage the space to match your listing photos, then lock up and send the report

The spots guests notice first

Hair in a shower drain, crumbs under toaster oven trays and sticky cabinet handles are the three complaints we see most often in short-term rentals. A hand-wipe on handles and a check inside the microwave takes a minute and prevents a lot of one-star language. Do not forget the sliding door track and the laundry area, because guests use them more than hosts expect.

Common turnover problems and fixes

Hosts often start by handling turnovers themselves, then outgrow it when the calendar fills. These are the issues that usually push them to hire help.

  • Late check-out. Agree on a buffer and tell guests the cutoff clearly in the listing.
  • Linen shortages. Keep two full sets per bed so laundry never blocks a turnover.
  • Missing supplies. Keep a restock list taped inside a closet and have us flag low items in the report.
  • Guest damage. Photos at arrival, before cleaning, make a claim simple.
  • Odors. Check the fridge, trash cans and bathroom fan before leaving.

One more habit: check the outdoor areas. A porch with cobwebs, a patio table with pollen or a grill left dirty will show up in photos and reviews. If you want those included, say so at the estimate and we add them to the list. The best hosts treat the turnover as part of the product, not a chore between guests, and they build a written routine anyone can follow.

Ask about a trial turnover if you are unsure. Plan the first turnover together. We walk the property with you, note which rooms guests use hardest, and write a checklist for that specific house so any crew member can follow it. Hosts who do this once rarely have a repeat problem.
