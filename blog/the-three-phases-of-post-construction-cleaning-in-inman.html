The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Inman

Post construction cleaning Inman SC explained in three phases: rough clean, final clean and touch-up, with what each covers and how to schedule them.

Perfect Services Team
November 23, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Post-construction cleaning works best in three passes: rough clean, final clean and touch-up.
  • Drywall sanding makes the finest dust, and it ends up inside cabinets and vents.
  • Prices come only after a free in-home estimate.

Wipe a countertop in a freshly sanded house and the cloth comes away white, and an hour later the counter looks dusty again. That is drywall dust, and it keeps falling out of the air, vents and ceiling fans for days. It is the reason post-construction cleaning is done in phases instead of one big pass. In Inman, where you find farmland, lake homes and new neighborhoods side by side, remodels, additions and new builds all end the same way: with a clean that has to happen in the right order.

Phase one: the rough clean

The rough clean happens while trades are still finishing, or right after the heavy work stops. Its job is to take out the bulk so the next trade is not working in debris. The team removes loose debris, vacuums subfloors and open surfaces, and clears dust from ledges, framing and window wells. Nothing is meant to look finished yet.

This phase matters for a practical reason. Floors that get walked on with sanding dust on them grind it in, and paint crews work better when dust is not drifting off the walls. If you have a general contractor, ask when the rough clean fits in the schedule, since a good one will already have an idea.

A common mistake is calling for the final clean too early. If the painter still has touch-ups, or the flooring crew is coming back for a transition strip, the dust returns and the clean has to be partly repeated. We would rather move a date by two days than clean around work that is not finished. When you call, tell us which trades remain, and we will suggest when the phases should fall.

Phase two: the final clean

The final clean is the one most people picture. It begins once paint, trim, fixtures and flooring are in. The team works top to bottom and room by room, which means ceilings, fans, vents and light fixtures first, then walls, trim and windows, then cabinets and counters, and floors last. Our post-construction cleaning in Inman covers these items:

  • Fine construction dust removed from all surfaces, ledges and trim.
  • Inside cabinets, drawers and closets.
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills.
  • Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive removed.
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans.
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished.
  • Floors vacuumed and washed, with multiple passes.

Multiple passes on the floors are on purpose. The first vacuum lifts the coarse material, the wash lifts what is left, and a second vacuum after drying picks up the fine dust that settles late.

Phase three: the touch-up before move-in

A few days after the final clean, a thin layer of dust usually shows up on horizontal surfaces, especially if HVAC has been running for the first time or the house has been opened for walkthroughs. The touch-up is a final pass before you move furniture in or hand over the keys: a dusting of ledges, a wipe of the bathrooms, a quick floor pass and a look at the glass.

People often ask about sticker residue and paint specks, since they are small and stubborn. Window stickers come off with patience and the right blade angle, and paint specks on glass need a careful scrape that will not scratch. Adhesive on new appliances and cabinet hardware dissolves best with a gentle product and time. These small jobs are why the final clean takes longer than it looks like it should from the doorway.

Fine dust also hides inside places you cannot see right away. Cabinet interiors, drawer tracks, the top of the range hood and the inside of closet shelves all collect it during sanding. If it is skipped, the first time you put dishes away you will see a film. That is why the checklist is not only about surfaces you can see.

How much does post-construction cleaning cost in Inman?

It depends on factors we can only judge by looking at the project:

  • Project size, meaning square feet and the number of rooms affected.
  • Dust level, since drywall sanding creates the most fine dust.
  • Phases, because rough clean, final clean and touch-up can be booked together.
  • Windows and glass, where residue and stickers take time.
  • Deadline, as closing or inspection dates may need a larger team.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need all three phases for a kitchen remodel in Inman?

Usually not. A smaller renovation often needs the final clean and maybe a touch-up. We decide at the free estimate after seeing how far the dust spread.

Why does dust come back after the final clean?

Fine drywall dust stays in the air, vents and fans for days. The touch-up pass picks up what settles after the first clean.

Can you clean while the contractor is still working?

We can do a rough clean while trades finish, but the final clean works best after paint, trim and fixtures are in.
