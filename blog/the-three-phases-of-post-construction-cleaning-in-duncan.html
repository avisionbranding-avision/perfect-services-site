The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Duncan

Post construction cleaning in Duncan, SC works best in three phases: rough clean, final clean and touch-up. See what each covers and how pricing works.

Wipe a freshly sanded wall with a dry cloth and the cloth turns white. Wipe the same shelf the next morning and there is more dust there. That is why post construction cleaning in Duncan, SC works better as three passes than as one long day, and why we plan the work in phases.

Phase 1: the rough clean

The rough clean happens when the work is nearly done and trades may still be on site. It is not about shine. The goal is to get debris and heavy dust out so painters and flooring crews can finish on clean surfaces.

  • Remove leftover scraps, packaging and loose debris
  • Vacuum floors, ledges and window sills of heavy dust
  • Wipe cabinets, counters and flat surfaces roughly
  • Clear vents and light fixtures of loose material

This pass also gives us a first look at the condition of the space, which helps plan the next two. Builders and general contractors often book it so the final clean starts from a manageable baseline.

Phase 2: the final clean

This is the main visit. After the last trade leaves, the team works room by room, top to bottom, with more than one pass on the floors. Our post-construction cleaning in Duncan includes:

  • Fine construction dust removed from surfaces, ledges and trim
  • Inside cabinets, drawers and closets
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills
  • Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive removed
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished
  • Floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes

The Brazilian detail-first method matters most here. Stickers on new windows, a line of caulk dust on a tub edge and a film inside a drawer are the details a new owner finds on day one. We check for them deliberately instead of hoping.

Phase 3: the touch-up

Dust from sanding and cutting keeps settling after a clean, and furniture delivery adds fingerprints and scuffs. A short touch-up pass just before move-in or a final inspection catches what drifted down. It is usually the shortest visit, and it is the one that makes the house feel finished. If a closing date is close, we plan it for the day before.

Why not do everything at the end? Because the order of the trades matters. If the floors are cleaned before the painter touches up, the floors need cleaning again. If the windows are cleaned before the final sanding, they collect dust again. Phasing lets each clean happen at the moment it will last, so you pay for work that stays done instead of work that has to be repeated. That is also why builders in a hurry often book the rough clean and the final clean together.
