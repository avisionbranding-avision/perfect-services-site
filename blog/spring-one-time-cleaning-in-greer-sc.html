Picture the first warm Saturday in April. The windows are open, the yellow pollen is on the car, and you finally notice what the winter did to the baseboards. Nobody wants to start a full house reset on that day. If you plan ahead, a spring one-time cleaning in Greer can be on the calendar before the pollen arrives, and the house is ready when the weather turns.

Start with the goal, not the checklist

People book a single spring visit for different reasons, and the reason decides what the team focuses on. A family hosting Easter wants the kitchen, bathrooms, living areas and guest room in order. Someone coming off a long winter of working from home wants the dust and the grime gone from the places they stopped looking. A first-timer wants to try one-time house cleaning in Greer before deciding on a regular schedule.

Say what you need at the estimate. The same crew will clean the same kitchen differently if the goal is "ready for twenty guests on Sunday" than if it is "reset after a long winter."

What winter leaves behind in a Greer house

Closed windows and running heat keep dust in motion. It collects on the tops of doors, the ceiling fan blades you did not use, and the vents. Wet shoes and winter coats leave marks on mudroom floors, and red clay from wet yards ends up ground into entry rugs. In kitchens, months of holiday baking leave a film on the range hood and cabinet fronts. Bathrooms pick up mineral spots from hard water.

When spring pollen arrives, anything left on those surfaces mixes with it. Cleaning first, then opening the windows, keeps the house from needing a second round in May.

A spring prep checklist for the week before the visit

  • Pick the date now and confirm it with a call or text.
  • Pack away winter coats, boots and holiday decorations.
  • Clear counters, nightstands and the floors of kids' rooms.
  • Decide on add-ons: inside oven, fridge, cabinets or windows.
  • Tell us about pets, allergies and products you prefer or want avoided.
  • Plan where the team can park and how they will get in.

Here is what the team does in the first hour on a visit like this. We walk the house with you, note the areas you care about most, and set up in the kitchen. While one person starts on high dust, another works on degreasing the stovetop and range hood, because that takes the longest to loosen. By the time the dust has settled, the bathrooms are ready for the scrub and the rinse. You will usually see the order of work in the first hour, and it rarely changes.

The spots people miss are the same in most homes: door frames at hand height, the tops of baseboards, cabinet handles, the track of a sliding door and the light switches. A spring reset is the right moment to get them done, since they are what shows when the sun comes in through clean windows.

How long does it take, and when to book

A typical Greer house takes a team of two or three a half day to a full day for a first one-time visit, depending on size and condition. Heavier jobs, with more build-up or lots of add-ons, take longer. If you want a visit within a week, ask when you call. We will look at the calendar and tell you what is open after the estimate.

Booking in January or February gets you the pick of dates. Waiting until late March means competing with Easter weekend, graduation parties and the usual rush before family visits. Our team works across Greenville and Spartanburg counties, off I-85, SC-14, SC-101 and US-29, so travel inside Greer is short.

One more planning tip: write down the three things that bother you most about the house right now, whether that is the shower glass, the kitchen floor edges or the cabinet fronts. Hand the list to the team at the start of the visit. A short list gives the crew a clear priority, and you will know right away if something on it needs more time than the visit allows.

If guests are coming, book the clean for two or three days before they arrive, not the morning of. That leaves room for last-minute cooking and gives you a margin if something comes up.
