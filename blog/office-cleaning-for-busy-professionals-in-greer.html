Start with the schedule, not the checklist

The most useful first question is when the space is empty. Most professional offices have a window after the last person leaves, and a cleaner who works in that window needs a few simple things: a way into the building, a list of what to skip and a point of contact. At the estimate we agree these, and we also note the alarm, the cleaning closet and any room that stays closed.

After-hours work also lets the team do floors and restrooms without anyone in the way, which is when a mop actually gets a floor dry before morning.

The checklist an office needs

The commercial and office cleaning checklist covers the surfaces people use and the rooms visitors judge you by:

  • Desks, counters and workstations wiped
  • High-touch points: door handles, light switches and keypads
  • Restrooms cleaned, disinfected and restocked
  • Break room, sink, microwave and fridge exterior
  • Trash and recycling removed
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped
  • Glass doors and entry areas
  • Reception and waiting areas dusted

One habit that separates a good office clean from a quick one is the monitor and keyboard. We dust around them rather than moving your papers, and we leave anything that looks like work in progress exactly where it is.

Restrooms and the break room are what staff notice

Clients see the reception desk, but your staff live in the restroom and the break room. A refrigerator that is wiped on the outside only, a microwave with a splatter ring, a sink that holds yesterday's coffee: these are the complaints that reach the office manager. The team handles the restroom and break room on every visit, and cleaning the outside of the fridge and the inside of the microwave is part of the checklist. We do not clean out the contents of the fridge, which belong to your team, so a monthly clear-out is worth putting on someone's calendar.

Glass, entry and the first impression

Fingerprints on the front glass and a gritty entrance floor are visible from the parking lot. Glass doors and entry areas are on the list, and after rain the team pays extra attention to the mat and the threshold, since that is where grit comes in. If your space has a sliding door or a glass partition, say so, because the track needs its own attention.
