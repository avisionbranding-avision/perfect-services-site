Move-out cleaning checklist for Inman renters

A room-by-room move out cleaning Inman SC checklist for renters who want a clean handover, with what the oven, fridge and bathrooms really need.

Perfect Services Team
November 20, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start with the places landlords look first: oven, fridge, bathrooms and baseboards.
  • Clean an empty home from top to bottom and finish with the floors.
  • Price comes after a free in-home estimate, and tight move dates may need a larger team.

Most move-out walkthroughs we hear about start in the kitchen, with someone opening the oven door. If that door lets out a smell, the rest of the inspection tends to be stricter. This checklist is built for renters in the Inman area who are packing up and want the handover to go smoothly, and it follows the same route our team takes on a move out cleaning in Inman, SC.

Inman is a small town with farmland, lake homes and new neighborhoods, so we see everything from older rentals along US-176 to newer townhomes near I-26. The work is the same: empty rooms, every surface visible, nothing to hide behind furniture.

Before you start: three ground rules

Our move-in and move-out cleaning team starts every job with these same three rules.

  1. Clean after the furniture is out, not before. Dust falls, and you will redo half the work.
  2. Work top to bottom and back to front: ceilings and fans first, floors last, and finish at the front door.
  3. Read your lease for any cleaning list. Some landlords supply one, and it overrides any general advice.

Room by room: kitchen first

Plan the kitchen before anything else, because it is the biggest job and the one that decides how the inspection feels.

  • Inside the oven, including racks and the door glass
  • Inside the refrigerator and freezer, with shelves and drawers washed and the rubber seals wiped
  • Inside the microwave
  • Range hood, stovetop and backsplash degreased
  • Inside and outside of all cabinets and drawers, plus the handles
  • Sink, faucet and the area behind the faucet

People tell us they planned an hour for the oven and spent three. Baked-on spills from a rental that was lived in for years come off slowly, and the fridge seals hide mold spots you only see up close. The oven and the fridge are the jobs people put off, and heavy grease is what adds time. If you are short on hours, these two are the best use of a professional visit.

Bathrooms

Do the bathrooms after the kitchen, while the oven cleaner or degreaser does its work.

  • Toilet inside and out, including the base and behind it
  • Shower, tub, glass and grout
  • Vanity top and the inside of the vanity
  • Mirrors and chrome without streaks
  • Exhaust fan cover

If the shower door has a white haze, it is almost always hard water, and a gentle scrub with patience works better than a harsh cleaner that scratches the glass. Hard water spots and soap film are the usual trouble, and grout is the slowest part. A stiff brush helps, but expect to spend a while there.

Bedrooms, living areas and closets

  • Closets: shelves, rods and floors
  • Baseboards, doors, door frames and light switches
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds
  • Ceiling fans, vents and light fixtures
  • Walls: spot-wipe scuffs, but do not scrub paint
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped edge to edge, with carpet vacuumed in lines

Do not forget the garage door side of the house, the front entry and the laundry area if you have one. Wipe the washer and dryer door seals and the lint trap, and the shelf above them. Window tracks catch more dust and dead bugs than almost anything else, and a lot of renters forget them. In the Upstate, red clay tracked in during the last week of packing also settles along baseboards and in the corners of closets.

Do it yourself or hire it out?

If you have a full weekend and a lot of patience, you can do all of this. Most renters we work with choose the team because the move already eats the week, the deposit depends on the result and the lease ends on a fixed date. A team of two or three finishes the checklist in a few hours for most homes, which means you can clean the day before the keys go back. If the place has gone a long time without a good clean, we may suggest a deep cleaning as part of it.

Booking this week and finishing on time

A last piece of advice from the team: do the walkthrough in daylight. Streaks on mirrors and film on glass show clearly with the blinds open, and they vanish under evening lamps. Take photos of each room once it is clean, for your own records, before you hand over the keys.
