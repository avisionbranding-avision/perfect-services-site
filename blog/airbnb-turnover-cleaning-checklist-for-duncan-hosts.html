Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Duncan hosts

A step-by-step Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Duncan, SC hosts: reset order, restocking, damage reports and how to book a free estimate for your rental.

The hardest part of an Airbnb turnover in Duncan is usually the clock: one guest checks out at eleven and the next one is arriving at three. A checklist keeps that window from turning into a scramble. This one follows the order we use when we reset a rental, from the first look at the door to the final staging pass.

Start with a walk-through, not a mop

Before cleaning anything, walk the whole property with your phone. Look for left-behind items, broken things, stains and anything that smells. Take photos of whatever you find. Damage and missing-item reports with photos are part of our turnover service because hosts need that record for claims and for their own tracking. Do this first, while the place still looks the way the guest left it.

The reset checklist, in order

  • Strip all beds and start the laundry (linens are host-provided).
  • Open windows and check for odors; run the fan in each bathroom.
  • Kitchen: empty the dishwasher and fridge of anything guests left, then wash dishes, wipe counters and clean appliance fronts, including the microwave and coffee maker.
  • Bathrooms: clean and disinfect toilet, shower, tub, sink and mirror; replace towels and restock paper and soap.
  • Living areas: dust surfaces, wipe remotes and switches, fluff cushions.
  • Bedrooms: make beds with fresh linens, check under beds and in drawers.
  • Floors: vacuum, then mop, finishing at the door.
  • Trash removed and bins reset with fresh liners.
  • Restocking check against your supply list.
  • Staging to match your listing photos.

Why the order matters, and how tight a turnover can be

Laundry goes first because it takes the longest and runs on its own. Bathrooms come before floors so any drips end up on a floor that is not yet mopped. Staging comes last because nothing should be touched after it. If you do it in a different order, you end up cleaning the same surface twice.

Small habits that lower your turnover time

A few host habits help every cleaner. Keep a labeled supply closet. Leave a short note listing what to restock. Use washable throw covers instead of dry-clean-only items. Place a laminated list of house rules and check-out steps where guests will see it, such as asking them to put used towels in the tub. And when a guest leaves a stain, tell us the product or situation, because red wine and coffee are treated differently.

Hosts who live out of town can rely on a photo report after each turnover. You can also reach us through the free estimate form. For longer stays, a deep cleaning between guest seasons keeps the property in better shape, and a one-time clean can reset a unit that has been closed for a while.

One more thing hosts forget: check the places guests actually touch and sit. Remote controls, the coffee machine, the underside of the toilet seat, the inside of the microwave door and the handle of the fridge are the spots that show up in reviews. We clean the handles and switches on every turnover because a guest who sees a sticky handle assumes the rest of the home is the same. Likewise, open the closet and the drawers once a month. Guests leave chargers, socks and snacks, and a forgotten item in a drawer is easier to deal with before the next arrival than during their stay. Finally, keep your listing photos handy for the final staging pass, so pillows, throws and towels match what guests were promised.
