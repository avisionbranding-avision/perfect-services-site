Office cleaning in Greer: floors, baseboards and edges

Office cleaning floors in Greer: carpet, tile and hard floors, baseboards and edges in a commercial space, plus how the schedule is set after a free estimate.

November 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Carpet, tile and hard floors in the same office each need different care.
  • Edges, baseboards and the spots under desks are where an office floor looks tired first.
  • The right schedule depends on traffic and floor types, so Perfect Services estimates after a free walkthrough.

Floors are the first thing that shows wear in an office. Walk into a small business in Greer after a rainy week and you can read the day from the floor: gray lines along the carpet where people turn, a dull path across the tile, grit by the door. Office cleaning floors Greer SC businesses ask about is rarely only vacuuming. It is a schedule, a sequence, and a different method for each surface.

First, take stock of the floor types

Many offices have three kinds of flooring in one suite: carpet in the work area, tile or vinyl at the entry and break room, and a hard surface in the restroom. Each one gets dirty in a different way, so the first thing we do at the walkthrough is walk the whole space and mark which is which.

Floor typeWhere it usually isWhat it needs
CarpetWork area, private officesFrequent, slow vacuuming and detail along edges
TileEntry, break room, restroomsVacuum or sweep, then mop, with attention to grout
Hard floors (wood or vinyl)Reception, hallsDust control before mopping to avoid scratching

Carpet: it is about direction and edges

Carpet in an office does not show dirt quickly. That is the problem, because by the time it shows, a lot is in the pile. A regular vacuum on a set schedule keeps that from building up. We vacuum slowly in the traffic lanes, because a quick pass only picks up the top layer, then work along the edges and under desks where the machine does not usually reach.

Chair casters leave a recognizable pattern under desks. If you see crushed fibers in a circle under each seat, that is not a cleaning failure but wear, and we will tell you so at the estimate. Spots and stains are a separate question we can talk through when we see them.

Tile and hard floors: grit first, then water

On tile and hard floors, grit is the enemy. Sweep or vacuum first, then mop. If the order is reversed, the mop drags grit across the surface and scratches it. That sounds basic, but it is the most common mistake we see in offices that clean with whoever has time.

The entry takes the worst of it. Greer sits in the red-clay Upstate, and a wet day brings an orange tint into the first few feet of floor. A mat helps, and we vacuum and wash around it every visit. In spring, pollen adds a yellow layer that settles in the same area.

Baseboards and edges in a commercial space

Offices tend to skip the edges, since nobody sees them on a daily basis. They are still the clearest sign of a cleaning schedule that is only skimming the surface. A baseboard with a gray line, a door frame with scuffs, and a corner with a dust ridge show up in a client's first meeting.

  • Baseboards in halls and private offices, wiped by hand.
  • Door frames and the bottom of doors.
  • Corners behind filing cabinets and plants.
  • Floor edges below the break room counter.
  • Under desks and around chair legs.
  • Entry thresholds and the glass door track.

The standard office checklist already covers floors vacuumed and mopped, and the glass doors and entry area. Detail work along edges is part of how our Brazilian team approaches the job: by hand, with the small spots done each time rather than saved for a deep clean. How often should office floors be cleaned?

It depends on traffic. A clinic or salon with clients all day needs a daily or near-daily pass on entry and reception floors. A small professional office with a few desks can run on a weekly schedule with entry floors touched twice. Monthly service works for very low traffic spaces, but it means more detail work on each visit and a floor that is not always at its best. In our office cleaning in Greer service you choose daily, weekly or monthly, and we adjust after the first few visits if the floor says it needs more.
