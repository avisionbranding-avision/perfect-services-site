What changes the price of post-construction cleaning in Greer, SC

What changes the price of post construction cleaning in Greer, SC? Dust level, phases, glass and deadlines explained. Exact pricing after a free estimate.

November 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Drywall sanding creates the most fine dust, and fine dust is the biggest driver of time.
  • Booking rough clean, final clean and touch-up together can make the schedule easier.
  • Prices come only after a free in-home estimate.

Cleaning up after a Greer bathroom remodel is not the same job as cleaning up after a new build in the neighborhoods near GSP Airport, and the price should not be the same either. People search for post construction cleaning rates hoping for a single figure, but what actually moves the work is a short list of things you can see in the house. Here is what we look at and why.

Fine dust comes first

If drywall was hung and sanded, there is dust on everything: ledges, inside the cabinets, on top of door frames, in the tracks of new windows. More sanding means more passes by the crew, because the dust has to be lifted before it is wiped. A small renovation that did not touch the walls may be a lighter job than an addition with three rooms of new drywall, even if the square footage is similar.

The factors in plain language

Every post-construction cleaning estimate in Greer starts with these five items:

  1. Project size. Square feet and number of rooms affected.
  2. Dust level. Light, medium or heavy, depending on the trades involved.
  3. Phases. Rough clean, final clean and touch-up can be booked together or one at a time.
  4. Windows and glass. Labels, paint overspray and adhesive take extra time.
  5. Deadline. A closing, inspection or move-in date may need a larger team.

None of these can be seen from a photo or measured over the phone, which is why Perfect Services gives the price only after a free in-home estimate. What the work looks like, step by step

Understanding the process helps you see where the hours go.

  • Rough clean: debris, packaging and the first layer of dust
  • Top-down vacuuming of vents, fixtures, fans and frames
  • Detailing cabinets inside, drawers and closets
  • Glass, tracks and sills, with sticker and paint removal
  • New fixtures and bathrooms polished
  • Floors vacuumed and washed in several passes
  • Final look before move-in

Hardwood and stone floors need more care than plain vinyl, since a gritty floor can scratch under a mop. That shows up in the estimate too.

Homeowner versus builder work

Homeowners finishing a remodel usually have one or two rooms and want them to feel like a house again. Builders and general contractors need a clean ahead of a buyer's walk-through, often on a closing date they cannot move. The first case is mostly about detail; the second is mostly about timing and crew size. Tell us which one you are, and the estimate fits. Several local builders book a final clean as a standard step, much like our post-construction cleaning service describes.

How to avoid paying for dust twice

Tell your contractor to finish dusty work before the cleaning team arrives. A crew can do a perfect final clean, but if tile saws and sanders return afterward, the dust returns too. Ask whether the HVAC filters were changed; construction dust loves ductwork. Cover or remove what you can before the visit, and keep pets and children out of the room until the final pass is finished.

Timing matters for price in a subtle way. Rushed schedules may need a bigger team on a single day, while a bit of notice lets the crew spread the work and keep the touch-up on the next day. In many cases we can book an estimate visit within the week, even if the cleaning itself is later.

A closer look at windows, glass and trim

Windows are where homeowners are surprised. New glass carries labels, tape lines, paint overspray and a film from the sanding, and the tracks hold a powder that does not wipe up easily. Each window takes several minutes of careful work, and a house with many windows near the BMW plant side of Greer, where the newer builds tend to have large ones, naturally takes longer than a small remodel with three. The same goes for painted trim, where specks and adhesive come off best with patient hand work rather than strong chemicals.

Light fixtures, ceiling fans and vents get the same attention. They sit above everything else, so they are cleaned first, which is also why an honest estimate considers ceiling height and the number of fixtures.

Will the price change on cleaning day?

It should not. The estimate visit exists so that the number we give is based on what we saw. If the project changes before cleaning day, say the contractor adds a room or more sanding happens, tell us and we revisit the scope together rather than surprising you at the door.
