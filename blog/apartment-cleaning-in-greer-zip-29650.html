Why apartments need their own approach

Smaller does not mean simpler. In an apartment the kitchen, living room and bedroom share air, so cooking smells and dust travel. Bathrooms are compact, which means soap scum and mirrors are right in your face. Carpet is common, and hallway lines show wear. A cleaner who works in apartments every week knows to start with the kitchen and bathroom (the rooms that need the most attention) and to leave floors until the end.

What is on the checklist

  • Kitchen counters, sink, stovetop and appliance fronts
  • Bathroom toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped
  • Dusting of surfaces, shelves and furniture
  • Beds made and trash emptied
  • Baseboards and door frames
  • Balcony or patio door glass
  • Light switches and handles sanitized

You can see the whole list on the page for apartment cleaning in Greer. Extras such as inside the oven or fridge can be added; ask at the estimate.

Who books apartment cleaning

From our work in town, the people who use it fall into four groups: renters in apartment communities who want a clean before a lease inspection, condo and townhome owners with busy schedules, young professionals and students who would rather spend a Saturday elsewhere, and seniors in smaller homes who prefer not to climb or bend. Each group wants something slightly different. A senior may want the same person each visit; a student may want a once-a-month reset.

What to do before the team arrives

  1. Pick up clothes and loose items from the floor and counters.
  2. Leave a gate code or entry instructions if you will not be home.
  3. Tell us about pets, and put away anything fragile or valuable.
  4. Point out the problem spots: a stained tub, a sticky stovetop, a cabinet that needs attention.

You do not need to scrub before we come. That defeats the purpose.

Stairs, parking and building rules

Townhomes and second-floor condos bring their own logistics. If the unit is up a flight of stairs, mention it, since the team carries a vacuum and supplies. If your community limits parking to a few guest spaces, tell us which ones to use so nobody gets a notice on the windshield. Some buildings ask vendors to check in at an office; add that to your booking notes and the first visit will go smoothly.

How long does it take?

Usually less than a house, but the time depends on bathrooms, floors and how long it has been since the last clean. We set the expectation at the estimate rather than guess.

A closer look at the rooms that matter most

The kitchen. In a small apartment kitchen, grease from the stovetop settles on the cabinet fronts and the wall behind the range. The team degreases the stovetop and wipes appliance fronts, and handles and the microwave door are sanitized. If you cook a lot, tell us at the estimate; it may be worth adding the oven.

The bathroom. With only one bathroom, there is no hiding a dirty one. Shower glass, the track of the tub door, the base of the toilet and the faucet get the most attention, since that is where residue builds. Mirrors are cleaned last so nothing drips on them.

The bedroom and living area. Surfaces are dusted high to low, beds are made, and carpet is vacuumed in lines so the pattern shows the job was done. Door frames and baseboards are wiped, which is a detail many people never notice until it is done.

The entry and balcony door. The glass is cleaned, and the handles and switches near the door are sanitized, since they are touched most often.
