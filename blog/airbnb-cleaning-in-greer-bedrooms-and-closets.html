Airbnb cleaning in Greer: bedrooms and closets

How a pro team handles bedrooms, beds and closets at an Airbnb turnover in Greer, SC: linens, under-bed checks, staging and the free in-home estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 18, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Bedrooms hide the things guests leave behind: check under the bed, in drawers and in closets.
  • Make beds to match your listing photos so the room looks as booked.
  • Prices come after a free in-home estimate, never before the visit.

Most items left behind by guests are not on the nightstand. They turn up under the bed, behind the closet door, or in a dresser drawer the guest used as a catch-all. A good bedroom turnover in a short-term rental is part cleaning, part detective work, and part staging. Here is how we approach Airbnb cleaning in Greer for the sleeping areas.

Start with the bed, because everything else depends on it

The bed is the biggest visual element in the room and the first thing a guest sees in your listing photos. We strip it completely: sheets, mattress protector, pillowcases and pillow protectors. While the bed is bare, we look at the mattress for stains, check the seams for anything unusual, and flag problems in the host report with a photo.

Linens are provided by the host. Some Greer hosts keep two full sets per bed so a team can swap and go, with dirty sets washed off site. Others ask us to run laundry on site. Either works, but the second takes more time on the schedule, which is why linen handling is one of the factors in the price.

What we do in a typical bedroom, in order

  1. Open the curtains or blinds and turn on every light so nothing hides in the shadows.
  2. Strip the bed and bag used linens separately from the trash.
  3. Check drawers, nightstands, the closet floor and under the bed for forgotten items and set them aside for the host.
  4. Dust from high to low: ceiling fan blades, picture frames, headboard, lamps, nightstand, baseboards.
  5. Wipe handles, switches and the door. These are the touch points guests carry hands over all weekend.
  6. Remake the bed with clean linens, tightening corners and lining up pillows like the listing photo.
  7. Vacuum the floor, including under the bed as far as we can reach, then do the edges along the baseboards.
  8. Do a final check from the doorway, the same view a guest has when they arrive.

Closets are where short-term rentals go wrong

Many hosts forget the closet because guests rarely complain about it. They do complain about a musty smell, or hangers that are missing, or a luggage rack with someone else's crumbs on it. Humid Upstate summers make this worse, since a closed closet with no airflow can hold a damp smell for days.

  • Check that the closet has enough hangers and put them back evenly spaced.
  • Wipe the shelf tops and the door tracks.
  • Vacuum the closet floor, including corners.
  • Look at the luggage rack or bench and wipe it down.
  • Smell test: if anything is off, tell the host so it can be traced, not covered.
  • Count extra blankets and pillows against your inventory list.
  • Leave the closet door the way your listing photos show it.

Pillows, protectors and what we flag

Pillows are the part of a bed that quietly goes bad. They hold oils and odors long after the case looks clean. We do not wash them, but we do check them at every turnover: a flat, yellowed or damp pillow goes in the host report so you can swap it. The same goes for a mattress protector with a stain or tear. A guest who finds a yellow edge at the corner of a pillow rarely says anything to you, but it can show up in the review as "not as clean as expected."

If your rental has bunk beds or a sleeper sofa, tell us when we visit. Bunk ladders, side rails and the underside of the top bunk collect dust that no one thinks to wipe, and a pull-out sofa deserves a look underneath the cushions at every turnover. We add these spots to your property checklist so the same things get checked every visit, whoever on the team is working that day.

Windows, blinds and the light in the room

Bedrooms in Upstate rentals often face the road or a yard, and the blinds take on dust and pollen. Guests lie in bed and look at the window, so the sill, the blind slats and the glass at that height matter more than the glass at the top. We wipe the sill and the lower slats, and note any blind that is bent or broken for the host.

Staging that matches your listing

Guests arrive with the photos in their head. If the throw pillows are on the floor or the second bed is made differently from the picture, they notice. Part of our service is staging to match the listing, so the first impression lines up with what they booked. Bring us your photos during the estimate and we will note exactly how each bed is dressed.
