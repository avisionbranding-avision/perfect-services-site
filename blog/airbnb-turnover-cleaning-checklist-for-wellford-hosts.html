Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Wellford hosts

Airbnb cleaning Wellford SC: a turnover checklist built around your checkout window, with restocking, damage reports and how the free estimate works.

Key takeaways

The hard part of a turnover is rarely the cleaning itself. It is the gap between a guest who leaves late and the next one who arrives early. If you host a short-term rental in Wellford, this checklist is built around the clock first and the chores second, so you can see what fits in your window and what needs help from an Airbnb cleaning service in Wellford, SC.

Plan the turnover around your window

Wellford is a small Spartanburg County town (ZIP 29385) along US-29 with I-85 close by, about 7 miles east of our base in Greer and a 15-minute drive. Guests often arrive after a long day on the interstate, which makes the first impression of the entry and the bathroom matter more than any decoration.

Before you plan chores, write down three times: checkout, check-in and the earliest your cleaner can enter. The gap between them decides how the work is sequenced. The table shows how we usually think about it.

WindowWhat to prioritizeWhat to move to another day
Wide window, next guest arrives lateFull reset, laundry on site, restock checkNothing; use the time for staging
Standard windowKitchen, bathrooms, beds, floors, trashDeep tasks like oven interiors
Same-day tight windowBeds, bathrooms, kitchen surfaces, floorsDetail work, grout, inside appliances

Same-day turnovers need tighter scheduling, and that is a pricing factor too. A tight window is workable, but it should be planned a few days ahead and not decided the morning of.

Seasons change the picture too. In humid Upstate summers, a closed-up rental can smell stale by the time the next guest walks in, so a quick window opening and a check of the bathroom drains help. In fall and winter, the entry mat and the floor near the door pick up leaves and wet footprints. Holiday weekends bring back-to-back bookings, and those are the dates when a spare hour in the schedule is worth more than any extra chore.

The checklist, one pass through the property

  • Strip the beds and start laundry first so linens are drying while you clean
  • Run the kitchen: dishes, counters, appliance fronts, microwave, and clear the fridge of anything left behind
  • Bathrooms cleaned and disinfected, mirror polished, towels replaced
  • Dust surfaces, switches and door handles, and wipe the glass on sliding doors
  • Make the beds with fresh linens (host-provided)
  • Vacuum and mop, finishing at the exit
  • Remove trash and reset the bins
  • Stage the rooms to match your listing photos

Our crew works top to bottom and back to front, so dust and crumbs fall onto floors that are not done yet. If you only do one thing differently after reading this, make it that.

Keep one rule for yourself: never plan a turnover with zero slack. If a guest leaves forty minutes late or a bathroom needs a second pass, a buffer of even thirty minutes keeps the arrival on time.

Check for damage and missing items before you clean

Walk the property once before touching anything, and note what looks different from your listing. A chipped mug, a missing remote or a stain on the couch is easier to document when nothing has been moved. We include a damage and missing-item report with photos for owners who are not on site, which is useful if you live out of town. For more on running a rental from a distance, see our post on managing a rental from out of town.

Restocking is part of the turn

Look at the things guests notice when they run out: toilet paper, soap, trash bags, coffee supplies, dish soap. A restocking checklist keeps you from finding out at 9 p.m. that the spare roll is gone. Keep a closet or labeled bin for supplies, and note on your list how many of each item should be on hand after a turn. Our cleaning visit can check the supplies and flag what is low, though the supplies themselves are yours to provide.
