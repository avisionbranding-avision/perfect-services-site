Three kinds of space we see near I-85

I-85 runs through Greer alongside SC-14, SC-101 and US-29, and it puts a mix of businesses within a short drive of GSP Airport and Downtown Greer. In our experience the spaces fall into three groups, and each one needs a slightly different plan.

Start with the path a client walks

When we do a walk-through, we begin at the front door and follow the route a visitor takes. If a client parks, enters, waits and uses the restroom, those four stops get the careful pass. A desk in the back can wait. This order is the same for a salon, a clinic or a home office that sees one client a week.

  • Entry mat and door glass
  • Door handles, switches and keypads
  • Waiting or reception area, dusted and wiped
  • Restroom cleaned, disinfected and restocked
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped from the farthest corner toward the door
  • Trash and recycling removed

Home offices: keeping the line between work and home

The home office is the one most people overlook. The desk gets wiped, but the keyboard edges, the monitor base, the cable tangle and the baseboard behind the chair do not. We clean those as part of a regular visit, and we do it in the same trip as the rest of the house if you also use our office cleaning in Greer service for a separate workspace.

If paperwork covers the desk, we work around it. We do not stack, sort or move documents, because it is your business and we would rather leave a pile where it is than guess. Tell us which surfaces are off limits and we mark them on the checklist.

How often should it happen?

For a space with regular visitors, weekly is a common starting point. A home office with no clients can go every other week and still stay presentable. Daily service is mostly for busy retail or shared spaces with heavy restroom use. Our suggestion is to start slightly more often, then reduce once you see how the space holds up.

Small things that make a visit go well

Three habits help a small workspace stay clean between visits. Keep a bin by the entry so paper and packaging do not pile on the desk. Leave the restroom stocked, or tell us what you prefer us to restock. And clear the floor of cables, boxes and bags before we arrive, since a vacuum cannot reach under a stack. None of this is a requirement, but it lets the team spend the time on edges and corners instead of moving things.

If clients come to your home office, give them a clear route from the door to the desk and tell us about it. We will focus the careful work along that route, and treat the rest of the house as the household space it is.
