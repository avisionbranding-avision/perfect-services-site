What changes in a rental when the weather turns

The big change is what walks through the door. Leaves, grass clippings and red clay come in on shoes, and guests wiping their feet on a thin mat do not fix that. The second change is heat. The first weeks the furnace runs, dust that settled in the vents and on baseboard heaters burns off with a dry smell and lands on the nearest surface. And the third change is length of stay: families visiting for Thanksgiving unpack, cook and use every room.

The entry is the first review

Guests arriving at dusk in November see the porch before the living room. Sweep leaves off the steps, empty any planter saucer with standing water, and wipe the door handle and lockbox, which collects fingerprints. Our crews treat the entry as part of the turnover, since a muddy mat tells the guest the whole place was rushed. If your property sits close to GSP Airport or the BMW plant, you probably get business travelers and relocation stays that arrive late, so the entry light and the first floor need to look right in the dark.

Holiday bookings need a longer kitchen pass

Thanksgiving guests actually cook. That means grease on the stovetop and backsplash, a sticky oven rack, a fridge with leftovers a guest forgot and a trash can that fills past what the bin liner expects. The kitchen gets the most time in a fall turnover:

  • Empty the fridge and wipe shelves, including door bins.
  • Degrease the stovetop and the wall behind it.
  • Wipe the microwave inside and the handle of every appliance.
  • Check cabinets and drawers near the stove for stuck-on drips.
  • Reset the coffee station, the dish rack and the sponge.

A host who wants the oven cleaned inside can add it as an extra, which is easier to plan before the holiday week than during it.

A fall prep list for hosts

  • Add a second, sturdier doormat and a boot tray if guests visit in wet weather.
  • Replace the furnace filter before the first long stay.
  • Stock extra blankets and a spare set of sheets for each bed.
  • Remove décor that cannot survive a full house, such as fragile candles near the dining table.
  • Send us your booking calendar so the team can plan around back-to-back stays.

Linens, throws and the cold-weather laundry load

Fall bedding is heavier. A duvet, an extra blanket and a couple of throw pillows turn a one-sheet change into a bigger load, and a rental with several beds can double the laundry time compared with August. Decide early whether linens are washed on site or sent out, because that choice affects both the turnover window and the final price. Hosts who keep a second full set of bedding for every bed save the most time, since the clean set goes on while the used one is washed. If a guest spills red wine or coffee on a comforter, say so in your house notes so the team treats it right away rather than finding it at the next change.

Fireplaces and gas logs, where a rental has them, need a quick look too. Ash and soot settle on the hearth and mantel, and a guest sitting by the fire for a holiday weekend leaves more than a summer guest leaves in the living room. We sweep and wipe the hearth area and flag anything that needs a specialist rather than a cleaner.

Usually a bit longer than summer for the same unit, because of the kitchen, the entry and the extra laundry from heavier bedding.
