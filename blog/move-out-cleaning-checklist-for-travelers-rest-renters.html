Move-out cleaning checklist for Travelers Rest renters

Renting in Travelers Rest? Use this room-by-room move out cleaning checklist for Travelers Rest, SC to leave the place ready for the final walkthrough.

Before you start: empty first, clean second

Cleaning around boxes doubles the time. Get the rooms as empty as you can first, including closets and the garage shelf, and keep a small kit by the door: a bucket, microfiber cloths, a scrub brush, a trash bag, a vacuum, a mop and a few paper towels. Travelers Rest sits on the Swamp Rabbit Trail with homes stretching toward the mountains along US-25 and US-276, so many renters here have been hiking, biking and tracking trail dust through the entry for months. Look at the floor by the front door and the mat first.

The order that saves you time

  1. Top to bottom, back to front. Start at ceiling fans and vents, then shelves, then countertops, then floors. Dust falls, so floors come last.
  2. Kitchen before bathrooms. Grease needs soak time. Spray, let it sit while you move to another room, then wipe.
  3. Bathrooms next. Hard water and soap film also need dwell time.
  4. Bedrooms and living areas. Mostly dusting, baseboards and vacuuming.
  5. Floors, edge to edge, backing out the front door. Mop toward the exit so you do not walk on wet floor.

Room-by-room checklist

Kitchen

  • Inside and outside of every cabinet and drawer, including the shelf liner crumbs.
  • Inside the refrigerator and freezer: shelves, drawers, door seals. Leave doors propped open after.
  • Inside the oven and microwave; scrape the racks.
  • Range hood, stovetop and backsplash degreased. The hood filter is the part renters forget.
  • Sink, faucet and around the drain.

Bathrooms

  • Toilet inside, outside and around the base where the bolts collect grime.
  • Shower and tub scrubbed, grout lines brushed, glass door tracks cleaned.
  • Vanity top, interior of drawers and under the sink.
  • Mirrors and chrome polished last so they are streak free.

Bedrooms, closets and living areas

  • Closet shelves, rods and floors; wipe the closet door tracks.
  • Baseboards, doors, door frames and light switches. Hand marks show up fast under bright lights.
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds.
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped edge to edge, including under where the furniture stood.

The spots that cost renters deposits

From what we see in many move-outs, the same few places get flagged. Grease on top of the cabinets, a film inside the oven, hair around the bathroom floor edges, and dust in the window tracks. None of these is hard. They are just easy to leave for last and then run out of time. The same logic is in our article on getting your security deposit back, which also covers photos and paperwork.

Take dated photos of each room when you finish. Your lease and your landlord decide what counts as normal wear, so read that section too, and keep the clean to the standard you received at move-in.
