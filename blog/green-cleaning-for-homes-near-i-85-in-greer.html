Step 1: let the air work

A green visit starts before the first wipe, with a few small decisions that cut down on product and fragrance for the rest of the day.

Before anything gets wiped, we open a window if the weather allows. In late fall and winter that may mean a few minutes at a time, but it clears loose dust and gets fresh air through rooms that have been shut. If anyone in the house reacts to smells, tell us up front and we will choose the fragrance-free option from the start.

Step 2: dry dust before wet cleaning

In practice this is where road dust shows up. Light, fine dust settles on the track of a window or the top of a door frame, and it is easy to push around with the wrong tool. Microfiber, a light hand and a second pass in the same spot lift it for good. We also shake out and replace cloths often, so we are not carrying yesterday's dust to a clean shelf.

Microfiber cloths pick up dust and hold it, where a feather duster sends it back into the air. We work from the top down: fans, shelves, picture frames, window sills, then baseboards and door frames. A dry pass first matters because wiping dust with a wet cloth turns it into a film that is harder to remove.

Step 3: kitchen and bathrooms with gentler products

These two rooms take the most time, and the method matters most here because they get the most use and the most contact with hands and food.

  • Counters, sink and backsplash wiped by hand with low-odor, family- and pet-friendly products
  • Stove top and appliance exteriors cleaned and degreased
  • Showers, tubs, toilets and vanities cleaned and disinfected
  • Mirrors and glass polished with a surface-safe solution
  • Handles, switches and cabinet pulls wiped, since these are touched most

Between steps, we pay attention to the small spots that people touch all day: light switches, handles on the fridge and the dishwasher, the remote on the coffee table. They rarely look dirty, but they carry the most use. A hand wipe with a clean cloth is gentle, quick and does the job, and it is the kind of detail that separates a tidy house from a clean one. Hand-wiping uses less product than spraying everything down, and it lets us feel where a spot needs a second pass.

Step 4: floors last

The floor is the last thing finished and often what you notice first when you walk back in.

If you want to keep the result going between visits, a few habits help in a home near a busy road: a doormat at each entrance, shoes off at the door, and a quick damp microfiber pass on window sills once a week. None of this is required, but it stretches the time between professional visits.

Vacuuming comes before mopping, and mopping comes last, so every footprint we leave is behind us as we go. Hardwood, tile and laminate each get a surface-safe solution. In a home with pets we pay extra attention to the edges and the spaces under furniture, where hair collects.
