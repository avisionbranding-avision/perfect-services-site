Questions to ask before hiring office cleaning in Greer

Hiring office cleaning in Greer, SC? Ask about schedule, restrooms, keys, supplies and the free in-home estimate before you sign with a cleaning company.

Perfect Services Team
November 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Settle keys, alarm codes and after-hours access before the first night.
  • Ask what happens to restrooms, trash and the break room on every visit.
  • Price comes after a free walk-through of the space.

Most office cleaning problems in Greer are not about the cleaning. They are about the key that did not work, the alarm code nobody wrote down, or the trash that was left because the contract never mentioned the break room. If you are about to hire a cleaning company for a small office, clinic, salon or shop, these are the questions that prevent those problems.

Who has the keys, and how do we get in?

Start with access. Most offices are cleaned after hours, which means the crew arrives when nobody is there to open the door. Ask how they handle keys, codes and the alarm, who at the company knows them and what happens if a team member changes. Write it down: which door, what time, whose job it is to lock up. This is a boring conversation that saves a lot of trouble later, especially in strip-center offices along US-29 and Wade Hampton where the landlord also controls the entry.

What gets cleaned on every visit?

Get the list, in writing. The office cleaning service in Greer from Perfect Services covers desks and workstations, high-touch points like handles, switches and keypads, restrooms, the break room sink and fridge exterior, trash and recycling, floors and glass doors. Other companies may leave out the high-touch points or charge extra for the break room. A useful way to check is to ask for a list split into daily, weekly and monthly tasks.

  • Every visit: trash, restrooms, floors, high-touch points
  • Weekly: desks, break room, glass, reception dusting
  • Monthly: baseboards, vents, deeper floor care

Who supplies the paper, soap and trash bags?

Restrooms are where offices get judged by clients. Ask whether restocking is included, who buys the supplies and what happens if you run out in the middle of the week. Some companies restock from their own inventory and bill you; others use what you leave on a shelf. Either approach works if it is clear.

Will we have a walk-through before pricing?

A company that quotes an office by phone is guessing. A visit lets them count restrooms, see the floor types, ask about hours and understand where the foot traffic is. How much does office cleaning cost in Greer?

The factors are square footage, how often you want service (daily, weekly or monthly), the number of restrooms and how busy they are, the hours you need and the floor types: carpet, tile and hard floors each need different care. We do not publish dollar amounts. The exact price comes after the walk-through, and you can compare it with other quotes line by line.

What to settle on the first night

The first visit is the one that decides whether the arrangement works. Agree on a walk-through before the crew starts so you can point out the spots that matter to you: the conference table, the front counter where clients lean, the one restroom that gets the most use. Ask the team to leave a short note after the first night about anything they could not reach, like locked rooms or a closet full of boxes. It takes two minutes, and it keeps small gaps from becoming habits.

Also talk about what you will do on your side. Staff who clear their desks, empty personal mugs and keep food out of the trash cans make the nightly work faster, which is good for everyone. In offices with a fridge, agree on a day when old lunches get thrown out, since cleaning the exterior is part of the service but the contents are not.

If you share a building, ask whether the landlord already provides common-area cleaning. That avoids paying twice for the hallway and lets the quote focus on your suite.

What if something goes wrong?

Ask how complaints are handled. Is there a person to call or text? How soon do they respond? Do they inspect their own work? A good company welcomes a short note like "the second restroom was missed" and fixes it on the next visit or sooner. A company that makes this hard to do will make every other thing hard too.

Also ask about insurance and how they handle damage. You do not need a legal lecture, just a plain answer.
