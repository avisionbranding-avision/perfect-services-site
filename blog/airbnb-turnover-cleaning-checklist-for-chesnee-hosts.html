Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Chesnee hosts

Airbnb cleaning Chesnee SC: a host checklist for turnovers, restocking and damage reports, plus how pricing works after a free in-home estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 12, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Run every turnover in the same order so nothing gets skipped, from the first trash bag to the last staging touch.
  • Photograph damage and missing items before you clean, not after.
  • Chesnee is about 40 minutes from Greer, so book recurring turnovers on a set schedule instead of ad hoc.

If your guests check out at 11 and the next ones arrive at 4, you have a five-hour window and no room for guessing. This is the checklist we would hand a host who wants consistent Airbnb cleaning in Chesnee, SC, whether you run the turnover yourself or hire it out.

Chesnee is a rural foothills town in Spartanburg County (ZIP 29323), reached on US-221 and SC-11, about 25 miles northeast of our base in Greer. Many rentals out here are farmhouses, cabins and country homes with porches and gravel drives, and guests tend to track in more dirt than a town condo would see. That shapes the order of the work.

Start outside, then work in

Do a quick lap before you touch anything inside. Check the porch, the steps and the door mat, since a muddy entry is the first thing a guest notices and the first thing that ends up on your floors again. Empty the outdoor trash and reset the bins. Then walk the interior once, without cleaning, and note anything that looks different from your listing photos.

Take your own photos at this point. If a lamp is cracked or a towel is missing, you want a record dated before the cleaning starts. Our crew includes a damage and missing-item report with photos for exactly this reason, because a host who is two hours away cannot walk the property.

The turnover checklist, in the order we work

Top to bottom, back of the house to the front door. That way dust and crumbs fall onto floors that have not been done yet.

  • Strip all beds and start laundry before anything else, so linens are drying while you clean.
  • Kitchen first: run the dishes, wipe counters, clean the appliance fronts, empty the fridge of anything left behind.
  • Bathrooms next: disinfect the toilet, tub or shower and sink, polish the mirror, replace towels.
  • Dust surfaces, switches and door handles, then check the tracks of sliding doors.
  • Make the beds with fresh linens (host-provided), then vacuum and mop floors, finishing at the exit.
  • Remove trash and reset the bins.
  • Stage the rooms to match your listing photos: pillows, throws, remote controls where they belong.

The staging step is the one hosts skip when they are rushed. It is also what makes a guest feel the place looks like the pictures.

Restocking and what to report

A turnover is also your supply check. Look at toilet paper, soap, trash bags, coffee supplies and anything else you promise in the listing. A restocking list, kept on the fridge or in a notes app, turns this into a one-minute scan rather than a memory test.

Report things in the same message each time: what was damaged, what is running low, what was left behind. In a rural area where the nearest store may be a drive away, catching a shortage before the next arrival matters more than it would in town.

A short note on guest-ready details

Guests rarely complain about the big things. They notice a hair in the shower drain, a sticky cabinet handle, a coffee ring on the nightstand or a faint smell in the fridge. In a country rental, add the porch light switch, the screen door handle and the boot scraper to your list, because those are touched the moment someone arrives with luggage.

It also helps to keep one laminated page in the house with the turnover order, so anyone covering for you, a family member or a neighbor, follows the same steps. A cleaner who works from the same list each time makes it easier for you to spot when something is off.

Deciding between doing it yourself and hiring out

If you live close and have two or three turnovers a month, doing it yourself can work. If your rental is in Chesnee and you live in another city, the drive itself becomes the cost. A recurring cleaner who knows the property, knows where you keep the spare linens and sends photos after each turnover turns a stressful day into a notification on your phone.
