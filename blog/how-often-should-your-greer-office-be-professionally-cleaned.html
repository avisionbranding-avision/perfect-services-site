How often should your Greer office be professionally cleaned?

How often should your Greer office be professionally cleaned? A practical guide to daily, weekly and monthly office cleaning service, with a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 10, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Restrooms and the break room need the most frequent attention; storage and private offices can wait longer.
  • Most small offices land on two or three visits a week, or one weekly visit for very low-traffic spaces.
  • Perfect Services prices office cleaning after a free walk-through, not from square footage alone.

There is no single right answer to how often your office should be cleaned, but there is a right way to find yours. It comes down to how many people come through the door, how many restrooms they share and whether customers walk in. A three-person accounting office and a clinic with a waiting room can sit on the same street in Greer and need completely different schedules.

Below is the way we help owners work it out, starting with the rooms that matter most. If you are comparing providers at the same time, our post on how to choose a commercial cleaning company in Greer covers that side of the decision.

Start with traffic, not square footage

Square footage tells us how long a visit takes. Traffic tells us how often it needs to happen. Ask three questions:

  • How many people use the restrooms in a typical day?
  • Do customers or clients come in, or is it staff only?
  • Does anyone eat in the office, or is there a shared kitchen?

If the answers are "a handful", "no" and "no", you can often go weekly. If they are "a dozen or more", "yes" and "yes", plan on visits several times a week, because restrooms and the break room show wear first.

Treat these as starting points. After a month, you will see which rooms are fine and which are not, and the schedule can shift. Our commercial and office cleaning is set up for that kind of adjustment.

What gets cleaned every visit and what rotates

A good office schedule is not the same list every time. Some tasks are every visit, others are monthly or quarterly, and the second group is what keeps the space from looking tired.

Every visit: desks and counters wiped, high-touch points such as handles, switches and keypads, restrooms cleaned and restocked, trash and recycling removed, floors vacuumed and mopped, entry glass and reception dusted. On a rotation: the break room fridge exterior and microwave, baseboards, vents, the tracks of sliding doors and glass partitions, and the corners under desks that daily vacuuming misses.

Seasonal pressure in the Upstate

Greer offices see the same seasonal swings as homes. Spring pollen blows in whenever the door opens, and the front entry gets a yellow film that a daily wipe can handle. Summer humidity makes restrooms and break rooms need more attention. Fall and winter bring wet shoes and red clay onto carpet near the door. A mat at the entrance and a quick entry-floor pass can cut the work for the rest of the building.

Who in the office can help

One person should own the cleaning relationship, usually the office manager or the owner. That person gets the visit notes, reports anything missed and approves changes. Staff can help in small ways without taking on cleaning: rinse mugs, clear desks of loose papers on the nights the team comes, and keep food out of the trash that sits under desks. Cleaners can only clean surfaces they can reach, and a cleared desk is a cleaned desk. Add a note on the door for the cleaning team if any room needs to stay untouched, such as a records room or a lab bench.

How to test a schedule before you commit

If you are unsure between two options, start with the lower frequency for a month and track three things: how the restrooms look on the day before a visit, whether staff are wiping the break room themselves, and whether visitors comment on the entry. If the restrooms are in poor shape the day before the cleaners arrive, add a visit. If everything looks acceptable on the night before, you may have room to hold or lower the frequency.
