Brazilian cleaning during Upstate pollen season in Greer

Why hand-wiping beats spraying when pollen season hits Greer SC. How Brazilian house cleaning handles sills, baseboards, floors and fabrics in spring.

Key takeaways

  • Pollen moves when it is sprayed or flicked; hand-wiping and damp cloths pick it up instead.
  • The corners and edges are where spring dust actually lives.

There is a quick way to tell whether a house was cleaned by wiping or by spraying. In April, run a finger along the top of a window frame. If it comes up yellow, the surface was sprayed and flicked, not wiped. Brazilian house cleaning is built on the second approach: cloth in hand, surface by surface, which is exactly what pollen season in Greer calls for.

Why pollen defeats a quick clean

Pollen is light and sticky. A dry duster lifts it into the air and drops it a few feet away. A heavy spray on a dusty surface turns it into a streak. Neither removes it. What does remove it is a damp microfiber cloth used with enough pressure to pick it up, folded so a clean side is always facing the surface, and changed often. It takes longer, and it is why a Brazilian clean feels different when it is done.

In Greer, spring pollen arrives alongside the usual red clay on shoes and humid air from the Upstate. Homes along SC-14, SC-101 and US-29, from older houses near Downtown to newer ones near GSP Airport, are all affected, and the heaviest accumulation is usually where the air stops moving.

The spring list, the way our team works

  1. Start high. Ceiling corners, fan blades and the tops of door frames, so nothing falls onto a clean floor later.
  2. Window sills and tracks. Vacuum or brush first, then wipe damp. Never wet pollen first.
  3. Baseboards by hand. This is where pollen and pet hair collect along the wall.
  4. Furniture moved. Small items lifted and the surface under them cleaned, then returned.
  5. Floors last, along the walls. Hand-washed edges first, then the open area.

What we look at in the first hour

Before touching anything, the team walks the home. They are checking which windows face the yard and are likely to have the most pollen, where the dog sleeps, which bathrooms get daily use and which rooms need extra time. Those notes set the order of the day. It also tells us what to ask you about, such as whether you prefer us to use your own products. The Brazilian detail-first method is, in plain terms, a checklist and a walk-through done the same way every visit.

Spots people miss

  • The track of a sliding patio door, where pollen and soil gather
  • Light switches and door handles
  • Door frames, top and sides
  • Baseboards and the edge where the floor meets the wall
  • Under the edge of the couch and bed
  • Bathroom exhaust fan covers

A first visit to an average home is usually a half-day to a full day, depending on size and condition. Fabrics, bedding and soft surfaces

Pollen does not stay on hard surfaces. It gets into curtains, couch cushions and the bed. The team does not wash your linens unless asked, but we vacuum upholstery edges, wipe the headboard and the window ledge beside the bed, and shake out removable throw rugs outside before putting them back. If someone in the house has seasonal allergies, tell us which room matters most. The bedroom is usually the answer, and we will start there when the schedule allows.

A simple habit at home helps between visits: leave shoes by the door, and wipe the pet's paws after a walk. Pollen travels on fur as much as on shoes, and those two habits reduce what we find on the floor by the next visit.

What to do before the team arrives

Clear the surfaces you want cleaned under, such as nightstands and the kitchen counter, and put away anything fragile. Leave a note if there is a window that sticks or a surface you want us to avoid. Close the windows the evening before, since an open window overnight in April can undo a lot of work. If a pet is nervous around visitors, tell us so we can plan the order of rooms.

Then let the team work. A Brazilian clean is a slow, ordered process, and the result is easiest to see when the house is left alone for the final hour.

Frequently asked questions

Why is hand-wiping better than spraying during pollen season?
