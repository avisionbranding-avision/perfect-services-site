Apartment cleaning checklist for Duncan renters

Room-by-room apartment cleaning checklist for Duncan, SC renters, with deposit-friendly details and how apartment cleaning is priced after a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 26, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Small spaces get dirty in specific places: balcony door tracks, bathroom grout and the oven hood.
  • A written checklist keeps a monthly clean from turning into a monthly argument about what counts.
  • Apartment pricing is set after a free estimate, based on unit size, bathrooms, pets and access.

Duncan renters have a different cleaning problem from homeowners: there is less space, so everything piles up in the same few places. A one-bedroom unit near the I-85 corridor collects dust on the balcony track, grease behind the stove and hair in a single bathroom that gets used all the time. The checklist below goes room by room and shows what to do yourself and what to hand to a service.

Duncan, in Spartanburg County (ZIP 29334), is a fast-growing town along I-85, US-29 and SC-290, only five miles east of our Greer base. Many of the newer communities have gates and parking rules, which matter when a cleaner needs access, so we ask about them early.

Kitchen

  • Wipe the counters, then the cabinet fronts and handles.
  • Clean the stovetop and pull the drip pans if they come out.
  • Wipe the inside of the microwave and the fridge shelf spills.
  • Check the range hood filter, since grease collects there first.
  • Clean the sink and wipe appliance fronts.

Bathroom

  • Scrub the toilet, including the base and behind it.
  • Shower and tub: soap film and any grout that has darkened.
  • Mirror and vanity, including the faucet base.
  • Floor edges, where hair collects along the baseboard.

Bedrooms, living room and entry

  • Dust surfaces, shelves and the tops of frames.
  • Vacuum under the bed and the couch.
  • Wipe light switches, door handles and the thermostat.
  • Clean baseboards and door frames.
  • Glass of the balcony or patio door, and the track it slides in.

The balcony track is the one most renters forget. Sand and pollen settle there and turn into a gray paste. A toothbrush and a damp cloth fix it in minutes.

Time and scheduling

A small apartment is often finished in a couple of hours. We can often get you on the calendar within the week, although the days before holidays and month-end move-outs fill up quickly. If your landlord does a walk-through, book at least two days ahead.

Keep the deposit in mind

Take dated photos of the unit when you move in and again after cleaning. Landlords commonly deduct for dirty ovens, stained grout and marked walls, so those are worth spending time on.

Renters in other towns follow the same logic, and our Gray Court renters checklist shows how it changes in a smaller town. Nearby, we also serve Lyman.

Duncan-specific things worth knowing

Newer subdivisions and apartment communities around I-85 and US-29 are often built with builder-grade carpet and light-colored paint, which shows scuffs and footprints quickly. Wipe walls near light switches and door frames with a damp cloth, and spot-check carpet near the entrance, where grit from the parking lot gets carried in. Spring pollen drifts onto balconies, so the glass of the sliding door needs wiping more often than you would expect, and the track collects a yellow-green film from March through May.

A realistic weekly routine for a small unit

If you only have thirty minutes, do this: bathroom first (while the cleaner works, the tub drains), then kitchen counters and stovetop, then vacuum the main walking path, then take out the trash. Do the baseboards and appliance exteriors once a month. A short routine kept up weekly takes far less time than a big clean every two months.

What to prepare for a visit

  • Put away loose clothes and papers so surfaces can be wiped.
  • Tell us about pets and anything fragile.
  • Leave a parking spot or visitor pass if your community requires one.
  • Mention any area you want special attention on, such as the shower grout.

With that done, the visit can focus on cleaning rather than moving boxes around.

Common mistakes renters make

Three come up often. Using harsh cleaners on a shower surface that is not tile can dull it. Leaving the oven for move-out day means baked-on grease that takes far longer to remove. And skipping the baseboards makes an otherwise clean unit look dusty in a landlord's photos. A little regular attention avoids all three, and a service can step in when you run short of time.

Pets, plants and small-space habits

In a small unit, a litter box or pet bed affects the whole air of the place, so wash pet bedding often and wipe the floor beside food bowls. Plants on a balcony shed soil onto the tile, which a quick sweep handles. These are minor habits, but they keep a monthly clean from turning into a major one.
