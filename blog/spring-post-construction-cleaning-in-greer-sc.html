Spring post-construction cleaning in Greer, SC

Starting a remodel to finish by spring? Plan spring post construction cleaning in Greer, SC with phases, pollen tips and a free in-home estimate.

If you start a kitchen remodel, addition or bathroom renovation in the next few months, it will probably finish right as the Upstate pollen season does. Contractors open doors and windows to ventilate paint and adhesive, and a thin layer of yellow pollen comes in with the breeze. Planning your post construction cleaning in Greer now, in November, lets the schedule fit your contractor's.

Why spring finishes are messier than they look

Construction dust and spring pollen are two separate problems on the same surfaces. Drywall powder is white and heavy, and it collects on ledges and in vents. Pollen is lighter, but it settles on the same window sills and new countertops, and it sticks to any damp surface. The result, when you walk into a freshly finished room in April, is a film that wipes up grey-green and shows on dark floors. A final clean needs two passes: one for the construction residue and another once the last doors are closed.

Book the cleaning in three phases

Many people hire one cleaning for the end of the project. A project that runs several weeks works better with up to three visits.

  1. Rough clean: after demolition and framing, to clear debris and heavy dust before finish trades arrive.
  2. Final clean: after paint, trim and fixtures are done, covering cabinets, windows, floors and bathrooms.
  3. Touch-up: a short visit a few days later, after dust and pollen settle from the HVAC and open doors.

You do not need all three. A smaller project like a single bathroom may only need the final clean. The estimate visit is where we decide which phases make sense.

What the final clean covers

  • Fine dust removed from surfaces, ledges, trim and the tops of door frames
  • Inside cabinets, drawers and closets
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills, with paint specks and sticker residue removed
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished
  • Floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes
  • A last touch-up pass before move-in

The team works from the ceiling down, so dust falls onto surfaces still to be done. Floors always come last, which is the opposite of what many homeowners expect and the reason their DIY efforts end with a second sweep.

Talking with your contractor about the cleaning

Some builders include a basic sweep at the end of the job, others do not, and it helps to know which kind you have. Ask what the contractor considers finished: whether they wipe down surfaces, whether the tape and protective film come off, and who removes the leftover materials. A professional final clean assumes the debris is gone and the trades are done, so the less that remains, the more time the team can spend on detail.

It also helps to agree on a handoff. One approach we see work is for the contractor to text the homeowner on the last day of work, and for the homeowner to share that date with us right away. That way the clean follows within a day or two, before furniture comes back and before anyone starts living in the space. If the schedule slips, as remodels often do in wet weather, a call or text is enough to move the date.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to wait until the project is fully finished before booking?

No. It helps to book the estimate early so a team is ready when the last trade leaves, but the final clean itself should happen after all work is done.

Will spring pollen undo the final clean?

Some pollen can settle again if doors are open. That is why a short touch-up visit a few days later is an option on larger projects.

Do you clean inside new cabinets and closets?

Yes. Inside cabinets, drawers and closets are part of the final clean, since sawdust and shims often end up there.
