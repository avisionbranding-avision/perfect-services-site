Move-out cleaning checklist for Greenville renters

Start with the room you will want to quit on

Do the kitchen first, while you still have energy. It is where most deposit disputes begin, and it is also the slowest room. A lot of the move-outs we walk into in Greenville have a clean countertop and an oven that has not been opened since the last tenant.

  • Empty the refrigerator and freezer, pull the shelves and drawers, and wash them in the sink. Wipe the door gaskets, where mold likes to hide.
  • Oven: spray, wait, and scrub the floor of the oven and the inside of the door glass. Pull the racks out and soak them.
  • Range hood and filter. Grease collects there faster than anywhere else.
  • Inside and outside of every cabinet and drawer. Crumbs live in the corners of the silverware drawer.
  • Dishwasher filter, microwave interior, and the sink drain area.

Bathrooms: the details that read as neglect

Shower glass film and dark grout are what stand out. Soap scum comes off with patience more than with force, so give the cleaner a few minutes on the surface before you scrub. Do the toilet base and the wall behind it, since that is a spot people forget. Wipe inside the vanity, the tub overflow plate, and the mirror edges.

The overlooked list

These are the items that rarely make a rental checklist but show up in an inspection photo:

  • Baseboards and the tops of door frames
  • Light switch plates and door handles
  • Window sills and the tracks of sliding doors
  • Closet shelves and the floor underneath where boxes sat
  • Ceiling fan blades and vent covers
  • Blinds, slat by slat

Work from top to bottom. Dust falls, so vents and fans come before the floors. Vacuum the carpet last, and mop hard floors from the far corner back to the door so you do not walk across wet tile with your boxes.

How long it takes, honestly

A one-bedroom that was lived in carefully can take one person most of a day. A three-bedroom with a heavy oven, two bathrooms and carpet will take a team several hours and a single person a full weekend. If your keys are due before noon, that math matters. A realistic evening plan

If you only have one evening, split it. Hour one: kitchen appliances, because the oven and fridge need soaking time. While the oven cleaner works, move to the bathrooms and spray the shower. Hour two: dusting from the ceiling down, then cabinets and closets. Hour three: baseboards, tracks and sills. The last stretch is floors. Keep a bag for trash and a second one for things you are donating, so nothing gets scooped into the wrong pile at midnight.

Downtown condos near Main Street often have elevator reservations and parking limits that cut into cleaning time, and apartment communities off I-85 sometimes require the carpet to be left a certain way. Read the move-out sheet before the day, not during it.

Mistakes we see in empty rentals

  • Cleaning the oven last, when it needed the most soak time
  • Wiping a shower wall without touching the track or the drain cover
  • Leaving a few forgotten items in closets and cabinets that then count as trash left behind
  • Forgetting the garage or storage closet, which some leases include
  • Skipping photos, then having nothing to show when a charge appears

None of these take long to avoid, but they are the ones that cost deposits. A move-out is also a good time to check small repairs like loose cabinet handles or nail holes your lease says to patch, since those are separate from cleaning.
