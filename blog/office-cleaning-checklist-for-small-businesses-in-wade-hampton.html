Daily office cleaning tasks

Daily work keeps the office presentable and the shared spaces sanitary. Most small offices do these tasks after hours or before opening.

  • Empty trash and replace liners in all bins
  • Wipe reception counters, desks that are cleared and conference tables
  • Disinfect high-touch points: door handles, light switches, shared phones and elevator buttons
  • Clean and restock restrooms: toilets, sinks, mirrors, soap and paper
  • Vacuum high-traffic carpet and mop hard floors at the entrance
  • Clean the break room counters, sink and microwave exterior

Weekly tasks

Weekly tasks are the details that people notice only when they are skipped.

  • Dust desks, shelves, monitors and window sills
  • Full vacuum of all carpet, including corners and under desks
  • Wipe glass doors and interior windows
  • Clean the inside of the break room refrigerator and microwave
  • Sanitize shared equipment such as the copier and keyboard area

Monthly and seasonal tasks

  • Dust vents, blinds and the tops of cabinets and door frames
  • Spot-clean walls and doors
  • Deep-clean restroom grout and floors
  • Wash exterior glass at the entry and clean entry mats

How long does it take?

A small office of a few rooms and one restroom is often a short visit, while a larger suite with multiple restrooms and a break room takes longer. Recurring visits tend to be quicker than the first one because the first clean sets the baseline.

Restroom and entry standards

Customers judge a business by two spaces they see quickly: the entryway and the restroom. In a busy Wade Hampton retail suite or professional office, both deserve extra attention.

  • Check the toilet base, the area behind the toilet and the floor edges, where grime hides
  • Keep soap and paper stocked, with a spare at hand
  • Wipe mirrors and faucets so water spots do not build up
  • Vacuum or mop the entry daily, since sand, rain and pollen track in
  • Clean glass doors inside and out, including handles and push plates

Seasonal needs in the Upstate

Office cleaning changes with the year. Spring pollen collects on window sills and entry mats. Summer humidity makes restroom and break room surfaces need more frequent wipe-downs. Fall and winter bring wet shoes, salt and mud onto carpet, so entry mats and vacuuming schedules matter more. Many offices add a deeper clean at the start of each quarter to reset vents, baseboards and carpet edges.

Questions to ask before you hire

  • Is the scope written down, and does it list each task by frequency?
  • Who supervises the team, and how do you reach them with a problem?
  • What is the key and alarm process, and who holds the codes?
  • Is there a trial period, and how are missed items corrected?
