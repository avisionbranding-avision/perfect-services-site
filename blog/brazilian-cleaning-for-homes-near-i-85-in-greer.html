People who live close to I-85 in Greer often tell us the same thing: the house looks fine from the couch and feels gritty at the door. Homes near a busy road, with SC-14, SC-101 and US-29 close by, pick up fine dust on sills and entry floors, and in spring the Upstate pollen adds a yellow layer to everything flat. Brazilian house cleaning is a good match for that kind of house, and there are a few myths about it worth clearing up first.

Myth: it is a special product or recipe

It is not. Perfect Services is a Brazilian-owned team based in Greer, and what we bring is a method. Surfaces are wiped by hand, corners and baseboards are cleaned with a cloth or small brush, and the team moves small furniture to clean under it. The tools are ordinary. The care is the difference.

Myth: it is only for deep cleans

The detailed approach works for a regular visit too. A deep clean is a bigger version, useful for a first visit or a home that has gone a long time without one. After that, the regular Brazilian clean keeps the edges from building up again, so you are not paying for a reset every time.

Fact: homes near the interstate need an entry-first plan

This is where our experience near I-85 shows. The team works the entry first: the door frame, the threshold, the floor just inside, and the track of any sliding glass door. Grit collects in those spots and gets walked through the house if it is left. We clean them by hand rather than relying on a mop pass.

  • Door frames, switches and handles detailed
  • Window sills and tracks wiped
  • Baseboards cleaned by hand
  • Floors hand-washed along the walls
  • Bathrooms scrubbed including grout and toilet base
  • Kitchen degreased: stovetop, hood, cabinet fronts

Fact: pollen season changes the order

In spring we dust higher surfaces first and wipe sills before floors, so pollen that falls during the visit does not land on a finished room. We wrote about this in Brazilian cleaning during Upstate pollen season in Greer. Red clay tracked in during wet weather is a similar story, and it is why entry floors are washed, not just vacuumed.

What to expect on the day

The team arrives, walks the house with you, and then works in a set order: kitchen, bathrooms, living areas and bedrooms, floors last. A final walk-through against the room checklist closes the visit. You do not need to tidy beforehand, but moving fragile items and clearing the counters saves time for the detail work.

Two homes, two plans

A newer house near GSP Airport and an older one closer to Downtown Greer can both be close to the interstate and still need different attention. In the newer home, the open floor plan means one large hard floor that shows every line, so the hand-wash along the walls matters most. In the older home, there are more corners, more trim and often more window tracks, so the detail work around frames and baseboards takes the longer share of the visit. We see both on the same day and the checklist handles either.

If you have hardwood, tell us at the estimate. The floor method changes with the finish, and we would rather ask than guess.

Keeping the gains between visits

A few habits stretch the result. Put a mat inside and outside the main door. Take shoes off at the entry if your household can manage it. Wipe sills during pollen weeks with a damp cloth. None of this replaces a visit, and the team will still wash the edges, but it keeps the grit from building up before the next one.

One more note for owners who work from the road: if you travel on I-85 for your job and are not home on weekdays, we can agree on access at the estimate, whether that is a key arrangement or a lockbox, so the visit goes ahead without you. The checklist is left in the home, and you can reply with anything you want done differently next time.
