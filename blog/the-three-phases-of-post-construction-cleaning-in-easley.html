The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Easley

Post construction cleaning in Easley, SC happens in three phases: rough clean, final clean and touch-up. See what each covers and how to book a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Construction cleaning works best in three passes: rough clean, final clean, then a touch-up before move-in.
  • Drywall sanding creates the finest dust, and it ends up inside cabinets, vents and window tracks.
  • Book the phases together so the team knows the schedule and the closing or inspection date.

Drywall dust is finer than flour, and it keeps coming out of a house for days after the sanders leave. That is why a single pass never finishes post construction cleaning in Easley, SC. The dust settles, gets walked through, and settles again. Experienced crews split the work into three phases so each one handles the right kind of mess at the right time. Here is how that works in a remodel, an addition or a new Easley subdivision home.

Phase one: the rough clean

The rough clean happens while trades are still finishing, or just after the heaviest work ends. It is not about shine. It is about clearing the way so the next trades can work and the real cleaning is not buried under debris.

  • Remove packaging, leftover material and loose debris from every room.
  • Vacuum floors, subfloors and corners to pick up the heavy dust and grit.
  • Wipe down the top surfaces of cabinets, ledges and window sills.
  • Clear dust from open vents and the tops of door frames.

The rough clean is optional on a small kitchen remodel and almost essential on a whole-house project. Skip it on a large job and the final cleaning takes much longer, because the crew is clearing the debris before it can start on details.

Phase two: the final clean

This is the main job, and it starts after the last trade has left. The aim is to leave every surface clean enough to live with, and that means working through the house in a fixed order from the top down.

Top to bottom, room by room

The team starts with ceiling fans, light fixtures and vents, because dust falls. Then come walls and ledges, windows, cabinets and counters, and finally the floors, which get several passes, because a single mop just moves fine dust around. Our post-construction cleaning in Easley includes:

  • Fine construction dust removed from all surfaces, ledges and trim.
  • The inside of cabinets, drawers and closets.
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills.
  • Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive removed.
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans.
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished.
  • Floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes.

New fixtures, countertops and appliances come with protective film and stickers that stay on until someone peels them. Stickers on glass leave adhesive behind, and that is the slowest part of any final clean. The same goes for paint specks on window glass and trim.

Phase three: the touch-up before move-in

After the final clean, a house still gets walked through. Furniture arrives, doors open and close, and a few days later there is a new layer of dust on the surfaces you cleaned. The touch-up pass is short. The team wipes the high-visibility surfaces again, checks the windows and mirrors for smudges, and does one last floor pass before you move in or before the inspection or walkthrough.

If you are the homeowner and the work is still going on, a few habits help the final clean. Ask the contractor to run a magnetic sweep and a shop vacuum at the end of each day, keep doors to finished rooms closed, and leave the protective film on new floors and counters until the last day. Resist the urge to move in early. Boxes and furniture sitting in a half-dusty house make the final pass slower, because everything has to be cleaned around or moved. Our post-construction cleaning service is built to follow the trades, and the earlier we know the schedule, the better it fits.

Easley projects and what to expect

Easley is a growing city with established neighborhoods and many new subdivisions along US-123 and SC-93, which means two kinds of jobs: remodels in older homes and new construction closings. A remodel often leaves a house partly lived in, so dust control matters more, and the team has to protect finished areas. New construction is usually empty, which means faster passes but more attention to fine dust inside cabinets and closets. Builders and general contractors often book the phases together so the schedule lines up with the closing date.

If the house also needs a deeper reset after the work, deep cleaning can follow once you have moved in and the dust has finally stopped settling.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need all three phases of post construction cleaning?

Not always. Small remodels may only need the final clean, while whole-house projects and new builds benefit from all three. We recommend a plan at the estimate.

Why does construction dust keep coming back after cleaning?

Drywall dust is extremely fine and settles slowly, and it also sits inside vents and cabinets. That is why we do multiple floor passes and a touch-up before move-in.
