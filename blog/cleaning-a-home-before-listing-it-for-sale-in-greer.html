Cleaning a home before listing it for sale in Greer

Cleaning a home before listing it in Greer, SC: what to clean before photos, what buyers notice, and how a move out cleaning service fits your closing date.

Perfect Services Team
November 11, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Clean for the photos first, then again before the final walk-through.
  • Buyers notice kitchens, bathrooms, closets and floors before anything else.
  • The price depends on the home and comes after a free in-home estimate.

Photos for a listing usually get taken before the furniture leaves, which means the house has to be clean while you are still living in it, and clean again once it is empty. Those are two different jobs, and sellers in Greer who plan for both avoid most of the last-minute scramble.

Greer straddles Greenville and Spartanburg counties (ZIPs 29650, 29651 and 29652), and listings range from older homes near Downtown Greer and Trade Street to newer neighborhoods near GSP Airport. The buyers walking through are the same in both: they open cabinets, check closets, look under sinks and look closely at floors. A move-out cleaning service in Greer covers the emptied-house stage, and this guide shows how to manage everything before it.

Stage one: before the photographer arrives

At this stage the house is still full, so the goal is to make it look light and cared for. Clear the kitchen counters down to one or two items. Clean the stovetop and the front of the refrigerator, because listing photos catch both. Wipe the bathroom mirror and chrome, close the toilet lid, and put away bottles from the tub edge.

  • Windows and sills wiped, with tracks free of dead insects
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped, with attention to corners
  • Baseboards and door frames wiped
  • Light fixtures dusted and burned-out bulbs replaced
  • Pet bedding washed and litter boxes out of sight
  • Closets tidied so doors close without a fight
  • Garage and entry swept, since the walk to the front door is the first impression

Spring pollen coats window sills and porches in Greer, so if you list in March or April, wipe the porch and the front door too. Humid summers show up as mildew in bathrooms and on shaded porch rails.

Stage two: showings and the open house

A house on the market gets visited at awkward times, sometimes with an hour of notice. That is why a daily routine works better than a big weekend clean: ten minutes each morning keeps the house ready, while a single deep effort fades by the second showing.

While the house is on the market, it has to stay presentable on short notice. Keep a one-page routine on the fridge: dishes done, counters cleared, beds made, trash out, floors swept. Dust shows quickly on dark furniture and hardwood, so a quick pass with a microfiber cloth before each showing goes a long way. We cover the open house itself in a separate piece on cleaning before an open house in Greer.

Stage three: the empty house

Once the furniture is out, the house looks different. Everything that was hidden is now visible: scuffs behind sofas, rings where the bed stood, grease behind the stove and dust in closets. This is the stage a move-out clean is built for. It follows the standard a buyer or an inspector would expect at a final walk-through.

Timing it with the closing date

A move-out clean takes the longest when it is squeezed into the last hours before the keys change hands, so it pays to book early. Tell us when the movers finish and when the walk-through is, and we plan the visit between them.

Work backward from the closing. The empty-house clean belongs after the movers finish and before the final walk-through, ideally with a day of cushion in case a buyer asks for a re-clean.   1. Four weeks out: decide what stays, donate or toss the rest
  2. Two weeks out: deep clean the kitchen and bathrooms, wash windows
  3. Photo day: counters cleared, floors done, beds made
  4. During showings: keep the one-page routine going
  5. After the movers: the move-out clean, before the walk-through
