Post-construction cleaning before selling a home in Greer

Finished a remodel and ready to list? Learn how post construction cleaning for a home sale in Greer removes dust buyers and inspectors notice. Free estimates.

Perfect Services Team
November 23, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Drywall and sanding dust gets into vents, cabinets and window tracks long after the contractor sweeps.
  • A final construction clean should happen after the last tradesperson leaves and before listing photos.
  • Prices come only after a free in-home estimate, because dust level and project size vary widely.

A new kitchen or finished basement helps a listing, but only if the buyer sees the work and not the dust. Contractors sweep and haul away debris, yet fine powder from sanding drywall keeps drifting out of vents and lighting for days. If you are renovating to sell, post construction cleaning in Greer is the step that turns a job site back into a home.

What buyers and inspectors actually notice

Real estate photos pick up what the eye skips. A film of white dust on a dark countertop, paint specks on a window pane, and a smudged stainless steel fridge all read as unfinished. An inspector who opens an electrical panel or looks into a crawl space will see the same dust the photographer missed. Here is where it tends to hide after a remodel:

WhereWhat is left behind
Vents and ceiling fan bladesSettled drywall powder that restarts when the HVAC runs
Inside cabinets and drawersFine dust and cut-off screws, shims and caulk crumbs
Windows and tracksPaint specks, stickers, adhesive residue
New bathroom fixturesFilm from tile grout and protective wrap residue
FloorsGrit that scratches new hardwood if it is mopped too early

The order that protects your investment

New flooring is the most common victim of a rushed clean. We start high and work down, so dust that falls from ceiling fans and vents lands on floors we have not washed yet. In Greer, where many homes have older plaster or ductwork next to newly finished rooms, we also check that vents are wiped inside the visible area, not just on the face.

  1. Confirm with the contractor that all trades are finished, including paint touch-ups.
  2. Remove debris, leftover materials and tape from the house first.
  3. Dust and wipe from the ceiling down: fixtures, fans, vents, walls and trim.
  4. Clean inside cabinets, drawers and closets.
  5. Detail windows, glass, tracks and sills, removing stickers and paint specks.
  6. Polish new fixtures and bathrooms.
  7. Vacuum and wash floors in multiple passes, followed by a touch-up before photos.

A seller's timing checklist

  • Ask your contractor for the final completion date and a punch-list walk.
  • Book the estimate before the last trade finishes, so the cleaners follow right after.
  • Do the final clean before the photographer and before the first showing.
  • Keep a small touch-up window available for dust that settles after a few days.
  • Leave the HVAC filters replaced, since the old ones are likely full of dust.

What we look at during the estimate walk-through

The estimate visit is short, and it is where most of the planning happens. We walk each room that was touched by the project and look at four things: how much fine dust is visible on high surfaces, whether the HVAC was running during the work, how many windows still have labels or paint on them, and what the floors are made of. Newly finished hardwood and fresh tile grout both need gentler products, and we choose them before the cleaning day rather than on the spot.

We also ask what the listing agent plans. If photos are booked for a certain date, we work backward from it. If an appraiser or inspector is due, we make sure the panels, utility rooms and crawl space entrances are included. None of this changes the fact that the exact price is given only after the walk-through.

If you are renovating and also leaving, the two cleans are different jobs. Construction dust is finer and heavier than the dust of an ordinary home, so the post-construction clean comes first. After that, a post-construction cleaning service can be followed by a standard move-out clean before closing. Frequently asked questions

Should I clean before the contractor is completely finished?

A rough clean mid-project can help, but the final clean should wait until every trade is done, otherwise new dust settles on the freshly cleaned surfaces.

Will cleaners remove paint and sticker residue from windows?

Yes. Paint specks, stickers and adhesive residue on glass and tracks are part of the clean, and the estimate accounts for how much there is.

How soon before listing photos should the final clean happen?

Ideally the day or two before photos, after every tradesperson has left. A short touch-up pass can follow if dust settles.
