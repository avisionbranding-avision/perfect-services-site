Deep cleaning in Greer: the kitchen, step by step

How a deep clean of a Greer, SC kitchen goes: cabinets, hood, backsplash, grout, floors. A free in-home estimate sets the price, with no guessing.

Perfect Services Team
November 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Grease needs dwell time and heat, not just more scrubbing.
  • The hood, backsplash and cabinet fronts are where the hidden buildup lives.
  • Prices follow a free in-home estimate.

You can wipe a kitchen every day and still have a sticky cabinet door above the stove. That film is cooking oil that has turned to a hard varnish, and it takes a deep clean, not a damp cloth, to remove it. Here is how our team handles a kitchen when the job is deep cleaning in Greer, in the order the work is actually done.

1. Empty and protect

Counters are cleared, the small appliances move to a table and the floor near the stove is covered if a lot of degreaser will be used. Pans, dish towels and the fruit bowl go back only at the end. It sounds slow, but it prevents the classic problem of cleaning around objects and leaving rings behind them.

2. Dry dust first, wet later

Vent covers, the top of the refrigerator, light fixtures and the cabinet tops get a dry pass with a microfiber cloth so nothing turns to mud when the spray comes out. In many Greer kitchens the tops of the upper cabinets have not been touched in years.

3. Hood, backsplash and the stovetop

The range hood filter is soaked in hot water and degreaser while we work elsewhere. The backsplash and the wall behind the stove are sprayed and left to sit. Burners, grates and knobs come off if they can. Then comes the work that gives a deep clean its name: scrubbing the corners, the screw heads and the seam where the stove meets the counter.

Why dwell time matters

Degreaser works by loosening, not by force. Two minutes on a painted wall does more than ten minutes of scrubbing on a dry wall, and it saves your finish. This is also why a deep clean takes longer than a regular visit, even with more people on it.

4. Cabinets, handles and drawer fronts

Cabinet fronts are wiped from top to bottom, with handles and knobs done by hand. Wood and painted cabinets get a gentler product than laminate. The area by the trash can, the sink and the stove gets the most attention, because those are the three places hands and splash meet.

5. Sink, faucet and grout

  • Basin scrubbed and rinsed, drain edge brushed
  • Faucet base and handle crevices cleaned with a small brush
  • Backsplash grout lines scrubbed where they are stained by splatter
  • Microwave inside and out, including the ceiling of the cavity
  • Appliance exteriors polished last so they do not streak

6. Floors, edge to edge

The floor is vacuumed along the toe-kick and under the edges of lower cabinets, then mopped by hand and finished by the door. Floors come last because everything above drips onto them.

What typically turns up

A few things come up in kitchen deep cleans around Greer so often that we plan for them. The first is a hood filter that has not been washed in a year, heavy with grease that drips back onto the stove. The second is the gap between the stove and the counter, which collects crumbs and oil, and the third is the trim below the sink where dishwater has stained the cabinet base. None of these is a disaster. All of them are easy to fix when the right time is set aside.

Houses near the older streets by Downtown Greer often have painted wood cabinets with decades of finish, while newer homes toward GSP Airport tend to have thermofoil or laminate fronts. Painted wood needs a light touch, thermofoil should never get a steam cleaner, and laminate does not like abrasives. A cleaner who works in the area every week learns these differences quickly.

A word on safety and fumes

We open windows when we use strong degreaser, keep pets out of the room and rinse every surface that touches food. If anyone at home is sensitive, say so and we will pick a milder product and take more time. Green options are available through our eco-friendly cleaning service.

Oven and refrigerator interiors

These are separate tasks with their own time. If you want them included, say so at the estimate. Move-out and post-construction kitchens usually need them; many everyday households do not. A kitchen deep clean in a house we visit regularly is often faster, since the last visit set the baseline.

What to do between visits

Wipe the stovetop while it is still warm, run the hood fan when frying, and wash the filter every month or two. Use a cloth, not a scrubby pad, on cabinet fronts.
