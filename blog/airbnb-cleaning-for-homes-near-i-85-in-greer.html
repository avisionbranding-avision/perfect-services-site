A rental close to I-85 in Greer has one thing most hosts do not plan for: guests who arrive late and tired after a long drive. Why the highway changes the job

Greer sits along I-85 with SC-14, SC-101 and US-29 close by, and GSP Airport and the BMW plant are a short drive away. Hosts here see travelers, visiting family and people with work trips. That mix means check-ins at odd hours and short stays, so rentals get turned over more often. More turnovers means less time to catch up, so we do the same job in the same order every time.

The first ten minutes: walkthrough

We start with the walkthrough, before anything is wiped. We check the front door, the kitchen, each bathroom and each bed for damage, left-behind items and anything that needs a host decision. Photos are taken as we go. If something is missing from the supply shelf, it goes on the restocking checklist right away rather than getting lost in the final minutes.

The order we clean in

  1. Trash and dishes first. Bags out, dishwasher run or emptied, sink cleared.
  2. Kitchen. Counters, stovetop, microwave, appliance fronts and the sink.
  3. Bathrooms. Toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity, disinfected. We check the shower door track, because it holds soap and hair.
  4. Bedrooms. Beds made with the linens you provide, nightstands wiped, a quick look under the bed.
  5. Living areas. Dusting, couch cushions straightened, remotes and light switches wiped.
  6. Floors last. Vacuum and mop, working back toward the door.
  7. Staging and photos. We match the room to your listing and send the damage and missing-item report.

What about the things that never get cleaned between guests?

Baseboards, the tops of door frames, the inside of the oven and the tracks of sliding doors are the usual misses. We do not stretch them into every turnover, but a periodic deep cleaning clears them out, and hosts near the highway notice the difference, since road dust and pollen settle on window sills quickly. Our Airbnb and vacation rental cleaning page lists what a standard turnover includes.

The bathroom deserves a second look, because it is where guests judge a rental fastest. After the toilet, shower and vanity, we check the mirror from an angle where streaks show, wipe the faucet base where water spots form, and look at the corners of the floor where hair collects. The kitchen gets the same treatment: the inside of the microwave, the front of the dishwasher and the handle of the refrigerator are touched by every guest and noticed by the next one.

For hosts with a vacation home or a unit they only visit now and then, we also take a short look at the outside entry: the doormat, the porch light and the lockbox area. Guests who arrive after dark on a long drive see those first. We cannot fix a broken light, but we can tell you it is out.

Hosts who live out of town ask what they will actually receive after a visit. The answer is a short report: what was done, what we found, what to restock and any photos of damage or missing items. If a guest left something behind, we photograph it and note where we put it. That way you can answer the guest quickly and decide what happens next without driving to Greer.

If a booking falls on a holiday weekend, give us the dates as early as you can. The weeks around Thanksgiving, Christmas and spring break fill up fast, and a heads-up lets us hold the time between checkout and check-in for your property.
