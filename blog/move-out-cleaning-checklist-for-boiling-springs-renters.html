Move-out cleaning checklist for Boiling Springs renters

Move out cleaning checklist for Boiling Springs, SC renters: the order to clean in, what inspectors notice, and how to get a free in-home estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Clean top to bottom and finish with floors, kitchen first among the rooms.
  • Oven, refrigerator, cabinets and the small details inspectors notice cost the most deposits.
  • Prices come after a free in-home estimate, and month-end dates fill quickly.

Boiling Springs renters usually find out about move-out cleaning from a letter: the property manager sends a list, the lease ends on a Friday, and the deposit depends on what the inspector sees on Saturday morning. If that is you, this is a step-by-step plan for what to clean, in what order, and when it makes sense to hand it off to a team.

Boiling Springs is one of Spartanburg County's fastest-growing communities, with many new subdivisions around SC-9 and I-85 (ZIP 29316). It is about 16 miles from Greer, a 25 minute drive northeast. Newer homes and apartments here often have builder-grade finishes, which clean easily if the work starts before residue sets.

Work in this order

  1. Empty everything. You cannot clean inside cabinets or closets that still hold your stuff.
  2. Start at the top of each room: ceiling corners, light fixtures and vents.
  3. Do the kitchen next, since it takes the longest.
  4. Move to the bathrooms.
  5. Finish with baseboards, doors, windows and floors, in that order.

Floors last matters more than it sounds. Anything you knock off a shelf or shake out of a cabinet lands on the floor, and nobody wants to mop twice.

The kitchen, which is where deposits are lost

  • Inside and outside of every cabinet and drawer.
  • Inside the refrigerator and freezer: shelves, drawers and door seals.
  • Inside the oven and microwave.
  • Range hood, stovetop and backsplash degreased.
  • Sink, faucet and the cabinet under the sink.

The oven is usually the item people put off, and it is also the one that shows up on a deduction list. Fresh spills come off easily. Burnt-on spills from a year of dinners need time and elbow work.

Bathrooms and closets

Scrub the toilet base, the grout, the shower glass and the insides of vanity drawers. Hard water spots on glass need patience. In the closets, wipe the shelves and rods and clean the floor, since dust gathers where nobody looks.

The small things inspectors notice

The sliding door track, the window sills and tracks, light switches, door frames and the tops of baseboards. These are quick jobs that make a house look finished. They are also what a hurried tenant skips.

Move-out cleaning vs. a regular cleaning

A regular maid visit does not go inside the cabinets, the refrigerator and the oven. A move-out clean does, and it also covers baseboards, doors, frames, window tracks and edge-to-edge floors. If you book a regular cleaning for a move-out inspection, expect to be disappointed. Ask for the right service by name.

Perfect Services gives prices only after a free in-home estimate, with no commitment. Because the home is often empty, the team can walk through it quickly and see exactly what is there. The price depends on:

  • Home size: rooms and square feet.

How long does it take?

An empty apartment or small house can take a few hours with a team, and larger homes take most of a day. If your deadline is the keys, plan the cleaning for the day before. That leaves a day of buffer for a missed spot or a re-clean, and it is the first thing we suggest when someone calls near a month-end.

If you are the landlord or the next tenant

The same checklist works in reverse. Move-in cleaning before the furniture arrives is easier than after, and landlords turning over rentals often schedule the clean right after the walkthrough so photos for the next listing show a finished home. Families moving in appreciate not having to scrub someone else's oven.

A last walkthrough before you hand in the keys

Whether you clean yourself or hire help, do a final walkthrough with your phone camera. Photograph each room, the inside of the oven and refrigerator, and the bathroom. Open every cabinet. Look at the home from the doorway of each room with the lights on, then with the lights off and the windows open, since daylight shows streaks and dust that a ceiling light hides.

If your lease or move-out sheet lists specific items, check them off one by one. Photos with a date are useful if there is ever a disagreement about the condition of the home when you left.

Deposit disputes and what helps

Most deposit disputes are about evidence. A landlord who sees a dirty oven in a photo from the walkthrough has an easy case, and a tenant with dated photos of a clean one has an easy answer. Keep the receipt if a professional team did the work, and keep your move-in photos too, since pre-existing wear is not your cost. Read the lease for any clause that names a cleaning standard, because some Boiling Springs landlords require professional cleaning and some only ask for the home to be left broom clean.

Frequently asked questions

What does move-out cleaning include in Boiling Springs?

Cabinets and drawers, the inside of the refrigerator, oven and microwave, bathrooms, closets, baseboards, doors, window tracks and floors.

Is a regular cleaning enough for a move-out inspection?

Usually not. Regular visits do not go inside cabinets, the oven and the refrigerator, which move-out cleaning covers.

How far ahead should I book move-out cleaning?

As early as you know your date. Month-end lease dates fill fast, and a day of buffer before the keys are due helps.
