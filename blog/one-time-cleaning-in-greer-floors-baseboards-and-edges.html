Our floor routine, room by room

A one-time clean includes vacuuming and mopping floors, plus baseboards and door frames. The order is what makes the result last through the evening:

  1. Dust and wipe everything above the floor first.
  2. Wipe baseboards and door frames, working in one direction around each room.
  3. Vacuum edges and corners, then the open floor.
  4. Mop, starting at the back of the house and finishing at the front door.
  5. Do a last walk with a dry cloth for drips and footprints.

If we cleaned the floor before the baseboards, we would drop dust on a wet floor. Small order choices like this are how a team that cleans houses every week saves time and gets better results.

What is special about floors around Greer

Homes in ZIP codes 29650, 29651 and 29652 have the same ground under them: Upstate red clay. It comes in on boots after yard work and after a rain, and it leaves orange marks on light carpets and mats. Spring brings pollen to the thresholds, and a humid summer makes bare feet leave prints on hardwood and tile. In fall and winter, holiday guests bring in leaves and mud.

Nothing here is unusual, but knowing it affects how much time we plan for entry areas.

Edges and corners checklist

  • Baseboards in each room, including behind doors
  • Door frames at hand height
  • Corners where the vacuum cannot reach
  • The floor under chair edges and table legs
  • The strip along the bottom of kitchen cabinets
  • Stair edges, if you have stairs

Getting the most from a single visit

A one-time clean has a finite window, so priorities matter. Tell us which floors guests will see first, and we start there. If you have a big family gathering, the kitchen, dining area and living room are usually the first priorities, with bedrooms after. Mention if you plan to host overnight guests, since hallways and guest bathrooms then count more. A short note before the visit lets us bring the right tools, and a few minutes of walking through with you at the start helps us avoid surprises. Many households who try one-time house cleaning in Greer come away with a clearer sense of which rooms they want help with long term, and that is a perfectly good reason to try it.

A detail many homeowners ask about is furniture. We move what is light and safe to move, and we clean around what is heavy. If you want the floor under a bed or sofa done, say so, and we will plan for it. For homes with a lot of area rugs, it helps to know which ones can be lifted and which are better left in place, so nothing is damaged. Small instructions like these make the visit smoother and the result closer to what you pictured.
