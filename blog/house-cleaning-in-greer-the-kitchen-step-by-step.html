House cleaning in Greer: the kitchen, step by step

House cleaning in Greer, SC starts in the kitchen. See the order our team follows, what gets wiped by hand and how a free in-home estimate sets the price.

Perfect Services Team
November 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • The kitchen is cleaned top to bottom so crumbs never fall on a finished counter.
  • Floors are the last thing touched, done by hand and edge to edge.
  • Price comes after a free in-home estimate, never before.

Open a Greer kitchen on a Tuesday morning and you will usually find the same story: a coffee ring on the counter, crumbs behind the toaster, a stovetop that was wiped but not degreased, and a sink full of water spots. House cleaning in Greer, SC is judged here first, so our team plans the whole visit around the kitchen order.

This is the sequence we follow in a regular visit as part of house cleaning in Greer, and why each step sits where it does.

Step 1: clear the surface, then look

Before any spray comes out, small items move off the counters: the fruit bowl, the mail pile, the knife block. Nobody can clean under a toaster they have not lifted. This also shows what the counter really looks like, which tells us whether we need a plain wipe or a bit of dwell time for dried-on spills.

Step 2: work from high to low

Dust falls, so the top of the refrigerator, the range hood edge and the upper cabinet fronts come first. Handles and light switches get a second pass because they are the most touched spots in any Greer home, from the older houses near Trade Street to the newer builds around GSP Airport. Only after the high spots are done do we reach the counters.

Step 3: the sink and the stovetop get time to work

A cleaner sprays the sink and the stovetop early and then moves on, so the product has a few minutes to loosen grease and mineral film. Coming back later takes far less scrubbing. Burner grates on a gas range are lifted and wiped one at a time, and the drip area underneath is the spot most often skipped in a quick cleanup.

  • Counters wiped in long overlapping strokes, never in circles
  • Sink basin scrubbed, faucet base dried so water spots do not return
  • Stovetop degreased and appliance fronts polished with the grain
  • Microwave wiped inside and the door seal checked
  • Cabinet handles and the drawer pulls closest to the stove wiped by hand

Step 4: appliance fronts last, floors very last

Stainless and black appliance fronts streak if wiped before the room settles, so they come near the end. The floor is always the final surface. We vacuum first, so grit is not dragged around, then mop by hand, working backward toward the door. A mop pass that ends at the exit means nobody walks on a wet floor.

What the walkthrough tells us before we start

At the first visit the lead cleaner opens the cabinet under the sink, checks the oven door seal and runs a hand along the top of the refrigerator. None of it is for show. A greasy hood filter, a sticky pantry shelf or a dishwasher gasket with residue tells us where the time will go, and whether something belongs in a regular clean or in a deeper one. Greer homes near the older part of town often have laminate counters with worn seams where moisture sits; newer homes near the BMW plant tend to have granite or quartz that shows every streak. The product and the cloth change with the surface.

We also ask a few plain questions. Do you cook with a lot of oil? Is there a dog who eats in the kitchen? Does anyone in the house react to strong smells? The answers decide the product and the time, and they cost nothing to give.

The small things a quick wipe leaves behind

Three spots turn up again and again. The first is the strip of countertop right behind the faucet, where water and soap dry into a ring. The second is the front edge of the stove, where splatter bakes on, and the third is the lower cabinet doors next to the trash can, which collect fingerprints at hand height. They take seconds each when you know to look. A team that cleans Greer kitchens every week learns where grime hides in each house, and the second and third visits get faster and better.

How long it takes

In a typical Greer home the kitchen is about a quarter of the visit. On a recurring schedule it is quick; on a first visit with built-up grease it takes longer, and if the oven or refrigerator interior needs work, that is better handled by a deeper visit. Keeping the kitchen easy between visits

You do not need to do much. Wipe the stovetop while it is still warm, empty the sink strainer daily and keep a dry cloth for the faucet.
