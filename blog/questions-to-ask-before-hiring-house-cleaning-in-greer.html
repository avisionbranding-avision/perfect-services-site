Questions to ask before hiring house cleaning in Greer

Hire house cleaning Greer SC: eight questions to ask a maid service about insurance, checklists, the first visit and price, and how our free estimate works.

Perfect Services Team
November 15, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Ask who comes, what is on the checklist and what happens if something is missed.
  • A reliable company wants to see the home before it names a price.
  • Write the answers down so you can compare two or three companies fairly.

Most people pick a cleaning company from a phone call that lasts four minutes. The cleaner on the line sounds nice, gives a number, and everyone is happy until the first visit, when it turns out the oven was not included and the baseboards were never on the list. Better answers come from better questions. Here are eight to ask when you hire house cleaning in Greer, SC, and what a good answer sounds like.

Questions about who comes into your home

1. Who will be in my house, and will it be the same people each time? A steady team learns your home: where the pet bed goes, which cabinet handles are greasy, how you like the towels. Ask whether the crew rotates and whether you will be told in advance if it changes.

2. Are your cleaners employees, and is the work insured? A cleaner will be moving furniture and working near breakable things. The company should be able to explain how it handles an accident. If the answer is vague, move on. We do not make claims for anyone else, so ask each company to answer in its own words.

3. How do you handle keys, codes and being away? Many Greer households are empty during the workday. You should hear a clear process for access, locking up and what happens if a pet is loose.

Questions about the work itself

4. What exactly is on your checklist? Ask for it by room. A good answer names the kitchen counters, stovetop, appliance fronts, bathroom surfaces, floors, dusting and trash, and says which details, such as baseboards and door frames, are done every time and which are on a rotation. Our regular house cleaning includes baseboards and door frames touched up on a rotation, for example, so we say so up front.

5. What is not included? This matters more than the list. Inside the oven, inside the refrigerator, windows and cabinets are usually extras. A company that tells you plainly is easier to trust than one that says everything is included.

6. What happens if you miss something? Ask how to report a problem and how quickly it gets fixed. A good company wants to hear from you after a visit and has a way to come back.

Questions about the first visit and the price

7. Will you see my home before telling me the price? This is the most useful question on the list. A price from a call or a text photo is a guess. Size, bathrooms, pets, condition and how long since the last clean change the work. At Perfect Services we only quote after a free in-home estimate, a short visit to see the space, with no commitment.

8. Is the first clean different from the next ones? It usually is. The first visit is often deeper, so the home reaches the maintenance standard. After that, regular visits stay lighter, and weekly and biweekly visits cost less per visit than monthly ones.

Take notes on how each company answers, not only on what it says. A company that explains why the first clean is deeper or why a price needs a visit is usually one that has thought about the work. Short, rehearsed answers are worth noticing. So is a company that asks you questions back about pets, floors and what bothers you most in the house.

Ask about a trial too. Starting with a single visit lets you judge the work before you commit to a schedule. If you plan weekly or biweekly service, ask what changes after the first visit and how to skip or move a date. Greer calendars fill with school breaks, tournaments and travel, and a flexible company will not make that harder.

Finally, ask what the company does when it cannot finish something, such as a locked room or a blocked bathroom. A good team tells you before it leaves, rather than skipping it silently. That small honesty is a better sign than any promise on a website.

Red flags worth noticing

  • A price given before anyone has asked about the size, bathrooms or pets
  • No written checklist, only "we do everything"
  • Pressure to sign a long contract before the first clean
  • No clear answer on what to do if something breaks
  • A promise to clean a whole house in an unrealistic time
