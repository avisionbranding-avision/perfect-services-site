Seasonal cleaning guide for Greer homeowners

A housekeeping service in Greer sees the same pattern every year: pollen, humidity, red clay and holiday guests. Here is what to clean in each season.

Key takeaways

  • Each Upstate season leaves a different mess: pollen in spring, humidity in summer, red clay in fall and closed-up air in winter.
  • Keep the weekly routine steady and shift the extra attention to whatever the season is doing to your house.

Ask anyone who runs a housekeeping service in Greer when the house gets hardest to keep clean and you will hear the same thing: it depends on the month. The dust on your bookshelves in April is not the same problem as the film on your shower door in August, and a plan that works in one season quietly fails in the next. This guide walks through the year the way our team sees it, one season at a time.

Why a housekeeping service in Greer changes with the seasons

Greer sits in both Greenville and Spartanburg counties, between I-85 and the foothills, and the weather here is not gentle on a house. Spring brings a yellow layer of pollen. Summer is humid enough that bathrooms and window tracks never fully dry. Fall means leaves and red clay coming in on shoes. Winter keeps the windows shut and the heat running, so dust just circulates.

A good routine does not change every month. The core visit stays the same: kitchen, bathrooms, floors, dusting, the work described on our house cleaning service page. What shifts is the extra attention, the one or two spots we spend a few more minutes on because of what the season did to them.

Spring: pollen is the main event

From March into May, a lot of Greer homes get that fine yellow dust on cars, porches and every flat surface near a door. It comes in on clothes and pets too. Wiping it dry just pushes it around, so we use damp microfiber on sills and furniture and vacuum before we mop. If you have allergies, the window tracks and the tops of ceiling fan blades matter more than most people expect.

  • Wipe window sills and tracks while they are still dry-dusty, before the first rain.
  • Vacuum upholstered chairs and the edges of rugs, where pollen settles.
  • Wash entry mats and bedding more often than usual.
  • Dust fan blades and vents with a damp cloth so the dust does not fall back down.

If you want a standing slot, weekly maid service in Greer keeps the pollen from building up between visits. Spring is also the best time to ask about a deeper visit. Our pollen season deep cleaning plan covers that in more detail.

Summer and fall: humidity, then red clay

Summer humidity is hardest on bathrooms. Water spots dry slowly, soap film builds up, and grout in a shower that stays damp can darken faster. Wiping the glass after the last shower of the day helps, and so does running the exhaust fan longer than feels necessary. Kids at home all day mean more cups, more snacks and more bare feet on the kitchen floor, so those areas get a little extra time.

Fall brings the opposite problem. Red clay tracked in on shoes leaves orange-brown marks on light floors, and leaves and dirt come in with pets. A shoe tray by the door and a washable mat make a bigger difference than any product. On a regular visit, we spend more time on entry floors and the baseboards nearest the door, which pick up the clay splatter. If you want to see how this fits a weekly schedule, our page on house cleaning in Greer shows what each visit includes.

Winter: holidays and closed-up air

By November the windows are shut and the dust that was floating around all summer settles on shelves, vents and TV stands. At the same time, the holidays arrive. Guests use the bathroom you rarely clean, the kitchen runs all day, and the floors get more traffic than they have in months.

If you are hosting, book the visit two or three days before people arrive, not the same morning. That gives you time to reset the kitchen after the cleaners leave. If the house has gone a few months without a thorough clean, it may be worth asking about deep cleaning first, then moving to a regular schedule.

A simple year-round plan

You do not need to treat every month as a project. A steady rhythm works better.

  1. Keep a regular visit, weekly or biweekly, so nothing builds up.
  2. Add a deeper visit once a year, usually in spring, when pollen and winter dust are both at their worst.
  3. Book a pre-holiday visit in November or early December if you host.
  4. Tell your cleaner what changed: a new pet, a baby, a renovation, a season of allergies.
