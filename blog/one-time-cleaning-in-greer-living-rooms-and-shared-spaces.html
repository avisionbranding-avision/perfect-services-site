It is the third week of November, the family is coming to Greer for Thanksgiving, and the living room is where everyone will end up. If you are thinking about one-time cleaning for the living room and shared spaces, you are probably in one of two situations: guests are on the way, or you have not had a professional in the house before and want to see what it is like. Both are good reasons, and this is what we do on a first visit.

What we do in the first hour

When our team arrives for a one-time cleaning, we start with a short walk through the rooms the guests will see. We ask which spots bother you most, since a sofa you are proud of and a rug you have given up on need different attention. Then we get to work.

  1. Dust the high spots. Ceiling fan, shelves, frames, the top of the TV.
  2. Wipe surfaces. Coffee table, end tables, mantel, consoles.
  3. Glass and mirrors. Mirrors and glass are polished as part of the visit, including any glass tabletops.
  4. Baseboards and door frames. These are included in the one-time clean.
  5. Floors. Vacuumed and mopped from the far side of the room toward the door.
  6. Trash emptied before we leave.

Bedrooms and living areas are dusted, and the kitchen and bathrooms get the heavier work. You can also add the inside of the oven, fridge or cabinets as optional add-ons.

Why the living room takes more effort than it looks

Most living rooms in Greer homes do double duty. In a house near Downtown Greer it might be the family room that opens to the kitchen, and in a newer home near GSP Airport it is often an open plan where cooking smells, kids and pets share the same air. That means more dust, more hair and more fingerprints than a formal sitting room would collect.

The spots people miss, and we do not:

  • The seam where sofa cushions meet the back
  • The tops of door frames and picture frames
  • The remote controls and light switches
  • Baseboards behind furniture that rarely moves
  • The ledge under the TV and the cord area
  • The glass in the front door

Preparing the living room for a first clean

You do not need to hire a cleaner after cleaning yourself, but a little prep helps us spend time on dirt rather than on clutter.

  • Clear coffee tables and side tables.
  • Put away toys, blankets and stray shoes.
  • Tell us about fragile items or furniture finishes.
  • Mention pets, since hair and dander add time.
  • Decide whether you want the fridge or oven done as add-ons.

After the guests leave

A one-time clean is not only for before an event. Many Greer families book it for the day after the holidays, when the living room holds wrapping paper dust, cookie crumbs in the carpet and a tree that has dropped needles along the wall. The same steps apply: surfaces first, then upholstery, then floors. If you cannot decide between before and after, think about when you will be more tired. Our one-time cleaning in Greer team can schedule either, and some households book one visit before and a lighter one later. We will tell you what is open once we have talked through the home and the dates.

If you are preparing for guests, think about the path they will take. They walk through the front door, hang a coat, sit in the living room and ask for something to drink in the kitchen. Those few steps are where a clean home registers. Putting our time into that route, plus the guest bathroom, is often a better use of a single visit than spreading effort evenly across every room. We are glad to build the visit around that route if you tell us at the estimate.
