The handoff in five steps

Each step has a purpose, and skipping one usually means doing another twice.

Step 1: Walk the project with the builder or homeowner

ZIP 29651 is part of Greer, in Greenville and Spartanburg counties, near I-85, SC-14, SC-101 and US-29. The jobs here run from kitchen and bathroom renovations to additions and new construction closings. Before anyone picks up a vacuum, we walk through the project and look at the dust level, the floors, the glass and the deadline. This is also when we learn whether the trades are truly finished, because cleaning before the last installer leaves means cleaning twice.

Step 2: Rough clean of the big debris

Sawdust, cut-offs and packaging are removed first. This pass is not meant to look nice. It is meant to clear the way, so the detail work does not push grit around. Many general contractors schedule this stage while painters finish, which is why the stages can be booked together or separately.

Step 3: Dust from the top down

This is the longest step. Fine drywall dust lands on every flat thing, then settles lower over time. The work moves from the ceiling to the floor.

  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans
  • Ledges, trim and door frames
  • Inside cabinets, drawers and closets
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished

The reason this takes patience is simple. Wiping dust with a dry cloth only moves it. Dust needs to be lifted, then the surface cleaned, and then lifted again as the room dries.

Step 4: Glass, paint specks and stickers

New windows come with labels and residue. Paint specks land on tracks and sills, and adhesive from protective film is stubborn. We work on these with care, because scraping the wrong surface can cause a scratch that is far worse than the speck. Windows, glass, tracks and sills are all part of the service.

Step 5: Floors and the final touch-up pass

Floors are vacuumed and washed in more than one pass, since the first pass picks up what the dust-down knocked loose. Then a last touch-up pass runs before the handoff, when the lights are on, the blinds are open and the home is ready for the walkthrough. A buyer or inspector sees the house in that light, so we check it in that light.

Mistakes that make the clean take longer

Most delays we see are avoidable. Booking the clean before the last trade is done is the most common, because dust from sanding or tile cutting lands on a finished surface. Another is skipping the walkthrough and discovering paint on a window track after move-in. A third is expecting a normal house cleaning crew to handle fine dust with household equipment, which spreads it around instead of removing it.

Before we arrive, it helps to move the project materials out, tell us which areas are finished, and let us know about delicate surfaces such as new stone, unsealed wood or special finishes. Those details change the tools we choose and the order we work in.

One more tip from the field: take a few photos of the finished rooms before the crew starts, so you have a baseline if you want to compare afterward. It also helps us understand which surfaces are new and need extra care.

Homeowners finishing a kitchen remodel often ask whether they should cover the rest of the house. If the work was close to the living areas, fine dust travels, and we will include the surrounding rooms in the walkthrough so you are not surprised later.
