What does a green clean change?

Three things. First, the products: low-odor, family- and pet-friendly options on request, used in kitchens and bathrooms. Second, the cloths: microfiber, which holds dust instead of pushing it from one shelf to the next. Third, the technique: hand-wiping surfaces, which uses less product to begin with.

The floors are cleaned with surface-safe solutions, and you can ask the team to use your own preferred products if you have a brand you trust. If anyone in the home is sensitive to scent, there is a fragrance-free option.

Will it clean as well as regular products?

For routine dirt, yes. A kitchen counter, a bathroom mirror, a hardwood hallway or a dusty window sill do not need harsh chemicals. Where gentler products do slow things down is heavy build-up: soap scum that has sat for months, or baked-on grease around a range. Those jobs take more time and more hand work, which is why the condition of the home is one of the main things we look at.

The checklist itself does not shrink. The team still works room by room, from the kitchen through bathrooms, bedrooms and living areas, then floors. Switching to green does not mean skipping baseboards or door frames.

Which households benefit most?

  • Families with babies and young children, because floors and low surfaces are where little hands and mouths end up.
  • Pet owners, since bowls, beds and counters often share the same space.
  • People with allergies or asthma, who often react to strong fumes in small rooms like bathrooms.
  • Households that are sensitive to fragrance, including guests who get headaches from heavily scented products.

Upstate pollen in spring and humid summers also add a layer that gentle cleaning handles well: dust on window sills and damp residue near sinks and tubs. Wiping, not spraying, does most of the work.

Common mix-ups about green cleaning

A few ideas come up again and again, and they are worth clearing up before you book.

"Natural" does not automatically mean "weaker" or "safer to mix." We do not blend products, and we do not use anything on a surface it was not meant for. "Fragrance-free" is also different from "low-odor": a low-odor product may still carry a light scent, while the fragrance-free option is for households where even that is too much. Say which one you need and we plan around it.

Another mix-up is that green cleaning means leaving out disinfecting. Bathrooms are still cleaned and sanitized as part of the visit. The difference is in how: more hand work, fewer sprays, and cloths that are rinsed and changed between rooms so grime is not carried from the bathroom to the bedroom.

Finally, some families assume they must buy special supplies. You do not. We bring what we need, and the option to use your own products is there if you want it.

What happens on the first visit?

The first clean is usually the longest, because the team is bringing the home up to a standard that can then be maintained. Expect a few hours for an average family home, with a window we confirm at the estimate. If you choose recurring visits afterward, they get shorter and lighter because the heavy work is already done.

A few things help the visit go smoothly. Put away toys and loose laundry so floors can be reached. Tell us about any surface that reacts badly to a product, such as natural stone or older finishes. If you have a pet that gets nervous, let us know where they will stay. Booking this week is realistic for most schedules, with early morning visits filling first.
