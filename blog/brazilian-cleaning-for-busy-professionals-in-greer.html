What the first visit actually looks like

Expect the first visit to take longer than the rest. The team is not only cleaning, it is resetting the home to a standard that recurring visits then maintain. A typical first visit goes in this order:

  1. A short walkthrough with you or by phone to confirm priorities
  2. Top surfaces and door frames first, so dust falls to the floor
  3. Kitchen next: stovetop, hood and cabinet fronts degreased
  4. Bathrooms, with grout and the toilet base scrubbed
  5. Furniture and small items moved, cleaned under and put back
  6. Floors last, washed by hand along the walls
  7. A final walk-through against the room checklist

Because the checklist is the same each time, you do not have to remember to ask for the same things again. That is the feature people with full calendars value the most.

What you will notice after a few visits

The changes are small and cumulative. The edge of the hardwood stops turning gray. The bathroom grout holds its color. The tops of door frames and the back of the stove stop collecting what you cannot see. A rushed clean is easy to spot after a week, because the surfaces are wiped but the corners are the same as before. A hand-cleaned home does not have that problem, since the corners are done on every visit and not saved for a deep clean.

If you share the home with a partner or roommates, agree on one person who approves the checklist, so the team gets one set of instructions instead of three. Changes after that are a text message away, and the checklist stays the reference for every visit. People often start with the kitchen and bathrooms as priorities and add bedrooms and the living room once they see the result, which is a perfectly normal way to build up the schedule.
