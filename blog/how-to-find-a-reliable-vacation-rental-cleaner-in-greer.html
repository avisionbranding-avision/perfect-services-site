How to find a reliable vacation rental cleaner in Greer

What a reliable cleaner does that a one-off cleaner does not

Anyone can mop a floor. A turnover cleaner is paid for the things that happen around the mopping. Our team treats every changeover as a reset to match your listing photos, and that includes the unglamorous parts: kitchen dishes, appliance fronts, bathrooms cleaned and disinfected, beds made with the linens you provide, floors vacuumed and mopped, trash out and bins back in place.

The part that separates a dependable cleaner is the report. After each visit you should get a short note, with photos, about anything damaged or missing, plus a list of what needs restocking. That report is what lets a host who lives out of town stay calm, and it protects you if a guest disputes the condition of the home.

Six questions to ask before you hire

  1. Who actually comes to the house? A steady small team learns your property. A different stranger every time does not.
  2. What happens when something is broken or missing? You want photos the same day, not a vague text a week later.
  3. How do you handle linens? Some hosts want laundry done on site, others send it out. Agree on this before the first booking.
  4. Can you work around a tight window? A same-day changeover needs a different schedule than a Sunday-to-Friday gap.
  5. Who checks supplies? Paper goods, soap and coffee pods run out at the worst moment. Ask who counts them.
  6. Will you look at the home first? A cleaner who quotes a house they have never seen is guessing.

Red flags worth walking away from

A few signs tell you early that a cleaner will not last. They will not say how long a turnover takes at your size of property. They promise a price over the phone without seeing the home. They have no answer when you ask what happens after a guest damages something. And they treat the restocking list as your job. None of these is a deal breaker alone, but two of them together usually means you will be redoing the work yourself on a Sunday night.

Run one trial turnover first

Do not move every booking over on day one. Pick a single changeover and treat it as a test. Be at the home afterward, or have a friend walk it. Check the places people forget: the track of the sliding door, the inside of the microwave, the shower floor, the top of the fridge handle, under the bed edge where guests drop things.

Then look at how the cleaner communicated. Did they message when they arrived and when they left? Did they flag the light bulb that burned out? A trial turnover tells you about attitude as much as about skill. If the walkthrough went well, schedule the next three and see whether the quality holds.

A checklist to hand your cleaner

  • Door code or key location, and where to leave the key afterward
  • Which linens belong to which bed, and where the spares live
  • Items that must never be moved (art, a guest-book, locked closets)
  • Supplies to count and the level at which to restock
  • Who to text about damage, and how fast you want to hear
  • Check-in and checkout times for the next three bookings
