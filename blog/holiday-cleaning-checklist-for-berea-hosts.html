Holiday cleaning checklist for Berea hosts

A holiday cleaning checklist for Berea, SC hosts: what to clean first, what one-time cleaning covers, and how to get a free in-home estimate.

November 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Clean the rooms guests will see first: entry, kitchen, dining area, bathroom and guest bedroom.
  • A one-time cleaning covers kitchen, bathrooms, floors, baseboards and glass, with oven and refrigerator as add-ons.
  • Prices come after a free in-home estimate, and holiday weeks book quickly.

The week before Thanksgiving is when Berea kitchens get tested. The oven that was fine for ordinary dinners suddenly has to hold a turkey, the guest room has been a storage room since March, and somebody's mother-in-law is arriving Wednesday. If you are hosting this year, this holiday checklist for Berea homes puts the work in the order that saves the most time, and shows where a one-time cleaning fits in.

Berea, in Greenville County (ZIP 29617), is a community of established residential neighborhoods west of Greenville, and a lot of homes here are the kind that get passed around for family gatherings year after year. That means real kitchens, real dining tables and plenty of foot traffic along US-25 (White Horse Rd) on the days people come and go.

Start with the rooms guests will actually see

Before any scrubbing, decide which rooms are on the tour. Guests see the entry, the kitchen, the dining area, the nearest bathroom and wherever they are sleeping. They do not open your hall closet. Cleaning in that order keeps you from spending a Saturday on a utility room nobody will visit.

Walk through with a laundry basket and a trash bag first. Clear surfaces, collect the strays, and only then start wiping. Wiping around clutter is the single biggest time waster we see when we walk into a home the day before a party.

The holiday host checklist

  • Kitchen: counters cleared and degreased, sink scrubbed, stovetop and the front of every appliance wiped, microwave steamed out.
  • Refrigerator: toss expired jars, wipe the shelves, and make room before the groceries arrive.
  • Oven: wipe out burnt-on spills now, not on the morning you need it.
  • Bathrooms: toilet, shower, vanity, mirror, and a fresh trash bag. Guests notice the bathroom more than any other room.
  • Guest bedroom: dust the nightstands and headboard, change the sheets, empty a drawer or two.
  • Dining area: table and chair legs, the rungs under chairs, and the baseboards where chair backs bump the wall.
  • Entry and floors: vacuum, then mop last so nobody tracks over a wet floor.
  • Glass: the storm door, the sliding door and the mirrors near the entrance.

What a one-time cleaning covers before a holiday

A single, no-commitment visit is built for exactly this situation. For a holiday, the team's usual one-time cleaning includes full kitchen cleaning and degreasing, bathrooms cleaned and disinfected, bedrooms and living areas dusted, floors vacuumed and mopped, baseboards and door frames, mirrors and glass, and the trash emptied. If you want the inside of the oven, the refrigerator or the cabinets done before company, those are optional add-ons you can mention when you book.

How long it takes, and when to do it

A typical one-time visit for a family home takes a few hours with a team, depending on the size and the add-ons. The best timing is two or three days before guests arrive. That leaves you a buffer for the last-minute cooking mess, and the house still looks fresh on the big day. Cleaning the same morning leaves no room for the dog to shed again or a child to find the one clean mirror.

After the holiday, a second round is just as useful. Hosts in Berea who invite family usually end up with a refrigerator full of leftovers, a dishwasher that has run four times and carpet that has seen a lot of shoes. A short reset the week after is easier than waiting for the January slump.

If you host as a short-term rental

Some Berea hosts rent a room or a whole home for holiday weekends. The same checklist applies, with one addition: check the places a guest will touch first, like light switches, remote controls and the handle of the refrigerator. Our team follows a detail-first method that comes from Brazilian cleaning habits, which means hand work on the small things rather than a quick pass with a spray. For a recurring rental schedule, a different service may fit better, and the estimate visit is the time to talk it through.
