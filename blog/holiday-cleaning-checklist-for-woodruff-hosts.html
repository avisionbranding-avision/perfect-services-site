Holiday cleaning checklist for Woodruff hosts

Holiday cleaning checklist for Woodruff hosts: what to do first and what a one time house cleaning in Woodruff, SC covers before Thanksgiving guests arrive.

Key takeaways

The week before Thanksgiving is when Woodruff phone calls start: someone is hosting twelve people, the oven has not been touched since last year, and the guest bathroom still has a box of paint cans in it. This checklist is for those hosts, and it is built around what a one time house cleaning in Woodruff, SC visit usually covers and what you can do yourself so the visit goes further.

Start with the rooms guests will actually see

Hosts tend to scrub the kitchen and forget the path a guest walks. Think about the route from the front door: entry floor, coat spot, hallway, the guest bathroom, then the table. In older Woodruff homes near town the entry often has a small mat and a lot of foot traffic marks on the baseboards. In the newer rural subdivisions off SC-101 it is usually a garage door entry into the laundry room, which collects dog hair and dust faster than anywhere else in the house.

Clean in the order guests move. Hall and entry first, bathroom second, dining and living area third, kitchen last because it gets used the longest before the party.

The pre-holiday checklist

  • Clear counters and the dining table completely before any cleaning starts, since wiping around objects leaves rings.
  • Pull out the guest towels and bath mats you plan to use so the bathroom can be finished in one pass.
  • Decide on the oven now. A baked-on oven smokes the moment you preheat it for a turkey.
  • Empty the fridge shelves you need for leftovers and dishes you are bringing in.
  • Move pet beds and toys to one corner so floors can be vacuumed and mopped edge to edge.
  • Gather the linens for any guest room and put them on a chair, not the bed, until the room is dusted.
  • Take decorations down from the high shelves first, because dust falls onto surfaces you already wiped.

A few spots get missed in the rush before guests arrive. Cabinet handles near the stove collect grease that you stop seeing. The tracks of a sliding door and the base of the toilet are easy to skip. Light switches near the kitchen and the guest bathroom take dozens of touches a day once family arrives. These are the details the Brazilian detail-first method is built around, and they are the ones a visitor notices when they stand in the kitchen with a drink.

If you host in a smaller older home, think about storage too. Coats, bags and shoes pile up fast, and a bed or closet in a spare room can hold them if you clear it ahead of time. A cleaned and cleared guest room is also a useful place to set pet gates or crates if animals will be shut away during dinner.

Small jobs to keep for the day before

Leave a few things for yourself: a quick wipe of the bathroom sink, fresh hand towels, the trash bags changed, and a final sweep of the entry.
