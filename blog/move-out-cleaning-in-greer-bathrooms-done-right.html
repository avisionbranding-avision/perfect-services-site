Move-out cleaning in Greer: bathrooms done right

The bathroom is where most security deposit arguments start. A walk-through inspector in a Greer rental will open the vanity, glance behind the toilet and look at the bottom edge of the shower glass before they check anything in the living room. If you want a move out cleaning bathroom Greer SC landlords will sign off on, the work is mostly about the small, awkward places.

What inspectors look at first

After a few hundred move-outs in the Greer area, the pattern is clear. The first check is the toilet: the base, the hinge bolts, the gap where the tank meets the wall. Next comes the tub or shower, especially the lower track of glass doors and the caulk line. The last stop is usually the vanity, and the inspector will open the doors and run a finger along the inside edge.

None of that is covered by a quick wipe-down. It is the reason move-out cleaning in Greer is handled as its own job rather than a bigger version of a weekly clean.

The order we work in

We start by clearing the room. Anything left behind, such as a half bottle of cleaner or a shower caddy, comes out first so the surfaces are bare. Then the order is always high to low:

  1. Exhaust fan cover and light fixture, because dust falls.
  2. Mirror and medicine cabinet, inside and out.
  3. Shower walls, glass and fixtures, left to soak while we move on.
  4. Vanity top, sink, faucet base and the cabinet interior.
  5. Toilet, from the tank down to the base and the floor behind it.
  6. Floor last, edge to edge, with the baseboards done by hand.

Letting the shower product sit while we do the vanity is the whole trick. Soap film and hard water spots come off with far less scrubbing when the product has a few minutes to work.

Grout, caulk and glass: the three problem spots

Grout in a Greer home that has had several years of humid summers tends to darken at the floor line and around the tub. A brush and the right product take most of it back toward its original color. Grout that is stained rather than dirty will not change completely, and we tell you that during the visit instead of promising a miracle.

Caulk is a different story. If it has black mold growing inside the bead, cleaning only helps so far, and the landlord may want it replaced. Glass doors collect hard water, and the metal track at the bottom collects everything else. We detail the track with a small brush because that is exactly where inspectors point a flashlight.

A bathroom move-out checklist

  • Toilet base, hinge bolts and the wall behind the tank wiped
  • Inside of every vanity door and drawer
  • Faucet base and drain ring free of buildup
  • Shower glass track and door seal cleaned
  • Grout lines brushed, caulk checked for mold
  • Exhaust fan cover removed and washed
  • Mirror streak-free, light switch plate wiped
  • Floor washed to the baseboard, trash can removed

How long it takes

A single hall bathroom in a typical move-out usually takes a trained pair a bit over an hour when it is in decent shape. A primary bath with a soaking tub and double vanity can take noticeably longer. Heavy buildup adds time, which is why we look first.

Doing it yourself first

If you want to start before we arrive, the best help is removing your belongings and trash and, if you can, running the exhaust fan with the door open so the room dries. When the bathroom is the last room standing

The most common story we see in Greer is a tenant who has packed everything but the bathroom, because they are still using it the night before the keys go back. That is fine, but plan for it. Leave the towels and the toothbrush in a bag by the door and let the crew start in the morning. If the shower is still wet from the last use, the glass will take extra time to dry and the first pass shows spots. A few minutes with the exhaust fan on before we arrive makes a visible difference.

We also see a lot of rental bathrooms where a previous tenant used a heavy air freshener or left a bathmat on the floor for a year. The mat leaves a clean rectangle on a dirty floor, which is easy to spot at inspection. We wash the whole floor so the color is even from wall to wall.
