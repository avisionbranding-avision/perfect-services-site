Questions to ask before hiring green cleaning in Greer

Looking for eco-friendly cleaning in Greer, SC? Ask what products are used, if fragrance-free is possible and whether you can supply your own before you book.

Perfect Services Team
November 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • "Green" is not a regulated label, so ask what products are actually used and let the company name them.
  • Fragrance-free and your-own-products options should be offered without fuss.
  • Heavy build-up takes longer with gentler products, which the free estimate accounts for.

There is no official rule for what a cleaning company can call "green." One crew means plant-based sprays, another means the same products with a leaf on the van. If you have a baby crawling on the kitchen floor, a dog that licks everything or a family member with asthma, that gap matters. These are the questions to ask before you book eco-friendly cleaning in Greer, SC.

What products do you actually use?

Ask for names, not adjectives. A company that is serious about green cleaning can tell you what goes in the bucket and show you the labels. Ours uses low-odor, family- and pet-friendly products on request, with surface-safe solutions for floors. If a company says "natural" and cannot name a single product, keep asking.

It is also fair to ask what they do not use. Some households want to avoid bleach, ammonia or strong disinfectants altogether. Others want a stronger product in the bathroom and gentler ones everywhere else. A good crew adjusts to the household.

Can I use my own products?

Yes, and the answer to this question tells you a lot. People with allergy, asthma or fragrance sensitivities often already have a trusted brand under the sink. We keep an option to use your preferred products, and we do the same room-by-room checklist either way. A company that refuses, or insists on its own in every case, is putting its routine ahead of your household.

Is fragrance-free actually fragrance-free?

This one trips people up. Some "unscented" products mask odor with another chemical. Ask whether the company offers a true fragrance-free option and whether the crew also avoids scented air fresheners and laundry products. For households sensitive to fragrance, small things like a dryer sheet smell on a wiped counter are enough to cause a problem.

How do you keep dust from just moving around?

Green cleaning is not only about products. Technique matters, especially in Greer, where spring pollen settles on every window sill and red clay tracks in on shoes year round. Our team uses microfiber cloths that capture dust instead of spreading it, and a hand-wiping technique that reduces how much product is needed. Ask any company what they use on dusty surfaces and floors, and whether the vacuum has a filter that traps fine particles.

  • Which products are used in kitchens and bathrooms?
  • Are cloths reusable and washed between homes?
  • Does the vacuum have a sealed filter?
  • Can the crew skip scented sprays entirely?

Does green cleaning take longer?

Sometimes, and an honest company will say so. Gentler products need more contact time and more hand work on heavy build-up. A bathroom that has not been cleaned in months, or a greasy stovetop, may take a bit longer than it would with a strong chemical. For homes that already stay clean, there is little difference. If the home is in rough shape, we may suggest a first reset visit and lighter visits after.

How much does green cleaning cost in Greer?

Ask the company to explain how the price is decided, not only what it is. At Perfect Services the price follows the size of the home, whether you want our products or yours, how often you book, the condition of the home and the number of bathrooms, which are the most time-intensive rooms. Recurring visits cost less per visit than a single one. We give an exact price only after a free in-home estimate, where the team sees the home and talks through your products and sensitivities, with no commitment. We do not quote by phone or photo.

What to expect on the first visit

Expect a walkthrough before any work starts. The team asks about the products you already use, which rooms matter most and whether anyone in the house reacts to specific smells. They look at the stovetop, the shower and the floors, because those show how much work the first clean will be. Many homes need a thorough first visit to get to the maintenance standard, and lighter visits after that.

Be ready to talk about babies' play areas, pet bowls and any surface you worry about, such as a natural stone counter. A careful crew will ask before using anything on stone, and will tell you if a product you own is a poor fit for a surface. That conversation is a good sign.

After the visit, the home should smell like very little. A strong "clean" smell, even a pleasant one, is the thing a fragrance-sensitive household will notice first. If it lingers, tell the company, because that feedback shapes the next visit.
