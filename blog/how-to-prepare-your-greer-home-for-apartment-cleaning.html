How to prepare your Greer home for apartment cleaning

Prepare for apartment cleaning in Greer, SC: what to put away, what to leave, and what we check in the first hour. Book a free in-home estimate today.

Key takeaways

  • You do not need to clean before we arrive, just clear the way.
  • Put away small valuables and tell us about pets, gate codes and parking.
  • The first hour includes a walkthrough that sets the order of work.

Before a team comes to your apartment, the question we hear most is if you need to clean first. You do not. What you can do is make the first ten minutes easier, which in a small unit makes a visible difference in how much detail the team has time for.

Below is what we do in the first hour of a visit, and what you can do in advance so that hour goes well.

The first ten minutes: the walkthrough

When the team arrives at an apartment in Greer, they start with a walk through the whole unit before touching anything. They look at which rooms get the most use, where the pet sleeps, what the floors are made of, and whether any surface needs a gentler approach. This is also where we ask about the things you would like us to focus on. Most renters name one or two spots: the shower, the stovetop, the sliding door, the area behind the bed. That list sets the order of work.

If you can be home for the walkthrough, the visit goes better. If you cannot be, a short note on the counter does the same job.

What to do before we arrive

These tasks take about fifteen minutes in most apartments:

  • Put away small valuables, jewelry, cash and paperwork
  • Clear the counters of loose items so they can be wiped
  • Move clothes and shoes off the floor of the bedroom and entry
  • Rinse dishes or load the dishwasher so the sink is free
  • Secure pets in a room or tell us how they behave with visitors
  • Confirm the gate code, parking spot and building entry plan

Notice that none of that is cleaning. The goal is access. A floor with nothing on it is a floor we can vacuum and mop edge to edge, and a counter with nothing on it is a counter we can wipe underneath the items too.

The first hour: the order we work in

After the walkthrough, the team works top to bottom and room to room, so dust you knock down gets picked up later. A typical order in an apartment is:

  1. Dusting of surfaces, shelves and furniture, starting high and moving low
  2. Kitchen: counters, sink, stovetop and appliance fronts
  3. Bathroom: toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity
  4. Baseboards and door frames, a step many people skip
  5. Beds made and trash emptied
  6. Floors vacuumed and mopped last, working out toward the door

Floors go last because everything above them drops something onto them. The balcony or patio door glass and the light switches and handles get their turn near the end. It is the Brazilian detail-first method in a small space: the team keeps going past the visible surfaces into the places guests, landlords and visitors notice.

Special cases to mention

A few situations change the preparation, and we would rather hear about them early.

Move-out. If you are leaving a lease, tell us. A move-out cleaning follows a different checklist than a routine visit, and the unit has to be empty. Pets. Hair collects on baseboards, under couches and along the edge of the carpet. Tell us where the pet spends most of its time. A long gap since the last clean. If the unit has not been cleaned in months, we may suggest a first visit that goes deeper, then a lighter schedule afterward. If you are wondering how recurring visits work, our article on what to expect from your first apartment cleaning in Greer covers it step by step.

A word on timing. In a small unit the whole visit is shorter than most people expect, so choose a time when you can be reachable by phone or text in case the team has a question about a surface. Morning visits tend to leave the apartment fresh for the evening, and being out of the way on the first visit lets the team move freely from room to room. If a maintenance visit or a delivery is scheduled the same day, mention it so nobody is surprised at the door.

After the visit

Hosts and tenants who give us feedback after the first clean get a better second one, because we note the preferences: which products you avoid, which rooms matter most and which time of day works. When the team finishes, take a short walk with them or look through the unit on your own. If something was missed or a spot needs another pass, tell us right away. We would rather fix it that day than hear about it later. Many people choose a recurring schedule after the first visit, once they see how the unit feels. Frequently asked questions

Do I need to be home during apartment cleaning in Greer?

Not necessarily. Being home for the first walkthrough helps. If you cannot be there, leave a note and make sure the team has the gate code and parking details.

What should I put away before the cleaners arrive?

Small valuables, cash, paperwork and anything fragile. Clear loose items from counters and floors so the team can clean the surfaces and not around objects.

Should I tell the team about my pets?

Yes. Let us know where the pet stays during the visit and where hair builds up most. It changes how we approach baseboards, furniture and floors.
