Winter post-construction cleaning in Greer: indoor season tips

Winter post construction cleaning Greer SC: how cold weather affects dust, drying and timing after a remodel or build, plus a checklist and free estimate info.

Perfect Services Team
November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Winter keeps dust inside because windows stay shut and the heat circulates it.
  • Cold slows paint, caulk and adhesive curing, so the cleaning date may shift.

In July you can open every window and let the drywall dust find the door. In January you cannot. If you have just finished a kitchen remodel, an addition or a new build in Greer this winter, the dust has nowhere to go but your air handler, your ceiling fans and the top of your new cabinets. That is the main thing that changes in post-construction cleaning once the weather turns cold.

What winter does to a construction site

Cold slows everything. Paint, caulk and adhesive need longer to cure, so cleaning too early can smear wet or tacky finishes. Contractors sometimes run heaters to keep materials workable, which moves dust around. Mud and red clay get tracked in on boots because the ground is wet, and a damp floor plus fine drywall powder turns into a film that is hard to lift with a dry mop. Because the HVAC system is running, any dust left in vents gets blown back out when the heat kicks on.

Timing the clean in cold weather

  • Wait until painting and caulking have cured enough to touch.
  • Ask your contractor whether the HVAC filter has been changed after sanding.
  • Plan the clean when the heat is running so the team can work comfortably.
  • Schedule the touch-up for just before move-in, since dust settles again.

The order of the work

Our team works top to bottom. Ceiling fans, vents and light fixtures come first, then cabinets, drawers and closets, inside and out. Next come windows, glass, tracks and sills, where paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive hide. Bathrooms and new fixtures are polished, and floors are vacuumed and washed in multiple passes. A final touch-up pass finishes the job. The post-construction cleaning page spells out each step.

Common winter problems after a remodel

Three issues come up repeatedly in winter. The first is condensation on new windows, which can leave water spots and residue on fresh trim, so we wipe sills dry and tell you to keep an eye on them. The second is salt and grit tracked in on shoes, which scratch new hardwood and tile. Put a mat and a shoe rule in place the moment the floor is cleaned. The third is dry air from the heater making dust cling to surfaces with static, especially on electronics, black appliances and glass. A damp microfiber cloth lifts it better than a dry duster.

If your project included a new range, dishwasher or bathroom fan, ask us to check the vents. Protective film on stainless steel and plastic wrap on fixtures can come off with the right care, but they are easy to scratch. We peel them, wipe the surface and leave the manuals where you can find them. Small things like these separate a house that is technically clean from one that feels finished.

Tips for protecting new surfaces

Newly installed surfaces are often more delicate than they look. Unsealed grout, fresh paint and new stone all react to harsh cleaners, so tell us what products the installer recommended and we will follow them. Avoid taping signs to new paint, and keep ladders, tool bags and fans off finished floors. If you plan to bring in furniture right away, ask for felt pads under every leg.

Cold also affects flooring. New hardwood and luxury vinyl expand and contract with temperature, so a house that is kept cold during a build and then heated quickly can show gaps. Run the heat at its normal level for a few days before the cleaning if you can, so the team works on floors that have settled.

Protect the clean afterward

  • Change the HVAC filter after the final clean.
  • Keep a mat at every exterior door.
  • Do not sand or cut anything else in the cleaned rooms.
  • Wait until the touch-up before bringing furniture in.
