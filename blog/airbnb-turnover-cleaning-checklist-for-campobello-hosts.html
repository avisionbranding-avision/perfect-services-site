Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Campobello hosts

A turnover checklist for Campobello Airbnb hosts: what to set up, the order we clean in, and how rural homes change the job. Free in-home estimate first.

A cabin or farmhouse rental in Campobello often belongs to someone who lives somewhere else. The owner cannot swing by at noon to fix a missed spot, and the guest who arrives after a drive up I-26 or US-176 will notice every one of them. That is why Airbnb cleaning in Campobello, SC works best as a written checklist the cleaner follows the same way every turnover, with a report the host can read from a phone.

Campobello sits in Spartanburg County, about 13 miles north of Greer, and our team usually plans around a 25-minute drive. Below is the checklist we use for Airbnb turnover cleaning in Campobello, in the order the work actually happens.

Before the cleaner arrives: what the host sets up

Most missed items trace back to the handoff, not the cleaning. A few things take ten minutes and save the whole visit:

  • Check that the entry method works (lockbox, keypad or smart lock) and that the code is current.
  • Leave clean linens in the same closet every time. Linens are host-provided, so the cleaner should never have to hunt for them.
  • Keep restock supplies (paper towels, toilet paper, soap, coffee, trash bags) in one labeled spot.
  • Send the check-in time and any same-day arrival so the schedule is realistic.
  • Tell the team about anything fragile, any room that stays locked and any quirk, such as a back door that sticks.

The turnover in the order we do it

  1. Walkthrough first. Before touching anything, we walk every room and look for damage, missing items and anything left behind. Photos are taken at this point, not after cleaning, so the report is honest.
  2. Trash and dishes. Bins are emptied and reset with new liners. Dishes are loaded or washed, and the dishwasher is checked for last-night leftovers.
  3. Kitchen reset. Counters, sink, stovetop, microwave and appliance fronts are cleaned. The fridge gets a quick look for abandoned food.
  4. Bathrooms. Toilet, shower, tub, vanity and mirror are cleaned and disinfected. We look at the shower wall and drain for hair, since that is what guests photograph.
  5. Bedrooms. Beds are made with fresh linens, and we check under the bed and behind doors, where guests forget chargers and clothes.
  6. Floors last. Vacuum, then mop, working toward the door so we do not walk on a finished floor. Country homes bring in more grit and dried mud than town rentals, so entryways get extra passes.
  7. Staging. Pillows, throws and the welcome basket are set to match your listing photos.

What a rural property adds to the job

Campobello is a foothills community of farms and country homes, and a rental here tends to behave differently from a town condo. Boots come in off gravel drives. Wood stoves and fireplaces leave ash. Screened porches collect pollen in spring and leaves in fall, and the Upstate's red clay shows up on baseboards and door mats long after the guest has left.

When we plan a turnover, we ask about the porch, the mudroom or entry, any outdoor furniture and any hot tub or fire pit area, because those are the spots a standard apartment checklist never mentions. If a property has several of them, the turnover simply needs a longer window.
