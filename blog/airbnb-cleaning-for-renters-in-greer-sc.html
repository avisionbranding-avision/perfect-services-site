Airbnb cleaning for renters in Greer, SC

Airbnb cleaning for renters in Greer, SC: what to check in your lease, what a turnover includes and how a free in-home estimate sets your price.

Perfect Services Team
November 22, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Renters who host should confirm the lease and local rules allow it before the first guest books.
  • A written turnover checklist keeps your cleaning the same from one guest to the next.
  • The price comes after a free in-home estimate, based on size, linens, restocking and how often you host.

Hosting from a rented place works only if the unit looks the same to every guest and your landlord never has a reason to regret saying yes. That second part is where cleaning decides things. A rental that gets handed back with soap film on the glass and grease on the stove hood is the one that ends a hosting arrangement. This post is for renters in Greer who host, or are thinking about it, and want Airbnb cleaning in Greer that protects both the reviews and the lease.

Start with the lease, not the listing

Before anything else, read what your lease says about subletting and short stays, and ask your landlord or property manager in writing. Some communities allow it, many do not, and local rules can apply on top of that. We are a cleaning company, so we will not tell you what is permitted. What we can say is that hosts who are clear with their landlord from the start tend to have fewer surprises, and a clean, documented unit makes that conversation easier.

What a turnover looks like in a rented apartment or house

Most rentals we see around Greer are smaller than a typical vacation home: one or two bathrooms, a compact kitchen, carpet in the bedrooms and hard flooring everywhere else. That makes the order of work matter more than the hours. The team walks every room first, then works from the top down and from the farthest room to the door. The Airbnb and vacation rental cleaning service follows the same list each time:

  • Full kitchen reset: dishes, counters, sink, stovetop and appliance fronts
  • Bathrooms cleaned and disinfected, with mirrors and fixtures wiped
  • Beds made with fresh linens that you provide
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped, including edges and under the bed
  • Trash removed and bins reset
  • A restocking checklist for your supplies
  • A damage and missing-item report with photos
  • Staging to match your listing photos

The photo report matters more for renters than for owners. If a guest chips a countertop or a blind goes missing, you have a dated record before your own landlord asks questions.

The spots that cost renters reviews

Guests rarely complain about the big things. They notice the shower door track, the microwave ceiling, the hair around the vanity edge, the dust on top of the door frames and the smell of the fridge. We check these every visit, because a one-line comment about a hair on the pillow can outweigh twenty good ones.

How long a turnover takes

A one-bedroom rental in good shape is usually a short visit, and a larger home with two bathrooms takes longer. We give you a realistic window at the estimate, and we confirm it again when you book. If your guest calendar fills quickly around holiday weeks, ask about a standing slot so you are not looking for a cleaner the night before check-in.

A simple routine between guests

Renters who host usually have no storage, so the routine has to fit a closet. Keep one labeled bin for spare linens, one for restock items such as toilet paper, soap and coffee filters, and one shelf for anything guests may need, like an extra key or a Wi-Fi card. When the team arrives, the bins tell us what is stocked, and the shelf tells us what to put back.

Leave a short note for each stay if something specific happened: a spill on the couch, a guest with a dog, a late checkout. A two-line note changes how we spend the hour. We would rather read it before we walk in than guess at the stain on the rug.

Noise, neighbors and shared walls

In an apartment building, a cleaning crew coming and going is another reason a neighbor might talk to the office. Our team keeps the visit quiet: no moving furniture across the floor, no vacuuming at odd hours, and trash goes out in the same bags guests would use. If your community has rules about contractors, parking or gate access, tell us at the estimate and we follow them. Staying on good terms with the people next door is part of keeping a rental arrangement going.

Guests, pets and extra mess

Some stays leave more behind. Guests who travel with a dog leave hair along the baseboards and under the couch cushions, and a family with children leaves crumbs in places you would not guess. Mention it in your note, and the team spends the extra time where the stay demands it. At the estimate we also ask if your listing allows pets, because that changes how we plan the vacuuming and the fabric work in each room.

One last thing renters tend to forget is the closet and the entry. Guests drop bags there, and the floor takes the red clay and pollen that Greer carries inside on shoes. We wipe the entry floor and the door threshold on every visit, since it is the first impression a guest has and the first thing a landlord sees.
