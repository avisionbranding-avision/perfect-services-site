Move-out cleaning before the holidays in Greer

Move out cleaning before the holidays in Greer SC: how to schedule around Thanksgiving and Christmas, what to clean, and how the free estimate works.

Perfect Services Team
November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Holiday move-outs are a scheduling problem as much as a cleaning one.
  • Decide the cleaning date first, then plan the movers and travel around it.
  • A free in-home estimate gives you a fixed price and a date you can plan around.

Plenty of Greer families end up moving the week before Christmas. A lease ends December 31, a job starts January 2, a house closes the Friday before Thanksgiving. And while everyone is thinking about the truck and the boxes, the cleaning gets pushed to the last night. This post is a scheduling guide for exactly that situation, because with holidays the date matters more than the checklist.

Start with the hard dates

Write down three things before anything else: the day your keys are due, the day your movers are booked, and any travel. Then work backward. The cleaning must come after the last item leaves the house and before the key handover. That window is often a single day. If you also have family in town or kids out of school, a day-long cleaning is the part you most want off your plate.

Why December fills up

Holidays compress everything. Leases end at month-end, home closings try to beat the year-end, and many people travel between Thanksgiving and New Year, so crews get booked days or weeks ahead. In Greer, relocations connected to the Upstate's manufacturing and airport-area jobs also bunch around the end of the year. The earlier you book the estimate, the more options you have for the cleaning day.

A move-out clean covers inside and outside of cabinets and drawers, inside the refrigerator, freezer, oven and microwave, the range hood, stovetop and backsplash, bathrooms with toilet base, grout, glass and vanity interiors, closets with shelves, rods and floors, baseboards, doors, frames and light switches, window sills, tracks and blinds, and floors vacuumed and mopped edge to edge. Holiday-specific things people forget

  • Nail holes, tape marks and sticky hook residue from holiday decorations.
  • Pine needle debris and wax drips on hearths and mantels.
  • Oven grease from holiday baking.
  • Gift boxes and packing paper left in closets, which hides dirt until the last minute.
  • The garage or entry floor where wet boots and trees were carried.

Cleaning in a busy household

If you still have family staying with you, ask the team to clean a few rooms in an order that fits your move: for example, upstairs bedrooms first, then the kitchen last. Keep a clear path to the kitchen and bathrooms and leave the heat on. Our Greer crews work from the north end of town to the airport area on I-85, SC-14 and US-29 every day, so a holiday-week address is not far out of range. Two real-world scenarios

Here are two situations we see most often. In the first, a family is closing on a house on a Friday before Christmas and the movers finish Thursday evening. The cleaning is scheduled Friday morning, with a quick walkthrough as soon as the team leaves. It works because the cleaning day was fixed weeks earlier. In the second, a renter's lease ends December 31 and the new place is in another state. They book the estimate early in the month, travel with the truck on the 28th, and let the team clean on the 29th while they are on the road. The keys are returned through the property manager.

Both plans depend on one thing: someone can confirm the work was done. If you will not be there, ask for a walkthrough call or a set of photos of finished rooms, and make sure the landlord or agent has access. Do not leave valuables behind, and leave appliances plugged in or out as the lease says.

If your plans change, tell us. A one-day delay is usually easy to absorb when we know ahead. A same-day change in the middle of holiday week is much harder, which is why the early estimate matters so much.
