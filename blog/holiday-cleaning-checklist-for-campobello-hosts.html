If you are hosting this holiday season in Campobello, the guest room is rarely what causes stress. It is the kitchen the night before, the bathroom everyone shares and the muddy entry that sees twenty pairs of shoes. A single professional visit timed a few days before company arrives handles the heavy parts, and this checklist shows how to split the work so the day itself stays calm. Many Campobello homes sit on farm land or country lots off US-176, which means gravel, red clay and leaves come inside all fall, so the floors and entry deserve extra attention.

Start with the calendar, not the mop

The order matters more than the effort. Work backward from the day guests walk in, and decide what a team should do once and what you can keep up yourself.

  1. Two weeks out: clear the clutter. Pack away what will not be used, empty the guest closet and sort the pantry so the kitchen has room for holiday cooking.
  2. One week out: book your cleaning date. Holiday weeks fill early, and a visit three to four days before the meal leaves time to cook without redoing anything.
  3. Cleaning day: let the team do the kitchen, bathrooms, floors, baseboards and glass while you wash linens and set up beds.
  4. Day before: light touch only. Wipe the sink, sweep the entry, take out the trash and put out fresh towels.

The add-ons hosts ask about most

The oven is the big one. If the turkey or ham is going in it, cleaning the inside beforehand keeps smoke and burnt smells out of the house, and it is available as an add-on. Inside the fridge is the second request, mostly because every shelf will be full by Thanksgiving week. Inside cabinets help if you are rearranging dishes for a large table. Tell the team during the walkthrough so they can plan the time.

Pets change the job too. Hair gathers under sofas and along baseboards, and guests with allergies notice it faster than you do. Mention how many animals live in the house and the team will adjust the order of work.

What the team checks in the walkthrough

Before the first room, the cleaners usually walk the whole house and ask where guests will spend time. In a lot of country homes that means the kitchen, the bathroom nearest the guest room and the entry where coats pile up. Knowing that, they spend the detail time there. They also look at the floors: gravel dust and red clay settle into grout lines and the corners of hardwood, and those corners are where a quick vacuum falls short. After a long dry spell, fine dust also collects on window sills and ceiling fan blades, so the team wipes both when time allows. Tell them about any surface that needs a gentler touch, such as an antique table or a stone counter, so nothing is cleaned the wrong way.
