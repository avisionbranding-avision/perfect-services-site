The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Marietta

The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Marietta, SC: rough clean, final clean and touch-up, plus what affects price and a free in-home estimate.

Key takeaways

After a remodel in Marietta, the contractor's truck leaves and the house looks done until the sun hits the windows. Then you see it: a white film on every surface, paint specks on the glass and a gritty feel underfoot. Construction dust is finer than regular dust, and it takes a staged approach to remove. Here are the three phases of post-construction cleaning, and what happens in each.

Why one pass is never enough

Drywall sanding creates the most fine dust, and it settles on ledges, inside cabinets and into vents. Wipe it once and some lifts, and some smears. Come back to a space that has been cleaned the same day and more dust has settled from the air. That is why the work is done in phases, with the dustiest work first and the finishing touches last.

Phase one: rough clean

This happens while construction is winding down, before painting and fixtures are finished. The goal is to remove debris and the heaviest dust so the other trades can finish in a cleaner space. Typical work includes sweeping and vacuuming, removing leftover material and wiping the worst of the dust from surfaces.

Not every job needs a rough clean. A small bathroom renovation might go straight to the final clean. Larger projects with many trades usually benefit.

Phase two: final clean

This is the main job, done after the construction crew is finished. It covers fine construction dust on all surfaces, ledges and trim, the inside of cabinets, drawers and closets, windows, glass, tracks and sills, light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans, new fixtures polished in the bathrooms, and floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes. Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive are removed too, carefully, so surfaces are not scratched.

Phase three: touch-up before move-in

A day or two later, a final pass catches what settled. Dust that was suspended in the air lands on freshly cleaned surfaces, footprints appear and a few fingerprints show up on new glass. The touch-up visit takes much less time than the final clean and is what makes the house look like a new home on the day you hand over keys or move in.

A checklist for homeowners

  • Ask your contractor which phases they already cover, so nothing is paid for twice.
  • Keep HVAC filters changed during and after the work.
  • Cover or close vents during sanding if you can.
  • Book the final clean after the last trade leaves, not before.
  • Leave stickers on new appliances and windows until the cleaning team arrives, if you can, so they can be removed properly.

How long does it take?

A final clean on a mid-sized home commonly takes most of a day for a team, and longer for a large addition or a whole-house renovation. The estimate visit sets a realistic schedule, so you can plan move-in or furniture delivery around it.

Special surfaces after a remodel

New surfaces need care. Fresh countertops, new tile and recently installed floors can be scratched by the wrong tool, and grout and caulk may need time to cure before they are scrubbed. Ask your contractor what the manufacturer says about cleaning new materials and share it with the team. Stone, hardwood and new stainless fixtures each have their own rules.

Dust also gets into places you would not guess: the tops of door frames, the inside of window tracks, the grooves in cabinet hinges and the lip of a recessed light. Those are the spots a final clean has to reach, and the reason a homeowner doing it alone often needs a second round the following week.

Timing around inspections and closings

If the project ends with a city inspection or a closing, work backward from that date. The final clean goes after the last trade finishes and before the inspection or walkthrough. The touch-up goes as close to move-in as you can manage. Builders in the Marietta area who juggle several homes often book both visits together, which keeps the team on the same property and the dust from coming back between visits.
