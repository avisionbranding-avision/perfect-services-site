Questions we hear from 29651 business owners

Do you clean during business hours?

Most offices prefer after-hours visits so staff and clients are not walking around wet floors. Salons and clinics sometimes want early mornings instead. We agree the window at the estimate and put it in writing so the front desk knows who is coming and when.

Who brings the supplies?

We bring cleaning products and tools. Paper goods, hand soap and liners are restocked from what you keep on site, and we tell you when something is running low rather than leaving the restroom empty.

Can you clean around our schedule?

Yes. A retail store that closes at a set hour and a medical-style space with early appointments need different plans, and the visit time is part of the plan, not an afterthought.

What if we have carpet in one room and tile in another?

That is normal. The team vacuums carpet and mops hard floors with the right method for each, and notes any spot that needs more than a routine pass.

What a regular office visit covers

This is the checklist we work from for small and medium offices, retail stores, salons, studios and common areas:

  • Desks, counters and workstations wiped
  • High-touch points cleaned: door handles, light switches and keypads
  • Restrooms cleaned, disinfected and restocked
  • Break room sink, counters, microwave and fridge exterior
  • Trash and recycling removed
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped
  • Glass doors and entry areas
  • Reception and waiting areas dusted

Anything outside that list, such as inside the fridge or a window wall, can be added. Just say so at the estimate so it is planned for.

Picking a schedule that matches your traffic

A shop with walk-in customers wears out its entry and floors faster than a quiet back office, so it usually needs more visits. A small professional office with few visitors may be fine weekly. Monthly visits make sense for deeper tasks on top of a regular service, not instead of one.

Our advice is to start a little more often than you think, watch the restrooms and break room for a few weeks, and then adjust. It is easier to lower a schedule than to recover from a dirty one. The same walk-through also tells us how long a visit takes, which we share with you once we have seen the space.

The detail-first method in a work setting

Perfect Services is Brazilian-owned, and the habit we bring is attention to edges: door frames, the base of toilets, the track of a glass door, the corner under a reception desk. In an office these are the spots clients notice because they are at eye level or underfoot. It is slower than a quick wipe, and it is why people keep us on a schedule.

What we check in the walk-through

The estimate visit is short, but we look at specific things. We count the restrooms and see how much they are used. We note where the trash is collected, whether the break room has a full kitchen or just a sink, and what the floors are made of. We also ask who opens the building in the morning, because a cleaner who finds a locked door or an alarm code that does not work loses the visit, and so do you. Once those answers are on paper, the schedule is easy to write down and easy for your staff to follow.

A few owners ask us to leave a notes sheet at the front desk. That works well: if a client spilled coffee in the waiting area or a light is out, you write it down and we handle it on the next visit.
