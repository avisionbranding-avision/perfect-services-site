The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Taylors

Post construction cleaning Taylors SC explained in three phases: rough clean, final clean and touch-up. Know what happens and when before move-in.

Perfect Services Team
November 9, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Cleaning after a remodel works best in three phases, not one big pass at the end.
  • Drywall dust is the hardest part: it settles in vents, on ledges and inside cabinets.
  • Booking phases together can help you meet an inspection, closing or move-in date.

A remodel is exciting until the work is done and every surface is covered in fine gray dust. That is where professional post-construction cleaning in Taylors comes in. The best results come when the cleanup is planned in three phases instead of one rushed pass at the end. This guide explains each phase and what to expect.

Taylors, in Greenville County (ZIP 29687), is a community of established neighborhoods and family homes, many of them updated room by room. Kitchen remodels, bathroom renovations, finished basements and additions are all common projects, and they all leave the same thing behind: dust that gets into everything.

Why construction dust is different

Normal household dust is mostly lint and skin. Construction dust, especially from sanding drywall, is much finer and clings to surfaces. A broom pushes it into the air, where it settles again. Fine dust ends up:

  • On top of door frames, ledges and trim
  • Inside vents, ceiling fans and light fixtures
  • In drawers, cabinets and closets
  • On window glass, tracks and sills
  • In the grooves of new floors

Cleaning it properly takes the right order and several passes. That is why the work is best divided into phases.

Phase 1: The rough clean

The rough clean happens while the work is finishing, or right after the heavy work ends and before the final trades come back. The goal is to clear the big mess so painters, floor installers and fixture crews can do their jobs on a clear surface. It usually includes:

  • Removing debris, packaging and scraps
  • Vacuuming and sweeping floors
  • Wiping the heaviest dust from surfaces and ledges
  • Clearing out rooms so the final steps are not slowed down

Not every project needs a rough clean, but larger jobs benefit from one. It keeps dust from being tracked everywhere.

Phase 2: The final clean

This is the main event. It starts once the construction work, painting and installs are finished. A detailed final clean covers the whole space from top to bottom:

  • Fine dust removed from surfaces, ledges and trim
  • Inside cabinets, drawers and closets
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills
  • Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive removed
  • Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished
  • Floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes

Our Brazilian detail-first method works from the highest points down, so dust you move does not land on something you have already cleaned. The full scope is on our post-construction cleaning service page.

Phase 3: The touch-up pass

Dust has a way of settling after the main clean, and small things show up once the lights are on and the furniture goes in. The touch-up is a shorter visit right before move-in or an inspection. It covers:

  • Any dust that settled after the final clean
  • Smudges, fingerprints and marks from movers or installers
  • A last look at glass, floors and bathrooms

It is the difference between "mostly clean" and ready for a family to live in.

What to expect on cleaning day

Before the team arrives, walk the space with your contractor and make sure the work is truly finished. If painters or installers come back after the final clean, dust and marks come back with them. When we arrive, we start by walking the rooms with you to note any areas of concern, such as new countertops, specialty glass or freshly sealed floors. That gives us a chance to match products to the material, so nothing gets scratched or dulled.

From there the team works in a set order: high surfaces and fixtures first, then cabinets and built-ins, then windows and glass, then bathrooms, and floors last. Floors get several passes because fine dust keeps lifting as the room is worked. At the end, we do a walk-through with you and note any spot you would like us to look at again.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Cleaning before the final trade has left, which means cleaning twice.
  • Using a dry cloth on fine dust, which just spreads it.
  • Forgetting the inside of drawers, cabinets and closets, where dust settles during construction.
  • Skipping the HVAC vents and returns, which can blow dust back into the home.
  • Waiting until the day before move-in to book, when schedules are tight.

Planning the phases early with your contractor is the easiest way to avoid every one of these.

Tips for homeowners and contractors

  1. Ask your contractor to remove large debris before cleaners arrive.
  2. Share your move-in or inspection date as early as possible.
  3. Point out special surfaces, like new stone or specialty flooring.
  4. Keep the HVAC filters changed, since dust gets into the system during a remodel.
  5. After move-in, consider a deep cleaning visit to reset the rest of the home.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need all three phases of post-construction cleaning?

Not always. Smaller kitchen or bathroom projects may only need the final clean, while larger remodels and additions benefit from all three phases.

When should post-construction cleaning be scheduled in Taylors?

The final clean should happen after painters, floor installers and fixture crews finish. Contact us early so we can match your move-in or inspection date.

Will you remove paint and sticker residue?

Yes. Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive on glass and surfaces are part of a post-construction final clean, handled carefully to avoid damage.
