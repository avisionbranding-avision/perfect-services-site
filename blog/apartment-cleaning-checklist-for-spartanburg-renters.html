Apartment cleaning checklist for Spartanburg renters

A room-by-room apartment cleaning checklist for Spartanburg SC renters, plus how pricing works and when a free in-home estimate beats doing it yourself.

Key takeaways

Most apartment walk-throughs in Spartanburg are decided in the first five minutes, and they are decided in the kitchen and the bathroom. If the stovetop has baked-on grease and the shower floor has a gray film, the rest of the unit gets judged more harshly. This checklist is written for renters who want to handle that walk-through well, whether the lease ends next week or you simply want a Saturday routine that works.

Spartanburg has a mix of housing that changes the job. Near Morgan Square and the colleges you find older buildings with original floors and narrow bathrooms. Out along I-26 and I-85 there are larger complexes with balconies, shared parking and carpet throughout. The steps below work for both, and where the two differ we say so.

Start with the order, not the products

The biggest time-waster we see is cleaning in the wrong direction. Dust that falls from a ceiling vent lands on a counter you already wiped. A mopped floor gets tracked on while you carry a trash bag out. So the order matters more than any spray bottle:

  1. Open windows if the weather allows and take out everything that is trash, recycling or laundry.
  2. Dust high first: vents, ceiling fan blades, light fixtures, top of the fridge, shelves.
  3. Do the kitchen, then the bathroom, then bedrooms and living space.
  4. Vacuum every carpeted area and every rug, then mop hard floors last, finishing at the front door.

The room-by-room checklist

Print this or keep it on your phone. It mirrors what a professional apartment cleaning covers, so you can compare later if you decide to hand it off.

Kitchen

  • Stovetop and drip pans: soak, then scrape, because heat sets grease like glue.
  • Inside the microwave: a bowl of water with a splash of vinegar for three minutes loosens everything.
  • Refrigerator shelves and drawers, plus the door seal where crumbs hide.
  • Cabinet fronts and handles, especially around the stove and the trash cabinet.
  • Sink and faucet, with the drain edges wiped by hand.

Bathroom

  • Toilet base and behind the seat hinges, which most people skip.
  • Shower walls, door track and the corners of the tub.
  • Mirror, vanity top and the inside of the vanity drawers.
  • Exhaust fan cover, since lint collects there and inspectors do look up.

Bedrooms and living areas

  • Baseboards and door frames wiped by hand, not just dusted.
  • Light switches and door handles, the spots everyone touches and nobody cleans.
  • Closet floors and shelves.
  • Balcony or patio door glass and the sliding track, where red clay dust from the Upstate settles after a dry week.

What people usually miss in a Spartanburg apartment

A lot of the units we walk through have the same blind spots. The sliding door track is the first: it holds dirt, pet hair and dead bugs, and a vacuum hose with a narrow tip is the only thing that reaches it. The second is the inside of the oven. Renters wipe the door glass and forget the floor of the oven, which is where the smell comes from. The third is the baseboard behind the bedroom door, the one nobody sees until the door is closed during the walk-through.

If you have a pet, add the corners where fur gathers along walls, and check under the bed. If you smoke or cook with a lot of oil, wash the kitchen walls near the stove with a damp cloth and a little dish soap. Do not use abrasive pads on the walls or the vanity top; scratches cost more than the grime did.

How long it takes, and when to hand it off

A tidy one-bedroom takes most renters two to three hours working alone, and a move-out can stretch to a full day once the oven, fridge and closets are counted. A two-person team that does this every week is naturally faster, which is part of why many renters in Spartanburg book one visit right before the final inspection and do the packing themselves. If your deposit depends on the result, that trade is usually worth considering. Spots in the final week of the month go quickly, so asking early helps; you can usually book this week if the schedule has an opening after the walkthrough.
