Apartment cleaning checklist for Cowpens renters

A room-by-room checklist for apartment cleaning in Cowpens, SC, from the kitchen to the balcony door. Learn what to expect and book a free in-home estimate.

Key takeaways

  • In a small apartment the bathroom and kitchen take most of the time, so start there.
  • Baseboards, door frames and the balcony door glass are the spots renters forget.

A one-bedroom apartment does not take long to clean, and that is exactly why the small things show. Fingerprints around the light switch, a ring in the tub, dust along the track of the balcony door. If you rent in Cowpens, this checklist covers the whole unit in the order that saves the most time, and it matches what our team does on an apartment cleaning visit in Cowpens.

The kitchen comes first

Apartment kitchens are compact, which means grease from the stovetop lands on the cabinet next to it and the microwave door in the same week. Clear the counters, then work from the top down.

  • Wipe the range hood or the cabinet above the stove, where grease collects.
  • Clean the stovetop, then the oven door and the knobs.
  • Wipe fridge and dishwasher fronts and handles, which are the most touched surfaces in the unit.
  • Scrub the sink and faucet, then dry the chrome so it does not spot.
  • Wipe cabinet fronts around the sink and trash pull-out.
  • Sweep and mop last, so crumbs from the counters are already on the floor.

Bathroom: the slowest room per square foot

A small bathroom with a tub-shower can take longer than the living room. Soap film and hard water spots need a few minutes to soften before they wipe off, so spray first, move on to another task, and come back.

Do the toilet last in the bathroom and use a separate cloth for it. Check behind the toilet base, the vanity edge and the bottom of the shower curtain or glass track. These are the spots a landlord looks at during a walk-through.

Living area and bedroom

In an apartment the living room, dining nook and entry are often one open space, so dust travels. Dust shelves, TV stands and window sills first, then baseboards, then vacuum. Many apartment complexes have carpet, and the vacuum lines in the hallway are a good sign the job was done carefully.

In the bedroom, pull the bed away from the wall if you can and vacuum under it. Wipe the nightstands and the closet door handles, strip and remake the bed, and empty the trash.

The spots people forget

After enough visits you learn where residents skip. These are the ones we check on every apartment:

  • Baseboards and door frames, especially near the front door where shoes scuff.
  • The track of the balcony or patio sliding door, and the glass itself.
  • Light switches, thermostat and door handles, which we sanitize.
  • The top of the refrigerator and the tops of cabinets.
  • Vents and the bathroom exhaust fan cover.

Small spaces, specific problems

Apartments have their own quirks. Carpet in the hallway shows every footstep, so vacuuming in one direction and then the other leaves clean lines and lifts the pile. Shared walls mean noise, and cleaning products should not leave a strong smell that carries into the corridor. Many units also have limited ventilation in the bathroom, so moisture sits and grout darkens faster than in a house.

If you have a washer and dryer closet, check the lint trap and wipe the top of the machines. If you share a laundry room, wipe down the handles and dispensers you touch. These are the little details that make a unit feel clean the moment you walk in.

Pets and balconies

Pet hair settles along baseboards and in the corners where the carpet meets the wall, and a vacuum with a crevice tool picks it up in a minute. On a balcony or patio, sweep first, then wipe the door glass from the inside and outside. Cowpens sits right off I-85, so a bit of road dust on exterior glass is normal.

Finally, take out the trash and recycling before the cleaners arrive or before you leave for work. It frees the kitchen and bathroom corners for wiping, and it keeps odors from building in a small space.
