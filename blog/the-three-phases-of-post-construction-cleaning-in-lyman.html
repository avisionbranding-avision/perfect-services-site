The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Lyman

Post construction cleaning in Lyman, SC happens in three phases: rough, final and detail. See what each includes, how long it takes and how to book.

Why construction dust is different

Normal household dust is mostly skin, fiber and pollen. Construction dust is fine drywall powder, sawdust, adhesive residue and paint overspray. It is gritty, it settles into places a vacuum does not reach and it can scratch glass and finishes if wiped dry. It also drifts: dust that begins in one room ends up on the top of door frames, inside vent covers and across the whole home.

Phase 1: Rough clean

The rough clean happens while trades are still finishing, or right after the heavy work is done and before painting and flooring are final. The goal is a safe, workable space.

  • Remove debris, packaging, scrap wood and large leftovers
  • Sweep and vacuum floors with a HEPA-style vacuum to capture fine dust
  • Wipe down rough surfaces, window sills and the tops of cabinets
  • Clear the dust from open vents and the inside of fixtures

Phase 2: Final clean

Once painting, trim and flooring are complete, the final clean makes the home look finished. This pass is the most visible, and it is where cleaning teams spend most of their time.

  • Clean windows, glass and mirrors, and remove stickers and tape marks
  • Degrease and wipe cabinets inside and out
  • Scrub bathrooms, tile, tubs and shower glass
  • Clean appliances, door frames and baseboards
  • Vacuum and mop all floors, including the edges

Phase 3: Detail clean

The detail clean is the one many people skip, and it often separates a house that looks clean from one that is move-in ready. A day or two after the final clean, a quick walk-through catches dust that settled after the air cleared.

  • Re-wipe horizontal surfaces such as ledges, sills and shelves
  • Check light fixtures, ceiling fans and switch plates
  • Look at the floors in raking light for streaks and smears
  • Touch up what the walk-through finds

How long does it take?

A small remodel can be finished in one visit, while a larger home may need a full day or a second visit for the detail pass. Giving the team room to work, with the other trades finished and power and water on, helps the schedule.

Tips to make cleaning easier

  • Do not dry-dust: it pushes particles into the air. Use damp microfiber or a HEPA vacuum
  • Replace the HVAC filter after the cleanup, since it will have caught a lot of dust
  • Keep pets and children out until the final clean is done

Mistakes that make cleanup harder

Construction cleanup goes wrong in predictable ways. A few habits will save you both time and scratched surfaces.

  • Using a broom first. Sweeping lifts fine dust into the air, where it settles on surfaces you just finished.
  • Wiping dry. Dry cloths drag gritty particles across glass, stainless steel and new finishes. Damp microfiber lifts them instead.
  • Forgetting the vents. Dust inside registers and returns will blow back into the room the first time the HVAC runs.
  • Skipping the tops. Cabinet tops, door frames and ledges are invisible until the light hits at an angle.
  • Cleaning too early. If trades are still sanding, painting or installing trim, a final clean will be undone within a day.

Who should schedule which phase

Builders and general contractors usually book the rough clean to keep the site safe and the final clean for the walk-through. Homeowners finishing a remodel in an occupied house often need all three phases compressed into a shorter period, with extra care around furniture and personal items. Either way, tell the team what work was done, which rooms were affected and what finishes were installed, since stone, hardwood and fresh paint each call for different methods.

Lyman homes near US-29 and SC-292 also pick up pollen in spring and red clay after rain, so a clean that is finished during a wet week may need a quick touch-up at the entry and floors once the weather clears.
