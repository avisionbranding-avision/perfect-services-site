Someone searching "how much does apartment cleaning cost" in Greer usually wants one number. We understand that, but a 600-square-foot one-bedroom with a single bathroom and a 1,400-square-foot condo with two bathrooms and a dog do not take the same time, and time is what you are paying for. So instead of a number we can show you the five things that decide it, and how to get an exact price without any guesswork.

The five factors behind the price

FactorWhy it changes the time
Unit sizeA studio, a one-bedroom and a three-bedroom are different amounts of floor, dusting and wiping
BathroomsBathrooms are the slowest rooms: shower glass, grout, toilet base, vanity
FrequencyA recurring visit starts from a cleaner baseline, so it costs less per visit
PetsHair and dander add time on floors, furniture and baseboards

Condition matters too. A place that was last professionally cleaned a month ago is a different job from one that has not been cleaned in a season. That is the reason two neighbors in the same building can hear two different answers.

What is included in a standard visit

People are often surprised how much a standard apartment visit covers. Our team works through the kitchen counters, sink, stovetop and appliance fronts, then the bathroom: toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity. Floors are vacuumed and mopped, surfaces, shelves and furniture are dusted, beds are made and trash is emptied. We also do baseboards and door frames, the glass on a balcony or patio door, and sanitize light switches and handles.

That last group is where the Brazilian detail-first method shows. Door frames and handles are the things you stop seeing after a few weeks, and they are the first thing a visitor touches.

A walkthrough usually takes a few minutes. We note the number of bedrooms and bathrooms, check the floors, ask about pets and ask how you want the visits to repeat. If you rent in a community with a gate or tight parking, tell us then, so the visit is planned around it.

How long a visit takes

People also ask how long a cleaner needs for an apartment. A studio with one bathroom is a short visit, and a three-bedroom condo with two bathrooms and a pet is a long one, so the honest answer is that we see it first. During the walkthrough we note the layout and give you a realistic window, so you know whether to be home, whether to leave a key, and whether the building needs a gate code. Most renters prefer to be out of the way, and that works well for us too.

It also helps to know that the first visit usually takes longer than the ones after it. The first clean brings the unit to a baseline: baseboards, door frames, the bathroom grout and the oven front. After that, recurring visits are about keeping it there.

Ways to keep the cost in check

  • Choose a recurring schedule if you can. Every two weeks or monthly costs less per visit than a single clean.
  • Clear the surfaces before we arrive: dishes in the dishwasher, laundry off the bed, papers off the counters.
  • Keep pet hair under control between visits with a quick pass on the sofa and the area near the front door.
  • Tell us up front about extras like the inside of the oven or fridge, so they are planned, not rushed.
  • Share parking and gate details when you book.

One-time clean or recurring visits?

There is no wrong answer, only a different fit for your budget and your calendar. A single deep reset is a good start if the unit has been neglected, and many people then switch to a regular schedule.

If you are moving in, getting ready for a lease inspection or hosting family in a one-bedroom for the holidays, a single visit makes sense, and a Greer apartment cleaning appointment can be booked for that alone. If you want the place to stay at a steady level, recurring visits are easier on the budget and on your weekends.
