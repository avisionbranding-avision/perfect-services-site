An apartment is a small space doing a big job: you sleep, cook, work and relax within a few hundred square feet, so mess shows up fast. Apartment cleaning in Travelers Rest SC, room by room

Start with the kitchen

In a small apartment the kitchen is open to everything else, so grease and smells travel. Work from top to bottom.

  • Wipe counters, the backsplash and the sink, and run the drain clean.
  • Clean the stovetop and the front of the oven, including knobs.
  • Wipe the microwave inside and out.
  • Wipe appliance fronts, cabinet handles and the top of the fridge.
  • Take out the trash and wipe the bin.
  • Sweep and mop last, so crumbs do not travel.

The bathroom, where small rooms hide the most

A single bathroom takes more wear than any other room. Spray the shower and tub first and let the product sit while you do the toilet, the vanity and the mirror. Then go back and scrub the shower walls, the grout lines and the base of the toilet, and finish with the floor along the wall. Wipe the door handle and the light switch on your way out. These are touched constantly and rarely cleaned.

Living area and bedroom

These rooms mostly need dust control. Start high, with the tops of shelves, the TV stand and window sills, and work down to the furniture and floor. Move what you can: pull a chair away from the wall, lift the rug corners, and vacuum under the bed and the couch. If you have carpet, go slowly in overlapping lines. Make the bed last so you do not shake dust over fresh sheets.

The spots most renters miss

When the team walks into an apartment, these are the places we look at first because they show up on any move-out walk-through.

  • Baseboards, especially behind the couch and along the bed.
  • Door frames and the tops of interior doors.
  • The track and glass of a balcony or patio door.
  • Light switches, thermostat and entry door handles.
  • Inside the closet floor, where dust collects out of sight.
  • Window blinds, slat by slat.

Pets, access and small-apartment details

Some details change how a visit goes. Pet hair and dander add time, especially on fabric furniture and along the baseboards. Gate codes and parking also matter: tell your cleaner about both so the team is not waiting outside. If you work from home, let us know which desk areas to leave alone.

A simple weekly rhythm for a small space

You do not need to clean everything every week. A rhythm that works in most apartments: wipe the kitchen and bathroom surfaces a couple of times a week, vacuum and mop once a week, and save the baseboards, blinds and balcony door track for a monthly pass. Doing one small thing each day, such as running a cloth over the counters after dinner, keeps the weekend job short. When life gets busy, a recurring visit from a team picks up the weekly work and the monthly details, so you only need to handle the daily wipe-downs.
