That visit is also your chance to size us up. Ask anything. Most Greer homeowners who try a one-time clean are curious about the same few things, and the answers are easier to give standing in your kitchen than over a text thread.

What the walkthrough checks

We look at the spots that decide how long the job takes: grease behind the stove, soap scum on shower glass, pet hair along the stairs, and how long it has been since a professional last cleaned. Greer homes vary a lot. A historic house near Downtown Greer may have older tile and tall baseboards, while a newer home near GSP Airport tends to have large open rooms with a lot of floor to cover. Neither is better or worse, they simply take different plans.

From the walkthrough we write up the scope, and only then do we give you the price. That is deliberate. A number given before anyone has seen the space is a guess, and we would rather be right.

Cleaning day, in the order we work

The team arrives together and splits up so the house is finished in one stretch. The usual order:

  1. Walk in, confirm your priorities and any no-go areas.
  2. Dust high to low in each room: ceiling corners, door frames, shelves, then baseboards.
  3. Kitchen: degrease the stovetop and backsplash, wipe the cabinet fronts and handles, clean the sink and counters.
  4. Bathrooms: disinfect, polish mirrors and glass, clean around fixtures where buildup hides.
  5. Bedrooms and living areas: dust, tidy surfaces, wipe switch plates.
  6. Floors last: vacuum, then mop, working back toward the door so nobody steps on a wet floor.
  7. Final walk-through and trash emptied.

That is the standard one-time house cleaning checklist, and the Brazilian detail-first method shows up in the small things: the track of a sliding door, the cabinet pulls people touch all day, the edge of the tub.

What to do before we arrive

  • Put away valuables, medications and anything fragile or sentimental.
  • Clear floors and counters of clutter, so the team cleans surfaces rather than moving piles.
  • Tell us about pets and where they will stay during the visit.
  • Mention any surface you worry about, such as natural stone or unsealed wood.
  • Leave a way in: be home, or give clear entry instructions.

You do not need to pre-clean. People often apologize for the mess, and we hear it so often it barely registers. A messy house is the reason you called us.

Common first-timer worries, answered plainly

People ask whether the team will move furniture. Light items, yes, so we can clean under them, but we do not lift heavy pieces or touch anything you have asked us to leave. People ask about keys and alarm codes, and we handle those the way you tell us to, with a clear plan for locking up. People ask about pets, and the answer is to tell us. A dog that loves strangers and one that does not need different plans, and we would rather know at the estimate than at the door.

One more worry is feeling judged. The team has seen every kind of kitchen, from lived-in to neglected, and the job is to fix it, not to comment on it. Most first-time clients in Greer say the nervousness was the longest part of the day.

Timing around the holidays

November and December fill the calendar in the Upstate. Guests arrive, ovens get used, and families want the house ready by a certain afternoon. If your goal is a clean house before a family visit, book the estimate early so the visit lands a few days before the date, not the morning of.
