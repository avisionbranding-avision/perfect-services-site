Post-construction cleaning before the holidays in Greer

Post construction cleaning before the holidays in Greer SC: how to finish a remodel in time for guests, what contractors leave behind, and how to book.

Perfect Services Team
November 25, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • A realistic deadline beats a rushed one; contractors finishing late is common before holidays.
  • The final clean works best after the last trade, with a touch-up right before guests arrive.
  • A free in-home estimate gives you a price and a date to plan the party around.

"We just need it done by Christmas." We hear that a lot from Greer homeowners whose kitchen remodel was supposed to finish in October. The contractor is behind, the cabinets are in, the countertop is waiting for a template, and there is a family dinner on the calendar. This post is for that situation: how to work backward from a holiday date and still end up with a clean house, not a dusty one with a tree in the corner.

Work backward from the party

Pick the day you want the house ready. Subtract one day for the touch-up, two to three days for the final clean, and a buffer for the contractor's last punch-list items. If the contractor says "next week" three times, move the cleaning to the following week and tell the family. Holiday dates are the only ones that do not move.

What contractors leave behind

Even a tidy crew leaves things. Sawdust in drawer corners, caulk smears on tile, tape residue on window glass, paint specks on hardware, dust inside new cabinets and on top of the refrigerator. We also find fine drywall powder inside return vents and on the blades of ceiling fans, which gets blown into the room when the heat runs. If you are hosting, wipe-down alone will not catch these. The post-construction cleaning page lists what our team removes.

A short holiday checklist for a freshly built or remodeled home

  • Fine construction dust removed from ledges, trim and fixtures.
  • Inside cabinets, drawers and closets wiped before you put dishes away.
  • Windows, glass, tracks and sills cleaned of residue.
  • Bathrooms and new fixtures polished.
  • Floors washed in multiple passes before rugs go down.
  • Ceiling fans, vents and lights dusted last, because they drop dust on everything below.

Who should do what

One more way to protect your holiday date is to divide the work clearly. The contractor removes debris and large leftovers. The cleaning team handles dust, residue and detail. The homeowner makes decisions: where furniture goes, which rooms get priority, what to leave until after the holiday. When all three share one calendar, the house is ready. When they are guessing, a plumber arrives the morning of the touch-up and tracks mud across a clean floor.

It also helps to decide which rooms matter most. Most families care about the kitchen, the dining room, the main bathroom and the entry. If time runs short, finish those first and let the garage, laundry room or a back bedroom wait until January. A calm house on holiday day beats a perfect house that was never ready.

We also see homeowners ask about the tree. Set it up after the touch-up, not before, because the needles and the stand create their own mess. Wrapping gifts in a freshly cleaned room is a pleasant way to end the project, and a good reason to protect the work you just paid for.

A quick note on supplies

If you need a few things at home on the day, keep a short list: spare HVAC filters, floor mats for each door, a dust mask for anyone helping, and a bottle of the cleaner your installer recommended for stone or tile. None of this replaces the final clean, but it keeps the house in good shape between our visit and the party. Do not use heavy cleaners on new finishes, and do not wax new floors until the manufacturer says it is safe.

Finally, consider booking a short follow-up visit in January. Houses settle, dust shows up where it was not visible before, and new surfaces sometimes need a gentle second pass after the holiday rush is over.
