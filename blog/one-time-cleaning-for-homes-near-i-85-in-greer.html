Minutes 0 to 10: the walkthrough

The team lead walks the house with you before any product comes out. We are looking at the things that set the pace: how many bathrooms, what the floors are made of, whether there are pets, and where the most buildup has collected. Greer has a wide mix of homes, from older houses near Downtown Greer and Trade Street to newer neighborhoods near GSP Airport and the BMW plant, and each one needs a slightly different plan. This is also when you tell us what matters most. Some people care about the kitchen above everything else. Others just want the bathrooms to feel new.

Minutes 10 to 30: dust first, floors last

Once the plan is agreed, the team splits up so two rooms can move forward at once.

The order matters more than people expect. We work from the top of each room to the bottom, so dust that falls ends up on a surface we have not cleaned yet, not one we just finished. In practice that means ceiling corners and fans, then window sills and tracks, then furniture and shelves, then baseboards and door frames, and only then the floor. In homes near a busy road, window sills and tracks often hold a gray film that a quick pass will not lift, so we give them a little extra time.

Minutes 30 to 60: kitchen and bathrooms

By this point the room is dust-free, and the heavier work begins.

These are the two rooms that take the most time and that people notice most. In the kitchen we degrease the stove top and hood, clean the counters and sink, and wipe the outside of the appliances. In the bathrooms we disinfect, descale where we can and polish glass and mirrors. If you asked for the inside of the oven or fridge, we do it in the same block, so the whole kitchen is finished at once.
