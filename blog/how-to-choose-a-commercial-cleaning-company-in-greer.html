How to choose a commercial cleaning company in Greer

Key takeaways

  • Compare companies using the same written scope.

Choosing a cleaning company for your office, clinic or shop is a different decision from hiring someone for your home. You are trusting a team with keys, alarm codes and the first impression your customers get. If you are looking at commercial cleaning in Greer, SC, this guide covers what to ask, what to compare and how to avoid surprises.

Greer's business mix includes professional offices, retail, light industrial space and service businesses near I-85, SC-14, SC-101 and US-29 (ZIPs 29650, 29651 and 29652). Many are small teams without a facilities manager, so the cleaning partner needs to be reliable without being managed every day.

Start with your scope

Before you call anyone, write down what you need. A clear scope gets you comparable quotes and fewer misunderstandings.

  • Square footage and the number of offices, restrooms and break rooms
  • Floor types: carpet, tile, vinyl or concrete
  • Days and hours you want service, and when the space is empty
  • Special areas: exam rooms, kitchens, glass storefronts, warehouse offices
  • Extras: windows, carpet cleaning, floor care or supplies restocking

Questions to ask every company

  1. Is the scope written? A task list by frequency prevents arguments later.
  2. Who will clean? Ask whether the same team comes each time and who supervises.
  3. What are the access rules? Discuss keys, alarm codes and how they are handled.
  4. Is the company insured? Ask for proof of insurance, since accidents and damage can happen.
  5. What are the supplies and equipment? Find out whether the company brings its own products and whether you can request green options.
  6. How are problems fixed? A good company responds quickly when something is missed.
  7. Can I see references? Ask for contacts from businesses with similar needs.

How much does commercial cleaning cost in Greer?

Commercial pricing is based on the building and the schedule. The factors are square footage, the number of restrooms, how many days a week you want service, floor types, the level of cleanliness expected, after-hours access and any extras such as windows or carpet care. Some companies quote per visit, others per month or per square foot, so ask for a total monthly figure to compare fairly. Red flags to avoid

  • A price that is far below every other quote, with no written scope
  • No insurance information or reluctance to provide it
  • A long contract with no way to leave if service falls short
  • No clear contact for problems
  • Vague answers about who will have keys

Start with a trial period

If you are unsure, begin with a short trial: a few weeks of service with a clear checklist. Walk the space after the first visit, note any misses and see how the company responds. That response tells you more than any sales pitch.

What a good start looks like

The first month tells you almost everything. A good provider will walk the space with you, confirm the scope, introduce the team and follow up after the first visit. You should know exactly who to call, how quickly they respond and how supplies are handled. In a small office, little things count: trash emptied every time, restrooms stocked, door glass clear and floors consistent from visit to visit. If you are in a shared building, ask how the company coordinates with the landlord or property manager so you do not end up paying for common areas.

Also think about seasons. Greer entry areas collect pollen in spring, mud in rainy weeks and salt or grit in cold snaps. A provider who adjusts the plan for the season will keep your floors and glass looking sharp all year.
