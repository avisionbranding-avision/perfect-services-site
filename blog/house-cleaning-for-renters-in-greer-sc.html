Start with your lease, not the cleaner

Read the cleaning clause first. Leases differ on what counts as normal wear, what the landlord expects at move-out and whether carpets or appliances need attention. We do not rewrite your lease and we cannot promise what a landlord will accept, but we can clean to a detailed standard and you can compare that with the checklist in your agreement.

Regular visits in a rental

Recurring service in a rental keeps the grime that causes deposit disputes from building up. Bathrooms, kitchen grease and floors are where wear shows. Even one visit every few weeks is easier on a small space than one marathon before the inspection. A visit includes the kitchen counters, sink and stovetop, appliance fronts, bathrooms, hand-mopped floors, dusting, and baseboards and door frames on a rotation.

Spots renters usually miss

  • The track of a sliding patio door.
  • Cabinet handles and the tops of lower cabinets.
  • Behind the toilet and around its base.
  • Baseboards in the hallway, where shoes scuff them.
  • Light switches and door frames.
  • Appliance handles and the front of the dishwasher.

Moving out of a rental

If you are leaving the apartment, a separate service is built for it. Our move-out cleaning in Greer goes through an empty home with the inspection in mind. Many renters use regular cleaning while living there and move-out cleaning at the end.

Rentals have a rhythm of their own. The kitchen takes the hardest wear, since stovetops and oven fronts collect grease that landlords notice. Bathrooms are next, because soap scum and hard water spots build in showers and around faucets. Floors come last: carpet that has traffic marks, and hard floors that scuff along hallways. A steady schedule tackles each of these in small pieces instead of one big, stressful push.

Many tenants in Greer also share a home. If roommates split the cost, a recurring visit is often easier to divide than it looks, and the checklist stays the same each time, which prevents arguments about whose turn it was. Keep common areas clear so the team can reach counters and floors.

Renters sometimes worry that a cleaning service is a luxury. In practice, a clean home is easier to live in, and photos taken at move-in and move-out help show the condition you handled. Ask your landlord about the inspection process early.

Timing matters in a rental. Leases in the Upstate often end at the same times of year, and the weeks around those dates fill quickly. If you are planning a move, book your estimate early. If you are staying, a recurring plan can start any time, and you can ask us to be flexible when your landlord schedules an inspection or a repair visit.

Storage is another common issue. In small apartments, clutter makes cleaning slow because every surface has to be cleared before it can be wiped. A tidy counter and a clear floor let the team spend the time on the actual cleaning, which means a better result for the same visit.

If a rental has older fixtures, tell us what the landlord has said about them. Some finishes need gentle products, and we would rather ask than damage something you might be charged for.
