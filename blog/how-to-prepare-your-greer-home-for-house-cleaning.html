How to prepare your Greer home for house cleaning

How to prepare for house cleaning in Greer, SC: a 20-minute pickup plan, what to leave for the team, and how the free in-home estimate works.

Perfect Services Team
November 14, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Twenty minutes of pickup before the team arrives matters more than any scrubbing you do yourself.
  • Clear counters, floors and bathroom surfaces so cleaning time goes to cleaning, not to moving things.
  • Perfect Services prices every home after a free in-home estimate, so you do not need to guess or send photos.

The most common thing we see on cleaning day in a Greer kitchen is a counter buried in mail, backpacks and half a dozen coffee mugs. None of it is dirty, but all of it has to be moved before the counter can be wiped, and that is time taken away from the work you are paying for. If you want to prepare for house cleaning in Greer, the goal is simple: make every surface reachable, and leave the cleaning itself to the team.

Start with the floors and the counters

Floors and counters decide how fast a visit goes. A hand mop and a vacuum need open floor to travel, and a sponge needs bare counter. Everything else is secondary. Here is the order that works for most homes, from the Trade Street area to the newer streets near GSP Airport.

  1. Walk through with a laundry basket and drop in everything that belongs in another room: shoes, toys, chargers, mail.
  2. Clear the kitchen counters down to the things you use daily, such as the coffee maker.
  3. Empty the dishwasher and load the sink so the sink basin is free to clean.
  4. Pick up floors in the bedrooms, especially under the bed and in front of closets.
  5. Clear the bathroom counters and the shower floor of bottles and razors.
  6. Put out the trash bags you want replaced and tell the team where the cans are.

Most homes need about twenty minutes for this. You do not need to scrub anything first. A common mistake is spending the weekend deep-cleaning before the cleaners arrive, which defeats the purpose.

What to leave alone

Do not wipe down the baseboards, soak the shower or spray the stovetop the night before. Product left to dry on a surface often makes it harder to clean, not easier, and you may use something that does not suit your countertop. Leave the dust where it is. The team works top to bottom, so dust on shelves and ceiling fans comes down first and gets vacuumed with the floor.

The same goes for laundry in progress. A damp load in the machine is fine, but sheets stripped off a bed and piled on the floor are not. If you want the beds made, leave the clean linens folded on the mattress. The house cleaning in Greer visit covers beds, trash and the usual surfaces, so those small tasks can go on the team's list.

A quick pickup checklist

Print this or screenshot it. It fits on a phone screen and takes the thinking out of the morning.

  • Kitchen counters cleared except daily items
  • Dishes in the dishwasher, sink empty
  • Floors clear of toys, shoes, cords and pet bowls
  • Bathroom counters and tub ledges cleared
  • Laundry in baskets, not on the floor
  • Valuables and paperwork put away
  • Pets settled in a room or crate if they are nervous with visitors
  • A note about anything delicate or off-limits

Pets, kids and access

Pets are the part owners worry about most. Dog and cat hair is normal and the team expects it, so you do not need to vacuum before they arrive. What helps is keeping a nervous dog in a room with the door closed, or telling us if a cat hides under the bed we are about to vacuum under. Pet bowls should stay where they are unless you want them washed, and a quick note covers that.

For access, decide how the team will get in, whether you will be home or not, and where the alarm panel is. If you have a gate code or a tricky garage door, write it down. A lot of Greer homes sit on a cul-de-sac with limited street parking, so tell the team where to park so they are not blocking a neighbor.

Breakables get the same treatment. Move anything you would hate to see knocked over, or anything you simply want left alone, to a closet shelf. It takes thirty seconds and removes the worry.
