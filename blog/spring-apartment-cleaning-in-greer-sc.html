Spring apartment cleaning in Greer, SC: a prep plan

Prepping ahead for spring apartment cleaning in Greer, SC. A room-by-room plan for pollen, patio doors and closets, plus how to book a free in-home estimate.

Key takeaways

  • Apartments close up for winter, so spring cleaning means dealing with a season of stale air and stored items.
  • Do the closets and balcony before the pollen peaks, then keep up with light weekly cleaning.
  • Prices come only after a free in-home estimate.

In an apartment, the windows have mostly stayed shut since October, the heat has run for months, and everything you did not want to look at has gone into the closet. That is why a spring reset in a Greer apartment feels different from one in a house. This is a planning guide for February and March, written in November so you can decide now what you want to do yourself and what to hand to apartment cleaning in Greer professionals.

What winter leaves behind in a small unit

Dry heat moves dust. In a small space with one return vent, it collects on the vent cover, the top of the refrigerator, and the shelves you stopped touching. Closed windows hold cooking smells in the kitchen and curtains. Wet boots and coats leave marks on the entry floor and the first stretch of carpet.

Then comes pollen. Greer sees it every spring, in a thick yellow layer on cars and balcony rails. As soon as you open the patio door, it comes in with the air, so the best time to clean the glass and the track is just before you start airing the place out, and again after the peak.

A prep calendar, starting this winter

  • December and January: sort the closets, donate what you did not wear, and make space for seasonal swaps.
  • February: wash curtains and bedding, clean the vent covers, and wipe the tops of cabinets and the fridge.
  • Early March: clean the balcony door glass and track, dust blinds, and wash the inside windows you can reach.
  • Late March: schedule the professional visit for the kitchen, bathroom and floors, so the finish is fresh when you start opening windows.
  • April onward: a light weekly wipe of door frames and sills keeps pollen from building up.

Where the time goes in a spring clean

The kitchen takes the longest. The stovetop, the range hood filter, the inside of the microwave and the cabinet fronts all hold a winter of cooking. The bathroom is next, because soap scum and hard-water spots show on the glass and fixtures. Bedrooms and the living area are mostly dust, but dust on baseboards and door frames takes patience in a place with a lot of furniture along the walls.

If you do it yourself, expect a full day for a two-bedroom unit, and longer with pets. If a team does it, the visit is shorter and the order is set, since they know where to begin. Our team in Greer starts with high dust and ends with the floors, and checks the places renters often miss, such as the sliding door track and the light switches.

The extras are where an apartment spring clean differs from a regular visit. Inside the oven, inside the fridge and the insides of cabinets are the usual add-ons, since they are the places no one reaches in a weekly wipe. Wall and baseboard marks from winter, when furniture was shifted to make room for a tree or visitors, are another common request. Tell us at the estimate which areas you want included, and we will plan the time for them.

If you have a balcony, think about what it holds over winter. Stored chairs, a grill cover and dried leaves in the corners all end up on the sweep list. Door tracks fill with grit and pollen, and the glass shows streaks in low spring sun. We clean the glass on both sides and clear the track so the door slides easily again.
