Apartment cleaning checklist for Campobello renters

Apartment cleaning checklist for Campobello, SC renters: room-by-room tasks, a time-saving order of work, and how pricing works after a free in-home estimate.

Key takeaways

  • A small space gets dirty in concentrated spots: kitchen, bathroom and the entry floor.
  • Work top to bottom, and finish with floors, so each task does not undo the last one.
  • Pricing depends on unit size, bathrooms, frequency, pets and access, and it comes after a free in-home estimate.

An apartment is small enough that you can clean it in one afternoon, and small enough that every missed spot shows. A greasy stovetop is right there beside the sofa. The bathroom door is a few steps from the bed. This checklist is written for Campobello renters who want to keep a unit in shape, whether you do it yourself or hand it to a apartment cleaning service in Campobello.

Start with the kitchen

In a small unit the kitchen is usually part of the living space, so its mess spreads. Clear the counters first, then work in this order:

  1. Stovetop and the wall behind it, where grease collects.
  2. Counters and the backsplash.
  3. Sink and faucet, including the drain edge.
  4. Appliance fronts and handles, which pick up fingerprints daily.
  5. Floor last, after crumbs have fallen from everything above.

The bathroom

Bathrooms in apartments tend to have limited ventilation, so moisture lingers. Spray the shower and tub first and let the product sit while you do the toilet, the mirror and the vanity. Then go back and wipe the shower down. Check the caulk lines and the bottom of the door for mildew. Wipe the exhaust fan cover if you can reach it, because dust there slows the airflow.

Bedroom and living area

Dust from the top: shelves, the top of the television, window sills and then furniture. Make the bed, vacuum under it, and clear the clutter that collects on nightstands. Pets add hair and dander to the couch and curtains, so give those a pass with the vacuum attachment. Take extra time with the glass of the balcony or patio door, since that is what you see every evening.

The apartment checklist

  • Kitchen counters, sink, stovetop and appliance fronts.
  • Bathroom: toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity.
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped.
  • Surfaces, shelves and furniture dusted.
  • Bed made and trash emptied.
  • Baseboards and door frames wiped.
  • Balcony or patio door glass cleaned.
  • Light switches and handles sanitized.

Small-space habits that save time

People in apartments often ask how to stop the place from feeling messy a day after cleaning it. Most of the answer is storage, not scrubbing. A tray for keys and mail keeps the entry clear. A basket by the sofa swallows the blankets and remotes. A laundry hamper with a lid keeps the bedroom floor open, which makes vacuuming a five-minute job instead of a twenty-minute one. When there is less on the surface, every wipe-down goes faster.

Odor control is another point in a small space. The kitchen trash, the bathroom and the pet area share the same air. Take the trash out before it becomes a problem, wipe the inside of the bin when it gets sticky, and let the bathroom fan run after a shower. If you rent, these habits also help at move-out, because deposit inspections tend to focus on the kitchen, the bathroom and the carpet or floors.

Finally, think about the things you do not see daily: the top of the refrigerator, the vent above the stove, the tracks of the sliding door. These collect grime slowly, and a quick wipe every month or two prevents a big job later. A cleaning service can handle them on a rotation so nothing falls through the cracks.
