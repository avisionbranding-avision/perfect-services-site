How often should you book post-construction cleaning in Greer?

How often post construction cleaning Greer projects need: one clean per project, split into phases. Builders and remodelers get a free in-home estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 14, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Post-construction cleaning is booked per project, not on a calendar, and larger jobs are usually split into rough, final and touch-up phases.
  • Builders who finish several homes can set up a repeat arrangement so every closing gets a final clean.
  • Perfect Services prices each project after a free in-home estimate, never from a photo or a phone call.

Picture a Greer family a week after the kitchen remodel wraps. The cabinets are in, the counters are set, and the crew has swept up. Then the sun hits the room and a fine gray film shows on the window sills, the tops of the cabinets and the inside of the drawers. That is drywall dust, and it lands again and again until the last bit of work is done. It is the reason people ask how often post construction cleaning in Greer should be booked.

The honest answer is that the clean follows the project, not a schedule. Most remodels get one final clean. Bigger jobs benefit from two or three visits at different points. A kitchen with new cabinets and counters, for example, often needs only the final clean, while a first-floor addition with new drywall in several rooms is better served by a rough clean once the sanding is done, then a final clean and a touch-up before furniture comes in.

One project, up to three visits

We split post-construction work into phases, because dust and debris show up at different times. You can read the detail in our post on the three phases of post-construction cleaning, written for Travelers Rest but the logic is the same in Greer.

PhaseWhen it happensWhat it covers
Rough cleanAfter framing, drywall and heavy workDebris and heavy dust so trades can keep working
Final cleanWhen the work is finishedFine dust on every surface, windows, cabinets, fixtures, floors
Touch-upJust before move-in or inspectionFingerprints, final dust, a last pass on floors and glass

A small bathroom update may only need the final clean. A whole-house remodel or an addition is where the three-visit approach pays off, because drywall sanding creates the finest dust and a single early clean leaves too much to settle afterward.

How often by project type

  • Kitchen or bathroom renovation: usually one final clean, sometimes a touch-up if the punch list runs long.
  • Room addition or finished basement: a final clean, plus a rough clean if the dust is spreading into the rest of the house.
  • Whole-house remodel: rough, final and touch-up phases, timed with the trades.
  • New construction before closing: a final clean and a touch-up before the walkthrough.
  • Builders and contractors with several homes: one visit per home, set up as a repeat arrangement.

The rule of thumb is simple. Clean after each stage that leaves a layer of dust behind, and do the real final clean only once the last tradesperson has left. If a painter or flooring crew returns after your clean, the touch-up is how we catch what they left. It is also why we ask you to tell us about any punch-list items still open when you book, since a leaking faucet fix or a last coat of paint can bring fresh dust and mess right back into a room we just finished.

What the team does that a regular clean does not

A normal house cleaning would stop at visible dirt. Post-construction work includes fine dust removed from every surface, ledge and piece of trim, the insides of cabinets, drawers and closets, windows, glass, tracks and sills, light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans, new bathroom fixtures polished, and floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes. Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive come off glass and fixtures with the right tools instead of anything that scratches.

We always work top to bottom: fans and fixtures first, then walls and cabinets, floors last. Dust that falls on a finished floor means starting over, so the order matters. Frequently asked questions

How many times should a remodel be professionally cleaned in Greer?

Small jobs need one final clean. Larger remodels and new builds usually get a rough clean, a final clean and a touch-up, spaced around the trades so dust does not settle back on finished surfaces.

Why does construction dust keep coming back after cleaning?

Fine dust from drywall sanding floats in the air and lands on surfaces for days. It also lives in vents and light fixtures. Cleaning top to bottom and timing the final clean after the last trade leaves is the best way to stop it.
