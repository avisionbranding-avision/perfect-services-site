Airbnb cleaning for pet owners in Greer, SC

If your Greer rental allows dogs, or you live there between bookings with your own, the first complaint in a review is rarely about dirt. It is about a smell at the front door or a single hair on a white pillowcase. Airbnb cleaning for pet owners is really a question of what a guest notices in the first thirty seconds, and that is what this page is built around.

What guests notice first in a pet-friendly rental

Guests who bring a dog tend to forgive a little fur. Guests who did not bring one do not. Either way, the first things people judge are the entry, the couch and the bed. Near the door, we look at the mat and the baseboards, where dander settles. On the couch, we pull the cushions and vacuum the seams, because that is where hair packs in after a week-long stay.

Odor is the second test. A rental that has had a wet dog in it after a rainy weekend can smell fine to you and stale to a stranger. We open windows when the weather allows, wash what the host leaves for us to wash, and clean hard floors last so nothing gets tracked back across them.

A pet-aware turnover, step by step

Our Airbnb cleaning in Greer follows the same route every time, and pets change what we do at each stop:

  1. Walk through first. Before we touch anything we look for chewed corners, scratched doors, stains and anything left behind. The damage and missing-item report with photos is part of the service, so you hear about a problem the same day.
  2. Strip and bag the linens. Pet hair clings to fabric, so we shake out duvets away from the made beds. Linens are host-provided, and laundry is done on site or off site depending on your setup.
  3. Dust high, then low. Fur rides on air currents, so shelves, window sills and ceiling fan blades come before the floors.
  4. Vacuum upholstery and seams. Couches, dog beds that stay in the unit and the fabric of chairs near windows.
  5. Kitchen and bathrooms. Water bowls and feeding spots get washed and put back where the host wants them, and bathroom tubs get checked for fur, since guests often rinse a muddy dog there.
  6. Floors last. Vacuum, then mop, working toward the door so the entry is the cleanest spot when the next guest walks in.

Spots that quick turnovers miss

When a rental has had pets, these are the places that usually need a second look:

  • The gap where the couch meets the wall, and the underside of loose cushions
  • Stair edges and the first step, where fur gathers
  • Door frames and the lower part of doors at nose height
  • Heating and cooling vents near the floor
  • The back of the laundry room door and the lint trap, which fills fast after pet bedding
  • Baseboards along the wall where a dog likes to sleep

A lot of the rentals we see around Greer have a laundry room that doubles as a drop zone for wet towels and leashes. It deserves a few minutes at every turnover, so we include it.

Hosts who live out of town

Greer sits right off I-85 and close to GSP Airport, so many guests come for work at the plants or for a weekend trip, and plenty of hosts manage from another city. Pet damage is the thing remote hosts worry about most. The photo report after each clean gives you a record of the unit as we left it and as we found it. We also keep the restocking checklist for host supplies, so a missing lint roller or trash bags shows up before the next check-in, not after.
