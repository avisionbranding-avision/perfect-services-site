The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Mauldin

Post construction cleaning in Mauldin SC happens in three phases: rough clean, final clean and touch-up. See what each includes and how to book an estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 17, 2026 · 5 min read

Key takeaways

  • Three passes work better than one because dust keeps settling after the work ends.
  • Book the final clean after paint and fixtures, and the touch-up a day or two later.

Drywall dust is the thing that surprises Mauldin homeowners after a remodel. The contractor sweeps, the floors look fine, and then the first sunny afternoon shows a pale film on every windowsill. Good post construction cleaning in Mauldin happens in three passes, not one, because the dust keeps settling for days after the last tool leaves.

Mauldin sits in Greenville County (ZIP 29662) along I-385 and Butler Road, about twelve miles from our Greer base. Many of the projects we hear about here are kitchen and bath remodels in family neighborhoods, plus additions and the occasional new build near the corridor. The phases below apply to all of them.

Phase one: the rough clean

This pass happens while trades are still working or just after the heavy work ends. The goal is not shine. It is removing debris and the bulk of the dust so painters, floor installers and finish carpenters are not working in a mess.

  • Pick up scrap, packaging and loose fasteners
  • Vacuum floors, subfloors and the tops of cabinets
  • Remove stickers and protective film that can be taken off safely
  • Wipe window frames before paint goes on

Skipping this phase means the final clean takes far longer, because fine dust gets pushed into finished surfaces.

Phase two: the final clean

This is the big one, done after paint, trim and fixtures are in. It is where the home starts looking like a home. The team works top to bottom, starting with light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans, then walls and trim, then every flat surface, and the floors last.

What a crew actually spends time on:

  • Inside every cabinet and drawer, where sanding dust collects
  • Window glass, tracks and sills, including paint specks and sticker residue
  • Bathroom fixtures, vanity tops and glass, polished once adhesive is gone
  • Floors vacuumed and washed in more than one pass, since the first pass mostly lifts dust

Our Brazilian detail-first method suits this phase because it is hand work. Corners, door frames and the edges where trim meets the floor are wiped by hand rather than sprayed and passed over.

Phase three: the touch-up

A day or two later, the house tells you what it missed. Dust that was suspended in the air lands on counters and shelves. The touch-up pass wipes those surfaces again, checks the glass, and handles anything left by movers or last-minute tradespeople. Booking it together with the final clean is common, and it is the reason a home feels finished at move-in rather than almost finished.

Questions to settle before the crew arrives

Builders and homeowners get better results when they agree on a few things first. Is the power and water on? Are the dumpsters gone? Are the painters truly done? A final clean booked too early just means paying for the same dust twice. If the schedule is tight, plan the rough clean as soon as the trades leave, then place the final clean right before flooring protection comes off.

What a builder or homeowner can do first

Some prep makes any crew faster. Take down tape and protective plastic that the painters left, pick up stray screws, and bag the leftover trim. Keep boxes of tile or flooring out of the rooms being cleaned. These small steps stop a cleaning team from spending paid time on trash removal.

Homeowners often ask whether they can clean after the work themselves. You can, and a small bathroom remodel is a fair DIY project. A whole-kitchen remodel or an addition is different: new cabinets collect fine dust inside, vents carry it into the ductwork, and window glass keeps stubborn paint specks. A razor scraper used carelessly can scratch glass, which is why this part is better left to people who do it every week.

Mistakes that make dust last longer

  • Sweeping dry, which just sends fine dust into the air
  • Cleaning before the HVAC filters are changed, so dust blows back out of the vents
  • Washing floors before the walls and ledges are wiped
  • Using a household vacuum that blows fine dust back into the room

Changing the air filter after the work is done is a cheap step that helps a lot. In Mauldin homes along the I-385 corridor, where summer humidity can turn dust into a paste on glass, wiping with a damp cloth first matters more than spraying.

New floors, new cabinets and new paint deserve a first week of living that is not spent chasing dust, and three passes is how that happens in Mauldin homes.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need all three phases after a Mauldin remodel?

Not always. A small bathroom remodel may only need a final clean and touch-up, while an addition often benefits from all three. The free estimate visit helps decide.

Why is there still dust after my contractor cleaned?

Drywall sanding makes very fine dust that hangs in the air and settles later, which is why a touch-up pass a day or two after the final clean helps.

Can you clean before my closing or inspection date?

Often we can, depending on the size of the home. Tell us the date when you book the free estimate and we will plan the phases around it.
