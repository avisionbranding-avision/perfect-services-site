Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Lyman hosts

An Airbnb cleaning Lyman SC checklist for hosts: the order of a same-day turnover, restocking, damage photos and how estimates work with Perfect Services.

Perfect Services Team
November 19, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Work a turnover in a fixed order so the same steps happen every time.
  • Photos and a restock list protect your reviews and your supplies.
  • Perfect Services prices turnovers only after a free in-home estimate.

The checkout text arrives at 10:42, the next guest lands at 3:00, and in between somebody has to turn a lived-in house into the one in your listing photos. That window is where most Airbnb hosts in Lyman either keep their ratings or lose a star. This checklist follows the order our crew uses for Airbnb cleaning in Lyman, SC, written so you can hand it to any cleaner, including us.

Step one: walk in and look before you touch anything

The first five minutes of a turnover are not for cleaning. They are for finding problems. Before we pick up a sponge, we walk every room and note what changed since the last guest.

  1. Check each bedroom and bathroom for damage, stains or anything left behind.
  2. Photograph anything broken, missing or unusually dirty, with the room visible in the shot.
  3. Open the fridge and cabinets to see what was used or left open.
  4. Strip the beds and start the linens (host-provided) moving.

Those photos become a short damage and missing-item report for you. It is far easier to deal with a platform claim when the evidence was taken at the time, not three days later.

Step two: the kitchen reset

Guests cook, and they often leave the kitchen worse than they found it. We run the dishes first, then work top to bottom: counters, the microwave, the stovetop, the sink and the appliance fronts. The fridge gets emptied of anything left behind and wiped inside the door shelves. Last come the cabinet handles, which hold more fingerprints than anything else in the room.

Step three: bathrooms are where reviews are won

Hair in a shower drain gets mentioned in reviews. We disinfect the toilet, the base and the seat hinges, scrub the tub or shower and the faucet, wipe the mirror and vanity, and check the towels and paper are restocked. Our post on Airbnb bathrooms done right covers the room in more detail.

Step four: beds, floors and the last pass

Beds are made with fresh linens you provide, corners tucked the same way every time so the house looks staged. Then floors: vacuum first, mop after, finishing at the front door. Trash goes out and bins are reset with new bags. A final pass checks the places a hurried guest and a hurried cleaner both miss, such as the sliding door track, the top of the TV stand and the inside of the dishwasher filter.

Tight windows are the reason scheduling matters. If you regularly turn a property on the same day, tell the cleaner so the visit can be planned with enough time rather than squeezed.

Staging and restocking

A reset is not finished until the house matches your listing photos. We put pillows, throws and decor back where they belong, and run a restocking checklist for the supplies you provide: paper goods, soap, coffee, and anything in your welcome basket. If something is running low, you get a note, so you can restock before the next arrival. Hosts who live out of town tell us this report is the part they use most.

Mistakes we see in rental turnovers

A few habits cause most review problems, and none of them are about effort. The first is cleaning before inspecting, which means a stain or a broken item is found by the guest and not by you. The second is rushing the bathroom, since a single stray hair is enough for a guest to mention it. The third is skipping the restock check. Running out of paper towels on a Sunday is a small thing, but it shows up in comments.

The fourth is not keeping a record. A simple note after each turnover with what was restocked, what was replaced and what was reported saves you a lot of time at the end of the month. Our team leaves one after every visit. For hosts who manage a house in Lyman while living elsewhere, that note is how you know the property was handled.
