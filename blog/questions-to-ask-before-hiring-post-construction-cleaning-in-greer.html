Questions to ask before hiring post-construction cleaners

Thinking of hiring post construction cleaning in Greer, SC? Ask these questions about dust, phases, glass, deadlines and the free in-home estimate first.

Perfect Services Team
November 16, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Ask which phases are included: rough clean, final clean and a touch-up pass.
  • Ask how fine drywall dust is handled, since wiping alone spreads it.
  • Price comes after a free in-home estimate, because dust level and project size decide the work.

The contractor says the job is done, the dumpster is gone, and the new kitchen in your Greer house still crunches underfoot. That film on every ledge is drywall dust, and it is why post construction cleaning is a separate trade from regular house cleaning. Before you hire anyone, these are the questions that tell you whether they have actually cleaned up after a remodel.

What phases do you actually do?

Construction cleanups usually happen in stages. A rough clean removes the heavy debris and first layer of dust while work is still going on. The final clean handles every surface once the trades are out. A touch-up pass catches what settles over the next day or two. Ask which of these the company includes, and whether they can be booked together. Our own breakdown is on the Greer post-construction cleaning page, and the older post on the three phases of post-construction cleaning shows the sequence in detail.

How do you deal with fine dust?

This is the question that exposes shortcuts. Drywall sanding dust is so fine that a damp rag pushes it around, and a household vacuum without a good filter blows it back into the air. A crew that knows the work vacuums first, from the ceiling and light fixtures down to the floor, then wipes, then vacuums again. Ask them to describe the order. If the answer starts with mopping, move on.

  • Vents, ceiling fans and light fixtures done before floors
  • Tops of doors, ledges and window frames vacuumed before wiping
  • Inside cabinets and drawers, where dust hides
  • Floors washed more than once
  • A last look under good light at the end of the day

Who removes stickers, paint specks and adhesive?

New windows come with labels, new appliances with tape and new floors with residue. Some companies leave that to you. Perfect Services includes paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive removal in the final clean, along with glass, tracks and sills. Ask the same question of anyone you interview, and ask what they use on new stone or finished hardwood, since the wrong product can dull a surface that cost real money.

Will someone look at the site first?

A remodel in a 1940s house near Trade Street and a new build near the BMW plant are different animals. One has old dust in the walls behind the new ones, the other has square footage and a lot of windows. The free in-home estimate is a walk-through where the team sees how much fine dust there is, what the floors are made of and how soon you need it done. You can set it up through the form on our Greer booking page, by calling (864) 399-5168 or by texting (864) 666-8520.

How much does post construction cleaning cost in Greer?

Anyone who prices it by square footage alone is guessing. The factors are project size, how much fine dust the work created, how many phases you want, how many windows and glass surfaces there are, and how tight the deadline is. A closing or an inspection date might call for a larger crew on a single day. We never post dollar amounts. The exact price comes after the free estimate visit, with no commitment.

What about the schedule and timing?

Ask when they can start, how long they expect to be there and whether they can come back the next day for the touch-up. Dust does keep settling, so the best results come when the final clean is not rushed. A typical kitchen remodel might take a crew a day; a whole-house renovation or an addition takes longer, and we can often get an estimate scheduled within the week.

If you are working with a builder, ask whether the cleanup is already in the contract. Some general contractors bring their own crew, and some expect the homeowner to hire one. We work with both, including builders who need a clean before a final walk-through.

What should be done before the crew arrives?

A few things on your side make the cleaning go better. Ask the contractor to remove the dumpster, leftover materials and tools first, because a cleaner should not be hauling lumber. Confirm that water and power are on, and that the HVAC filter is new, since old filters hold dust that gets pushed back into the freshly cleaned rooms. If you are living in the house during the work, tell us which rooms were closed off with plastic; those usually stay cleaner and go faster. Finally, mention anything delicate, like unsealed stone or a new finish that the contractor told you to keep off for a few days.

Ask the company whether the same people will come back for the touch-up. Continuity helps, because the crew that did the first pass knows where dust was heaviest.

Do I need post construction cleaning if the contractor already swept?

Usually yes. Sweeping removes debris, not the fine dust inside cabinets, vents and on ledges. A final clean handles those.

Yes. Perfect Services can plan the rough clean, final clean and touch-up as one project once the team has seen the site.
