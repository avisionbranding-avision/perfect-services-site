Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Moore hosts

An Airbnb turnover cleaning checklist for Moore SC hosts: room-by-room resets, restock pass, damage photos and how to get a free estimate.

Perfect Services Team
November 17, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start with a quick walk to log damage before cleaning begins.
  • Work room by room and finish with a restock check.
  • Prices come after a free in-home estimate.

An Airbnb turnover in Moore usually has a hard edge: checkout at 11, check-in at 4, and a five-hour window where everything has to be reset. This checklist covers what a good turnover contains, in the order a team works through it, plus the extras that protect your reviews.

Moore (ZIP 29369, Spartanburg County) is a growing area south of Spartanburg along SC-290 and I-26, with newer subdivisions and short-term rentals within easy reach of the interstate. Hosts here often manage a property from a distance, which makes a repeatable list even more useful.

Before you start: the 60-second walk

The first thing a turnover team does is a quick walk through, looking for problems before touching anything: a broken lamp, a stain on the sofa, a missing remote. This is when the damage and missing-item report with photos is made. If you wait until the end, it is easy to miss something that was already there.

The reset, room by room

Kitchen

  • Run or unload the dishwasher and put everything back in its place
  • Wipe counters, the stovetop and appliance fronts
  • Clean the microwave and the fridge shelves, and check for leftovers
  • Empty trash and reset bins

Bedrooms

  • Strip beds, make them with fresh host-provided linens
  • Check under pillows and beds for left items
  • Dust the nightstands and lamp bases

Bathrooms

  • Clean and disinfect toilet, shower, tub and sink
  • Replace towels and restock toilet paper, soap and shampoo
  • Wipe mirrors streak-free, the thing guests photograph most

Living areas and floors

  • Fluff cushions, straighten throws and stage the room to match the listing photos
  • Vacuum and mop all floors, including under the sofa

The restock check

Hosts lose reviews over small things: no hand soap, one roll of toilet paper, dead batteries in the remote. A turnover checklist includes a supplies pass so the host knows what is running low before the next guest does. Keep a labeled shelf or bin for refills and the team can restock without calling you.

How long does a turnover take?

A one- or two-bedroom rental is often a couple of hours, and larger homes take longer. The first turnover is slower, because the team is learning where everything lives. After that, a written house list makes each reset predictable.

Holiday and winter notes

Late November through the New Year is busy for many hosts in the Upstate, with family visits and holiday weekend bookings, so book recurring slots early. Winter also means wet footprints at the entry, so ask for extra attention there.

Photos, linens and the things guests actually notice

Guests rarely complain about a spotless stovetop. They write about hair in the shower, a smell in the bedroom, or a missing item. The turnover list is built around those reviews. Hair and soap scum come first in the bathroom, and the bed linens should smell fresh and be wrinkle-free. If you provide a welcome basket or a stocked coffee station, add it to the list so it is reset each time.

Take a staging photo of each room when it looks right and keep it in a shared folder. The team can compare against it, which is the simplest way to avoid arguments later about where the pillows or the throw blanket belong.

Handling laundry

Linens are the biggest scheduling variable. If laundry is done on site, a turnover has to leave time for a wash and dry cycle. If linens are swapped from a stocked set and washed off site, the visit is faster. Decide which system fits your rental before you ask for an estimate, because it changes the time involved. Many Moore hosts keep two full linen sets per bed for this reason.

What to leave out for the team

  • A short written house guide and list of supplies
  • Access instructions, such as a lockbox or smart lock code
  • Spare linens and toilet paper in a labeled closet
  • A note about anything fragile or off-limits

When something is missing or broken

If the team finds a chipped dish, a stain on a rug or a missing remote, it goes into the report with a photo, and you decide what to do before the next guest arrives. Hosts who live out of town tell us this is the part they value most, because it replaces a long text thread with one clear message.

Keep your own list of replacement items in the house guide so a small fix like a new light bulb does not wait on a back and forth.
