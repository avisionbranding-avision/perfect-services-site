The three phases of post-construction cleaning in Gray Court

Post construction cleaning Gray Court SC in three phases: rough clean, final clean and touch-up. See what each includes and how to get an estimate.

Key takeaways

  • Post-construction cleaning works best in three passes: rough clean, final clean and a touch-up before move-in.
  • Drywall sanding dust is the main reason one pass is never enough.
  • Perfect Services prices the job only after a free in-home estimate that looks at square feet, dust level and your deadline.

Drywall dust is the first thing you notice after a build finishes in Gray Court. It settles on window sills, inside cabinets and along the top of door frames, and it comes back every time you open a door. That is why post construction cleaning in Gray Court is done in three phases instead of one long visit, and why homeowners who try a single pass often end up wiping the same ledges again a week later.

Here is how the work usually breaks down, and what a post-construction cleaning service in Gray Court does in each stage.

Phase one: the rough clean

The rough clean happens while the trades are still finishing, or right after they leave. The goal is to clear the heavy debris and bulk dust so later work does not grind it into the floors and fixtures.

  • Sweep and vacuum floors, including subfloor corners and stair edges
  • Remove packaging, stickers and loose scraps from rooms and closets
  • First wipe of window sills, ledges and trim
  • Clear dust from the tops of cabinets and the inside of drawers

This phase is not about shine. It is about getting the house to a point where painters, flooring crews and electricians are not working in a cloud of their own dust.

Phase two: the final clean

The final clean is the long one. Everything is done, the fixtures are in, and the team works through the home room by room. Fine dust gets wiped from every surface, windows and tracks are cleaned, and paint specks and adhesive are scraped away carefully so they do not scratch the glass or finish.

In bathrooms, new fixtures and the double vanity get polished and the mirrors are cleaned. In the kitchen, cabinet interiors, the range hood and appliance fronts come next. Light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans are cleaned because they hold dust long after the room looks done.

Floors get several passes. A first vacuum lifts the loose dust, and a wash comes after the other work, so the dust from the high surfaces is not tracked across a clean floor.

Phase three: the touch-up before move-in

Even after a thorough final clean, a house that has been closed up for a few days collects a light layer of dust again. The touch-up is a short visit just before the homeowner moves in or the inspection happens. It covers the small things: a final wipe of counters, a check of the bathrooms, fresh vacuum lines on the carpet and a walk-through against the room checklist.

Because many Gray Court projects are new homes or additions on rural lots south of Fountain Inn, there is often red clay on shoes and equipment, so the entry areas and floors near doors get extra attention in this last pass.

How long does each phase take?

There is no honest single number. A small bathroom remodel can be finished in a short visit, while a full new build takes a team most of a day or more across the phases. What changes the time is the size of the home, how much sanding was done, how many windows there are and how clean the builder left the site. Booking the phases together lets the team plan the days, and your closing or inspection date drives how large a team is needed.

What homeowners and builders can do to help

A few things make the cleaning go faster. Ask the crew to haul off scrap and boxes, keep the utilities on, and leave stickers on windows until the cleaners arrive. Contractors who finish a job in Laurens County and want a spotless handoff often book the final clean as soon as the last trade leaves. Frequently asked questions

Why does post-construction cleaning take more than one visit?

Dust keeps settling after the first pass, especially after drywall sanding. Splitting the work into rough clean, final clean and touch-up keeps the finished surfaces from being re-dusted.

Can I book only the final clean?

Yes. Many homeowners book just the final clean. Adding the rough clean or the touch-up depends on your project and timeline, and the free estimate will show which phases make sense.

Do you remove paint and sticker residue from windows?

Yes. Paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive are part of the post-construction clean, done carefully so the glass and frames are not scratched.
