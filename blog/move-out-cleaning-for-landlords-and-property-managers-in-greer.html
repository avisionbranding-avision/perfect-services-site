The turnover gap is where rentals lose money

An empty unit earns nothing. Between the last tenant's keys and the next tenant's lease, every day counts, and the cleaning is the step that most often slips because it is the last thing scheduled. If you own or manage rentals around Greer, a dependable move out cleaning service in Greer shortens that gap and keeps listing photos honest.

Where cleaning fits in the turnover order

The sequence matters more than the speed. Trades come first, then cleaning, then photos and showings. If the painter or the flooring crew arrives after the cleaners, you pay for the clean twice.

  1. Tenant moves out and returns keys.
  2. Walk the unit and note damage versus normal wear.
  3. Repairs, paint and any flooring work.
  4. Cleaning of the empty unit.
  5. Photos, showing and lease signing.

With post-repair dust on every ledge, it can make sense to book the post-construction cleaning team when a unit had heavy work, and the standard move-out clean for ordinary turns.

A checklist you can hand to any vendor

Property managers get better results when the scope is written down. This is the list our team works from in an empty home, and it doubles as your acceptance checklist:

  • Kitchen cabinets and drawers, inside and outside
  • Inside of the refrigerator, freezer, oven and microwave
  • Range hood, stovetop and backsplash degreased
  • Bathrooms: toilet base, grout, glass and vanity interiors
  • Closet shelves, rods and floors
  • Baseboards, doors, frames and light switches
  • Window sills, tracks and blinds
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped edge to edge

A short note on each unit helps: pets in the lease, smoking history, known problem areas. Greer rentals range from older homes near Trade Street to newer builds near the BMW plant and GSP Airport, and older fixtures and grout behave differently from newer ones.

Consistency is the real product here. A tenant who moves into a clean unit tends to keep it cleaner, and a unit that looks the same in every listing photo is easier to rent. That is why we suggest managers keep one written scope and attach it to every work order, even when the units differ. Add a few lines per property: appliances that stay, rooms that are off limits, anything with a special finish such as hardwood or stone. The team reads it before the visit, and any questions come back before the day, not during it.

It also helps to decide in advance what counts as a deposit deduction versus an owner expense. Many managers treat tenant-caused grease or neglect as a deduction and routine turnover cleaning as an operating cost, but that is a decision for you, your lease and South Carolina law. We keep the checklist and the photos available so you can document the condition you found.

Access is the detail that delays more turnovers than anything else. Decide how the team gets in: a lockbox, a key left with the office or the manager on site. Tell us whether utilities are on, since the team needs water and power for mops and appliances. If the unit has a gate code or a community office that closes early, mention that when booking.

Finally, think about the next unit. When one move-out happens, the neighbor's lease is often ending too. Ask us about scheduling two or three units in a single week with the same crew. We cannot promise capacity until we have seen the properties, but giving us the dates early is the best way to make it work.

How long a turn takes

A typical empty one- or two-bedroom can often be finished in a few hours by a team. Larger homes, heavy grease and long-term tenants take more. Since I-85, SC-14 and US-29 get you around Greer quickly, travel between your units is rarely the bottleneck, and we can usually discuss a slot within the week once we have seen the space.

One more practical point: keep the estimate visit and the cleaning date separate in your calendar. The visit tells us the scope; the clean happens after repairs. Managers who combine them end up rescheduling when a repair runs over. A line in your work order such as "clean after paint, before photos" keeps everyone on the same page, including your maintenance staff and the leasing agent who is waiting to list the unit.

If a tenant disputes a deduction, the written scope and the after-clean photos are what help. Hold on to both for each unit.
