Office cleaning checklist for small businesses in Boiling Springs

Office cleaning checklist for small businesses in Boiling Springs, SC: daily, weekly and monthly tasks, plus a free walkthrough before any price is given.

Perfect Services Team
November 24, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Split office cleaning into daily high-touch tasks, weekly floor work and monthly detail work.
  • Restrooms and the break room drive most complaints, so they come first.
  • Perfect Services prices only after a free walkthrough of your space.

A small office does not need a janitorial contract the size of a corporate campus, but it does need a plan. The offices that stay clean are the ones with a written checklist that separates what happens every day from what happens once a month. This one is built for small businesses in Boiling Springs, one of Spartanburg County's fastest-growing communities, where new subdivisions along SC-9 and I-85 keep bringing new storefronts, clinics and home-based offices.

Boiling Springs (ZIP 29316) is about 16 miles northeast of Greer, a 25-minute drive for our team. Below is the list we use as a starting point, which we adjust after walking the space.

The daily list

Daily tasks are about what people touch and what smells. They matter most for reception areas and shared rooms.

  • Desks, counters and workstations wiped
  • High-touch points: door handles, switches and keypads
  • Restrooms cleaned, disinfected and restocked
  • Break room counters, sink and the outside of the microwave and fridge
  • Trash and recycling removed
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped in entry and traffic areas

The weekly list

Weekly work catches what daily passes skip.

  • Glass doors and entry areas cleaned inside and out
  • Reception and waiting area dusted, including chair arms and tables
  • Full floor care: edges, under desks and behind doors
  • Break room appliances wiped more thoroughly
  • Door frames and wall smudges near switches

The monthly list

Monthly detail work keeps a space from slowly looking tired.

  • Vents and light fixtures dusted
  • Baseboards and corners wiped
  • Blinds and window sills cleaned
  • Chairs, cabinets and file units wiped down

Restrooms and the break room come first

Customers and staff judge a business by its restroom. In small offices it is usually shared, which means it takes heavier use than the square footage suggests. We restock soap, paper and trash liners and check for leaks or fixtures that need repair, then report them. The break room is the second priority: a microwave with old food or a sink with dishes sends a message even to people who never go in.

Entry and reception

The entry is the first thing visitors see. Glass doors show fingerprints and mats collect grit that scratches floors. Wiping the glass and vacuuming mats daily takes minutes and changes how the whole office feels. Reception chairs and tables need regular wipe-downs because visitors touch them.

Scheduling around your hours

Many small offices prefer cleaning before opening or after closing. Early evening visits work well for businesses that close around five, and an early morning visit suits shops that open mid-day. We build the schedule around when your staff leave and when the first customer arrives, and we agree on how the building is locked and alarmed.

What is not included unless you ask

Standard office cleaning covers surfaces, restrooms, floors, trash and the break room. Carpet shampooing, window cleaning on the outside and floor stripping and waxing are separate jobs. We list what is and is not included in the plan after the walkthrough, so the first invoice does not hold surprises.

Questions to ask any cleaning company

Ask who will be in your building and whether it is the same people each visit, how keys and alarm codes are handled, what happens if a visit is missed, and how you report a problem. Ask too whether the company is local. A team based about sixteen miles away in Greer can usually respond quickly if something is missed. A written checklist posted in the supply closet makes it easy to see what was done and to flag what was not.

One more habit helps: keep a short log by the door where staff can note a spill, a low supply or a spot that needs attention. The next visit starts with that list, and small problems never grow into complaints from customers.

How often should a small office in Boiling Springs be cleaned?

It depends on foot traffic. Many small offices do well with weekly or twice-weekly visits, with daily service for places with heavy public traffic.

Can cleaning be done after hours?

Yes. After-hours scheduling is common so staff and customers are not disturbed. We set the window during the walkthrough.

Do you clean carpet and hard floors differently?
