Touch the cabinet door nearest your stove. If it feels slightly tacky, that is cooked grease that has been building for months, and no amount of wiping the counters will remove it. A kitchen deep clean is the job that takes that layer off, along with everything else a weekly clean walks past.

Why the kitchen needs its own deep clean

Cooking sends fine grease into the air, and it settles on whatever is above and beside the stove. Mixed with household dust, it turns into a film that attracts more dust. The surfaces that suffer are cabinet fronts and handles, the range hood, the backsplash, the top of the refrigerator and the fan blade in the ceiling above. In a home with a gas range or a lot of frying, the layer builds faster.

Our full deep cleaning service list shows how the kitchen fits into a whole-house job. The kitchen also has the highest hand traffic in the house. Handles, drawer pulls, the refrigerator door and the faucet get touched by everyone, so they gather bacteria and fingerprints even when they look fine.

The order a pro team works in

  1. Dust the high spots. Light fixtures, the vent over the stove, and the tops of cabinets if they are reachable.
  2. Soften the grease. Spray degreaser on the range hood, backsplash and cabinet fronts and let it sit while other work gets done.
  3. Clean the appliances. Stovetop and burner areas, microwave interior, dishwasher front, refrigerator exterior and door seals.
  4. Wipe the cabinets. Fronts, edges and handles by hand, one door at a time.
  5. Counters, sink and faucet. Including the edge of the sink where the drain meets the basin.
  6. Grout lines. Scrub along the floor tile or behind the sink if the kitchen has tile.
  7. Floor last. Vacuum, then mop by hand, working toward the door.

Letting the degreaser sit is the part most people skip. Scrubbing right away takes twice the effort for half the result.

Kitchen deep clean checklist

  • Cabinet fronts and handles degreased
  • Range hood and filter area wiped
  • Backsplash cleaned, including behind the faucet
  • Stovetop and burner surrounds scrubbed
  • Appliance exteriors, handles and door seals wiped
  • Sink, drain edge and faucet base cleaned
  • Window sill above the sink wiped and the track cleaned
  • Baseboards and the toe-kick under the cabinets wiped
  • Floor vacuumed and mopped edge to edge

Cleaning the window above the sink matters more than people expect: the sill and the track collect dead insects, dust and moisture, and they are in plain view while you wash dishes. Add-ons come on top of that list: inside the oven, inside the refrigerator and inside the cabinets. They take real time, which is why they are priced separately. If you only want to do one yourself, start with the refrigerator: pull out the shelves, wash them in the sink and wipe the walls before you put the food back.

Keeping the kitchen clean afterward

What you do between visits decides how long the result lasts. Grease comes off easily when it is a day old and stubbornly when it is a season old, so a two-minute habit beats a two-hour scrub.

Wipe the stovetop after cooking while it is warm, and run a damp cloth over handles once a week. Replace or wash the range hood filter on a schedule. Recurring house cleaning then handles the rest, because the heavy layer is gone and what is left is routine work. A deep clean before Thanksgiving or Christmas is a common time to book, because the oven is in use more and guests end up in the kitchen.

How long does a kitchen deep clean take?

It depends on the size of the kitchen, how much grease has built up and whether the oven, refrigerator or cabinets are on the list. We confirm the time at the free in-home estimate.

Is the inside of the oven included in a deep clean?

Cabinet fronts, handles, range hood, backsplash and appliance exteriors are part of the deep cleaning list. The inside of the oven, refrigerator and cabinets are add-ons because they take extra time.

How often should a kitchen be deep cleaned?

Many households do it once or twice a year, often before the holidays or in spring. If you cook daily or fry often, a more frequent schedule keeps grease from building up.
