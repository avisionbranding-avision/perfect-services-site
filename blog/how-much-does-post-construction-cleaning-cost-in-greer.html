Why dust level matters more than square footage

People expect the size of the house to drive the price. In practice, the condition drives it just as much. A bathroom update with new fixtures leaves a lot less behind than an addition where walls were taped, mudded and sanded. The sanding dust gets into the HVAC returns, the tops of door frames, the inside of closets and the grooves of window tracks. If you have ever swept up a floor after a drywall job and found that the broom made the air hazy, you know the problem.

Our team checks for this in the first few minutes of the walkthrough. We run a hand along a ledge, look at the vents and open a few cabinets. It is quick, and it tells us more than a square footage number.

How long it takes. Most homeowners want a time and a price together, and the time follows the same factors. A small bathroom renovation is a short job. A whole-house remodel with an addition takes a team a much longer stretch, often split into a rough clean while trades are finishing and a final clean before move-in. We give a specific estimate for the hours after we see the home, not before.

Rough, final and touch-up: do you need all three?

This is where a lot of quotes quietly differ. If one company quotes a single clean and another quotes three phases, they are not comparing the same thing. The usual structure is:

  • Rough clean: after the heavy work, removing debris and the first layer of dust so trades can keep working.
  • Final clean: after the punch list, every surface, cabinet interior, window and floor.
  • Touch-up: a last pass right before move-in or the inspection, catching smudges and anything the final trades left behind.

Homeowners finishing a remodel often need only the final clean and touch-up. Builders with a schedule to meet tend to book all three. You can read how this plays out for a general contractor in our final-clean guide for Greer builders.

What you should expect to be included

When you compare quotes for post-construction cleaning in Greer, SC, ask what the price includes. Ours covers fine dust removal from surfaces, ledges and trim; inside cabinets, drawers and closets; windows, glass, tracks and sills; paint specks, sticker residue and adhesive; light fixtures, vents and ceiling fans; bathrooms and new fixtures polished; and floors vacuumed and washed in multiple passes. A last touch-up pass comes before move-in.

Use this as a checklist when you read someone else's quote:

  • Does it say inside cabinets and drawers, or only the outside?
  • Are windows and tracks listed?
  • Is residue removal (stickers, adhesive, paint) listed?
  • Does it say how many passes on the floors?
  • Is there a final walk-through or touch-up?

A few ways to keep the cost sensible

You cannot change the dust, but you can reduce the work. Ask the contractor to remove debris and bag leftover material before the final clean. Let the trades finish completely, since cleaning before the last painter or tile setter only means paying for it twice. Remove plastic coverings if you can. And decide whether you want a deep cleaning afterward for the rest of the house, since remodel dust often travels to rooms you did not touch.
