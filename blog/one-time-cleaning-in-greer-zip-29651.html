What does one visit actually include?

The team works room by room and finishes each one before moving on, so you never come home to a half-done house. The order is the same in a ranch, a two-story or a townhome, though the time each room takes will differ. In every home that means the same core list:

  • Kitchen cleaned and degreased, including counters, sink, backsplash and the outside of appliances
  • Bathrooms cleaned and disinfected, with mirrors and glass polished
  • Bedrooms and living areas dusted, including baseboards and door frames
  • Floors vacuumed and mopped
  • Trash emptied

Inside the oven, inside the fridge and inside the cabinets are optional add-ons. Say so during the estimate and they go into the plan, so nobody is surprised on the day.

Is a single visit enough, or do I need a recurring plan?

It depends on what you are trying to fix. A single visit is the right tool for a one-off event: the family arriving for the holidays, a party the weekend before, a showing, or a first look at how a cleaning service works. It is a poor tool if you expect the house to stay at that level for a month. Bathrooms and kitchens start collecting life again the day after. If you find yourself booking one-time visits every few weeks, that is usually a sign a recurring schedule would suit you better, and our one-time house cleaning service page explains how the two compare.

How long does it take?

What should I do before the team arrives?

You do not need to clean first, which is the part people find hardest to believe. A short prep makes the visit go further, though:

  1. Pick up toys, laundry and anything on the floor so every surface can be reached.
  2. Clear the kitchen counters and the bathroom vanity of personal items.
  3. Tell the team about anything delicate, such as a stone counter or a piece of furniture with a finish you want treated carefully.
  4. Mention pets, and let us know where they will be during the visit.

One more thing worth saying out loud: tell us what you will be using the house for afterward. A dinner for ten asks for a different focus than a showing, where we spend more time on entryways, glass and light fixtures, or a post-party reset, where the kitchen and the floors carry most of the work. A clear goal lets the team spend its hours where you will notice them.

Spots people forget to mention are the ones we reach on the first pass anyway: door frames at hand height, cabinet handles, the track of a sliding door and the baseboards behind the couch. Those are what make a house feel finished instead of just tidy.
