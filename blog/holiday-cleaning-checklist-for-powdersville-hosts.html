Holiday cleaning checklist for Powdersville hosts

Holiday cleaning checklist for Powdersville, SC hosts: what to clean before guests arrive, a week-by-week plan and how one-time cleaning pricing works.

Perfect Services Team
November 18, 2026 · 4 min read

Key takeaways

  • Start with the rooms guests use most: kitchen, bathroom, entry and living area.
  • Book holiday cleaning early because holiday weeks fill up.
  • Pricing comes after a free in-home estimate based on size, condition and add-ons.

The turkey is the easy part of hosting. The hard part is the guest bathroom you forgot, the fingerprints on the fridge and the pile by the front door. If you are hosting in Powdersville this season, you can break the work into a four-week plan so the last weekend is for cooking and not scrubbing. This checklist is how we help hosts book a one-time house cleaning in Powdersville that actually covers the rooms guests see.

A countdown that works

Three weeks out

Declutter. Clear entryway shoes, mail and the extra chairs from the dining room. Anything you will not use during the visit goes in a closet. A cleaner can only clean surfaces that are clear.

Two weeks out

Do the jobs that take longest: inside the oven, the fridge and the pantry. These are better done before groceries pile in. If you do not want to do these yourself, they are add-ons to a one-time visit.

One week out

Book the professional clean. Many Powdersville homes are newer and open plan, so the kitchen, dining and living area are one big visible space. That is where the team spends most of the time.

The day before

Touch up. Fresh hand towels, a quick wipe of the guest bathroom, and trash out. Leave the floors for the crew's visit if possible.

What guests see first

  • Entry: floor, mat and door glass without muddy prints
  • Guest bathroom: toilet, sink, mirror, faucet and a clean shower
  • Kitchen: counters, sink, stovetop, appliance fronts and handles
  • Dining table and chairs, including chair rungs where crumbs collect
  • Living room: dusted surfaces, vacuumed rugs, wiped light switches
  • Bedrooms for overnight guests: bed linens, nightstands, under-bed edges

Hosting and cleaning after the party

A lot of hosts forget that the second clean is the one that saves Monday. After a full house, you will have crumbs under the table, drink rings on wood and trash in the garage. A post-event visit puts the house back to normal, and it can be the same crew that cleaned beforehand. If you are not sure which cleaning you need, a free estimate helps you decide.

Powdersville is a fast-growing community with a lot of new homes along SC-153, and many of those homes have open floor plans with large windows. Fingerprints and dust on glass show quickly in winter sun, so ask about window and mirror polishing as part of the visit. New homes also often have builder dust in vents, and a deep cleaning can handle that before the holidays.

A realistic plan for the host who is short on time

Not everyone has three weeks. If you find out on Monday that the family is arriving on Thursday, simplify. Pick three rooms: the kitchen, the guest bathroom and the room where people will sit. Do those well and let the rest be tidy instead of detailed. A one-time clean can cover those rooms first and then the rest as time allows.

Here is how we usually suggest dividing the work between you and the crew.

  • You: declutter, set out towels and bedding, move what you do not want touched
  • Crew: kitchen degreasing, bathrooms, dusting, floors, glass
  • You: last-minute touches, fresh flowers or candles if you like them

Pets deserve a note. If the dog or cat will meet guests, vacuum furniture and the stairs the day before, since hair collects fast. Tell the crew if any room has a pet that should not be let out. We also check the entry for muddy paw prints and the laundry area for towels that need washing before they can be used again.

Finally, remember the leftovers. Clear a shelf in the fridge ahead of time, and wipe spills as they happen. Sticky fridge shelves are one of the first things a visiting relative sees when they put a dish away.

Weather matters at this time of year as well. Wet leaves and early cold bring mud and grit to the entry, so ask guests to use a mat and have a small basket for shoes. It keeps the floors you just paid to clean in good shape through the whole visit.
