Deep cleaning in Greer: floors, baseboards and edges

Deep cleaning floors Greer SC homeowners ask about: how we reset baseboards, grout, edges and hard floors, a four-step method and free estimates.

Key takeaways

  • Edges are done by hand, because machines and mops cannot reach them.

Down on your hands and knees, a floor tells the story of a house. The center of the room is usually fine, because that is where the mop goes. It is the first inch beside the wall, the grout in the kitchen and the top of the baseboard that show how long it has been since a deep clean. In Greer, with red clay on shoes and humid summers that keep grime from drying out, those low spots build up faster than most people expect.

What counts as "deep" on a floor

A regular clean vacuums and mops. A deep cleaning of floors in Greer, SC goes further. We hand-wipe baseboards and door frames, scrub grout lines in the kitchen and bathrooms, clean behind and around the toilet base, and wash the edges of hard floors by hand. The goal is to get dirt off, not spread it. That is the same Brazilian detail-first method we use in every home, applied to the part of the house closest to the ground.

A four-step method for floors

Step 1: Dry first

Before any water touches the floor, we vacuum or dust mop. Wetting grit turns it into paste, and that paste is what leaves gray lines along the wall. On carpet, the vacuum goes in slow overlapping passes with a crevice tool along the edge.

Step 2: Baseboards and frames

Baseboards are wiped top to bottom with a damp cloth, including the top edge where dust sits. Door frames and thresholds follow. Corners are done with a small brush so the dirt lifts out of the groove instead of smearing into it.

Step 3: Grout and the hard-water spots

Grout lines in the kitchen and bathrooms are scrubbed with a brush, and the floor around the toilet base is cleaned, since that is the spot that holds the most. This is the slowest part of the visit, which is why it is the part regular cleans do not reach.

Step 4: Wash and finish

Hard floors are washed by hand along the walls, then the open middle is mopped. We work out toward the exit so you do not step onto a wet floor. Finally, we check the edge from a low angle to see what a visitor would see.

Signs your floors and edges are ready for a deep clean

  • A gray line along the wall after you mop
  • Dark grout that does not lighten with a normal clean
  • Baseboards that leave dust on your finger
  • Crumbs and hair stuck in the corners
  • A floor that looks dull even right after cleaning

What you can do to help us reach the edges

Floor work goes faster when the floor is clear. Pick up rugs you can lift, move laundry baskets and shoes, and tell us which furniture can be moved and which should stay. Let us know about wood floors that have a special finish, and about any spots where a pet has had accidents. A clear floor lets us wash along the walls in one pass and not work around objects.

It is also worth deciding what you want done at the same visit. Many Greer homeowners add the oven or the inside of the fridge, since the team is already working at floor level in the kitchen. Others ask for the stairs and the front entry, where grit gathers most. A short list on the day of the estimate keeps the plan simple, and our deep cleaning service in Greer team can confirm what fits into one visit.

Frequently asked questions

Do you scrub grout during a deep clean?

Yes. Grout lines in the kitchen and bathrooms are scrubbed as part of deep cleaning. Heavily stained or sealed-over grout is discussed at the estimate.

Can a deep clean damage hardwood floors?

We use a lightly damp hand wash on hardwood and avoid standing water. Tell us your floor finish at the estimate and we plan around it.

How often should baseboards get a deep clean?

Many households do a deep clean once or twice a year and keep baseboards fresh between with regular visits that touch them up on a rotation.
