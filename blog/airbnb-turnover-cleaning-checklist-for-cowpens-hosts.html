Before you clean anything: the two-minute walkthrough

Walk every room with your phone before you touch a cloth. You are looking for three things: damage, missing items and anything the guest left. Take one photo of each problem where it sits. A chipped mug or a cigarette-burn mark is far easier to sort out with the platform when the photo shows the time you took it.

Our crews begin the same way, and it ends up as a short damage and missing-item report with photos. For hosts who live out of town, that report is often the only set of eyes on the house between stays.

The turnover checklist, in working order

Dust falls, water drips and floors get walked on. The order below keeps you from cleaning anything twice.

  1. Linens first. Strip the beds, start the wash and let the machine run while you work. Host-provided sheets go back on at the end.
  2. Air the house. Open windows or run the fans. Stale air is the first thing the next guest notices.
  3. Trash and recycling. Empty every can, then reset the bins with fresh liners.
  4. Kitchen reset. Dishes into the dishwasher, then counters, sink, stovetop, microwave and appliance fronts. Check the fridge for leftovers.
  5. Bathrooms. Toilet, shower, tub, mirror and vanity, with the floor last. Look behind the toilet and at the shower drain for hair.
  6. Dusting, high to low. Shelves, lamps, headboards and window sills before any floor work.
  7. Floors. Vacuum, then mop, working toward the door you leave through.
  8. Stage and photograph. Pillows, towels, remote controls and welcome notes go back to match your listing photos.

Spots that cost hosts reviews

Most complaints we hear about are small and repeatable. Hair in the shower corner. A greasy handle on the oven. Dust on the top of the headboard. A sticky patch under the coffee maker. Crumbs in the sofa seams. None of these take long, but each one needs to be on a written list or it gets skipped on a rushed day.

Red clay is also a real factor in this part of the Upstate. Guests who hike, hunt or work outside bring it in on boots, and it stains light grout and pale rugs if it sits. Wipe entry floors early in the turnover and check the mat by the door.

One more habit separates hosts who rarely get cleanliness complaints from those who do: they clean for the guest's first walk-through, not for their own. Stand at the front door and look at what a stranger sees first. Is the entry clear? Is there a smell? Does the kitchen counter look empty and wiped? Fix those three before anything else and the rest of the house gets the benefit of the doubt.
