Hair on the floor is the part you see. The rest settles into places a vacuum passes over:

Why the smell stays after you clean

Pet odor is almost never in the air. It sits in a few spots, and a candle does not fix any of them:

  • The floor along the walls, where a dog lies down and cat litter gets tracked.
  • Baseboards near the pet bed or the feeding corner.
  • Bathroom floors and the base of the toilet, if litter or pet baths happen there.
  • The door your dog uses, where wet paws and nose marks pile up after a summer rain.
  • Upholstery edges and the lower part of doors and walls.

Greer's humid summers and the red clay that paws carry in make this worse in July and August, and spring pollen adds another layer of fine dust that sticks to fur. After a deep cleaning the home smells like very little, which is the point.

Hair versus dander

They are not the same problem. Hair is large and visible, and a good vacuum with a clean filter picks most of it up. Dander is a fine, nearly invisible flake that floats, settles on flat surfaces and returns a day after dusting. That is why we dust high to low with a damp cloth, vents and fan blades first and baseboards last, and why the floors are always the final step.

What to do before the team arrives

Pets make a visit smoother or harder depending on how we prepare. A short list helps:

  1. Tell us about every animal, including any that bolt, bite or hide. A cat that slips out the door is a bigger problem than a hairy sofa.
  2. Decide where pets will stay during the visit: a bedroom, a crate or a yard.
  3. Pick up toys, leashes, bowls and chews so the floors are clear.
  4. Wash pet bedding the day before if you can, since a wet bed cannot be cleaned around.
  5. Point out accident spots or stains so we can plan the right approach.

If anyone in the home has allergies, mention it. Dust that is removed rather than moved is the difference, and we can plan gentler products for sensitive households.

Cats, dogs and the rest of the house

Different animals leave different traces. Dogs track in clay and moisture, and their hair collects along walls and under furniture. Cats scatter litter dust and shed fine fur that floats and lands on shelves and fan blades. Small pets like rabbits or birds leave dander and seed hulls. When we know which animals live in the house, we adjust the order of the clean and the amount of time at the floor edges, the bathroom and the vents.

Keeping it up afterward

A deep clean resets the baseline, and then upkeep is simpler. Pet owners tend to do best with a weekly or biweekly visit afterward, because the hair and dander come back at the same rate. A damp microfiber cloth along the baseboards each week, a vacuum with a pet attachment on furniture and a washable mat at the door make a big difference. Do I need to remove my pets during the visit?
