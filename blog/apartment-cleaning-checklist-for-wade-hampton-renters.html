Apartment cleaning checklist for Wade Hampton renters

A room-by-room apartment cleaning Wade Hampton SC checklist for renters, with move-out deposit tips and how free estimates work with Perfect Services.

Key takeaways

  • Clean an apartment in a fixed order, from the kitchen to the floors, so you never redo work.
  • Small units hide grease, hard water and dust in the same few spots.
  • Perfect Services gives a price only after a free in-home estimate.

Renting in Wade Hampton usually means a small kitchen, one or two bathrooms and carpet you do not own. That is exactly why the cleaning checklist has to be tighter than for a house: there is less space, so every dirty spot is closer to where you live. Below is the order we use when we clean apartments and condos along US-29 and near I-385, and it works just as well for a renter who wants to do the job alone on a Saturday.

Before you begin: three quick setup steps

  1. Open a window or run the bathroom fan so fumes and moisture can leave.
  2. Gather what you need in one basket: microfiber cloths, an all-purpose cleaner, a toilet brush, a scrub sponge and a vacuum.
  3. Pick up everything off the floor and counters first. Cleaning around clutter doubles the time.

Kitchen: grease and the spots near the stove

Apartment kitchens are small, so cooking residue lands on everything. Start with the stovetop and the wall behind it, then the microwave, the front of the fridge and the dishwasher, then the sink and counters. The cabinet fronts closest to the stove are often the stickiest, so wipe those before you finish. The inside of the fridge door shelves and the rubber seal are the places that collect the most that nobody remembers to touch.

Bathroom: hard water and mildew

Most apartment bathrooms have a tub or a shower with a small window, or none at all, which means moisture stays. Spray the tub or shower and let the cleaner sit while you clean the toilet, including the base and behind it. Then scrub the tub, rinse, wipe the mirror and finish with the vanity and faucet. Wade Hampton's humid summers make the grout line along the bottom of the tub the first place mildew shows up.

Living area and bedroom: dust, not mess

  • Dust from top to bottom: shelves, TV stand, window sills, then baseboards
  • Wipe light switches, door handles and the thermostat
  • Clean the track of the balcony or patio door and wipe the glass
  • Strip and make the bed, and vacuum the edges of the mattress area where dust gathers
  • Empty the trash and take out the recycling

Baseboards and door frames are easy to skip, and they are also the first thing a leasing agent checks during a move-out inspection. Our team works through them on every apartment visit, using a damp cloth rather than a spray so nothing drips onto the carpet.

Floors last, always

Vacuum carpet in slow overlapping passes, including along the walls and under the sofa. Then mop hard floors, starting in the farthest corner and backing toward the door. Floors go last because everything above them drops dust and crumbs onto them.

Protecting your deposit

If you rent, the most useful thing you can do is clean to the standard an inspector will look at. Check the oven, the inside of the fridge, the bathroom fan cover and the window tracks. Wipe the baseboards and door frames, fill nothing, and take dated photos once you finish. Keep your lease handy so you know what the community expects, since requirements differ from one building to another and we cannot speak for any landlord.
